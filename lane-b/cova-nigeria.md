## One-liner

Cova co-founder Olu'yomi Ojo chose to shut the Nigerian asset-tracking platform rather than keep spending without a clear path to revenue or profitability; operations ended on February 10, 2024, after at least $800,000 raised and a global user base only in the thousands.

## What happened

Cova let people aggregate bank accounts, investments, crypto wallets and property while leaving instructions for beneficiaries. On January 23, 2024, co-founders Olu'yomi Ojo and Yomi Osamiluyi emailed users that the company would cease operations on February 10 and refund subscriptions by February 13.

The product had raised at least $800,000 and attracted thousands of users globally, but it did not gain enough traction. Ojo told investors that continuing would consume funds without a clear path to decent revenue or profitability.

## Key decisions / turning points

- In January 2024, Ojo and Osamiluyi decided to wind down instead of financing an open-ended search for product-market fit.
- They set a specific February 10 closure date and a February 13 refund deadline, giving paying users a defined offboarding path.
- The decision followed two years of weak traction despite at least $800,000 in funding and a user base measured in the thousands.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A useful product category is not the same as a viable business. When adoption, trust and integration demands rise faster than revenue, preserving remaining capital can be more responsible than extending runway without evidence of a profitable market.
