## One-liner

SOWIT, a Casablanca-based precision agriculture and agrifinance startup founded in 2017, raised $2.12M from the International Finance Corporation (IFC) in November 2024 to expand digital farming tools and financing access to smallholder farmers across North and West Africa.

## What happened

SOWIT, founded by Hamza Rkha Chaham and Hamza Bendahou, built a precision agriculture platform combining AI, satellite imagery, and IoT sensors to help farmers optimize yields and resource use, paired with agrifinance tools connecting farmers to financing they otherwise couldn't access. This dual focus — technology plus financial access — addressed two linked barriers facing smallholder farmers simultaneously: a lack of precise agronomic data and a lack of capital to act on it.

The November 2024 $2.12M investment from IFC was earmarked to fuel product development and deeper market penetration across the company's existing footprint in Morocco, Senegal, Tunisia, and France, where SOWIT already served more than 22,000 farmers. As part of its growth phase, the company launched "Takamoul Al Falah," an integrated digital platform combining financing, continuous technical assistance, and regenerative agricultural practices, deployed across more than 3,000 hectares with a stated target of reaching 40,000 hectares by 2026.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Combined precision-agriculture technology (AI, satellite imagery, IoT) with agrifinance rather than offering either in isolation, addressing both the information gap and the capital gap that smallholder farmers face together.
- Expanded operations across four countries (Morocco, Senegal, Tunisia, France) from a Casablanca base rather than remaining single-market, building a regional West/North African footprint alongside a European presence.
- Attracted development-finance capital (IFC) specifically, rather than relying solely on commercial venture capital, aligning funding sources with the company's financial-inclusion and food-security-oriented mission.
- Launched "Takamoul Al Falah" as an integrated platform bundling financing, technical assistance, and regenerative practices together, rather than offering these as separate disconnected services, to drive higher farmer adoption and retention.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SOWIT demonstrates that agritech ventures serving smallholder farmers in North and West Africa can scale by combining precision-farming technology with the financing access farmers need to act on that data — technology alone, without a path to capital, often can't translate into adopted practice at the farm level. The company's ability to attract IFC development-finance capital for regional expansion also illustrates how agritech platforms addressing both productivity and financial inclusion can appeal to impact-oriented investors even when the underlying markets (smallholder agriculture in North/West Africa) may see less commercial VC interest than urban fintech or e-commerce.
