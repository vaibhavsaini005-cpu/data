## One-liner

Collect Africa co-founder Abraham Ojes announced on July 29, 2025 that the Nigerian merchant-payments product would close by August 31 so the team could move all resources to stablecoin platform Autospend, after Collect had processed more than $4 million for 5,000 registered businesses.

## What happened

Ojes and Wale Martins founded Collect Africa in 2021 to give small businesses a single dashboard for bank transfers, POS, QR codes, payment links, and direct debits. The platform registered 5,000 businesses, processed more than 50,000 transactions worth over $4 million, and reached about $5,000 in monthly revenue.

By 2025, the founders concluded that global, instant money movement presented a larger opportunity than the local merchant dashboard. They launched Autospend around stablecoin payments, savings, and expense management. On July 29, Ojes said Collect would be sunset on August 31 and all resources redirected to Autospend; users were told to withdraw remaining balances before closure.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Ojes treated merchant-payment experience as evidence for a broader cross-border stablecoin opportunity.
- Built Autospend as a separate product before announcing the legacy service's closure.
- Set an August 31 deadline and instructed users to withdraw balances, creating an orderly transition.
- Concentrated the whole team on the new platform rather than operating Collect and Autospend in parallel.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Collect Africa is a clean example of a founder-led resource-allocation pivot. The original product had customers, transaction volume, and revenue, but the founders still chose to close it when they believed the adjacent global market was materially larger. The explicit sunset date prevented the old product from becoming a permanent drag on the new bet.
