## One-liner

Edukoya founder and CEO Honey Ogundeyi chose in February 2025 to close the Nigerian edtech and return remaining investor capital rather than keep funding a synchronous-learning model that had reached 80,000 students but could not scale economically.

## What happened

Edukoya launched in 2021 to deliver online tutoring and K–12 learning content across Africa, backed by a $3.5 million pre-seed round. The platform ultimately served more than 80,000 students, answered over 15 million questions, and delivered thousands of live classes each day.

Despite that usage, the company told stakeholders in February 2025 that connectivity constraints, limited device access, weak household purchasing power, and broader macroeconomic pressure prevented its synchronous-learning model from reaching venture-scale economics. After exploring partnerships, acquisitions, and business-model changes, Ogundeyi elected to wind down and return the remaining capital instead of consuming it while chasing scale.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Ogundeyi built around synchronous tutoring, a model whose delivery requirements ultimately clashed with local connectivity and device constraints.
- Explored partnerships, M&A, and alternative business models before deciding the market was not ready at the necessary scale.
- Chose an orderly February 2025 shutdown while capital remained rather than extending the company's runway without a credible path to scale.
- Returned undisclosed remaining funds to investors after having raised $3.5 million.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Edukoya separates educational impact from venture viability. Large engagement numbers did not overcome the infrastructure and affordability constraints embedded in live online instruction. The disciplined choice to return capital shows that shutting down can preserve stakeholder value when usage has not translated into a scalable economic model.
