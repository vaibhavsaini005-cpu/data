## One-liner

CEO Njavwa Mutambo moved Caantin from restaurant procurement into AI data analysis after customers asked for dashboards rather than another ordering tool; by April 2024, the five-person company had reached its highest monthly revenue, with 12 North American customers and more than 30 overall.

## What happened

The Kenyan company began as Kibanda, digitising supplies for small restaurants, and later became TopUpMama, a B2B procurement platform for larger hospitality customers. Those customers were comfortable ordering through existing tools but wanted centralised visibility into payment cycles, retention and other operating data.

Mutambo treated that feedback as the basis for a product change. The company rebranded as Caantin and shifted to AI software that analyses and visualises business data, then expanded into the United States in late 2023.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Mutambo chose to solve the analytics problem customers said they would pay for, rather than keep pushing the capital-intensive procurement model.
- The company reduced to five employees and began targeting US manufacturers and logistics businesses in late 2023.
- By May 2024, Caantin had 12 North American customers and more than 30 in total; US customers generated most of its revenue, and April 2024 was its highest-revenue month to date.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Customer workarounds can reveal the real product. Caantin's buyers did not primarily need a new transaction channel; they needed decision-ready data. Moving toward that demand also replaced an inventory-heavy model with software economics and opened a higher-value market.
