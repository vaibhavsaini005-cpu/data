## One-liner

Ghanaian business-banking fintech Float, backed by Tiger Global and Y Combinator after raising a $17M seed round in January 2022, collapsed in 2023 amid allegations that CEO Jesse Ghansah forged transaction receipts and misappropriated client deposits, including losing the Ghana cedi equivalent of billions in risky FX trades.

## What happened

Float launched to provide cash-flow financing and business banking tools for commerce companies across Ghana and Africa, positioning itself as infrastructure for businesses needing working capital and payment management rather than a consumer product. The company's January 2022 $17M seed round, backed by Tiger Global and Y Combinator among others, made it one of the more prominently-funded fintech startups in Ghana at the time.

By September 2023, investigative reporting revealed that CEO Jesse Ghansah had allegedly sent fabricated receipts and misrepresented the status of client fund transfers, with one founder who had deposited millions of dollars with Float describing being told false information about where the money had gone. Separate reporting detailed how Float lost the equivalent of roughly N5 billion (Nigerian naira) of client deposits through risky, unauthorized foreign-exchange trades. At least one affected party filed complaints with Ghana's Economic and Financial Crimes Commission and Interpol against the CEO, and the company shut down abruptly amid the fallout.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Took custody of client business deposits as a core part of its cash-flow financing model, creating direct exposure and opportunity for the kind of fund misappropriation that ultimately destroyed the company.
- According to reporting, engaged in unauthorized, risky FX trading with client deposits rather than holding those funds in the manner customers understood — a decision that directly caused the reported N5bn loss.
- Reportedly fabricated documentation (receipts, transfer confirmations) to obscure the fund losses from clients and possibly investors for a period before the situation became public, compounding the damage by delaying detection and any possible remediation.
- The collapse triggered formal criminal complaints (EFCC, Interpol) against leadership, escalating the failure from a business collapse into an active fraud investigation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Float's collapse is a stark illustration of the acute governance risk in fintechs that take custody of client funds: when a company holding customer deposits engages in unauthorized, high-risk activity with that capital and then conceals the resulting losses through fabricated documentation, the failure mode is fraud rather than ordinary business underperformance — and it can happen even at well-funded, high-profile startups backed by top-tier investors like Tiger Global and Y Combinator. Investor due diligence and prestigious backing are not substitutes for independent custody controls and financial oversight when a fintech's core function involves holding client money.
