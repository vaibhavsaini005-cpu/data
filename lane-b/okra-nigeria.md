## One-liner

Okra, a Lagos-based open banking API startup that had raised $16.5 million from TLcom Capital and Susa Ventures, shut down in May 2025 after rising cloud and server costs made its business model unsustainable, with the company returning an estimated $4-5.5 million in unused funds to investors.

## What happened

Okra built open banking infrastructure connecting Nigerian banks to third-party applications via APIs, allowing fintechs and other businesses to access customer financial data with consent — a model similar to Plaid in the U.S. The company had raised a total of $16.5 million across funding rounds from TLcom Capital and Susa Ventures, positioning itself as foundational infrastructure for Nigeria's open banking ecosystem.

The company's shutdown, announced in May 2025, followed the earlier departure of co-founder Fara Ashiru Jituboh, who left to join UK-based startup Kernel. Okra's leadership cited rising cloud and server infrastructure costs as a central driver behind the decision to cease operations — a cost structure that open banking API providers are particularly exposed to given the continuous, real-time data-syncing demands of the model. Rather than continuing to operate at a loss or seeking a distressed acquisition, Okra opted for an orderly wind-down, returning an estimated $4 million to $5.5 million in unused investor funds.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built infrastructure-heavy open banking APIs with continuous cloud/server costs that scaled with usage, exposing the company to margin pressure as infrastructure costs rose without a corresponding path to sufficient revenue.
- Lost a co-founder (Fara Ashiru Jituboh) to a competing UK startup prior to the shutdown, a leadership change that preceded the company's final wind-down decision.
- Chose an orderly shutdown and return of unused capital to investors rather than pursuing a distressed fundraise, pivot, or fire-sale acquisition.
- Operated in Nigeria's open banking space without the regulatory tailwinds or aggregator-scale customer base that supported comparable infrastructure plays in other markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Okra's shutdown illustrates a structural risk specific to open banking and API infrastructure startups: the underlying cloud and server costs of continuously syncing financial data at scale can outpace revenue growth well before the business reaches sustainable unit economics, particularly in markets where paying customers for infrastructure-layer products remain a small population of fintechs rather than a broad enterprise base. The company's choice to wind down in an orderly fashion and return unused capital, rather than limping along or pursuing a rushed pivot, also reflects a disciplined failure pattern — preserving investor goodwill and capital rather than destroying additional value in a prolonged decline.
