## One-liner

Mada, a Jordanian fintech founded in 2018 by Mohammad Merie and Issa Ramadan offering Sharia-compliant buy-now-pay-later services, raised $1.55M in pre-seed funding led by Vision Ventures in September 2025 to move from Central Bank of Jordan's regulatory sandbox toward full licensing and regional expansion.

## What happened

Mada built a Sharia-compliant BNPL platform letting Jordanian consumers split purchases into four interest-free installments, framing its product around Islamic finance principles to serve a market where religiously-compliant financial products carry particular importance for a meaningful share of consumers. The company operated within the Central Bank of Jordan's regulatory sandbox — a supervised testing environment for new financial products — positioning itself to transition to full licensing once its testing phase concluded.

The September 2025 $1.55M pre-seed round, led by Vision Ventures with participation from D-Investments, is earmarked for continued product development, strengthening Mada's technology infrastructure, and supporting its path toward full regulatory licensing and expansion beyond Jordan into the broader region. Notably, Mada had already been operating for roughly seven years (since 2018) before this funding round, suggesting a longer gestation period building toward regulatory readiness and market validation before pursuing institutional capital at scale.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built the BNPL product specifically around Sharia compliance rather than a conventional interest-based installment structure, directly addressing religious and cultural preferences relevant to a meaningful share of Jordanian and regional consumers.
- Operated within the Central Bank of Jordan's regulatory sandbox before raising this round, using the sandbox period to test and validate the product under direct regulatory supervision ahead of pursuing full licensing.
- Sustained roughly seven years of operation (2018-2025) before this funding round, a notably long runway suggesting a deliberate, patient approach to regulatory readiness rather than a rush to scale before compliance groundwork was complete.
- Positioned the new capital explicitly around the transition from regulatory sandbox to full licensing, treating regulatory approval as a distinct, fundable milestone rather than an afterthought to product growth.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Mada demonstrates that building financial products around genuine religious and cultural compliance requirements (Sharia-compliant BNPL) — rather than adapting a generic Western fintech model — can be a meaningful differentiator in Middle Eastern consumer finance markets where such compliance directly affects product adoption. Its extended pre-funding operating period within a regulatory sandbox also illustrates a patient-capital pattern worth recognizing: some fintechs deliberately delay large institutional raises until regulatory pathways are clearer, reducing the risk of raising capital for a business model that later proves unlicensable or non-compliant.
