## One-liner

Qashio, a Dubai-based corporate spend-management fintech founded in 2021, raised $19.8 million in debt and equity in May 2025 to expand into Saudi Arabia — despite already being profitable, having posted over $1.2 million in profit in Q1 2025 after three consecutive years of 800% year-over-year revenue growth.

## What happened

Qashio, founded by Armin Moradi and Jonathan Lau, built a corporate spend-management platform letting businesses control expenses, issue corporate cards, and automate financial workflows, expanding to operate across 22 countries from its Dubai base. The company distinguished itself with an unusually strong financial profile for a fintech at its stage: three consecutive years of 800% year-over-year revenue growth culminating in over $1.2 million in profit during Q1 2025 alone — genuine profitability rather than the growth-at-all-costs, cash-burning trajectory common among venture-backed fintechs.

The May 2025 $19.8 million round, blending debt and equity, was raised specifically to fund expansion into Saudi Arabia — the GCC's largest economy — and to broaden Qashio's B2B loyalty program ecosystem. Notably, the company pursued this capital raise despite already being profitable, a deliberate choice reflecting confidence that additional capital could accelerate market-entry timing and competitive positioning in Saudi Arabia rather than a necessity driven by cash-flow pressure.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Achieved genuine profitability ($1.2M+ in Q1 2025 alone) before pursuing this growth round, giving the company negotiating leverage and de-risking the raise relative to typical loss-making growth-stage fintechs.
- Chose to raise capital despite being profitable, prioritizing accelerated Saudi Arabia market entry over organic, self-funded expansion — a deliberate strategic bet that speed to market outweighed the dilution or debt-servicing cost of external capital.
- Blended debt and equity in the same round rather than relying on a single financing instrument, likely matching different portions of the capital to different uses (working capital via debt, growth investment via equity).
- Expanded the B2B loyalty program alongside geographic expansion, broadening the product's value proposition for existing corporate customers at the same time as entering a new market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Qashio demonstrates that achieving genuine profitability early — rather than pursuing growth-at-all-costs — can strengthen a fintech's position when it does choose to raise capital, turning an optional rather than existential fundraise into a strategic accelerant for market expansion on the company's own terms. The decision to raise despite profitability also illustrates a mature capital-allocation mindset: recognizing that even a self-sustaining business can benefit from external capital to win a race for market position (in this case, Saudi Arabia) faster than organic growth alone would allow.
