## One-liner

Moroccan fintech WafR closed an oversubscribed $4M seed round in February 2026, co-led by LoftyInc Capital, Attijariwafa Ventures, and Almada Ventures, to expand its network of nearly 20,000 digitized neighborhood corner stores into a nationwide last-mile financial services infrastructure.

## What happened

Founded in 2021 by Ismail Bargach and Reda Sellak, WafR digitizes Morocco's independent neighborhood corner stores, turning them into access points for airtime sales, bill payments, and other everyday financial transactions — addressing the reality that most Moroccan consumers, especially outside major cities, rely on informal cash-based retail rather than bank branches or digital-only fintech apps. By the time of its 2026 seed round, the company's network had grown to nearly 20,000 active merchant locations.

The round was notable for its investor composition: LoftyInc Capital, Attijariwafa Ventures (the venture arm of one of Morocco's largest banking groups), and Almada Ventures co-led, with returning investors UM6P Ventures and First Circle Capital also participating. The involvement of a major incumbent bank's venture arm signals institutional validation of WafR's merchant-network model as complementary to, rather than purely disruptive of, traditional banking infrastructure. The round was also the first deployment from LoftyInc's newly launched Alpha Fund targeting high-growth African startups.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built around existing informal retail infrastructure (corner stores) rather than a direct-to-consumer app, meeting Moroccan consumers where their financial behavior already happened instead of asking them to adopt a new channel.
- Attracted a strategic investor tied directly to incumbent banking infrastructure (Attijariwafa Ventures), aligning the startup's last-mile network with an established bank's own distribution ambitions rather than positioning purely as a disruptor.
- Scaled to nearly 20,000 active merchant locations before this round, using network scale itself as the primary proof point for institutional investors rather than relying solely on revenue metrics.
- Planned expansion into peer-to-peer transfers and nationwide remittances as the next product layer, sequencing from payments/bill-pay utility into higher-value financial services once distribution was established.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

WafR demonstrates a financial-inclusion pattern specific to markets with large informal retail sectors: building fintech infrastructure on top of existing trusted local merchants can reach underbanked populations faster than consumer-app-first strategies, particularly when it earns buy-in from incumbent banks rather than triggering competitive resistance. The bank-affiliated strategic investor participating alongside pure venture funds is a useful signal for other last-mile fintech plays — incumbent financial institutions increasingly prefer to invest in and partner with merchant-network fintechs rather than compete with them directly for last-mile reach.
