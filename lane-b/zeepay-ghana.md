## One-liner

Zeepay, a Ghanaian fintech focused on cross-border remittances and mobile money, secured $18 million in senior secured venture debt in April 2025, structured by Verdant IMAP, to fund working capital and float financing needed to scale its remittance operations across Africa and the Caribbean.

## What happened

Zeepay built infrastructure for cross-border remittance payments and mobile money services, connecting African and Caribbean markets in a corridor that has historically depended on expensive, slow traditional money-transfer operators. As a remittance and mobile-money business, Zeepay's growth is fundamentally constrained by float — the working capital needed to bridge the timing gap between when senders deposit funds and when recipients withdraw them — making access to debt capital specifically structured for this need a critical growth lever distinct from typical equity-funded startup growth.

The April 2025 $18 million senior secured debt facility, arranged by Verdant IMAP, was structured specifically to address this float-financing requirement rather than as general-purpose growth equity. Notably, the deal introduced a newly structured security-sharing arrangement designed to enable future participation by other senior secured lenders on the same terms — a structural innovation aimed at making it easier for Zeepay to layer in additional debt capital from other lenders in subsequent rounds without renegotiating security terms each time.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Raised the capital as senior secured debt rather than equity, matching the financing instrument to the specific working-capital need (remittance float) rather than diluting founders and existing shareholders for a need that debt capital is well-suited to address.
- Structured the security-sharing arrangement to accommodate future co-lenders on equal terms, proactively designing the deal to ease subsequent debt-capital raises rather than treating each future facility as a one-off negotiation.
- Focused expansion specifically on the Africa-Caribbean remittance corridor, a specific geographic pairing with substantial diaspora-driven money-transfer volume, rather than pursuing a more generic global remittance strategy.
- Worked with a specialized debt-structuring firm (Verdant IMAP) rather than a generalist lender, likely securing terms and structural sophistication better suited to a fintech's specific float-financing needs.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Zeepay demonstrates that mature African fintechs with a proven remittance business model can access non-dilutive debt capital — rather than relying solely on equity — once their balance sheet and cash-flow characteristics support it, preserving founder ownership while still funding genuine growth needs like transaction float. The proactive security-sharing structure built into the deal also illustrates a forward-looking approach to capital-raising infrastructure: rather than treating each debt facility as an isolated transaction, structuring the first deal to ease future co-lending arrangements can meaningfully reduce the friction and cost of subsequent capital raises as the business scales.
