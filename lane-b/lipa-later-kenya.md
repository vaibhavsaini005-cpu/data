## One-liner

Lipa Later, a Kenyan buy-now-pay-later fintech founded in 2018, was placed under administration on March 24, 2025 after months of failed fundraising left it unable to pay employees and suppliers, with its questionable KES 250 million acquisition of Sky.Garden compounding the financial strain.

## What happened

Lipa Later built a BNPL platform allowing Kenyan consumers to split purchases into installments, competing in an increasingly crowded market against established players like M-Pesa and M-KOPA. The company's last significant capital injection was a $3.4 million debt raise in September 2023, after which it struggled to secure fresh funding despite ongoing attempts — leaving employees and suppliers unpaid since late 2024, months before the formal administration filing.

On March 24, 2025, Lipa Later was placed under administration, with Joy Vipinchandra Bhatt of Moore JVB Consulting LLP appointed as administrator; all financial decision-making authority was stripped from company directors, and creditors were instructed to submit claims by April 23, 2025. Contributing factors beyond the fundraising drought included intense competitive pressure from entrenched players, rising regulatory compliance costs, and a rapid multi-market expansion strategy that lacked market-specific tailoring. The company's December 2023 acquisition of e-commerce platform Sky.Garden for KES 250 million had already raised questions about its financial stability well before the administration filing.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Made a large acquisition (Sky.Garden, KES 250M) in December 2023 that raised financial-stability concerns even at the time, roughly 15 months before the company entered administration — a decision that in hindsight appears to have strained resources during a period when fresh capital was already becoming harder to secure.
- Expanded rapidly into multiple markets without market-specific strategies tailored to each, spreading resources thin rather than deepening its position in a single core market.
- Relied on debt financing ($3.4M in September 2023) as its last major capital injection rather than equity, adding repayment obligations during a period of mounting operational strain.
- Continued operating without paying employees and suppliers for several months (from late 2024) before formally entering administration, rather than restructuring or downsizing earlier when cash-flow problems first became apparent internally.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lipa Later's collapse illustrates how BNPL fintechs operating in markets with entrenched, well-capitalized mobile-money incumbents (M-Pesa, M-KOPA) face a structurally difficult competitive position, since consumer BNPL adoption in such markets often defaults to the trusted, already-ubiquitous platform rather than a newer standalone entrant. A large, financially-questioned acquisition made during a period of underlying capital scarcity — rather than from a position of strength — is also a clear warning sign worth recognizing in other cases: expansion moves that raise stability concerns at the time they're announced frequently prove to be early indicators of a company's terminal financial trajectory rather than isolated missteps.
