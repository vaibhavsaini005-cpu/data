## One-liner

Ghanaian fintech Dash, which built infrastructure connecting mobile money wallets and bank accounts across Africa, shut down in October 2023 as part of a broader wave of African startup closures that year.

## What happened

Dash positioned itself as interoperability infrastructure for Africa's fragmented mobile money landscape — letting users move funds between different mobile money wallets and bank accounts across providers and countries, a genuine structural gap given how siloed mobile money ecosystems (M-Pesa, MTN Mobile Money, and dozens of bank-specific wallets) had grown across the continent. The company shut down in October 2023.

Dash's closure came in the same year Nigerian startup shutdowns alone cost investors an estimated $79M, and amid a broader pattern of African fintech and logistics startups closing as the 2022-2023 global funding contraction hit venture-backed African startups particularly hard, given their typically longer paths to profitability in markets with lower average transaction values.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built cross-provider interoperability infrastructure — a genuinely valuable but capital-intensive category requiring integration deals with multiple incumbent mobile money operators before reaching meaningful transaction volume.
- Operated in a category (payments infrastructure) where revenue typically scales with integration partnerships and transaction volume, both of which take years to build, right as funding windows for that patience shortened sharply.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Dash's shutdown fits a broader 2023 pattern across African fintech: infrastructure and interoperability plays solve real problems but require sustained capital to reach the transaction-volume scale where they become self-sustaining, and 2023's funding contraction cut off exactly that runway. Solving a structurally real problem (mobile money fragmentation) wasn't sufficient without enough capital to survive the multi-year integration and adoption curve needed to reach scale.
