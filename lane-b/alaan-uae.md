## One-liner

Dubai-based corporate spend management platform Alaan, founded by former McKinsey consultants in 2022, raised a $48M Series A led by Peak XV Partners in August 2025 — one of the largest fintech Series A rounds in the Middle East — to expand into Saudi Arabia and build out its AI-powered finance automation suite.

## What happened

Alaan was founded in 2022 by Parthi Duraisamy and Karun Kurien, both former McKinsey consultants, to provide corporate card issuance, automated expense data extraction, and consolidated financial management tools for businesses across the Middle East. The company's customer base of over 3,000 startups, mid-market firms, and enterprises spans sectors including real estate, aviation, logistics, and retail — reflecting broad horizontal demand for corporate finance automation rather than a single-industry niche.

The $48M Series A, announced August 5, 2025, was led by Peak XV Partners (formerly Sequoia Capital India & SEA) with participation from Pioneer Fund, 885 Capital, Y Combinator, and 468 Capital, plus angel investors including founders of regional unicorns Tabby and Careem. The round ranked among the largest fintech Series A raises in the Middle East to date, with proceeds earmarked for deeper expansion into Saudi Arabia — the GCC's largest economy — and continued development of AI agents for finance operations.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Founded by McKinsey alumni rather than career fintech operators, bringing management-consulting-style horizontal market analysis to the corporate spend category rather than a narrower founder-market-fit story.
- Built a horizontal product (corporate cards plus expense automation) serving multiple industries simultaneously, rather than specializing in one vertical, broadening the addressable customer base from the outset.
- Attracted angel investment specifically from founders of other regional unicorns (Tabby, Careem), leveraging insider credibility from operators who had already scaled MENA-based consumer and fintech businesses.
- Prioritized Saudi Arabia as the primary expansion market for the new capital, targeting the GCC's largest economy rather than spreading resources across multiple new markets simultaneously.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Alaan's raise demonstrates that MENA fintech infrastructure serving businesses (rather than consumers) can attract top-tier international VC (Peak XV) at meaningful scale when it solves a horizontal, unglamorous problem (expense and spend management) that applies across industries — reducing dependence on any single vertical's health. Angel backing from founders of other regional unicorns also signals a maturing MENA startup ecosystem where successful operators are actively recycling capital and credibility into the next wave of companies, rather than that capital flowing in only from traditional VC or international investors.
