## One-liner

Farm to Feed, a Nairobi-based agtech startup addressing food waste by aggregating surplus produce from smallholder farmers, raised $1.5 million in November 2025 to expand its platform connecting that surplus supply with restaurants and food processors.

## What happened

Farm to Feed built a platform addressing a persistent structural problem in Kenyan agriculture: smallholder farmers routinely produce surplus fruits and vegetables that fail to meet the cosmetic or sizing standards of conventional retail buyers, leading to substantial post-harvest food waste even as urban food processors and restaurants face their own supply-consistency challenges. By aggregating this surplus produce directly from smallholder farmers and connecting it with restaurants and food processors who can use it, the company created a market for produce that would otherwise go to waste, generating income for farmers who would not otherwise capture value from it.

The $1.5 million funding round, announced in November 2025, came as Kenya's broader startup ecosystem attracted $984 million in funding that year — nearly a third of all African startup capital and the second consecutive year Kenya led the continent — with the country's ecosystem notably diversifying beyond its traditional fintech dominance into sectors like agritech, clean energy, and healthtech. Farm to Feed's model, directly linking underserved smallholder supply with commercial demand, exemplifies this diversification within Kenya's broader funded-startup landscape.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused specifically on aggregating already-existing surplus produce that fails conventional retail standards, rather than building new agricultural production capacity, addressing a waste-reduction and market-access problem rather than a production-volume problem.
- Built direct connections between smallholder farmer surplus and commercial buyers (restaurants, food processors) as its core business model, creating a new value-capture channel for produce that previously had no viable market outlet.
- Operated within Kenya's broader shift toward more sector-diverse startup funding, positioned in agritech and food-systems infrastructure at a time when investor attention in the market was expanding beyond its traditional fintech concentration.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Farm to Feed illustrates how agritech platforms can create meaningful economic value by addressing food waste and market access simultaneously — aggregating surplus smallholder produce that fails conventional retail cosmetic standards and connecting it directly to commercial buyers turns what would otherwise be pure loss into captured revenue for farmers, without requiring any increase in agricultural production itself. The company's funding also reflects Kenya's broader 2025 pattern of startup ecosystem diversification: as the country retained its position as Africa's top funding destination, capital increasingly flowed into sectors like agritech and food-systems infrastructure rather than concentrating almost exclusively in fintech, as had been more typical in earlier funding cycles.
