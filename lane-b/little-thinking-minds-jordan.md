## One-liner

Little Thinking Minds co-founder and CEO Rama Kayyali chose to sell the profitable Jordanian Arabic-literacy company to Seesaw on April 15, 2025; Seesaw retained its brand and all 70 employees, added 20 regional hires, and built an Arabic version of its platform on the acquired foundation.

## What happened

Kayyali and Lamia Tabbaa began Little Thinking Minds with Arabic children's videos, then rebuilt it as school software after piracy and weak consumer payment infrastructure undermined the original model. Its I Read Arabic and related platforms ultimately reached roughly half a million students in 10 countries.

By 2024–2025, the company was cash-flow positive and profitable but unable to raise the growth capital Kayyali wanted. She and the investors decided that it was the right time to pursue an exit. After meeting Seesaw, Kayyali chose the buyer because the companies aligned on education and literacy—and she made preservation of the Little Thinking Minds brand and team explicit deal priorities.

Seesaw completed the acquisition in April 2025. The later record resolves those priorities: all 70 employees stayed, the buyer added 20 hires in the region, and Kayyali confirmed in April 2026 that the combined team had created Seesaw in Arabic and Seesaw AI in Arabic. Little Thinking Minds' original products and brand also remained active.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Kayyali shifted the company from easily pirated consumer media to privacy-compliant school software and brought its technology in-house.
- After reaching profitability but not the desired funding scale, she and the board deliberately chose an exit process rather than indefinite slow growth.
- She selected Seesaw and negotiated around two non-financial requirements: keep the 20-year-old brand and retain the team.
- The resolved outcome was a completed April 2025 acquisition, 100% retention of the 70-person workforce, 20 additional regional hires, and a launched Arabic adaptation of Seesaw's platform.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Little Thinking Minds is a counterexample to the idea that only hypergrowth produces a valuable exit. Kayyali built steady reach, profitability, curriculum expertise, and institutional trust, then sold to a larger platform that needed exactly those capabilities. The later hiring and Arabic product work show that the mission protections survived integration rather than existing only in the announcement.
