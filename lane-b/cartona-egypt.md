## One-liner

Founder and CEO Mahmoud Talaat used Cartona's July 2024 $8.1 million Series A extension to deepen its asset-light FMCG and hospitality marketplace; the platform had grown annualised GMV to EGP 10 billion, served more than 180,000 retailers and was close to EBITDA profitability.

## What happened

Cartona connects Egyptian retailers and hospitality businesses with suppliers rather than owning warehouses and inventory itself. After initially improving technology, user experience and fulfilment, Talaat shifted attention to unit economics and embedded finance.

On July 30, 2024, the company announced a $5.6 million equity and $2.5 million debt extension. Talaat said the capital would expand market share in Egypt, deepen the FMCG and HORECA verticals and support possible regional expansion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Talaat retained an asset-light model that supports existing suppliers and wholesalers instead of replacing them with company-owned logistics.
- He directed the new capital toward deeper operations in established verticals and selective adjacent markets rather than a broad infrastructure build-out.
- By the financing date, annualised GMV had risen from EGP 2.3 billion in 2022 to about EGP 10 billion. More than 180,000 retailers handled over 40,000 SKUs from 4,500 suppliers across 17 cities.
- Embedded finance grew from 2–3% of GMV in 2022 to more than 20%, while Cartona approached full EBITDA profitability.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

In thin-margin B2B commerce, growth is more defensible when the platform improves an existing distribution network instead of duplicating it. Cartona paired that asset-light structure with local-currency financing, allowing credit volume and marketplace scale to grow without tying the model to dollar-funded inventory.
