## One-liner

Liwwa, a Jordanian peer-to-peer SME lending platform founded in 2013, secured a $5M loan from the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation in May 2024 to expand financing access for small businesses that make up 80% of Jordan's economy.

## What happened

Founded by Ahmed Moor and Samer Atiani, Liwwa built a peer-to-peer lending network connecting individual and institutional investors directly with small and medium businesses seeking loans — addressing a persistent financing gap in Jordan, where SMEs represent roughly 80% of all businesses but face chronic difficulty accessing formal credit from traditional banks, which often view small-business lending as too risky or operationally costly relative to the loan sizes involved.

The May 2024 $5M loan from the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) — a development-finance institution whose mandate centers on supporting private-sector growth and economic stability in emerging markets — provided Liwwa with capital specifically earmarked to expand SME financing across multiple sectors, rather than for general corporate purposes. This structure (a loan rather than an equity investment) reflects DFC's typical approach of providing debt capital to already-proven, revenue-generating fintech lenders rather than higher-risk early-stage equity funding.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a peer-to-peer lending model directly connecting investors with SME borrowers, rather than lending purely from the company's own balance sheet, distributing credit risk across a network of individual investors.
- Targeted the specific, well-documented SME financing gap in Jordan (SMEs comprise 80% of businesses but face persistent access-to-capital challenges) as its core market thesis, rather than a more generalized consumer or corporate lending product.
- Secured development-finance debt capital (DFC) specifically earmarked for expanding lending capacity, rather than pursuing dilutive equity financing for this particular capital need — appropriate for a company with an established, demonstrable lending track record.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Liwwa's DFC loan illustrates how proven fintech lenders in emerging markets can access non-dilutive debt capital from development-finance institutions once they've built a demonstrable track record of responsible SME lending — DFC and similar DFIs specifically look to fund the lending capacity of institutions already showing measurable economic development impact (job creation, SME income growth), rather than funding unproven early-stage models. It's a useful financing pattern for SME-lending fintechs broadly: debt capital from development-finance institutions can extend lending capacity without diluting equity, once the underlying credit model has been sufficiently proven.
