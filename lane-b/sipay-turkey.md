## One-liner

Sipay, a Turkish payments platform serving 6.3 million wallet users and 25,000 merchants, raised a $78M Series B in April 2025 at a valuation exceeding $875M — the highest in Turkish fintech at the time — while already profitable since 2023 and running at a $600M annual revenue run rate.

## What happened

Sipay built a Stripe-like payments infrastructure platform in Turkey, serving both consumer wallet users (6.3 million) and merchant customers (25,000 registered) with payment processing and related financial services. Unlike many venture-backed fintechs still burning capital toward profitability, Sipay had already been profitable since 2023 and was ranked the Fastest Growing Fintech in Turkey by Deloitte in both 2023 and 2024, with revenue growing 5x year-on-year to reach a $600M annual run rate by the time of its Series B.

The April 2025 round — $78M led by Elephant VC, with participation from Nik Storonsky's QuantumLight (Storonsky being the founder of UK fintech Revolut) — pushed Sipay's valuation past $875M, following an earlier $15M Series A led by Anfa in June 2024. The new capital is earmarked for establishing new partner networks and expanding internationally into emerging markets beyond Turkey, building on the company's proven, profitable domestic base.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Reached profitability by 2023 before raising a large Series B, giving the company negotiating leverage and a fundamentally different risk profile than typical cash-burning, growth-at-all-costs fintech startups seeking similar-sized rounds.
- Attracted a strategic angel/investor connection to Revolut's founder (via QuantumLight) — a signal of confidence from an operator who had built one of Europe's largest consumer fintech platforms, potentially bringing scaling expertise alongside capital.
- Served both consumer wallet and merchant-payments use cases simultaneously rather than specializing in one side, building a two-sided platform that likely reinforced network effects between users and merchants.
- Sequenced funding with a smaller Series A (2024) followed by a much larger Series B (2025) less than a year later, using demonstrated 5x revenue growth in the interim to justify the accelerated fundraising timeline and valuation jump.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sipay demonstrates that achieving genuine profitability before a growth-stage raise — rather than the more common pattern of raising ahead of proven unit economics — can produce outsized valuation outcomes even in markets (like Turkey) that don't always attract the same international VC attention as larger fintech hubs. The rapid sequencing of a small Series A followed by a much larger Series B within roughly ten months, backed by demonstrated 5x revenue growth, also illustrates how quickly capital can follow once a company proves a scalable, profitable model rather than merely a growth trajectory.
