## One-liner

Egyptian fintech ElGameya, which digitizes the traditional ROSCA (rotating savings and credit association) model, closed a 7-figure USD round in May 2025 after reaching roughly one million registered users and sustained 50% month-on-month growth.

## What happened

Founded in 2020, ElGameya took a culturally embedded, informal financial practice — the "gameya" (ROSCA), where groups of people pool money and take turns receiving the full pooled amount — and rebuilt it as a formal, trust-verified digital product. This is a financial behavior already widely practiced informally across Egypt and much of the Arab world, meaning the company's core challenge was digitizing and formalizing existing trust-based behavior rather than convincing users to adopt an unfamiliar savings mechanism.

By May 2025, the company had built to roughly one million registered users with sustained 50% month-on-month growth, and closed a seven-figure USD round led by AYADY for Investment and Development, with participation from Jedar Capital, Cubit Ventures, Ventures Notes, P-Maestro, and a group of angel investors — bringing institutional capital into a product category (digital ROSCAs) that also includes competitor MoneyFellows in the same market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Digitized an already-trusted informal financial practice rather than introducing an entirely new savings behavior, lowering the adoption barrier compared to novel fintech products.
- Built toward roughly one million users with 50%+ month-on-month growth before raising this round, giving investors real usage data rather than a pre-launch pitch.
- Assembled a syndicate of regional investors (AYADY, Jedar Capital, Cubit Ventures, Ventures Notes, P-Maestro) plus angels rather than relying on a single lead, spreading both capital and validation across multiple regional investors.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

ElGameya shows that formalizing an existing informal financial behavior — rather than creating a new one — can be a faster path to product-market fit in markets with strong pre-existing trust-based savings cultures. The presence of a direct competitor (MoneyFellows) in the same digital-ROSCA category, both raising funding around the same period, also suggests the underlying market opportunity in Egypt was large enough to support parallel bets rather than being winner-take-all.
