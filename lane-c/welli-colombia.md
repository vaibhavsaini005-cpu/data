## One-liner

Welli, a Bogotá-based fintech founded in 2022 by Felipe Jaramillo and Felipe Gómez that finances medical, dental, and fertility procedures at the point of care, closed a $25 million Series A in June 2025 and followed it with $75 million in structured debt financing later that year to expand its medical-credit platform across Latin America.

## What happened

Welli built a point-of-care lending platform that pre-approves medical financing loans within minutes directly inside participating clinics, with funds transferred straight to healthcare providers — eliminating the paperwork delays and payment friction that often force patients in Latin America to postpone or forgo procedures like dental work, fertility treatment, and aesthetic care due to upfront cost barriers. By working with 1,500 clinics across dental, fertility, and aesthetic specialties, the company positioned itself as embedded financial infrastructure for the region's private healthcare sector rather than a generic consumer lender.

The company's $25 million Series A in June 2025, followed within months by a much larger $75 million structured debt facility, reflects a financing pattern typical of consumer-lending platforms: equity capital to build the underwriting technology and clinic network, followed by larger debt capital specifically to fund the loan book itself as origination volume scales. The funding supported expansion into additional Latin American markets, extension of loan terms beyond 36 months, and the launch of a savings product to help patients build credit ahead of future procedures.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built point-of-care loan approval that completes within minutes inside the clinic itself, directly addressing the immediate cash-timing mismatch between when patients need care and when they can typically afford it.
- Focused specifically on elective and specialty medical procedures (dental, fertility, aesthetics) — categories often excluded from public healthcare coverage in Latin America — rather than general consumer credit or emergency medical financing.
- Sequenced capital raises strategically: a smaller equity Series A ($25M) to build technology and provider network, followed by much larger debt financing ($75M) specifically sized to fund loan originations as the platform scaled.
- Added a savings product for future-procedure credit-building alongside its core lending product, extending the platform's role in a patient's financial relationship with healthcare beyond a single loan transaction.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Welli demonstrates how healthcare financing fintechs can address genuine access barriers in Latin American markets where large segments of specialty medical care fall outside public health systems and require direct patient payment, by embedding lending decisions directly at the point of care rather than requiring patients to separately seek financing elsewhere. The company's capital-raising sequence — equity first to prove the model, then substantially larger debt to fund the loan book — also illustrates a capital-efficient scaling pattern common to consumer and healthcare lending fintechs, where debt financing sized to loan volume allows the company to grow its lending capacity without progressively diluting equity holders at each stage.
