## One-liner

Workfellow CEO Kustaa Kivelä filed the Finnish process-intelligence startup for bankruptcy on February 7, 2024 after talks to find a buyer for its team and product collapsed, ending an 18-person company that had raised a $3 million seed round.

## What happened

Workfellow built software for analyzing how enterprise teams perform digital work and identifying opportunities for automation and process improvement. Founded in 2019, it raised a $3 million seed round led by OpenOcean in 2021 and grew to roughly 18 people.

Kivelä said fiscal 2023 brought setbacks the company could not survive. Management tried to find a new home for both the product and team, but the final discussions failed in early February 2024. Kivelä then filed for bankruptcy and ended operations immediately on February 7.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Kivelä pursued a sale or other home for the entire product and team before closing.
- Continued negotiations until the week of the bankruptcy filing rather than separating the technology from its employees prematurely.
- Filed for bankruptcy immediately when the final rescue discussions failed.
- Closed the commercial operation rather than maintaining an underfunded service for enterprise customers.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Workfellow shows the fragility of enterprise software when financing and acquisition timing collide. A real product, specialist team, and institutional seed backing were not enough once the company lacked runway and the rescue transaction failed. For founders, a prospective acquisition is not a financing plan until it closes.
