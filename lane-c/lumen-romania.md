## One-liner

Romanian deep-tech startup .lumen, developer of "Glasses for the Blind" using Pedestrian Autonomous Driving AI to mimic a guide dog's function, closed a €5M round in July 2024 combining €4M from the European Innovation Council and €1M raised in a single day from 600+ private investors via SeedBlink.

## What happened

Founded by Cornel Amariei, .lumen built assistive smart glasses using proprietary Pedestrian Autonomous Driving (PAD AI) technology — adapting autonomous-vehicle navigation concepts to help blind and visually impaired individuals navigate physical environments independently, functionally replicating what a trained guide dog provides but through wearable technology rather than an animal requiring years of training and ongoing care. The product had been evaluated by more than 300 blind individuals across nearly 30 countries by the time of the 2024 raise, providing real-world validation across diverse environments and use cases.

The July 2024 funding round combined €4M from the European Innovation Council and Venture to Future Fund (EU institutional deep-tech funding) with €1M raised from private investors through equity crowdfunding platform SeedBlink — remarkably, the SeedBlink tranche was fully subscribed within a single day following a live pitch event that drew over 600 investors. This blended structure — large-scale EU deep-tech grant/investment funding combined with rapid crowdfunded retail investor participation — reflected both institutional confidence in the underlying deep-tech (autonomous navigation AI applied to assistive technology) and strong public investor enthusiasm for the product's social impact mission.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Adapted autonomous-vehicle navigation technology (Pedestrian Autonomous Driving) to an assistive-technology use case, applying deep-tech AI capability developed for one domain (self-driving cars) to a meaningfully different but technically related problem (pedestrian navigation for the blind).
- Validated the product extensively with real users (300+ blind individuals across nearly 30 countries) before scaling fundraising, building a strong evidence base for both the product's efficacy and its cross-cultural/cross-environment applicability.
- Combined EU institutional deep-tech funding (European Innovation Council) with retail equity crowdfunding (SeedBlink) in a single round, tapping both large-scale grant-style capital and broad public investor enthusiasm simultaneously.
- Set a concrete, numeric commercial roadmap (limited-series launch Q4 2024, US market entry Q4 2025, 10,000 units by end of 2026) rather than an open-ended R&D timeline, signaling investor-facing commercial discipline for a genuinely deep-tech, hardware-intensive product.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

.lumen demonstrates that deep-tech applied to clear social-impact problems (assistive technology for disability) can access a blended funding stack — EU institutional deep-tech capital plus rapid retail crowdfunding enthusiasm — that purely commercial hardware startups often can't tap as effectively, since the mission dimension broadens the pool of investors willing to back a technically ambitious, capital-intensive hardware product. The single-day SeedBlink oversubscription also shows how equity crowdfunding platforms can generate meaningful, fast-closing capital for startups with strong public narratives, complementing rather than replacing institutional deep-tech funding sources.
