## One-liner

Founder and CEO Giorgio Tinacci completed Casavo's shift from buying homes itself to an asset-light marketplace in 2024, selling €36 million of residual inventory and cutting production costs by almost 40% in one year.

## What happened

Casavo began as an instant buyer, using its own balance sheet to purchase and resell homes. Rising interest rates made that capital-heavy model harder to sustain, so Tinacci led a strategic reorganisation around a marketplace that connects sellers, buyers and agents.

By August 2024, Casavo said the transition was complete. It had also raised €20 million to fund marketplace growth and continue toward financial sustainability.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Tinacci moved the business from inventory ownership to an asset-light marketplace and concentrated resources on core markets.
- During the first half of 2024, Casavo sold the remaining 98 homes on its balance sheet, valued at €36 million at the end of 2023.
- The reorganisation reduced production costs by almost 40% in one year.
- Casavo reported €225 million in 2023 consolidated turnover, served more than 3,200 buyers and sellers, and distributed almost €5 million in commissions to partner agents.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

When financing conditions change, the balance sheet can become the product constraint. Casavo preserved its customer workflow and data advantage while removing the most capital-intensive part of the model.
