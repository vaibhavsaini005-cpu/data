## One-liner

Leasy, a Peruvian fintech founded in 2018 by Gregorio Gilardini and Alejandro Garay that finances vehicle purchases for ride-hailing drivers, raised a $28M Series A (a blend of $2M equity and $15M debt) led by Magma Partners in March 2024, after growing loan placements and revenue over 100% in Peru in 2023 while remaining profitable.

## What happened

Leasy built a vehicle-financing platform specifically addressing a structural barrier for ride-hailing drivers across Latin America: the upfront cost of purchasing a car needed to earn income on platforms like Uber or similar services. By financing vehicle purchases against future ride-hailing earnings, Leasy directly connects a driver's income-generating capacity to the loan underwriting, rather than relying on traditional credit history that many prospective drivers lack.

The March 2024 $28M round — structured as a blend of $2M equity and $15M debt, with participation from Magma Partners (lead), Entangle Group, DeBa Ventures, Noa Capital, and Grupo CAPEM, alongside investors spanning Latin America, the US, and Europe — came after Leasy demonstrated strong unit economics: loan placements and revenue grew over 100% in Peru during 2023 while the company remained profitable, a notable combination for a lending fintech. The capital is earmarked for scaling sales efforts, streamlining operations across its existing Peru and Mexico markets, and developing new financing options for a broader range of vehicles, with plans to originate 500+ individual loans in Peru and $1-1.5M in monthly loan volume in Mexico.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Structured underwriting around ride-hailing income potential rather than conventional credit history, directly addressing the access barrier facing prospective drivers who lack traditional credit profiles but have clear, verifiable future earning capacity.
- Combined equity and debt financing in its Series A ($2M equity, $15M debt) rather than pure equity, reflecting the capital-intensive nature of vehicle-financing businesses that require float capital to fund loan originations at scale.
- Achieved profitability alongside 100%+ revenue growth in 2023, demonstrating a rare combination for growth-stage lending fintechs, which often sacrifice near-term profitability for growth — this combination likely strengthened investor confidence ahead of the raise.
- Expanded specifically into Mexico alongside its Peru home base rather than pursuing a wider multi-country rollout, focusing capital and operational resources on two markets rather than spreading thin across the broader Latin American region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Leasy demonstrates that vehicle-financing fintechs targeting gig-economy drivers can achieve strong growth and profitability simultaneously when underwriting is tied directly to the borrower's verifiable future income stream (ride-hailing earnings) rather than conventional credit history — a model particularly well-suited to addressing access gaps in markets with large informal or credit-invisible populations. The equity-plus-debt financing structure is also a reusable pattern for other asset-financing fintechs: pure equity capital alone typically cannot fund the loan origination volume such businesses need, making blended capital structures a practical necessity for scaling lending operations.
