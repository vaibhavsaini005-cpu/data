## One-liner

CEO Ignacio Spiniak chose to sell Chilean HR-software company Talana to Visma in June 2025; thirteen months later Talana was nearly twice its earlier size, profitable across Chile and Peru, and serving more than 2,500 customers.

## What happened

Talana had grown into an all-in-one payroll and people-management platform in Chile and Peru. On June 27, 2025, buyer Visma announced the acquisition and quoted Spiniak saying Talana had chosen to join a group that understood Latin American HR technology so it could accelerate growth.

The deal had a measurable operating result rather than merely a change of ownership. By July 2026, Visma described Talana as nearly twice its size three years earlier with record customer and employee satisfaction. Gestión separately reported that the combined Chile-Peru operation was profitable, had more than 2,500 customers, and that the Peru business had doubled in each of the preceding three years.

## Key decisions / turning points

- On June 27, 2025, Spiniak publicly committed Talana to Visma while keeping the Chilean company as an operating platform within the buyer's regional ecosystem.
- Talana entered the deal with more than 2,300 customers and 600,000 users; Visma said the acquisition took its Latin American HR products past five million users.
- By July 2026, Talana had passed 2,500 regional customers and remained profitable when Chile and Peru were combined.
- Management used Visma's global product, cybersecurity and go-to-market capabilities while retaining operating autonomy, then prepared an AI product called Talía and targeted another year of triple-digit growth in Peru.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

An acquisition can be evaluated by what happens after the announcement. Talana retained its local regulatory expertise and autonomy while gaining a larger owner's technical and commercial infrastructure; the subsequent customer count, profitability and near-doubling of the company show that the integration produced a resolved growth outcome.
