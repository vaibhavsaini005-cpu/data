## One-liner

Dank Sociedade de Crédito Direto, a fintech lender based in Jaraguá do Sul, Santa Catarina, was forced into extrajudicial liquidation by Brazil's Central Bank on March 11, 2026, following "serious deterioration" of its finances and regulatory violations, after it had previously clashed with regulators over issuing bank guarantees typically reserved for full-license banks.

## What happened

Dank operated as a direct-credit fintech ("Sociedade de Crédito Direto") based in Jaraguá do Sul, in Brazil's Santa Catarina state, offering lending products under Brazil's fintech-specific regulatory license category — a lighter-touch alternative to a full banking license that nonetheless carries defined limits on which products a company can offer. The company had previously been in conflict with Brazil's Central Bank specifically over its attempts to issue bank guarantees, a product category typically reserved for institutions holding full banking licenses rather than direct-credit fintech licenses.

By September 2025, financial filings showed Dank carrying R$44.8 million in liabilities against just R$975 thousand in equity, alongside a R$1.355 million loss — a balance sheet position reflecting severe capital inadequacy relative to its obligations. On March 11, 2026, Brazil's Central Bank decreed the company's extrajudicial liquidation, citing "serious deterioration of the economic-financial situation and serious violations of legal norms" governing its licensed activities. The shutdown was part of a broader wave of fintech-related institutional actions by Brazil's Central Bank in the same period, including the high-profile collapse of Banco Master.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Attempted to issue bank guarantees, a product outside the scope of its direct-credit fintech license, triggering direct regulatory conflict with the Central Bank well before the eventual liquidation — an early signal of the company operating beyond its licensed product boundaries.
- Allowed liabilities (R$44.8M) to grow to roughly 46 times equity (R$975K) by September 2025, a capital structure imbalance that left essentially no cushion to absorb further losses or regulatory scrutiny.
- Continued operating with this severely inadequate capital position rather than raising additional equity or restructuring liabilities before regulators intervened, ultimately forcing the Central Bank's hand into an extrajudicial liquidation rather than a negotiated wind-down.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Dank's liquidation illustrates the acute regulatory risk fintech lenders face when operating under licenses with defined product boundaries: attempting to offer products (like bank guarantees) reserved for full-license institutions invites direct regulatory conflict that can precede and foreshadow deeper compliance and solvency problems. A liabilities-to-equity ratio as extreme as roughly 46-to-1 is a clear warning sign of capital inadequacy well before a regulator forces liquidation — reinforcing that fintech lenders operating on thin capital bases against growing liabilities face compounding regulatory and solvency risk simultaneously, not sequentially.
