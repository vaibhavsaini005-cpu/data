## One-liner

Swedish foodtech Mycorena, which had raised €35M since 2017 developing a mycelium-based protein ingredient for meat alternatives, filed for bankruptcy in July 2024 after failing to close a Series B round, with Belgian plant-protein company Naplasol subsequently acquiring the bankruptcy estate.

## What happened

Founded in 2017, Mycorena developed Promyc, a mycelium-derived protein ingredient positioned as an alternative-protein input for meat-substitute products — targeting the food manufacturing supply chain (as an ingredient supplier) rather than selling directly to consumers as a branded meat-alternative product. This B2B ingredient-supplier positioning required the company to build both fermentation production capacity and food-industry customer relationships simultaneously, a capital-intensive combination for an alternative-protein category that had cooled significantly in investor sentiment by 2023-2024 after several years of hype-driven overinvestment.

Having raised €35M across its lifetime, Mycorena attempted to raise a Series B round to fund continued scale-up but was unable to close it amid a broader alternative-protein and foodtech funding contraction. The company filed for bankruptcy in July 2024, and Belgian plant-protein company Naplasol subsequently acquired the bankruptcy estate — allowing some of Mycorena's technology and assets to continue under new ownership rather than being liquidated entirely.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a B2B ingredient-supplier model (selling mycelium protein to food manufacturers) rather than a direct-to-consumer branded product, requiring capital-intensive fermentation infrastructure investment ahead of proven large-scale commercial offtake agreements.
- Continued scaling production capacity through the alternative-protein sector's 2021-2022 investment boom, leaving the company more exposed once investor sentiment toward the category cooled sharply in 2023-2024.
- Failed to close a Series B round specifically — rather than a broader inability to raise any capital — indicating investors had specific concerns about the company's path to profitability or market size at that later funding stage.
- The bankruptcy-estate acquisition by Naplasol (rather than a full liquidation) suggests the underlying technology retained commercial value even though the standalone company could not sustain itself independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Mycorena's bankruptcy is emblematic of the broader 2023-2024 alternative-protein funding correction: category-level investor enthusiasm during 2021-2022 encouraged capital-intensive production scale-up bets that became unsustainable once follow-on funding tightened sector-wide, independent of any single company's execution quality. The acquisition of the bankruptcy estate by a strategic peer (Naplasol) rather than full liquidation also illustrates a common outcome pattern in capital-intensive foodtech: the underlying production technology and IP often retain value and get absorbed by better-capitalized competitors, even when the original standalone venture fails financially.
