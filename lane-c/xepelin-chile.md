## One-liner

Xepelin, a Santiago-based fintech founded in 2019 by Sebastián Kreis that acts as a "digital CFO" platform for small and medium-sized businesses across Latin America, secured a $15.75 million credit line from BBVA Spark in June 2025 to expand its financial management and financing services for SMEs in Mexico.

## What happened

Xepelin built a SaaS platform providing SMEs across Latin America with integrated financial management tools, including AI-powered accounts receivable and payable management, smart financing products, and performance-tracking dashboards — addressing the persistent working-capital and financial-visibility gaps that smaller businesses in the region often face due to limited access to traditional banking infrastructure and financial tooling. By the time of this financing, the company already served more than 50,000 companies across the region.

The $15.75 million credit line from BBVA Spark, announced in June 2025, was structured as debt financing specifically earmarked to strengthen Xepelin's services for Mexican SMEs, with CEO Sebastián Kreis describing the deal as "the beginning of a strategic relationship" intended to develop integrated solutions across multiple business areas jointly with BBVA. This built on Xepelin's longer funding history, which had previously included a $111 million Series B and a separate $140 million credit facility from Goldman Sachs, reflecting a financing pattern in which the company paired substantial equity rounds with targeted debt facilities from banking partners to fund the capital-intensive SME financing side of its business.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Structured this round specifically as a debt credit line rather than equity, matching the capital type to Xepelin's core business of extending financing to SME customers rather than diluting existing shareholders for working capital.
- Framed the BBVA Spark relationship explicitly as a strategic, multi-area partnership rather than a purely transactional credit facility, signaling intent to deepen collaboration with a major banking partner over time.
- Targeted the credit line specifically toward Mexican SME expansion, building out its presence in an additional Latin American market beyond its Chilean home base.
- Combined this debt facility with its earlier equity Series B and separate Goldman Sachs credit facility, building a diversified capital stack spanning both equity and multiple debt sources rather than relying on a single financing type.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Xepelin's continued use of targeted debt facilities from major banks (BBVA Spark, following an earlier Goldman Sachs facility) alongside its equity fundraising illustrates how SME-focused fintech lenders in Latin America can build diversified, multi-source capital stacks matched to the specific capital-intensive nature of their lending businesses, rather than relying solely on venture equity to fund loan originations. The company's explicit framing of the BBVA relationship as strategic rather than purely transactional also demonstrates how fintech lenders increasingly position bank credit-line providers as long-term partners for integrated product development, rather than treating debt financing as an arm's-length capital source disconnected from the broader business relationship.
