## One-liner

Founder and CEO Thomas Kimber initiated Karün's shutdown process on September 3, 2024 after funding failed; investors instead rescued it with US$5 million, and eighteen months later the eyewear company was operating with costs down about 65% and headcount reduced from more than 60 to about 20.

## What happened

Karün had raised almost US$35 million and sold about US$6 million a year across 17 countries, but its long production-to-cash cycle and financing costs created a cash crisis. Kimber told investors on September 3, 2024 that the promised capital needed to activate a profitability plan had not arrived and that the company would begin an orderly closing process.

That decision forced a resolution. Family offices WildSur and Petra completed a US$5 million recapitalization, took control, installed a new board and replaced Kimber as CEO. By September 2026, Karün had survived but become a materially different company: Europe was closed, the US operation was reduced, expenses were roughly 65% lower and the team had fallen from more than 60 people to around 20.

## Key decisions / turning points

- On September 3, 2024, Kimber began an orderly-close process after four months of cost planning failed to produce the required financing.
- Investors chose a US$5 million rescue and change of control instead of liquidation; Kimber stepped out of the CEO role and later ended his formal involvement in operations.
- New management abandoned broad international expansion, closed Europe, narrowed US distribution and concentrated on company-owned retail in Chile.
- By September 2026, Karün operated six stores and planned two more, while targeting 20% sales growth for 2026; profitability was still 18 to 24 months away.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

This was not a clean turnaround or a simple failure. The shutdown notice created the forcing event for a rescue, but survival required control changes and a severe retreat from the previous global strategy. The resolved outcome is a smaller operating company, not proof that the old model worked.
