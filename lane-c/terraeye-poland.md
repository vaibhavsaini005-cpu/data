## One-liner

CEO and co-founder Marek Wilgucki chose International Resources Holding as TerraEye's buyer on December 1, 2025; within three months the Polish mineral-exploration startup secured a two-year deployment across the buyer's projects and was generating revenue from both group and external customers.

## What happened

TerraEye had endured a difficult period in which it simplified its structure and refocused its satellite-imagery and machine-learning platform on mineral-exploration targets. When buyers from several regions showed interest, Wilgucki said the UAE group offered the best path to scale the platform. Deal records date International Resources Holding's acquisition to December 1, 2025, and investor Balnord reported a complete, profitable exit.

The strategic rationale converted into commercial work. On February 24, 2026, TerraEye and IRH subsidiary BMRC signed a two-year contract to deploy the platform across BMRC's active exploration portfolio. A June 2026 First Abu Dhabi Bank research report said TerraEye was already commercially active and earning revenue from both IRH internal projects and outside clients.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Before the sale, Wilgucki and the team narrowed the business toward the parts of the technology that delivered the most customer value, restoring growth and credibility.
- On December 1, 2025, they completed the sale to International Resources Holding rather than pursuing competing interest from the United States or Australia.
- The transaction delivered liquidity to early investors; Balnord reported a 20.08% internal rate of return and an increase in fund DPI to 0.24x.
- On February 24, 2026, TerraEye secured a two-year BMRC deployment covering ongoing exploration projects, while Wilgucki remained CEO and continued product development under the new owner.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A strategic sale is strongest when the buyer becomes a distribution channel and anchor customer. TerraEye's post-deal contract and verified commercial revenue show that choosing an industry owner resolved the earlier scaling problem rather than merely providing an investor exit.
