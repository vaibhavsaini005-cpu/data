## One-liner

Brazilian financial infrastructure platform QI Tech reached unicorn status on April 25, 2024, with a $50M Series B extension led by General Atlantic at a $1B valuation — just six months after closing a $200M Series B round.

## What happened

Founded in 2018, QI Tech built financial infrastructure — credit, banking, and anti-fraud rails — explicitly positioned to let other businesses offer financial services without depending directly on incumbent banks, a banking-as-a-service model. The company closed a $200M Series B round in October 2023, then followed with a $50M extension to that same round in April 2024 led by General Atlantic, pushing its valuation past $1B and making it one of Latin America's newer fintech unicorns.

The rapid sequence — a large $200M round followed by a further extension just six months later — signaled unusually strong and sustained investor demand for infrastructure-layer fintech (as opposed to consumer-facing fintech apps) in Brazil, even as the broader 2023-2024 funding environment remained cautious. QI Tech directed the new capital toward expanding its M&A strategy and moving into new business lines including foreign exchange and insurance.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built infrastructure/rails for other financial businesses (banking-as-a-service) rather than a consumer-facing product, positioning as a picks-and-shovels play within Brazil's broader fintech boom.
- Closed a large $200M Series B in October 2023, then extended it further just six months later rather than waiting for a full new round cycle — a sign of continuing rather than one-off investor interest.
- Used the unicorn-stage capital explicitly to fund an M&A strategy and expand into adjacent regulated categories (FX, insurance) rather than only organic growth.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

QI Tech's rapid re-raise illustrates that infrastructure-layer fintech — providing the rails other financial products run on — proved more resilient to the broader 2023-2024 funding slowdown than consumer-facing fintech apps, likely because its revenue scales with the volume of other companies' financial activity rather than requiring its own end-user acquisition. The explicit M&A mandate for the new capital also signals a consolidation phase in Brazil's fintech infrastructure layer, where well-capitalized platforms can acquire smaller point solutions rather than building every capability organically.
