## One-liner

Co-founder and CEO Manuel Oliva closed Colombian food-waste marketplace Planet Oliver in early 2024 after failed fundraising and difficult logistics, despite the company having rescued more than 150,000 kilograms of food over three years.

## What happened

Planet Oliver sold unsold food from restaurants, bakeries, supermarkets and farmers at a discount. The company pivoted in mid-2023 from restaurant surplus toward supermarkets and fresh produce, but last-mile delivery, storage and inflation kept the model operationally and financially difficult.

When expected funding deals fell through, Oliva and his co-founders concluded they could not finance continued operations. Oliva publicly announced the closure on May 6, 2024.

## Key decisions / turning points

- In mid-2023, the founders shifted from restaurant surplus to supermarkets and farm-fresh products in search of a more sustainable model.
- After capital commitments failed to close, Oliva and his partners decided in early 2024 to wind down rather than continue an underfunded logistics operation.
- The team communicated openly with investors, employees and suppliers during the shutdown.
- Planet Oliver closed after three years and more than 150,000 kilograms of food rescued.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Impact does not neutralize unit economics. A marketplace that moves perishable goods inherits costly physical operations, and a model proven in wealthier markets may not transfer when local suppliers, consumers and investors have different priorities.
