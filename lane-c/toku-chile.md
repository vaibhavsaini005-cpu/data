## One-liner

Toku, a Chilean fintech founded by CEO Cristina Etcheberry that automates recurring billing and payment collections for over 450 clients, raised $48M in Series A funding led by Oak HC/FT in April 2025 — the largest Series A ever raised by a female founder in Latin America.

## What happened

Toku, founded in 2020, built an accounts-receivable SaaS platform providing automated payment reconciliation, customizable payment portals, and collections management specifically for businesses with recurring-payment revenue models — industries like subscriptions, insurance, and installment-based services where manual billing reconciliation creates significant operational overhead and revenue leakage. The company served over 450 clients by the time of its Series A, demonstrating meaningful enterprise adoption across Latin America's fragmented payment infrastructure landscape.

The April 2025 $48M Series A was led by US-based Oak HC/FT, a healthcare and fintech-focused venture capital firm, marking a significant vote of confidence from an international investor in a Latin American recurring-payments infrastructure company. The round was widely reported as the largest Series A raised by a female founder in the region, with Etcheberry and Toku's achievement highlighted specifically in coverage of the deal alongside its core payments-infrastructure story.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused specifically on recurring-payment reconciliation and collections rather than building a broader payments platform, addressing a well-defined operational pain point (manual billing reconciliation) common across subscription and installment-based businesses.
- Attracted a US-based, sector-specialized venture capital firm (Oak HC/FT) rather than relying solely on regional Latin American investors, signaling international investor confidence extending beyond the immediate region.
- Built enterprise credibility through a substantial existing client base (450+) before the Series A raise, using demonstrated commercial traction rather than pure growth potential to support the fundraise.
- The deal's framing as the largest female-founder Series A in Latin America generated significant press visibility, likely amplifying Toku's market recognition beyond what the funding amount alone would have achieved.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Toku demonstrates that narrowly-scoped B2B payments infrastructure — solving one well-defined operational problem (recurring billing reconciliation) rather than building a broad multi-product platform — can attract substantial international venture capital when it demonstrates strong enterprise client adoption. The prominent "largest female-founder Series A in Latin America" framing around this deal also illustrates how funding milestones tied to founder demographics can generate outsized press attention and market visibility for the underlying business, beyond what the capital raised alone would typically produce.
