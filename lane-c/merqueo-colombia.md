## One-liner

Merqueo, a pioneering Colombian digital-grocery startup founded a decade earlier, permanently shut down its app and operations in Colombia in mid-2025 after a multi-year decline that began when pandemic-driven demand collapsed and the company could not raise fresh capital to survive.

## What happened

Merqueo built one of Colombia's earliest on-demand grocery delivery platforms, growing rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic when digital grocery shopping became one of the few viable ways for consumers to stock up safely. As pandemic restrictions ended and consumers returned to in-person supermarket shopping, the demand that had fueled Merqueo's growth reversed sharply, leaving the company with an infrastructure and cost base built for pandemic-era volumes.

By 2023, Merqueo had accumulated more than 25 billion Colombian pesos in debt and entered a formal Business Recovery Procedure (PRES) with the Bogotá Chamber of Commerce, acknowledging labor, tax, and other obligations it could not meet — including commitments tied to its international operations. The company managed to exit that recovery process in under three months by reaching private agreements with creditors and transferring several of its retail brands to Plan B Investments SAS (operator of Tiendas Ísimo, part of Grupo Olímpica). Despite this restructuring, and after also exiting Mexico, Merqueo was ultimately unable to secure new capital and shut down all Colombian operations — closing its app, website, and flagship Bogotá office — in July 2025.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Scaled operations and infrastructure to meet exceptional pandemic-era demand without building in flexibility for the inevitable post-pandemic demand reversion, leaving the company overextended once behavior normalized.
- Entered formal business recovery proceedings rather than an immediate shutdown, buying time through creditor negotiations and brand transfers rather than accepting insolvency outright.
- Exited international markets (Mexico) to conserve cash and refocus on its home Colombian market — a retrenchment strategy that ultimately still proved insufficient.
- Could not secure fresh growth or rescue capital in a Latin American venture funding environment that had sharply tightened since the 2021 peak, closing off the option that might have bridged the company through its restructuring.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Merqueo's decade-long rise and eventual 2025 collapse is a clear case study in demand-shock fragility: a company that scaled aggressively to meet an unusual, temporary spike in demand (pandemic grocery delivery) was structurally exposed once that demand reverted to pre-pandemic norms, and lacked the balance sheet or fresh capital access to absorb the multi-year adjustment. The gap between its 2023 restructuring survival and its 2025 final shutdown — roughly two additional years of operating under distress — also illustrates that formal recovery proceedings can extend a company's life without resolving the underlying demand problem that caused the crisis.
