## One-liner

Favo, a Peruvian social-commerce grocery startup founded in 2019 by Daniel Garcia that had raised a $35 million seed round and a $26.5 million Series A led by Tiger Global, officially shut down all operations on February 29, 2024, after five years — following an earlier retreat from Brazil in 2022 that shed 170 employees.

## What happened

Favo built a hybrid e-commerce and direct-sales platform inspired by successful Chinese social-commerce models like Pinduoduo, enabling underemployed entrepreneurs — predominantly stay-at-home mothers — to promote and sell groceries to their local communities through the platform rather than through traditional retail channels. This social-commerce approach positioned Favo as both an e-commerce company and an informal-economy income opportunity, distinguishing it from conventional online grocery delivery competitors.

The company expanded from its initial Lima, Peru launch into Brazil in 2020 under cofounder Marina Proença, but that international expansion proved short-lived: mounting economic difficulties and an inability to secure further investment forced Favo to cease Brazilian operations in June 2022, shedding 170 employees in the process. Despite having raised substantial capital — including the $26.5 million Series A led by Tiger Global — the company could not sustain its core Peruvian business against intensifying competitive pressure from traditional supermarkets and established delivery platforms like Rappi and PedidosYa, which had both expanded into overlapping grocery product categories, ultimately leading to full closure on February 29, 2024.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a differentiated social-commerce model (leveraging community sellers rather than pure logistics-driven delivery) inspired by Chinese platforms, betting that this approach would provide a defensible advantage against conventional grocery delivery competitors.
- Expanded internationally into Brazil in 2020 relatively early in the company's lifecycle, before the core Peruvian business model had been fully proven at scale, adding execution complexity across two distinct markets simultaneously.
- Retreated from Brazil in 2022 rather than continuing to fund an underperforming international expansion, cutting 170 jobs to preserve capital for the core Peruvian operation.
- Ultimately could not defend its position once established players (Rappi, PedidosYa) and traditional supermarkets expanded into directly competing grocery-delivery product categories, eroding whatever differentiation the social-commerce model had initially provided.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Favo's five-year trajectory — from a well-funded, differentiated social-commerce launch to full shutdown despite over $60 million in combined funding — illustrates how a genuinely novel go-to-market model (community-seller-driven social commerce) can still ultimately lose to well-capitalized, logistics-focused incumbents once those incumbents choose to expand into the same product categories. The company's early international expansion into Brazil, followed by a costly retreat two years later, also demonstrates the risk of geographic expansion before a core domestic model has proven durable: capital and organizational focus spent on premature internationalization can leave less capacity to defend the home market against the competitive response that eventually determined the company's fate.
