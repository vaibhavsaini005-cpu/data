## One-liner

Auxilius Pharma, a Warsaw-based cardiovascular biotech founded in 2019, raised $4 million (€3.7 million) in Series A funding in December 2025 from ff.VC, NIF ASI, and NanoGroup SA to advance AUX-001, a once-daily antianginal medication, into Phase 1b clinical trials ahead of a planned U.S. partnering process.

## What happened

Auxilius Pharma built its business around AUX-001, a once-daily medication based on nicorandil designed to control chronic stable angina symptoms while reducing cardiovascular risk and hospitalization rates — targeting a specific, well-defined cardiovascular indication rather than pursuing a broader therapeutic platform across multiple disease areas. By focusing narrowly on a single drug candidate with a specific dosing-convenience advantage (once-daily administration), the company positioned itself to pursue a clearly defined regulatory and clinical development pathway.

The $4 million Series A, announced in December 2025 with participation from ff.VC, NIF ASI, and NanoGroup SA, was earmarked specifically to advance AUX-001 into Phase 1b clinical trials — a critical early human-safety and dosing milestone in drug development. Notably, the company explicitly framed this funding as opening the path toward a partnering process focused on the U.S. market, reflecting a common strategy among smaller European biotechs: developing a drug candidate through early clinical stages domestically before seeking a larger pharmaceutical partner or licensing deal to fund the more capital-intensive later-stage trials required for U.S. market entry.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused narrowly on a single drug candidate (AUX-001) for a specific cardiovascular indication (chronic stable angina) rather than building a broader drug-development platform, allowing for a clearer, more capital-efficient development pathway.
- Built its investor syndicate from Polish and European sources (ff.VC, NIF ASI, NanoGroup SA) for this early-stage round, rather than seeking U.S. biotech investors at the Series A stage.
- Explicitly positioned the Phase 1b trial as a step toward a future U.S. partnering process, rather than attempting to independently fund the full multi-phase U.S. regulatory pathway from its own balance sheet.
- Developed a once-daily dosing formulation as a specific differentiator, targeting a practical patient-adherence advantage over existing angina treatments requiring more frequent dosing.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Auxilius Pharma illustrates a capital-efficient development strategy common among smaller European biotechs: raising modest early-stage capital domestically to fund a drug candidate through initial human trials, then explicitly targeting a partnering or licensing deal with a larger pharmaceutical company to access the much larger capital pools required for late-stage U.S. clinical trials and regulatory approval, rather than attempting to fund the entire multi-year drug development pathway independently. The company's narrow focus on a single, well-defined cardiovascular indication also demonstrates how biotech startups can build a credible, fundable development story around a specific clinical and commercial thesis rather than a more diffuse therapeutic platform.
