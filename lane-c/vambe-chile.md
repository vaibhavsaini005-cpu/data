## One-liner

Vambe co-founder Nicolás Camhi and his team pivoted in March 2024 from debt-collection CRM software to WhatsApp-based sales agents; annual recurring revenue rose from roughly $20,000 before the change to $1 million by November.

## What happened

Camhi, Matías Pérez Pefaur, and Diego Chahuán initially built a CRM for debt collection. Customers repeatedly asked whether the AI used for collections could also offer products and services during WhatsApp conversations. The founders interpreted that behavior as evidence that the valuable product was not the collection system but the communication layer.

In early March 2024, the Santiago team shifted fully to conversational AI agents that help small and midsize businesses automate customer communication and close sales. Berkeley's SkyDeck reported that ARR grew from $24,000 to $500,000 in five months; by November, Camhi told TechCrunch it had reached $1 million. Vambe then raised a $3.85 million seed round, with 15% of revenue already coming from Mexico.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Treated customer requests to sell through the collection bot as a stronger signal than traction in the original CRM.
- Switched the core product in March 2024 to AI sales and communication agents for WhatsApp.
- Designed onboarding for businesses without prompt-engineering expertise.
- Used the post-pivot revenue acceleration to raise $3.85 million and pursue Mexico and U.S. Spanish-speaking markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Vambe is a high-signal product pivot because the founders followed observed customer behavior and the result appeared rapidly in recurring revenue. The nearly fiftyfold ARR increase within eight months shows the difference between adding a requested feature and recognizing that the feature is the product customers actually want.
