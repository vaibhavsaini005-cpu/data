## One-liner

Kuwait-founded open banking fintech Spare, built by Dalal Alrayes and Saurabh Shah, closed a $3M round in December 2023 led by Vision Ventures to expand its open banking infrastructure across Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Bahrain.

## What happened

Founded in 2019, Spare built open banking infrastructure — letting other financial businesses securely access and act on customer banking data with consent — positioning itself as an early mover in a category the GCC region was actively working to regulate and standardize. By December 2023, the company closed a $3M round led by Vision Ventures, with participation from Wa'ed Ventures, Seedra Ventures, and 500 Global, to expand across Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and Bahrain rather than remaining single-market.

The regional, multi-country expansion strategy from a Kuwait base reflected a broader pattern among GCC fintechs: building open banking infrastructure requires regulatory relationships and integrations that scale poorly if confined to one market, making early multi-country expansion a deliberate strategic choice rather than premature scaling.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Entered open banking infrastructure early (founded 2019), ahead of the GCC region's broader regulatory push toward standardized open banking frameworks.
- Expanded across three GCC markets (Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain) simultaneously rather than deepening in a single home market first, betting on infrastructure economics that improve with regional scale.
- Assembled a syndicate of regional VCs (Vision Ventures, Wa'ed Ventures, Seedra Ventures) alongside international investor 500 Global, combining local regulatory/market knowledge with global fintech investment experience.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Spare illustrates that infrastructure-layer fintech in the GCC often needs multi-market presence to be viable at all, unlike consumer-facing fintech which can succeed within a single national market — open banking rails are only valuable to business customers operating across the region if the infrastructure provider already has the necessary integrations and regulatory relationships in each market. Early entry into a still-forming regulatory category (open banking, ahead of full regional standardization) also positioned Spare as an established player once formal frameworks matured.
