## One-liner

Muscat-based climate tech startup 44.01, winner of the 2022 Earthshot Prize, completed a pilot mineralizing 10 tonnes of CO2 in the UAE with ADNOC and Masdar in under 100 days in 2024, then announced scaling the project to 300 tonnes of CO2.

## What happened

44.01 developed technology to accelerate carbon mineralization — a natural process where carbon dioxide dissolved in seawater is injected into peridotite rock formations, where it permanently mineralizes into solid rock rather than remaining as a gas requiring ongoing storage management. This approach targets Oman and the UAE's unique geological advantage: both countries hold some of the world's largest peridotite rock formations, a resource most carbon-removal companies elsewhere don't have access to.

In 2024, 44.01 partnered with UAE energy giant ADNOC, the Fujairah Natural Resources Corporation, and Abu Dhabi Future Energy Company (Masdar) to mineralize CO2 within Fujairah's peridotite formations. The pilot successfully mineralized 10 tonnes of CO2 in under 100 days, and the partners announced plans to scale to 300 tonnes — a 30x increase in project scope. Notably, the mineralization process ran entirely on off-grid renewable energy, combining solar PV with battery storage for 24-hour operation, making the carbon-removal process itself carbon-conscious rather than relying on grid power that might partially offset the climate benefit.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built technology specifically leveraging Oman and the UAE's geological peridotite advantage, rather than a geography-agnostic carbon capture approach — turning a regional resource into a defensible technical moat.
- Partnered with a major regional energy incumbent (ADNOC) and renewable energy developer (Masdar) rather than pursuing carbon removal independently, gaining access to both industrial-scale execution capability and clean power infrastructure.
- Powered the entire mineralization operation with off-grid renewable energy from the pilot stage onward, addressing a common criticism of carbon-removal technologies (that their own energy consumption undercuts claimed climate benefits) proactively rather than retrofitting later.
- Sequenced a small, fast pilot (10 tonnes, under 100 days) before committing to a 30x scale-up, demonstrating technical viability before larger capital and operational commitment.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

44.01 shows how climate tech companies can build defensible technical advantages around geography-specific natural resources (peridotite rock formations) rather than competing purely on generic carbon-capture technology — a moat competitors without access to the same geology cannot easily replicate. Partnering directly with regional energy incumbents (ADNOC) for both execution scale and access to renewable power infrastructure also illustrates a capital-efficient path for climate tech startups: rather than building all supporting infrastructure independently, aligning with an established energy player accelerates both technical validation and access to the clean power needed to make the carbon-removal claim credible.
