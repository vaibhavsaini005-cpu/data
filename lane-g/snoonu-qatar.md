## One-liner

Founded in 2019, Qatari on-demand super-app Snoonu raised a $5M Series A in 2021 and a $12M Series B in 2023, consolidating shopping, food, grocery, pharmacy, and courier services into one app for Qatar's small consumer market.

## What happened

Snoonu launched in 2019 under founder Hamad Al-Hajri, building a single "super-app" that bundled 11 on-demand services — online shopping, food and grocery delivery, urgent pharmacy orders, and a personal-errand "Snoosend" picker service — rather than launching as a single-category delivery app. It raised a $5M Series A in 2021, then a $12M Series B in May 2023, reported at the time as one of the first Series B rounds closed by a Qatari startup.

The company built its super-app strategy in a market with a comparatively small population, betting that bundling many use cases into one app would generate enough transaction frequency and retention to justify the platform investment, rather than competing category-by-category against international delivery apps entering the Gulf.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Chose a super-app architecture from the start (11 bundled services) instead of launching narrow and expanding later.
- Raised in stages ($5M Series A in 2021, $12M Series B in 2023) that tracked growing service breadth rather than one large early round.
- Targeted the Qatari market specifically, where thin population density makes single-category delivery apps harder to sustain than in larger regional markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Snoonu's trajectory shows a super-app strategy working in a small, dense market where bundling services can offset a limited addressable population — a different calculus than in the much larger delivery markets where single-category players (food-only, grocery-only) tend to dominate. Being an early mover on the Series B stage in Qatar's still-forming venture ecosystem also let it access one of the first institutional growth rounds in the country's startup market.
