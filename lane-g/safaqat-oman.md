## One-liner

SafaQat, a family-founded Omani startup that digitizes the government and business tendering process, secured strategic funding from the Oman Future Fund and Idrak Group in July 2025 to enhance its AI-powered infrastructure and expand into government procurement, after growing from a simple quote-sharing tool into a platform serving over 2,700 registered suppliers.

## What happened

SafaQat, founded in 2020 by four brothers, began as a simple tool for sharing price quotes before evolving into a full-service digital tendering platform addressing the inefficiency and opacity that has traditionally characterized procurement and tendering processes in Oman's business and government sectors. By digitizing the tendering workflow, the platform earned support from Oman's SME Development Authority (Riyada), reflecting official recognition of its role in the country's broader digital transformation efforts for small and medium enterprises.

The strategic funding round, announced in July 2025 with investment from the Oman Future Fund and Idrak Group, was earmarked specifically to enhance SafaQat's AI-powered infrastructure — building tools for automated bid analysis and price forecasting — alongside expansion into government procurement and new markets. By the time of the round, the platform had already grown to more than 2,486 users, 1,260 tenders, and 2,784 registered suppliers, positioning it as a national leader in Oman's digitized procurement and supply-chain space.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Started as a narrow, simple quote-sharing tool rather than launching directly as a full tendering platform, allowing the founders to validate demand incrementally before building out more complex AI-driven functionality.
- Built specifically for Oman's SME-heavy business landscape, earning support from the national SME Development Authority (Riyada) as a validation and growth channel.
- Combined AI-powered bid analysis and price forecasting with SME-focused support services (training, free onboarding, community campaigns), addressing both the technical and adoption-barrier sides of digital transformation for smaller businesses.
- Attracted strategic, government-linked investment (Oman Future Fund) rather than purely private venture capital, aligning its growth capital with Oman's broader national digital-transformation priorities.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SafaQat demonstrates how procurement-and-tendering digitization startups can grow from a minimal viable product into national infrastructure by incrementally adding AI-driven capability once initial market validation and government-linked institutional support are established, rather than requiring the full AI feature set from launch. The company's combination of government-linked strategic capital (Oman Future Fund) with SME-support services also illustrates a growth pattern common in smaller Gulf markets: aligning a startup's growth trajectory directly with national digital-transformation and SME-development priorities can provide both funding access and institutional validation that might be harder to secure through purely private commercial channels alone.
