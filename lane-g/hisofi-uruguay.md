## One-liner

Uruguayan fintech hiSofi raised $1M — including matching funds from Uruguay's National Agency for Research and Innovation (ANII) — to build an AI and data science hub in-country and expand its AI-powered debt-collection platform across Latin America.

## What happened

hiSofi built an AI-driven debt-recovery platform (marketed under the product name "Sofi") aimed at making debt collection more effective and less adversarial across Latin American markets, a category traditionally handled through manual, relationship-heavy collection agencies. The company raised $1M from SaaSholic, with Uruguay's ANII contributing $250,000 through its matching-funds program — a structure that ties private investment directly to government co-investment in innovation.

Notably, the company chose to build its AI and data science hub domestically in Uruguay rather than offshoring engineering talent, explicitly framed as bringing AI operations "back to Uruguay" — using the funding to expand engineering, data, and product teams locally rather than through an internationally distributed remote team.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Applied AI specifically to an operationally manual category (debt collection) rather than building a generic fintech product, targeting a segment with clear efficiency gains from automation.
- Used a government matching-funds structure (ANII) alongside private capital (SaaSholic), reducing dilution while signaling government-backed validation.
- Deliberately built its technical hub domestically rather than defaulting to distributed/offshore hiring, investing in Uruguay's local tech talent base.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

hiSofi shows a financing pattern specific to smaller Latin American markets: pairing private venture capital with national innovation-agency matching funds can extend runway without requiring a larger, more dilutive round — particularly useful in markets like Uruguay where the venture capital pool itself is smaller than in Brazil or Mexico. The explicit choice to build technical capacity domestically, rather than outsourcing, also reflects a strategic bet on developing a defensible local talent moat rather than optimizing purely for lowest-cost engineering.
