## One-liner

ProNuvo, a Costa Rican insect-protein company producing sustainable animal-feed inputs, received a $2 million investment from the International Finance Corporation (IFC) in June 2024, following an earlier $3 million investment from Pomona Impact Fund II in December 2023 aimed at boosting production volume by 30%.

## What happened

ProNuvo built a production operation converting insects into protein for animal feed, positioning the business within the broader circular-economy model that insect farming enables: insects can be raised on organic waste streams and converted into a protein source that displaces more resource-intensive traditional feed inputs like fishmeal or soy, directly supporting Latin America and the Caribbean's circular-economy development goals. The company's Costa Rica base situates it within a country with an established reputation for environmental and sustainability-oriented enterprise, aligning naturally with ProNuvo's core value proposition.

The company's funding trajectory shows accelerating investor confidence: Pomona Impact Fund II invested $3 million in December 2023 specifically to increase production volume by 30%, and just six months later, in June 2024, the IFC — the World Bank Group's private-sector investment arm — added a further $2 million investment explicitly framed around boosting the circular economy across the broader Latin America and Caribbean region, not just Costa Rica specifically. This sequence of impact-oriented investors (Pomona, then IFC) reflects the company's positioning squarely within the sustainability and circular-economy investment thesis rather than pure commercial venture capital.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Positioned the business explicitly around circular-economy principles (converting waste-adjacent insect farming into protein feed) rather than framing it purely as an agritech production business, aligning with development-finance and impact-investor priorities.
- Secured sequential investment from two different impact-oriented investors (Pomona Impact Fund II, then IFC) within roughly six months, demonstrating sustained and escalating investor confidence rather than a single isolated funding event.
- Used the earlier Pomona investment specifically to scale production volume (30% increase target) before the larger IFC investment, building operational proof points that likely strengthened the case for the subsequent, larger institutional investment.
- Operated from Costa Rica, a country with strong existing sustainability branding, which likely reinforced the company's positioning with impact-focused investors evaluating the broader country and regulatory context alongside the company itself.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

ProNuvo demonstrates that insect-based protein and animal-feed companies in Latin America can build a credible impact-investment funding trajectory by explicitly framing their business within circular-economy principles, attracting sequential investment from impact funds and development-finance institutions like the IFC rather than needing to fit a pure commercial venture-capital thesis. The company's Costa Rica base also illustrates how a country's broader sustainability reputation can reinforce an individual company's positioning with impact-focused investors evaluating both the specific business and its operating environment together.
