## One-liner

Grateful founder Gastón Silberman sold the Uruguayan stablecoin-payments startup's operating assets to Exodus on November 10, 2025 for US$2.7 million; Exodus retained its technology, workforce, and trade name, then launched a live Punta del Este pilot in early 2026.

## What happened

Silberman built Grateful as a non-custodial gateway through which physical stores and ecommerce merchants could accept stablecoins. Exodus co-founder and CEO JP Richardson chose to acquire it as the merchant-facing half of a broader Latin American payments push, pairing Grateful's checkout and point-of-sale tools with Exodus wallets.

Although the announcement described an agreement to acquire, Exodus's later annual filing resolved the outcome: the company completed the purchase of substantially all assets of Grateful's legal entity, Gratitud Interna Ltd., on November 10, 2025. It paid US$2.7 million excluding transaction costs—US$1.5 million in cash and US$1.2 million in newly issued shares—and recorded the acquired technology, assembled workforce, and trade name on its balance sheet.

The integration progressed beyond paperwork. By January 2026, the combined team had launched a Grateful product pilot and brand activation in Punta del Este. At Festival Portal Serena, users paid through the product and received a measurable 10% ticket discount plus in-venue benefits.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Silberman built Grateful for stablecoin acceptance by both physical and online merchants, including QR checkout and settlement tools.
- On November 10, 2025, he sold substantially all of the company's assets to Exodus, whose CEO JP Richardson framed the purchase as the bridge between Exodus wallets and merchant payments in Latin America.
- Exodus paid US$2.7 million and retained the product technology, assembled workforce, and Grateful name rather than shelving the business.
- The resolved post-sale outcome was a live Punta del Este pilot in early 2026, where users received 10% off event tickets and used the product for on-site benefits.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Grateful is a small but fully resolved strategic exit: a public-company filing verifies the purchase and price, while a later market pilot shows the acquired capability was actually deployed. For founders, that combination is stronger evidence of success than a transaction announcement with an integration promise that never gets tested.
