## One-liner

Omani cloud-kitchen operator KitchenomiKs, which has delivered over one million meals across Muscat through 13 virtual brands, raised $3.2M led by Jasoor Ventures in October 2025 (bringing total funding to $6.7M) to expand its satellite kitchen network across the GCC.

## What happened

KitchenomiKs, founded in 2022 by Aankush Bhatia, Aju Samuel, and Shrikanth Shenoy, built a central-kitchen model running 13 virtual food brands out of shared cooking infrastructure, letting the company operate multiple delivery-only restaurant concepts without the capital cost of opening separate physical locations for each. Beyond its own brand portfolio, the company also manages enterprise-scale food and beverage operations for local and international partners, positioning it as F&B operational infrastructure rather than purely a delivery-brand company.

The October 2025 $3.2M round, led by Jasoor Ventures, brought KitchenomiKs' total funding to $6.7M. The capital is earmarked for expanding the company's satellite kitchen network across Oman and the broader GCC region, alongside continued investment in its proprietary AI-driven platform, KiKsIQ, which the company uses to improve operational efficiency and customer experience across its multi-brand kitchen network.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a multi-brand virtual restaurant model (13 brands from shared kitchen infrastructure) rather than a single restaurant concept, spreading fixed kitchen costs across multiple revenue streams and product lines.
- Extended beyond operating its own brands into managing enterprise F&B operations for other local and international partners, diversifying revenue beyond direct-to-consumer delivery sales.
- Invested specifically in a proprietary AI platform (KiKsIQ) for operational efficiency, treating technology as a core differentiator in a food-delivery category where operational margins are typically thin.
- Sequenced expansion from a Muscat home base outward to broader GCC markets, using demonstrated scale (one million+ meals delivered) as the proof point for regional expansion capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

KitchenomiKs demonstrates that the multi-brand cloud kitchen model — spreading shared kitchen infrastructure across many virtual restaurant concepts — remains a viable path to scale in Gulf food-delivery markets when paired with proprietary technology aimed at the category's persistently thin operational margins. Diversifying into managed F&B operations for enterprise partners, rather than relying solely on its own delivery brands, also gives the company a second, less delivery-platform-dependent revenue stream that can provide more predictable cash flow than pure consumer delivery sales.
