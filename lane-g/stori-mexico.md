## One-liner

Stori cofounder Marlene Garayzar committed MXN 7 billion on July 1, 2024 to deepen the fintech's Mexican infrastructure and reach; in 2025 the regulated business reversed a MXN 706.9 million loss into MXN 253.2 million of net profit while its loan book grew 35%.

## What happened

Stori built credit cards, personal loans, and savings products for Mexican consumers underserved by traditional banks. On July 1, 2024, cofounder and growth chief Marlene Garayzar announced that the company would invest MXN 7 billion over two years to increase its capitalization, expand its digital infrastructure, improve products, and extend its presence in Mexico.

The expansion did not end as an unresolved spending promise. By August 2025, cofounder and CEO Bin Chen said Stori had reached sustainable profitability: first-half net income was MXN 21.4 million, annualized revenue had risen 80%, the company served 3.7 million users, and cloud and AI deployment had cut customer-service costs by more than 35% over the preceding year.

Stori México's audited full-year accounts subsequently confirmed the turnaround. Net result improved from a MXN 706.9 million loss in 2024 to a MXN 253.2 million profit in 2025. Its performing consumer-loan portfolio rose from MXN 5.92 billion to MXN 7.98 billion, while interest income more than tripled from MXN 1.84 billion to MXN 5.75 billion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- On July 1, 2024, Garayzar publicly committed MXN 7 billion over two years to capitalization, digital infrastructure, product improvement, and Mexican expansion.
- Management used cloud computing and AI in underwriting, customer service, and marketing; Chen reported that those choices reduced customer-service costs by more than 35% within a year.
- The resolved outcome was a swing to MXN 253.2 million of audited 2025 net profit from a MXN 706.9 million loss, alongside 35% growth in the performing consumer-loan portfolio.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Stori shows that an aggressive growth commitment in inclusive consumer finance can produce a resolved success when capital deployment is paired with operating leverage. The meaningful result is not the announced investment alone: audited accounts show that the regulated business expanded its loan book and reversed a large loss, while later reporting identifies the technology-led cost reductions that helped make the growth sustainable.
