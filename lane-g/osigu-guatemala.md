## One-liner

Guatemala-founded healthcare-payments company Osigu received strategic investment from Visa in October 2024, building on a funding history that began with a $7.5M round in 2021 and positioned the company as infrastructure bridging health insurance claims processing with fintech payment rails across Latin America.

## What happened

Osigu built SaaS infrastructure for health insurance claims processing and e-prescriptions across Latin America, digitizing a claims and payments layer that had historically run through slow, paper-heavy, or fragmented processes between insurers, healthcare providers, and patients. The company's initial institutional funding came in December 2021, when it raised $7.5M led by IDC Ventures, with strategic investors including Jose Vargas, co-founder of Healthcare.com, joining the round.

By October 2024, Osigu had progressed to attracting strategic investment directly from Visa — a payments-network incumbent whose participation signals confidence that Osigu's health-claims infrastructure represents a meaningful new payment-volume channel worth Visa's direct strategic interest, not just a healthcare software company raising from generalist venture capital. This built toward a later oversubscribed Series B (led by Eos Ventures, with Visa and IDC Ventures continuing to participate), reflecting sustained multi-year investor confidence in the healthcare-fintech bridge thesis.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Positioned explicitly at the intersection of healthcare claims processing and fintech payment infrastructure, rather than as a pure healthtech or pure fintech company — creating a category that attracted both healthcare-focused and payments-focused strategic investors.
- Built the credibility to attract a payments-network incumbent (Visa) as a direct strategic investor, rather than remaining reliant on healthcare-sector or generalist LatAm VCs alone — broadening its investor base to include infrastructure players with a direct commercial stake in claims-related payment volume.
- Sustained investor relationships across multiple funding rounds (2021 through the 2025 Series B) with overlapping backers (IDC Ventures, Visa), building a compounding trust relationship rather than starting fresh with new investors each round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Osigu demonstrates that healthcare infrastructure companies serving underdeveloped claims-processing markets in Latin America can attract strategic capital from global payments incumbents once they demonstrate real transaction volume and infrastructure reliability — Visa's direct investment is a strong signal that healthcare claims processing is increasingly viewed as a payments-adjacent category worth strategic attention, not just a vertical SaaS niche. The multi-year, multi-round relationship with the same core investors (IDC Ventures, Visa) also shows how sustained investor trust compounds for infrastructure companies serving regulated, relationship-driven sectors like healthcare.
