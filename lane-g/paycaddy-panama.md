## One-liner

PayCaddy, a Panama City-based Banking-as-a-Service fintech, was selected into Y Combinator's Winter 2022 batch — one of 35 international fintech startups featured at that cohort's Demo Day — raising seed capital from Y Combinator, AAF Management, Cathexis Ventures, and Soma Capital to let businesses launch financial products via API.

## What happened

PayCaddy built a Banking-as-a-Service platform enabling businesses across Latin America to launch financial products — such as cards, accounts, and payment capabilities — through an API-based infrastructure layer, rather than requiring each business to build banking relationships and compliance infrastructure independently. This BaaS model addresses a structural barrier in Latin American fintech: building compliant financial products typically requires navigating complex banking partnerships and regulatory relationships that are costly and slow for individual companies to establish on their own.

Selection into Y Combinator's Winter 2022 batch gave PayCaddy access to YC's standard investment structure and global startup network, positioning the company among a cohort of 35 international fintech startups YC highlighted at that batch's Demo Day — notable validation for a company from Panama, a market that receives comparatively less venture attention than Latin America's larger fintech hubs like Mexico, Brazil, or Colombia. Beyond the YC investment itself, PayCaddy's seed round attracted additional venture investors including AAF Management, Cathexis Ventures, and Soma Capital, and the company was later selected for Mastercard's Start Path Emerging Fintech program, a further signal of recognition from established financial-services players.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a Banking-as-a-Service infrastructure layer rather than a single consumer-facing financial product, positioning PayCaddy to serve multiple downstream businesses rather than competing directly for end consumers.
- Pursued and secured Y Combinator acceptance specifically, using the accelerator's brand credibility and investor network to attract additional seed capital from AAF Management, Cathexis Ventures, and Soma Capital.
- Originated from Panama rather than a larger, more venture-covered Latin American fintech hub, demonstrating that BaaS infrastructure businesses can attract international accelerator and investor attention even from smaller regional markets.
- Was later selected for Mastercard's Start Path Emerging Fintech program, pursuing corporate-accelerator validation from an established payments incumbent alongside its venture-capital-backed growth path.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

PayCaddy demonstrates that Banking-as-a-Service infrastructure businesses — which sell to other businesses building financial products rather than directly to consumers — can attract top-tier accelerator and investor attention from smaller Latin American markets like Panama, since the addressable customer base (businesses across the region needing banking infrastructure) is regional rather than confined to the company's home country. Layering both a YC-network seed round and a corporate accelerator relationship (Mastercard Start Path) also illustrates a common fintech-infrastructure growth pattern: pairing venture capital validation with direct engagement from established financial incumbents who have both distribution relationships and payments-industry credibility to offer.
