## One-liner

Brinta co-founders Michel Golffed and Rodrigo Sánchez Prandi sold their three-year-old AI tax-compliance company to Vertex on March 2, 2026; the completed 100% acquisition valued the initial purchase consideration at US$22 million and moved Brinta's 40-person team into a global tax-software platform.

## What happened

Golffed, Brinta's CEO, and Sánchez Prandi, its COO, founded the Uruguayan company in 2023 to automate indirect-tax calculation, filing, and electronic invoicing across Latin America. By the time of the sale, Brinta had raised US$5 million and served customers including PedidosYa, dLocal, Rappi, and Pomelo with a team of 40.

The founders agreed to join Vertex rather than continue scaling independently. Vertex completed its purchase of 100% of Brinta's equity on March 2, 2026 and began consolidating the business immediately. Its subsequent SEC filing recorded US$22 million of purchase consideration, including US$10.1 million used to settle Brinta's pre-existing debt or borrowings. The filing also tied restricted stock and up to US$10 million of additional cash compensation to the sellers' continued service, confirming that the founders and business were retained rather than merely having their assets liquidated.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Golffed and Sánchez Prandi built Brinta around the unusually complex, real-time tax and e-invoicing requirements of Latin America.
- On March 2, 2026, they completed a sale of the entire company to Vertex and stayed to extend that regional product inside the buyer's global platform.
- Vertex assigned US$7.85 million of value to Brinta's developed technology and planned full integration to expand its Latin American coverage immediately.
- The resolved outcome was a closed US$22 million acquisition of 100% of the company, with the 40-person operation and founders continuing inside Vertex.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Brinta turned difficult local regulation into exportable product depth. Instead of treating Latin American compliance as a narrow regional niche, the founders built expertise that a global incumbent could use as more countries adopt real-time invoicing. The exit also shows why acquisition quality is better judged by the closed consideration and retained operating capability than by an announcement alone.
