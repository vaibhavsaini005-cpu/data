## One-liner

SkipCash, a Qatari fintech founded in 2019 by Mohammed Abdulaziz AlDelaimi that serves over 6,000 merchants and processed more than QAR 1 billion in transactions in 2025, raised a $4M Series A in February 2026 to expand its Tap-to-Phone payments infrastructure across the GCC.

## What happened

SkipCash built digital and mobile-first payment solutions spanning both in-store and online commerce, including a Tap-to-Phone point-of-sale product that turns ordinary smartphones into contactless payment terminals — removing the need for merchants to purchase dedicated card-reader hardware. By 2025, the platform was used by more than 6,000 merchants across Qatar and had processed over QAR 1 billion (roughly $275M) in transactions, demonstrating substantial commercial traction in a market where contactless and mobile-first payment adoption has been accelerating.

The February 2026 $4M Series A drew participation from a mix of institutional and strategic investors: Qatar Development Bank, Qatar Islamic Insurance Company, KBN Holding Group, Finjan Venture Investments, Ula Capital, and Doha Tech Angels. The capital is earmarked for enhancing SkipCash's technology stack, expanding Tap-to-Phone deployment, and entering new GCC markets beyond Qatar. The company separately attracted a strategic investment from Qatar International Islamic Bank later in 2026, further building out its institutional banking relationships.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built Tap-to-Phone technology specifically, letting merchants accept contactless payments via ordinary smartphones rather than requiring dedicated hardware, lowering the adoption barrier for smaller merchants across Qatar's retail and commerce sectors.
- Attracted a funding syndicate blending government-linked development capital (Qatar Development Bank), strategic insurance/financial-sector investors, and specialist venture funds, reflecting broad institutional confidence across different investor categories.
- Achieved substantial transaction volume (QAR 1B+ in 2025) and merchant scale (6,000+) before the Series A, using demonstrated commercial traction to support the raise rather than pure market-opportunity narrative.
- Secured a follow-on strategic investment from a major Islamic bank (QIIB) shortly after the Series A, building deeper institutional banking relationships that likely support future product distribution and credibility in the GCC's Islamic finance-oriented market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SkipCash demonstrates that removing hardware barriers to payment acceptance (via Tap-to-Phone technology) can drive substantial merchant adoption in Gulf markets where a large share of commerce still involves smaller retailers for whom dedicated card-reader hardware represents a meaningful cost and logistics barrier. Attracting a funding syndicate that combines government development capital with strategic financial-sector investors is also a notable Gulf-market fintech pattern: it signals institutional confidence extending beyond pure venture capital and often provides a smoother path to subsequent strategic banking partnerships, as reflected in SkipCash's follow-on investment from QIIB.
