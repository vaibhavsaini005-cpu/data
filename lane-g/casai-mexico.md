## One-liner

Mexico City-based proptech Casai raised $28M in equity plus $25M in debt to furnish and manage short-term rental apartments across Latin America, then shut down entirely within four years as the 2022 venture capital drought cut off its path to further funding.

## What happened

Casai launched in 2019 offering fully furnished, professionally managed short-term rental apartments in Mexico and Brazil, positioning itself against Airbnb hosts with a more consistent, hotel-like product. It raised backing from Andreessen Horowitz, Global Founders Capital, and Monashees, plus debt financing from TriplePoint Capital, and explored a merger with Loft's Nomah unit in mid-2022 as conditions worsened.

Layoffs began in 2022 across both the Brazil and Mexico teams. Brazil operations shut down at the start of 2023, and by the end of May 2023 — after four years of operation — Casai closed its Mexico operations entirely, selling its apartment portfolio to operators including Blueground, Charlie, Wynwood House, CAPITALIA, and Oasis Collections.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Scaled a capital-intensive, asset-heavy rental model (furnishing and managing units directly) across two countries simultaneously.
- Pursued a merger with a competitor (Loft's Nomah) as a survival move once the funding environment tightened in 2022.
- Ran layoffs in stages rather than a single restructuring, extending the wind-down over roughly a year.
- Ultimately liquidated its portfolio to multiple regional operators rather than being acquired as a going concern.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Casai illustrates how an asset-heavy, multi-country hospitality model becomes acutely exposed when venture funding tightens — the company needed continuous capital to furnish and hold inventory, unlike asset-light marketplace competitors. A proposed merger signaled the company recognized the risk before the collapse, but it wasn't enough to offset the loss of growth-stage capital during the 2022 downturn.
