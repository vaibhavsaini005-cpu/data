## One-liner

Foccuz co-founder and CEO Gabriel García Da Rosa chose an orderly shutdown and bankruptcy process in February 2026 after rescue efforts failed, leaving the Uruguayan-Chilean sales-software startup with only about US$600 in cash and debts above US$44,000.

## What happened

Foccuz built software to automate sales commissions and incentive plans. It had raised a reported US$980,000 and employed 10 people across Uruguay and Chile before a sudden liquidity crisis. García said the problem emerged from alleged financial mismanagement by a co-founder; that allegation is the subject of a legal complaint and is not treated here as an adjudicated fact.

After discovering that the company lacked the cash management expected, García spent weeks seeking investment, support, and other alternatives. On February 5, 2026, he announced that those attempts had not reversed the damage. Foccuz entered bankruptcy and worked directly with customers and partners to close contracts in an orderly manner.

## Key decisions / turning points

- García pursued financing and other rescue alternatives for roughly two months after the liquidity gap became apparent.
- He filed a legal complaint on behalf of Foccuz concerning the alleged conduct behind the financial shortfall.
- When the rescue attempts failed, he chose bankruptcy and an orderly wind-down of active customer and partner contracts in February 2026.
- The measurable endpoint was a 10-person company reduced to approximately US$600 in cash against more than US$44,000 in debt.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Foccuz is a governance failure rather than a simple product-market failure. Whatever the final legal findings, the case demonstrates the operational risk of concentrating financial control and relying on reporting without independent verification. When the real cash position surfaced, even founder-led emergency fundraising could not create enough time to recover.
