## One-liner

Ecuador-founded fintech Jelou, which processes financial transactions inside WhatsApp conversations via its Brain OS AI-agent platform, raised a $10M Series A led by Wellington Access Ventures, Krealo, and Collide Capital, bringing total funding to $13M as it expands from serving 500+ business customers across 13 countries into Brazil and the US.

## What happened

Founded in Ecuador in 2017 by Luis Loaiza and Alberto Vera, Jelou built Brain OS, a platform letting companies create and manage AI agents operating directly inside WhatsApp — enabling secure transactions, identity verification, and payment activation entirely within conversational chat, without requiring customers to leave WhatsApp for a separate app or website. This addressed a specific regional reality: messaging apps, especially WhatsApp, function as the dominant commerce interface across much of Latin America, but the execution layer behind those conversations had remained fragmented and insecure.

The $10M Series A — led by Wellington Access Ventures, Krealo, and Collide Capital, following an earlier $3M seed round from Act One Ventures and Arca Continental Ventures — will fund Jelou's expansion of Brain OS as the company scales past its existing footprint of 500+ business customers (including banks, retailers, and consumer goods companies) across 13 countries and more than $100M in processed financial operations. The company announced plans to open offices in Mexico and Colombia, deepen its Peru presence, and enter Brazil and the United States.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built infrastructure specifically for WhatsApp as the primary interface, rather than a standalone app, meeting Latin American consumers and businesses on the messaging platform they already use as the default commerce channel.
- Focused on the security and identity-verification layer within conversational commerce (secure transactions, identity checks, payment activation) rather than just conversational customer service, addressing the specific gap that made messaging-based commerce risky and fragmented.
- Served enterprise customers directly (banks, retailers, consumer goods companies) rather than consumers, positioning Jelou as B2B infrastructure powering other companies' WhatsApp commerce rather than a consumer-facing product itself.
- Sequenced funding rounds with increasing scale ($3M seed to $10M Series A) alongside expanding country coverage (13 countries reached before the Series A), using demonstrated multi-country traction as the basis for the larger raise.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Jelou demonstrates that building trusted transactional infrastructure specifically for the messaging platform already dominant in a region (WhatsApp in Latin America) can create a defensible enterprise fintech business — rather than competing to build a new standalone app, the company made an existing behavior (WhatsApp commerce) secure and scalable for enterprise customers. Its strategic investor Krealo (the fintech investment arm of Credicorp, a major Peruvian financial group) alongside international venture funds also shows how conversational-commerce infrastructure attracts both regional financial-institution strategic capital and international fintech-focused VCs simultaneously.
