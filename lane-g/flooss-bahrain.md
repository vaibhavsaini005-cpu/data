## One-liner

Flooss, Bahrain's leading Sharia-compliant digital consumer financing platform with over 500,000 app downloads and $100M+ in financing issued, secured a $22M credit facility structured by Abu Dhabi-based Shorooq in January 2026 — Bahrain's first private asset-backed financing structure.

## What happened

Flooss, founded in 2022 by Fawaz Ghazal, built a Sharia-compliant digital financing platform regulated by the Central Bank of Bahrain (CBB), providing cash financing to consumers underserved by traditional banks. The company's core competitive advantage is a proprietary AI/ML Credit Engine that uses alternative data inputs — including Open Banking and handset data — to underwrite customers who lack the credit history traditional banks require, directly addressing financial inclusion gaps for a segment with real income but limited formal credit footprint.

The January 2026 $22M credit facility, structured by Shorooq (an Abu Dhabi-headquartered investment firm regulated as a Fund Manager by the FSRA), represented Bahrain's first private asset-backed financing structure — a notable financial-market milestone for the country beyond just the company-specific funding event. By the time of the raise, Flooss had achieved substantial commercial scale: over 500,000 app downloads and more than $100M in cumulative financing issued. The capital is earmarked for scaling core Sharia-compliant cash-financing products, supporting portfolio growth, and enabling regional expansion into markets including Saudi Arabia.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built underwriting around alternative data sources (Open Banking, handset data) rather than traditional credit history, directly targeting financial inclusion for a segment structurally excluded from conventional bank lending.
- Structured its product offering around Sharia compliance from inception, addressing religious and cultural financing preferences relevant to a substantial share of the Gulf consumer base rather than adapting a conventional interest-based lending model.
- Secured Bahrain's first private asset-backed financing structure specifically, a novel local capital-markets instrument that likely required close coordination with regulators and structuring expertise beyond a standard equity or debt raise.
- Prioritized Saudi Arabia as the next expansion market, targeting the GCC's largest economy and consumer base rather than spreading resources across multiple smaller markets simultaneously.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Flooss demonstrates how alternative-data underwriting (Open Banking, handset data) combined with Sharia-compliant product structuring can unlock meaningful financial inclusion in Gulf markets, serving consumers with real repayment capacity but insufficient traditional credit history. Being the vehicle for Bahrain's first private asset-backed financing structure also illustrates how a maturing fintech's capital needs can drive genuine local capital-markets innovation — the company's growth necessitated a new financing instrument type for the market, rather than simply using an existing structure.
