## One-liner

Aviva, a Mexican fintech founded in 2022 by four former Konfío employees, closed an $18 million Series A round in July 2026 led by Valor Capital Group to expand its physical kiosk network to 1,000 locations, offering AI-assessed microloans of $100 to $1,000 to underbanked people and small businesses in Mexico's informal economy.

## What happened

Aviva built a hybrid physical-digital lending model specifically targeting Mexico's informal economy — people and small businesses in towns outside major metro areas who typically lack access to traditional bank credit. Rather than relying purely on a digital app (which assumes reliable internet access and digital literacy that may not be universal in its target demographic), Aviva operates physical kiosks where prospective borrowers complete a video-call application with a bot, with AI models assessing behavioral and identity signals to process loan decisions within minutes.

The $18 million Series A round, led by Valor Capital Group with participation from BID Labs, Caravela Capital, Endeavor, and existing investors Wollef, Igni, Krealo, and Newtopia, was explicitly earmarked to expand Aviva's physical kiosk network to 1,000 locations across Mexico and broaden its financial product offering. The founding team's background — four former employees of Konfío, one of Mexico's most prominent SME-lending fintechs — gave Aviva an experienced foundation in Mexican credit-risk assessment and lending operations before founding their own company focused specifically on the underbanked informal-economy segment that larger fintechs had not fully addressed.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a hybrid physical-kiosk-plus-AI model rather than a purely digital app, explicitly designed around the access and trust patterns of underbanked customers in Mexico's informal economy who might not engage with a purely app-based lending product.
- Founded by four former Konfío employees, giving the team direct prior experience in Mexican fintech lending operations before launching a company targeting an even more underserved customer segment.
- Focused on small loan sizes ($100-$1,000) matched to the specific credit needs of informal-economy borrowers, rather than pursuing larger loan sizes that would require different risk models and target a different customer base.
- Attracted a syndicate combining commercial venture capital (Valor Capital Group) with development-finance-oriented investors (BID Labs, the Inter-American Development Bank's innovation lab) and entrepreneurship-support organizations (Endeavor), aligning growth capital with financial-inclusion mission.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Aviva demonstrates that reaching genuinely underbanked populations in emerging markets often requires deliberately hybrid physical-digital infrastructure rather than assuming a purely digital, app-only approach will reach the target customer — physical kiosks staffed with AI-assisted video interactions can bridge trust and access gaps that pure digital products cannot. The founding team's prior experience at an established Mexican fintech (Konfío) also illustrates a common and effective pattern in emerging-market fintech: founders leaving a successful lending company to build a more targeted product for an underserved segment the original company's model did not fully address, leveraging institutional risk-assessment expertise for a new customer base.
