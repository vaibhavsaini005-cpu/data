## One-liner

Costa Rican healthtech SaaS company Huli grew from a $1.75M first venture round in 2018 to a $5.5M Pre-Series A in 2023 — its largest raise since founding in 2012 — by staying focused on practice-management software for independent healthcare providers.

## What happened

Founded in 2012, Huli built cloud-based practice-management and patient-record software aimed at independent doctors and clinics in Central America, a segment underserved by enterprise hospital software vendors. It raised a first venture round of $1.75M in 2018, then $2M led by Carao Ventures in 2019, and closed a $5.5M Pre-Series A in 2023 led by Intoku Health and Carao Ventures — bringing total funding past $10M and marking the largest single round in the company's eleven-year history at that point.

The company built a base of investors spanning regional (Carao Ventures, Pagani Ventures) and international backers (Visa Everywhere Initiative, VilCap Investments), reflecting sustained rather than one-off investor interest through multiple funding cycles in a region where Central American venture capital totaled just $30.8M across all sectors in 2023.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted independent practitioners and small clinics rather than competing for large hospital-system contracts from the outset.
- Built investor relationships incrementally across five years and multiple funding rounds instead of pursuing a single large raise early.
- Kept the product focused on practice management and records rather than expanding into adjacent healthtech categories before establishing a durable base.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Huli shows that a narrowly scoped SaaS product for an underserved regional segment can raise progressively larger rounds even during a broader regional funding slowdown — Central American VC investment fell from $123M in 2022 to $30.8M in 2023, yet Huli's 2023 round was its largest yet. Staying focused on one customer segment across multiple funding cycles built the track record needed to keep raising when capital broadly tightened.
