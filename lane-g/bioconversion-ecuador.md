## One-liner

Bioconversión, a Guayaquil-based agritech company, opened a $2.5 million insect-meal production plant in December 2021 — Latin America's first facility of its kind — producing an alternative protein source for Ecuador's large shrimp-farming industry.

## What happened

Bioconversión built a facility to convert insects into meal for use as animal feed, targeting Ecuador's substantial shrimp aquaculture sector, which relies heavily on protein-rich feed inputs. Rather than continuing to rely solely on traditional feed protein sources such as fishmeal — which carries sustainability concerns tied to wild-catch fishing pressure — the company positioned insect meal as a more sustainable, potentially lower-environmental-impact alternative protein source for shrimp feed formulations.

The $2.5 million plant, opened in December 2021, was the first insect-meal production facility of its kind in all of Latin America, giving Ecuador — and Bioconversión specifically — a first-mover position in a category still nascent in the region even as adoption grew in other markets like Europe. The facility's launch directly served Ecuador's shrimp industry, one of the country's largest export sectors, positioning insect-based feed as a regionally-produced alternative to imported or wild-catch-derived protein inputs.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted the shrimp aquaculture feed market specifically rather than a broader animal-feed customer base, aligning the business with one of Ecuador's largest and most feed-input-intensive export industries.
- Positioned as Latin America's first insect-meal production plant, establishing first-mover advantage in a category that was still emerging regionally even as it gained traction in European aquaculture and livestock feed markets.
- Invested in domestic production capacity ($2.5 million plant) rather than relying on imported insect-meal products, building local supply chain resilience for Ecuador's feed industry.
- Chose insect meal specifically as an alternative protein source, addressing sustainability concerns associated with traditional fishmeal inputs that depend on wild-catch fishing.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Bioconversión demonstrates that alternative-protein agritech ventures can find a defensible first-mover position by targeting a large, feed-input-intensive domestic industry (Ecuador's shrimp aquaculture export sector) rather than trying to serve animal feed markets broadly from the outset. Being first to bring an established but still-emerging global feed technology category (insect meal) to a major regional market also illustrates how agritech innovation often follows a geographic diffusion pattern — proven technology from one region (Europe) adapted and localized to serve a different region's specific dominant industry (Latin American shrimp farming).
