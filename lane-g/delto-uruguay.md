## One-liner

Hikko co-founder and CEO Sebastián Galli decided in 2024 to spin its fast-growing customer-automation division into Delto; by September, the standalone AI company had about 20 clients across more than 15 countries and had doubled revenue over the prior year.

## What happened

Hikko originally housed three business divisions, including a customer-service automation unit. As generative AI adoption accelerated, Galli and his co-founders invested in the technology and shaped the division into a product able to understand text, audio, and documents and execute customer-service tasks.

After nine months of growth and new customer wins, Galli said the founders decided in 2024 that the unit was ready to become a separate company. By September 6, Delto employed 22 people, served roughly 20 customers across more than 15 countries, and had doubled revenue over the previous year. Galli also reported that its automation had reduced certain banking processes from days to seconds.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Galli and his partners invested in generative AI to turn an internal automation line into a more capable customer-service product.
- Following nine months of demand growth, they decided in 2024 to spin the division out of Hikko as Delto.
- They focused the standalone company on large customers in banking and health, where automation can handle high-volume, document-heavy interactions.
- By September 2024, the decision had produced a 22-person company with about 20 clients in more than 15 countries and year-over-year revenue growth of 100%.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Delto is a demand-confirmed spin-out: management waited until an internal business line had sustained growth and customers before giving it an independent identity. Separating the unit created a focused vehicle for an AI-native product while preserving evidence that the opportunity existed beyond a speculative technology trend.
