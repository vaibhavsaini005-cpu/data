## One-liner

Video Game Insights co-founder and CEO Karl Kontus sold the Estonian gaming-analytics startup's entire share capital to Sensor Tower on March 14, 2025; the buyer retained the brand and team, incorporated its coverage of more than 150,000 Steam titles, and was still selling the product across Steam, Xbox, and PlayStation in 2026.

## What happened

Kontus started Video Game Insights as an evenings-and-weekends project and bootstrapped it into a gaming market-intelligence business. Startup Estonia identifies it as a gaming-analytics company from Pärnu. It first built its reputation by estimating sales, revenue, wishlists, pricing, and engagement across Steam, then extended its datasets to Xbox and PlayStation.

Kontus and the other shareholders chose to sell the whole company to Sensor Tower, whose existing strength was mobile-app and advertising intelligence. The cross-border transaction closed on March 14, 2025 and Sensor Tower announced it four days later. The buyer kept the full team and continued the service under the name “Video Game Insights by Sensor Tower.”

The integration was not merely promised. Sensor Tower reported that the acquired product covered more than 150,000 Steam titles, and its January 2026 product review still described VGI as the component providing market, competitive, and player data across Steam, Xbox, and PlayStation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Kontus bootstrapped a side project into a dedicated data product instead of raising capital to chase a broader gaming business.
- The team moved beyond Steam into console intelligence, making VGI complementary to Sensor Tower's mobile coverage.
- On March 14, 2025, Kontus and the shareholders completed the sale of 100% of Video Game Insights' issued share capital to Sensor Tower.
- The measurable, resolved outcome was retention of the entire team and brand, with data on more than 150,000 Steam titles incorporated into a continuing cross-platform product.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Video Game Insights shows how a narrow, bootstrapped dataset can become strategically valuable to a much larger analytics platform. The founders did not need to duplicate Sensor Tower's mobile breadth; they built depth in the buyer's missing PC and console categories, then closed an exit that preserved the product rather than ending it.
