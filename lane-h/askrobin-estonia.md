## One-liner

askRobin, an Estonian-founded fintech that built a financial services marketplace for underbanked customers across Latin America and grew to nearly 2 million customers, shut down in August 2022 after the COVID-19 pandemic drove losses the company could not recover from.

## What happened

askRobin was founded in 2017 by Rain Sepp, an entrepreneur with a background in financial services, to build a "fair credit" marketplace connecting underbanked customers — primarily in Latin American emerging markets — with financial services providers, addressing a large population underserved by traditional banking infrastructure. The company raised early funding, including a $1.7M round in 2020 to expand its emerging-markets reach, and grew its user base to nearly 2 million customers, a substantial scale for a fintech marketplace targeting underbanked populations in developing markets.

Despite this customer growth, the COVID-19 pandemic caused significant financial difficulties for the company, driving losses substantial enough that askRobin was unable to recover, and the company announced its shutdown in August 2022. The company did not detail specific operational metrics behind the losses, but the pandemic's disruption to underbanked and informal-economy customers across Latin America — many of whom faced income instability during COVID lockdowns — likely undermined the loan repayment and credit performance underlying askRobin's marketplace model.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted underbanked customers in Latin American emerging markets specifically, a segment with real credit-access needs but also disproportionate exposure to income volatility during macroeconomic shocks like the COVID-19 pandemic.
- Built a lending/credit marketplace model whose revenue and viability were directly tied to borrower repayment performance — a business model structurally vulnerable to exactly the kind of income-shock disruption the pandemic caused across its target customer base.
- Grew to nearly 2 million customers before the shutdown, demonstrating substantial market validation and reach that ultimately wasn't sufficient to offset the financial damage from pandemic-driven losses.
- The company's Estonian origin building specifically for Latin American emerging markets reflects a cross-border fintech model — European fintech expertise applied to a geographically distant target market — that added operational complexity on top of the pandemic-driven financial pressure.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

askRobin's shutdown illustrates the acute vulnerability of credit-marketplace fintechs serving underbanked, income-volatile populations to macroeconomic shocks: a business model dependent on borrower repayment performance can be severely damaged when a shock (like a pandemic) simultaneously disrupts income for a large share of the customer base, even after achieving substantial user-growth scale (nearly 2 million customers). Cross-border fintech models — building in one region for deployment in a geographically distant target market — also carry additional operational complexity that can compound financial pressure during a crisis, since local market conditions may be harder for a distant founding team to respond to quickly.
