## One-liner

Wamly, a South African HRTech startup building AI-powered hiring software, closed a $3.05 million (R50 million) Series A round led by Hlayisani Capital's Hlayisani Venture Fund II in mid-2026 to expand its AI hiring capabilities and enter new international markets.

## What happened

Wamly built software applying artificial intelligence across the hiring workflow — helping companies screen, assess, and manage candidates more efficiently than traditional manual recruitment processes — positioning itself within the broader HRTech category at a moment when South African and African startup funding overall had contracted sharply. The company had previously secured an earlier funding round in 2022, establishing a multi-year fundraising track record before this larger Series A.

The Series A, led by Hlayisani Capital's dedicated Hlayisani Venture Fund II, was announced in July 2026 against the backdrop of what regional coverage described as an 80% crash in African startup funding more broadly, with investors increasingly favoring debt financing over equity — making Wamly's ability to close a meaningful equity Series A round during that specific funding drought a notable signal of investor confidence in its underlying business. The capital is earmarked for three specific priorities: expanding the company's AI capabilities across the hiring workflow, growing its team, and entering new international markets beyond South Africa.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built specifically around AI-driven hiring workflow automation rather than a broader, more generic HR software suite, giving the company a focused technical differentiator in a crowded HRTech category.
- Progressed through a multi-year fundraising sequence (an earlier round in 2022, then this Series A in 2026) rather than requiring one large round to sustain operations, building investor track record over time.
- Secured equity funding from a dedicated South African venture fund (Hlayisani Venture Fund II) at a moment when many African startups were reportedly being pushed toward debt financing instead, reflecting investor confidence specifically in Wamly's growth trajectory.
- Explicitly planned international market entry as a stated use of the new capital, rather than continuing to focus purely on deepening its South African domestic position.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Wamly's ability to close a genuine equity Series A round during a period when broader African startup funding had reportedly crashed 80% and investors were shifting toward debt financing demonstrates that individual companies with clear product differentiation and an established fundraising track record can still access growth equity even when the broader regional funding environment turns sharply unfavorable. The case illustrates that macro-level funding contractions do not uniformly affect all startups — well-positioned companies in specific, differentiated technical categories (in this case, AI-driven HR workflow automation) can continue to raise meaningful equity capital even as peers in the same broader region are forced toward less dilutive but often more restrictive debt financing.
