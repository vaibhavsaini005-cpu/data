## One-liner

Handwave, a Riga-based biometric startup founded in 2021, raised €3.6 million ($4.2 million) in seed funding in August 2025, led by Practica Capital, to expand its palm-based payment and identity verification platform — letting users pay, verify age, or check in using only their palm — across Europe and the US.

## What happened

Handwave built biometric technology that scans both the surface patterns and subdermal vein structure of a user's palm to generate a unique, encrypted identifier, enabling payment, age verification, loyalty program access, and check-in functions without requiring cards, mobile phones, apps, or facial recognition. By using palm vein and surface pattern scanning rather than facial recognition, the company positioned its technology as a privacy-conscious alternative to face-based biometric systems, which have faced growing regulatory scrutiny and consumer privacy concerns in many markets.

The €3.6 million seed round, announced in August 2025 and led by Practica Capital with support from FirstPick, Outlast Fund, and Inovo.vc, brought Handwave's total funding to €4.3 million, including an earlier €682,000 angel investment. The capital was earmarked specifically for product development, regulatory certifications, and live retail pilots — reflecting the practical reality that biometric payment technology requires navigating specific regulatory approval processes in each market before commercial retail deployment, in addition to standard product engineering work.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Chose palm vein and surface pattern scanning over facial recognition as its core biometric method, differentiating on privacy positioning at a time when facial recognition technology faces increasing regulatory and public scrutiny.
- Built a multi-purpose biometric platform (payments, age verification, loyalty, check-in) rather than a single-use-case product, positioning the underlying palm-scanning technology as broader identity infrastructure rather than a narrow payments tool.
- Earmarked seed funding specifically for regulatory certifications alongside product development, acknowledging that commercial deployment of biometric payment technology requires navigating compliance processes as a first-order priority, not an afterthought.
- Targeted simultaneous expansion into both European and US markets from the seed stage, rather than proving the model in a single home market first before international expansion.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Handwave illustrates how biometric technology startups can differentiate through privacy-conscious design choices — palm vein and pattern scanning rather than facial recognition — as consumer and regulatory sentiment around biometric data collection grows more cautious, particularly regarding facial recognition specifically. The company's early allocation of seed capital toward regulatory certifications, rather than treating compliance as a later-stage concern, also reflects a broader pattern increasingly necessary for biometric and identity-verification startups: given the sensitive nature of biometric data, regulatory approval processes can become a binding constraint on commercial deployment speed unless addressed proactively alongside core product development.
