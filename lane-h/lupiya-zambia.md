## One-liner

Lupiya, a Zambian neobank founded in 2016 by Evelyn Chilomo Kaingu, extended its Series A funding round to a total of $11.25 million in February 2026 — up from an initial $8.25 million close in September 2025 — with backing from Alitheia IDF, INOKS Capital, Mastercard, and KfW DEG, to expand its digital banking model beyond Zambia into new Southern and East African markets.

## What happened

Lupiya built a digital banking platform serving unbanked and underbanked populations in Zambia, offering financial products designed to bring formal banking services to customers who had previously operated largely outside the traditional banking system. The company's neobank model — digital-first, without the overhead of physical branch networks — positioned it to serve customers efficiently across a market where traditional banking infrastructure remained limited outside major urban centers.

The company's Series A funding progressed in two stages: an initial $8.25 million close in September 2025, followed by an extension to a total of $11.25 million in February 2026, drawing a notably diverse investor syndicate that combined specialist financial-inclusion investors (Alitheia IDF), impact-oriented capital (INOKS Capital, KfW DEG — the German development finance institution), and a major global payments incumbent (Mastercard). The additional capital is earmarked for three specific priorities: strengthening Lupiya's technological infrastructure, broadening its suite of financial products, and scaling operations to reach a wider customer base across both Zambia and new regional markets in Southern and East Africa.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a digital-first neobank model specifically targeting unbanked and underbanked customers, rather than competing directly with incumbent banks for already-banked customers, addressing a genuine financial-inclusion gap.
- Structured its Series A as a staged raise — an initial close followed by an extension five months later — rather than requiring the full amount upfront, allowing the round to grow as investor interest built.
- Attracted a syndicate blending specialist impact/financial-inclusion investors with a major global payments company (Mastercard), combining patient development capital with strategic payments-industry expertise and credibility.
- Explicitly planned regional expansion beyond Zambia into Southern and East Africa as a stated use of proceeds, rather than continuing to deepen its position in the Zambian market alone.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lupiya demonstrates a viable capital-raising pattern for financial-inclusion-focused neobanks in smaller African markets: combining specialist impact investors, development finance institutions, and strategic corporate investors (Mastercard) in a single syndicate can provide both the patient capital and industry credibility needed to scale digital banking to underbanked populations. The company's staged Series A — growing from $8.25 million to $11.25 million over five months — also illustrates how extending an initial round rather than immediately pursuing a separate follow-on can let a fintech company build funding momentum incrementally while maintaining continuity of investor relationships established in the first close.
