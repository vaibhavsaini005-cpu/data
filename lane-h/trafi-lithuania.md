## One-liner

Trafi co-founder Martynas Gudonavičius and its board accepted Enghouse Systems' offer on April 14, 2025 as a faster route into new markets; the C$23.9 million acquisition closed with all 83 employees retained, and Trafi continues operating inside Enghouse Transportation.

## What happened

Gudonavičius co-founded Trafi in Vilnius in 2013 with Mantas Vizbaras, Algimantas Krasauskas, and Jurgis Pasukonis. The company evolved from a consumer journey-planning app into a white-label Mobility-as-a-Service platform used by transport authorities in cities including Berlin, Brussels, and the UK's Solent region.

When Enghouse made an offer in 2025, Gudonavičius said the board reviewed the buyer's expansion vision and decided that joining the larger enterprise-software group was a natural next growth stage. Trafi CEO Damian Bown similarly presented the combination as a way to pair Trafi's platform with Enghouse's global footprint and ticketing expertise.

Enghouse completed the acquisition on April 14, 2025. Its next quarterly report disclosed an aggregate cash purchase price of C$23.9 million, including C$3.9 million held in escrow, and consolidated Trafi's results from the acquisition date. LRT reported that all 83 employees remained with the Lithuanian company. Enghouse's current transportation portfolio confirms that Trafi continues developing and supporting the platform under its own name.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Trafi moved from a local journey planner to business-to-government software capable of integrating public transport, taxis, rentals, and micromobility.
- On April 14, 2025, Gudonavičius and the board chose Enghouse's offer because the strategic owner could bring the product into more markets.
- Management preserved the operating team: all 83 employees stayed with the Lithuanian business at closing.
- The resolved outcome was a completed C$23.9 million cash acquisition, consolidation into Enghouse, and continued operation of the Trafi product and brand.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Trafi's exit shows a viable destination for infrastructure startups whose customers are cities and public agencies: a strategic buyer can supply distribution, procurement credibility, and adjacent ticketing products that are costly to build independently. The founder's decision is supported not just by the offer, but by a closed price, retained workforce, and a still-operating product.
