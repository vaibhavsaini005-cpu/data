## One-liner

Planet42 co-founder and CEO Eerik Oja chose court-supervised reorganization over bankruptcy on August 15, 2024 after a €20.3 million annual loss and mounting debt; the court approved a plan in December that transferred ownership to creditors.

## What happened

Oja and Marten Orgna founded Planet42 to buy used cars for customers in South Africa who could not secure conventional bank loans, later expanding to Mexico. The company financed its growth with substantial debt and raised nearly €115 million over seven years, but never produced an annual profit.

After 2023 turnover of €32.9 million and a €20.3 million loss, worsening vehicle performance, customer cancellations, and currency pressure created a liquidity crisis. Oja suspended subordinated-loan payments and, on August 15, selected reorganization rather than bankruptcy. The plan required rescheduling and reducing loans and targeted profitability in both Estonia and South Africa. Creditors backed the proposal in November, and Harju County Court approved it on December 18, with creditors becoming owners of the company.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Oja pursued rapid debt-funded growth even though Planet42 had not reached annual profitability.
- When forecasts showed cash could run out, management suspended subordinated-loan payments rather than continue scheduled servicing.
- On August 15, 2024, Oja filed for reorganization, judging bankruptcy more destructive for the company and creditors.
- The measurable result was court approval four months later and a debt-for-equity transfer that made creditors the owners.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Planet42 is a balance-sheet pivot: the customer proposition remained intact, but the capital structure could not survive accumulated losses and shocks. Reorganization preserved the operating business by converting creditors into owners, illustrating how a financing model can require a fundamental reset even when revenue is substantial.
