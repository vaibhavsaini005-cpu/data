## One-liner

Printful CEO Alex Saltonstall and Printify CEO Anastasija Oleinika completed an equal-partner merger on November 20, 2024, combining Printful's owned production facilities with Printify's network of more than 85 partners across 117 locations while retaining both Latvian-born brands.

## What happened

Printful, co-founded by Lauris Liberts and Davis Siksnans, built an in-house print-on-demand production network. Printify, co-founded by Jānis Berdigans, Artis Kehris, and Gatis Dukurs, used a distributed partner model. Although the businesses had been competitors, their operating assets were complementary.

The companies announced an equal-partner merger on November 5, 2024 and completed it after regulatory approval and full shareholder support on November 20. They chose to preserve both customer-facing platforms while uniting teams, technology, and resources under a holding company. Saltonstall became CEO of the combined business, Oleinika became president and head of platform, and co-founders Liberts and Berdigans joined the board.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Leaders of two direct competitors chose a merger that paired owned fulfillment capacity with an external partner marketplace.
- They kept Printful and Printify as separate brands instead of forcing an immediate customer migration.
- On November 20, Saltonstall and Oleinika began leading a single integration team with representation from both companies.
- The completed combination brought together Printify's network of more than 85 print partners across 117 locations and Printful's in-house facilities; the companies had raised a reported US$184.1 million between them.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

This merger demonstrates a complementary-network strategy: one business contributed controlled production capacity while the other contributed marketplace breadth. Preserving both brands reduced near-term customer disruption, while unified leadership created a route to combine technology, product selection, geographic reach, and fulfillment.
