## One-liner

Hohm Energy director Ryan Steytler placed the South African solar marketplace into business rescue in July 2024, less than six months after an US$8 million funding announcement; operations halted and the company filed for voluntary liquidation by September.

## What happened

Founders Tim Ohlsen and Emir Gluhbegovic built Hohm Energy to connect homeowners with solar installers, suppliers, and financing. The company scaled for the surge in rooftop-solar demand caused by severe load-shedding and announced an US$8 million seed round in February 2024.

When South Africa's electricity supply improved, demand fell faster than Hohm could reduce its fixed costs. Following Ohlsen's resignation, director Ryan Steytler placed Hohm Energy into business rescue; corporate records put the start date at July 31. New sales stopped and support for existing customers moved to a third party. The rescue did not stabilize the company, and Hohm Energy Services filed for voluntary liquidation by September 16.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Management expanded capacity for a demand boom tied to widespread load-shedding.
- The company announced US$8 million in funding in February 2024 to advance its solar marketplace and financing strategy.
- As cash flow deteriorated, Steytler chose business rescue in late July, after founder-CEO Ohlsen resigned.
- The measurable outcome was a full trading halt followed by voluntary liquidation less than two months later.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Hohm Energy shows the danger of treating crisis-driven demand as a durable baseline. The platform had real utility, but the organization carried costs built for a boom that receded when grid reliability improved. Fresh funding could not compensate for a cost base that adjusted more slowly than the market.
