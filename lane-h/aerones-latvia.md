## One-liner

Aerones, a Latvia-based robotics company providing AI-driven robots that inspect, maintain, and repair wind turbine blades for clients including GE and Enel across more than 30 countries, raised $62 million (€54.3 million) in June 2025 co-led by Activate Capital and S2G Investments to scale manufacturing and R&D.

## What happened

Aerones built robotic systems that climb and operate on wind turbines to inspect, clean, and repair turbine blades — work that has traditionally required human technicians to perform difficult and hazardous manual maintenance at height, often requiring turbines to be taken offline for extended periods. By automating this maintenance process with AI-driven robotics, the company addressed both a safety problem (reducing human exposure to dangerous height-based turbine work) and an operational efficiency problem (reducing turbine downtime) for wind energy operators worldwide.

The $62 million round, announced in June 2025, was co-led by Activate Capital and S2G Investments, with new investors Carbon Equity and Overlap Holdings joining existing backers Lightrock, Blume Equity, Metaplanet, Change Ventures, and Extantia. This built on a funding trajectory that included a $30 million raise in 2023 and a €4 million EU Innovation Fund grant in 2024, reflecting sustained multi-year investor confidence as the company scaled its robotic maintenance operations to serve major energy clients like GE and Enel across more than 30 countries.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted a specific, hazardous, and high-cost maintenance task (turbine blade inspection and repair at height) with robotics, rather than building broader industrial robotics for multiple use cases, achieving deep expertise in one high-value niche.
- Built a client base including major global energy companies (GE, Enel) and expanded operations across more than 30 countries, demonstrating enterprise-scale traction well before this later-stage funding round.
- Sustained a multi-year, multi-round funding trajectory (2023 raise, 2024 EU grant, 2025 raise) with consistent participation from existing investors alongside new backers, reflecting durable investor confidence in the company's execution over time.
- Positioned its robotics explicitly around AI-driven capability rather than purely mechanical automation, aligning its value proposition with broader industry and investor enthusiasm for AI-enabled physical automation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Aerones demonstrates how robotics companies addressing narrow, hazardous, high-cost maintenance tasks within large existing industries (renewable energy infrastructure, in this case) can achieve substantial venture-scale funding by directly reducing both safety risk and operational downtime for major enterprise clients, rather than needing to create an entirely new market category. The company's sustained multi-year funding trajectory, with existing investors continuing to participate alongside new backers at each stage, also illustrates how deep-tech and hardware-robotics companies — which typically require longer development timelines than software startups — can maintain investor confidence through demonstrated operational scaling (expansion to 30+ countries, major enterprise clients) between funding rounds.
