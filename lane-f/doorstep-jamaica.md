## One-liner

Doorstep, a Jamaican online delivery service founded in 2018 by Victor Clemetson and Javed Cameron, secured investment from the newly launched $50 million Caribbean Venture Capital Fund in February 2025 to accelerate its expansion across Jamaica, the wider Caribbean, Latin America, and eventually Africa.

## What happened

Doorstep built an online delivery platform serving Jamaican consumers, growing into what local coverage described as a rapidly growing service within a Caribbean e-commerce and logistics landscape that has historically lacked dedicated venture capital infrastructure. The company had operated for roughly seven years before this investment, building operational track record in a market segment — last-mile delivery — that requires substantial logistics execution capability to scale.

The investment came from the Caribbean Venture Capital Fund (CVCF), a newly established $50 million fund managed by US-based Mscale LLC under CEO Ugo Ikemba, whose investor base includes the Development Bank of Jamaica (DBJ), Pan African Capital Holdings, businessman Joseph Matalon, and musician Burna Boy — a notably diverse mix of development finance, private capital, and celebrity investors. The fund's Investment Committee specifically approved the stake in Doorstep as one of its first investments, with the explicit strategic goal of accelerating Doorstep's expansion not just within Jamaica but across the broader Caribbean, into Latin America, and eventually into African markets.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built and sustained delivery operations for roughly seven years before securing this institutional venture investment, establishing operational credibility in a market segment requiring genuine logistics execution rather than just software.
- Became one of the first portfolio companies for the newly launched Caribbean Venture Capital Fund, positioning Doorstep as an early proof point for a fund explicitly designed to address the historical lack of dedicated Caribbean venture capital infrastructure.
- Attracted a notably diverse investor base behind the fund itself — combining a national development bank (DBJ), private equity (Pan African Capital Holdings), a prominent local businessman, and an internationally known musician (Burna Boy) — broadening the fund's network and visibility beyond typical venture circles.
- Set explicit multi-region expansion ambitions (Caribbean, Latin America, eventually Africa) as the stated purpose of the investment, rather than framing the capital purely around deepening the Jamaican market position.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Doorstep's investment illustrates how a newly launched, dedicated regional venture fund can unlock capital for Caribbean startups that had previously operated for years without access to institutional venture financing, given the historically thin venture infrastructure across the region. The fund's unusually diverse investor base — spanning development finance, private capital, and celebrity investment — also demonstrates an emerging pattern in underserved regional markets: combining traditional development-finance credibility with high-profile individual investors can help a new fund establish both financial backing and cultural visibility simultaneously, potentially easing its ability to attract further capital and deal flow.
