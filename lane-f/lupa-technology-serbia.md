## One-liner

Serbian-founded construction-tech startup Lupa Technology secured $1.8M in seed funding in 2024, led by South Central Ventures with additional World Bank/EU Commission backing, to expand its cloud-based construction data management platform into the US, UK, EU, and GCC markets.

## What happened

Founded in 2021 by Milan Jankovic and Dorde Nedeljkovic, Lupa Technology built a cloud-based SaaS platform transforming unstructured construction industry data — documents, drawings, and records from multiple disparate sources — into structured, analyzable information stored on a single platform. This addressed a specific pain point in construction: project documentation is typically scattered across email, paper, and disconnected systems, making quality control, compliance verification, and forensic analysis (e.g., for disputes or insurance claims) slow and error-prone.

The 2024 seed round, totaling $1.8M and led by South Central Ventures with an additional $300,000 contribution from the World Bank/EU Commission, funded Lupa's expansion into global markets including the US, UK, EU, and GCC regions. The company's client base — global real estate developers, general contractors, construction law firms, engineering companies, and insurance companies — reflects a B2B enterprise sales motion targeting multiple distinct professional roles that all interact with the same underlying project documentation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built for a specific, underserved data problem (unstructured construction documentation) rather than a broader construction-management platform, giving the product clear differentiation and a defined value proposition for technical due diligence and dispute-resolution use cases.
- Targeted multiple distinct customer types (developers, contractors, law firms, insurers) that all touch the same project documentation, rather than building for a single persona — broadening the platform's total addressable market within each construction project.
- Positioned as a "Serbian-American" company from early on, explicitly building for and selling into the US, UK, EU, and GCC markets rather than remaining Serbia- or Balkans-focused, reflecting an internationally-oriented go-to-market strategy from the seed stage.
- Combined a specialized regional VC (South Central Ventures) with a development-institution co-investor (World Bank/EU Commission), blending commercial venture capital with development-finance backing in a single seed round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lupa Technology demonstrates how a narrowly-scoped but high-value data problem (unstructured construction documentation) can support an internationally ambitious go-to-market strategy from a Balkans engineering base — building for multiple professional stakeholders around the same underlying data creates natural expansion paths within each customer account. The blended investor structure (regional VC plus World Bank/EU Commission co-investment) is also a useful pattern for Southeast European startups: it signals both commercial venture validation and development-institution confidence in the region's tech talent, potentially easing access to both capital and credibility in the international markets the company is targeting.
