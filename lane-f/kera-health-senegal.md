## One-liner

KERA Health Platforms, a Dakar-based digital healthcare startup founded in 2023 by AI researcher Moustapha Cissé, former telecom executive Papa Sow, and healthcare leader Hosam Mattar, secured a proposed $10 million equity investment from the International Finance Corporation in June 2025 to connect hospitals, labs, pharmacies, and insurers across West Africa on a unified data platform.

## What happened

KERA Health built an integrated digital healthcare system designed to close persistent gaps in healthcare access and coordination across West Africa, connecting otherwise disparate parts of the healthcare sector — hospitals, laboratories, pharmacies, and insurers — through a single platform that leverages shared health data to support clinical and administrative decision-making, reduce costs, and streamline service delivery. This kind of cross-institutional healthcare data integration addresses a structural fragmentation problem common across many West African healthcare systems, where these different provider types have historically operated with limited data interoperability.

The IFC's proposed $10 million equity investment, announced in June 2025 ahead of board approval later that month, was structured to support KERA's expansion specifically within the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU) region — the eight-country currency union spanning much of Francophone West Africa. Beyond capital, the IFC's involvement included support for strengthening KERA's corporate governance, social and environmental standards, and establishing formal employee grievance mechanisms, reflecting the development-finance institution's broader mandate of building sustainable institutional capacity alongside its direct capital investment.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a founding team combining distinct areas of expertise — AI research (Moustapha Cissé), telecom industry experience (Papa Sow), and healthcare leadership (Hosam Mattar) — rather than a single-domain founding team, likely strengthening the company's credibility across both the technical and healthcare-sector dimensions of its product.
- Focused on cross-institutional data connectivity (hospitals, labs, pharmacies, insurers) as the core value proposition, rather than building a single-provider-type solution, addressing the specific fragmentation problem underlying West African healthcare coordination gaps.
- Attracted the IFC specifically as an investor, gaining access not just to capital but to governance, environmental, and social-standards support that a purely commercial venture investor would be less likely to provide.
- Targeted the WAEMU regional bloc explicitly for expansion, aligning its growth strategy with an existing economic and currency union rather than pursuing a country-by-country expansion without a unifying regional framework.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

KERA Health illustrates how development finance institutions like the IFC continue to play a distinctive role in African healthtech funding, providing not just capital but governance and institutional-capacity support that shapes how portfolio companies build sustainable operations from an earlier stage than might occur with purely commercial venture capital. The company's focus on cross-institutional healthcare data connectivity within the WAEMU regional bloc also demonstrates how startups can align their expansion strategy with existing regional economic frameworks, using an established multi-country union as a natural expansion pathway rather than building market entry strategies from scratch for each individual country.
