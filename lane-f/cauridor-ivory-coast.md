## One-liner

Cauridor, an Abidjan-based fintech building cross-border payment infrastructure for Francophone West Africa, raised a $3.5 million seed round in February 2025 — a notably large seed round for a market segment that typically receives a small fraction of Africa's overall startup funding relative to Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, and Egypt.

## What happened

Cauridor built cross-border payment infrastructure targeting Francophone West Africa, a region that operates under the CFA franc monetary zone and faces distinct payment-infrastructure dynamics compared to the Anglophone African fintech hubs (Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa) that capture the vast majority of the continent's startup funding and press attention. Operating from Abidjan, Ivory Coast, the company positioned itself to address cross-border payment friction within and beyond the CFA franc zone.

The $3.5 million seed round, announced in February 2025, stood out because Ivory Coast and the broader Francophone West African fintech ecosystem have historically attracted only a small fraction of overall African startup capital, with Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, and Egypt together capturing roughly 72% of the continent's total 2025 startup funding. Cauridor's ability to raise a seed round of this size in a comparatively underfunded regional market reflects both the specific quality of its cross-border payments proposition and the broader recognition among some investors that Francophone West Africa remains a relatively underserved fintech opportunity.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built cross-border payment infrastructure specifically for the CFA franc zone and broader Francophone West Africa, a distinct regulatory and currency environment from the Anglophone African markets most fintech investors focus on.
- Based operations in Abidjan, Ivory Coast, rather than relocating to a more heavily-covered fintech hub, betting that proximity to its target regional market outweighed the visibility advantages of a larger, more internationally-connected startup ecosystem.
- Raised a seed round notably larger than typical for the region, demonstrating that well-positioned fintech infrastructure plays in underfunded African sub-regions can still attract meaningful venture capital despite broader investor concentration in a handful of larger markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Cauridor's seed round illustrates that Africa's startup funding concentration in Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, and Egypt reflects investor attention patterns as much as underlying market opportunity — a well-executed cross-border payments company addressing genuine friction in an underserved regional currency zone (the CFA franc area) can still raise a substantial seed round even while operating outside the continent's best-covered fintech hubs. The company's focus on Francophone West Africa specifically also highlights a structural gap in African fintech coverage: investors and press attention skew heavily toward Anglophone markets, leaving genuine infrastructure opportunities in Francophone Africa comparatively underexplored relative to their addressable market size.
