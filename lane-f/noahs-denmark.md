## One-liner

Danish foodtech Noahs, which lets convenience stores and fuel stations operate multiple food brands via modular kitchens and software, raised €1.9M at a €6.5M pre-money valuation in November 2025 to expand into the US and UAE while growing its European retail partnerships.

## What happened

Noahs, launched in 2020 by Daniel Baven, Peter Strøm, Carl Simonsson, Danny Cavender, and Robert Engström, built a three-part platform letting retailers operate multiple food brands inside existing retail spaces: Noahs OS (a software layer managing orders, menus, production workflows, and delivery-platform integrations), a brand catalogue giving retailers access to multiple food concepts, and compact modular kitchen units installable with minimal construction inside convenience stores, supermarkets, and service stations. This addressed a specific gap for retailers wanting food-service revenue without the capital and operational complexity of building and running full restaurant brands themselves.

The November 2025 €1.9M round, led by PSV Tech with angel participation from Kraen Nielsen (former Group CEO/CTO of Coop Denmark) and Bob Stein (President, RBS & Associates), came after the company grew revenue 200% over three years while working with major retailers including MAXOL, Q8, DSC, and MENY. The funding is earmarked for international expansion into the US and UAE alongside continued European deployment, with the round explicitly positioned as groundwork for a planned Series A in early 2026.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a three-layer platform (software, brand catalogue, modular hardware) rather than a single-component product, giving retailers a complete turnkey solution for multi-brand food service rather than requiring them to separately source kitchens, software, and food concepts.
- Designed modular kitchen units specifically for minimal-construction installation inside existing retail footprints, lowering the capital and operational barrier for retailers to add food service compared to building dedicated restaurant space.
- Attracted an angel investor with direct retail-sector executive experience (the former Coop Denmark Group CEO/CTO), bringing operational retail credibility alongside capital.
- Explicitly framed the round as a bridge to a planned early-2026 Series A rather than a standalone raise, signaling a deliberate staged fundraising strategy tied to international expansion milestones.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Noahs demonstrates that "picks-and-shovels" infrastructure for an existing retail category — letting convenience stores and fuel stations add food service without building restaurant operations from scratch — can achieve strong revenue growth (200% over three years) by removing both the capital and operational complexity barriers simultaneously through a combined software-plus-modular-hardware offering. Bringing on angel investors with direct sector operating experience (a former major retailer's CEO/CTO) is also a pattern worth recognizing for B2B infrastructure startups: it pairs capital with exactly the kind of retail-operations credibility that can accelerate enterprise partner trust and adoption.
