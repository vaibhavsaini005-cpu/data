## One-liner

Flexkeeping founder and CEO Luka Berger agreed to sell the Slovenian hotel-operations startup to Mews in September 2025 after its software had raised hotel-team productivity by 40% and reduced guest complaints by 45%.

## What happened

Flexkeeping spent more than a decade building scheduling, task-management, and communication software for hotel housekeeping teams. Its tools automated room assignments and inspections, connected housekeeping with maintenance and front-desk staff, and used analytics to help managers coordinate work across thousands of properties.

Berger decided that joining Mews was the next step for expanding the product's reach. The acquisition, announced as completed on September 30, 2025, made Flexkeeping the specialist housekeeping layer in Mews' wider hotel operating system. Mews reported that Flexkeeping customers had achieved 40% higher productivity and 45% fewer guest complaints; the buyer brought a base of 12,500 customers across more than 85 countries into which the product could be integrated.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Berger focused Flexkeeping on operational staff and housekeeping workflows, an area often secondary in hotel software suites.
- Built measurable efficiency into the product before pursuing an exit: customer results included 40% productivity gains and 45% fewer complaints.
- Chose integration with Mews rather than continuing as a standalone point solution, describing the sale as a natural next step for the team and technology.
- The completed acquisition made Flexkeeping Mews' direct specialist solution for large-hotel housekeeping and staff collaboration.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Flexkeeping shows how a focused vertical SaaS product can become strategically valuable to a broader platform. The outcome was not only an undisclosed exit: the buyer identified specific customer results, integrated the category into its core system, and retained the product's role in serving hotel operations at global scale.
