## One-liner

Embat co-founder Carlos Serrano led the February 2025 acquisition of US banking-connectivity company Necto, extending the Spanish treasury platform to more than 15,000 financial institutions and 70 currencies while serving over 300 corporate clients.

## What happened

Antonio Berga, Carlos Serrano, and Tomás Gil founded Embat in 2021 to digitize corporate treasury, accounting, and bank reconciliation. By February 2024, the company had 150 corporate clients and raised a $16 million Series A after choosing API connectivity and automated cash accounting as alternatives to manual ERP work and traditional protocols such as SWIFT.

One year later, Embat acquired Necto for an undisclosed amount. Serrano said Necto fit Embat's strategy of modernizing banking infrastructure through APIs and would broaden connections with large global banks. Necto had already integrated more than 30 banks and nearly 100 API services. At the acquisition date, Embat reported more than 300 corporate clients, integrations with over 15,000 banking institutions, operation in 70 currencies, and software capable of cutting operational time by as much as 75%.

## Key decisions / turning points

- The founders built around real-time APIs and automated accounting rather than relying only on file-based bank connections and manual ERP processes.
- In February 2025, Serrano and the team chose acquisition as the route to add Necto's bank API infrastructure and accelerate international coverage.
- Embat opened offices in Munich and London as it expanded into Germany and the United Kingdom.
- The measurable footprint at the deal announcement was more than 300 corporate clients, over 15,000 connected financial institutions, 70 currencies, and a workforce of about 100.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Embat illustrates a capability-led acquisition: it bought a specialized connectivity layer that directly reinforced the product's central promise. The move expanded bank coverage and supported geographic growth without changing the company's core treasury use case, doubling reported corporate clients from 150 to more than 300 in roughly a year.
