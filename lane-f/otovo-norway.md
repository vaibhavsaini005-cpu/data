## One-liner

Otovo CEO Andreas Thorsheim put the Norwegian solar marketplace on a lower-risk course on September 10, 2024, cutting 166 positions and centralizing services in Spain to reduce annual costs by NOK 200–225 million.

## What happened

Otovo entered 2024 with weak profitability as European residential-solar demand cooled. In the first half, sales fell from about NOK 592 million to NOK 322 million year over year, while the after-tax loss widened from roughly NOK 142 million to NOK 197 million.

Thorsheim responded by announcing a leaner, lower-risk setup on September 10. The plan reduced headcount from 366 at the start of the third quarter to about 200, with 166 roles affected across 13 European markets. It paired approximately NOK 150 million of workforce savings with NOK 50–75 million of administrative savings and a shared service center in Spain, for a total annual cost target of NOK 200–225 million.

## Key decisions / turning points

- After sales contracted and losses widened, Thorsheim explicitly chose a leaner operating course aimed at profitability rather than continued growth at the existing cost base.
- Otovo consolidated administrative work through a shared service center in Spain while retaining one common technology platform.
- The company planned to cut 166 jobs, or about 46% of the workforce, reducing headcount from 366 to approximately 200.
- The quantified target was NOK 200–225 million in annual savings, with the full financial benefit expected by the end of 2024.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Otovo is a scale-to-efficiency pivot triggered by a sharp market reversal. A shared technology platform gave management a way to keep serving multiple countries while centralizing support and shrinking the organization, but the size of the reduction also shows the risk of building a fixed cost base around temporary demand conditions.
