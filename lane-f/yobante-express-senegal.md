## One-liner

Yobante Express, a Senegalese logistics startup connecting independent couriers with businesses for domestic, cross-border, and last-mile parcel delivery, raised a $1.2M seed/bridge round in September 2021 backed by Grenfell Holdings, Libertad's Aguila Investments, R-Ventures, and Launch Africa Ventures, helping formalize an informal courier sector across West Africa.

## What happened

Founded in 2018, Yobante Express built a logistics marketplace matching independent couriers with businesses needing parcel delivery — addressing a West African delivery sector historically run through informal, unstructured courier networks with limited tracking, reliability guarantees, or cross-border capability. The company's model of formalizing this fragmented courier ecosystem, rather than building owned fleet infrastructure from scratch, allowed faster scaling by leveraging existing informal delivery capacity while adding technology-driven coordination.

The 2021 funding round brought together a syndicate of Africa-focused investors — Grenfell Holdings, Libertad (via its investment arm Aguila Investments), R-Ventures, and Launch Africa Ventures — alongside local and international angel investors, bringing Yobante Express's total raised to $2.12M. The company used this capital to expand its operations beyond Senegal into cross-border delivery across West Africa, extending its formalized courier network model to a wider regional footprint.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a marketplace model connecting existing independent couriers to demand, rather than owning and operating a delivery fleet directly — reducing capital intensity while still addressing the core reliability and coordination gap in the informal delivery sector.
- Assembled a syndicate of specialized Africa-focused investors (Grenfell Holdings, Aguila Investments, Launch Africa Ventures) rather than relying on a single lead investor, spreading both capital and market-specific expertise across the round.
- Expanded from domestic Senegalese delivery into cross-border West African logistics, betting that the formalization model would generalize across the region's similarly fragmented informal courier networks.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Yobante Express demonstrates a capital-efficient approach to logistics in emerging markets: rather than building costly owned-fleet infrastructure, formalizing and coordinating an existing informal courier network can address the same reliability gap with substantially lower upfront capital requirements — a pattern especially relevant in West African markets where informal delivery labor is abundant but poorly coordinated. The multi-investor syndicate structure, combining regional specialists with international angels, is also a useful funding pattern for logistics startups needing both market-specific operational knowledge and broader capital access.
