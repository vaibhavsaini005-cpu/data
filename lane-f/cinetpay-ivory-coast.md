## One-liner

CinetPay, a newly-licensed, Flutterwave-backed Ivorian payment processing fintech, became the subject of a Senegalese cybersecurity investigation in September 2025 into alleged money laundering and fraud, after Senegal's Special Cybersecurity Division arrested the company's Dakar-based business development manager over ties to illegal betting and fake loan companies.

## What happened

CinetPay, headquartered in Ivory Coast and backed by pan-African payments giant Flutterwave, provides payment processing infrastructure across West Africa. In September 2025, shortly after obtaining new regulatory licensing, the company became embroiled in a serious scandal when Senegal's Special Cybersecurity Division (DSC) began investigating complaints of harassment linked to fake loan companies operating through CinetPay's Dakar operations, arresting a CinetPay business development manager (identified as Mr. Faye) as the alleged primary contact for related transfers.

Investigators traced funds moving through CinetPay's platform to Nectar Microcrédit Technologie, another Ivory Coast-based company, with allegations that CinetPay's payment rails were being used to launder money connected to scam operations and illegal betting activity. The investigation represented a serious reputational and regulatory crisis for a company that had only just secured new licensing, undermining the compliance credibility that licensing was meant to establish. The company continued operating afterward, but subsequent reporting (into 2026) also surfaced a separate employee embezzlement incident and a cyberattack-related settlement backlog, compounding governance concerns around the platform.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Operated payment infrastructure across multiple West African markets (Ivory Coast, Senegal) with apparently insufficient oversight of how local business development staff and connected merchant relationships used the platform, allowing alleged fraudulent activity to occur under its own operations.
- The scandal emerged shortly after CinetPay secured new regulatory licensing, an unfortunate and damaging sequence that directly undercut the compliance narrative the licensing was meant to support.
- Company statements framed the response as reactive (terminating a merchant contract, filing its own complaint) after the misuse was discovered, rather than describing internal controls that would have caught the activity proactively.
- Subsequent incidents (employee embezzlement, cyberattack settlement backlog reported into 2026) suggest the September 2025 scandal was not an isolated event but part of a broader pattern of governance and internal-control gaps at the company.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

CinetPay's scandal illustrates that regulatory licensing alone does not guarantee the internal controls needed to prevent a payments platform's rails from being exploited for fraud or money laundering by connected merchants or even internal staff — licensing represents formal regulatory approval, not proof of operational fraud-prevention maturity. The compounding pattern of multiple governance incidents (the fraud investigation, employee embezzlement, and a cyberattack settlement backlog) at the same company within roughly a year also suggests that when a payments fintech's internal controls fail once, it's worth scrutinizing whether that reflects a systemic weakness rather than an isolated incident.
