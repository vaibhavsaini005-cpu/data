## One-liner

Sunrise Robotics, a Ljubljana-headquartered industrial robotics startup founded in 2023 by Tomaž Štolfa, Marko Thaler, and Joe Perrott, emerged from stealth mode in June 2025 with $8.5 million (roughly €7.3 million) in funding led by Plural to bring simulation-trained autonomous robotic cells to factory automation.

## What happened

Sunrise Robotics develops intelligent, autonomous robotic cells trained through simulation rather than traditional manual programming, aiming to make industrial manufacturing automation more adaptable and faster to deploy than conventional fixed-function robotics — a technical approach that lets robots handle a wider variety of manufacturing tasks without requiring extensive reprogramming for each new use case. The company operated in stealth mode for roughly two years after its 2023 founding, developing its core simulation-training technology before going public with both its product and its funding.

The June 2025 funding round, led by Plural with participation from Tapestry, Seedcamp, Tiny.vc, and Prototype Capital, brought together a syndicate of European and international investors with track records in deep-tech and industrial-technology startups. The round was explicitly framed as enabling the company's emergence from stealth — meaning the capital both validated roughly two years of pre-launch technical development and funded the company's transition into active commercial deployment of its simulation-trained robotic cells for manufacturing customers.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Operated in stealth mode for approximately two years after founding, prioritizing deep technical development of its simulation-training approach before any public product launch or funding announcement.
- Built the company around simulation-based robot training specifically, rather than traditional manual programming approaches, positioning Sunrise Robotics against a technically differentiated thesis about how factory automation should evolve.
- Assembled a three-person founding team combining CEO, CTO, and CCO roles with what the company described as experienced backgrounds, rather than a single-founder structure, distributing leadership across technical and commercial functions from inception.
- Attracted a syndicate of specialist deep-tech investors (Plural, Seedcamp, Tapestry) for its stealth-exit round, rather than generalist regional funds, aligning investor expertise with the technical complexity of industrial robotics.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sunrise Robotics demonstrates that a small European country like Slovenia — not traditionally associated with deep-tech robotics startups — can produce companies capable of attracting internationally recognized specialist investors (Plural, Seedcamp) for a genuinely technical, capital-intensive category like industrial robotics. The company's extended stealth period before any public funding announcement also illustrates a deliberate, patience-first approach to deep-tech company building: rather than raising visible seed rounds early and iterating publicly, some hardware and robotics startups may benefit from a longer heads-down development phase before entering the market with a more mature, differentiated product.
