## One-liner

Cameroonian fintech Diool, which pivoted from a mobile-recharge app into a financial-services aggregation platform for small merchants, raised $3.5M in February 2021 from the Lundin family and existing investors, bringing total funding to $4.6M after signing over 2,000 merchants processing $120M in transactions.

## What happened

Diool began in 2015 as a simple mobile-recharge project, letting small merchants sell prepaid airtime top-ups from a single app — a narrow utility product. Over time, the company pivoted into a broader financial-services aggregation platform, letting small merchants accept customer payments and pay suppliers through multiple payment methods from one unified interface, addressing the fragmentation of Cameroon's mobile-money and payment landscape where merchants otherwise needed separate relationships with each mobile money provider.

By the time of its February 2021 funding round, Diool had built payment integrations with all major mobile money providers operating in Cameroon and established a regulatory partnership with Société Générale, giving it both technical interoperability and banking-sector legitimacy. The $3.5M round — funded by the Lundin family (a Swiss-based Swedish-Canadian family with substantial mining and oil industry wealth) alongside existing investors — brought Diool's total funding to $4.6M, capital the company used to scale a platform that had already processed over $120M in transaction volume across more than 2,000 signed-up merchants.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Pivoted from a narrow single-utility product (mobile airtime recharge) to a broader financial-services aggregation platform, expanding the addressable problem from one transaction type to the full range of small-merchant payment needs.
- Built integrations with all major mobile money providers in Cameroon rather than partnering with just one, positioning Diool as payment-method-agnostic infrastructure rather than tied to a single mobile money operator's ecosystem.
- Secured a regulatory partnership with an established bank (Société Générale) rather than operating purely as an unregulated intermediary, providing compliance legitimacy that likely eased both merchant trust and investor due diligence.
- Attracted a non-traditional investor type (a wealthy family office with mining/oil industry origins, the Lundin family) rather than relying solely on typical fintech-focused venture capital, diversifying its capital sources.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Diool's evolution from single-purpose recharge app to multi-provider payment aggregation platform illustrates a common and effective growth pattern for fintechs in fragmented mobile-money markets: rather than competing with individual mobile money operators, building interoperability infrastructure across all of them creates a stickier, harder-to-replicate value proposition for merchants who would otherwise juggle multiple separate accounts. Its bank regulatory partnership (Société Générale) is also a useful model for fintechs operating adjacent to formal banking — partnering for compliance legitimacy rather than attempting to operate entirely outside the regulated banking perimeter.
