## One-liner

Vinaver Medical, a Novi Sad-based startup led by Stevan Vrbaški and a team of Serbian physicists, programmers, and radiologists, secured investment from the Digital Serbia Initiative's business angel network in May 2025 to advance AI-and-spectral-CT software that improves cancer diagnostics and radiotherapy planning.

## What happened

Vinaver Medical built proprietary software combining artificial intelligence with spectral CT imaging technology to enhance tissue characterization for cardiac and abdominal diagnostics, with its greatest impact specifically in particle radiotherapy planning for oncology. Rather than requiring hospitals to adopt entirely new hardware, the software is designed to integrate into existing clinical imaging systems regardless of manufacturer, lowering the adoption barrier for healthcare providers.

The May 2025 investment came from the business angel network of the Digital Serbia Initiative, a domestic organization supporting Serbia's tech ecosystem, reflecting confidence in a deep-tech medical software venture built around a genuinely interdisciplinary team spanning physics, radiology, and software engineering. By the time of the investment, Vinaver Medical's technology was already being tested in collaboration with clinics in the region as well as in the US and EU markets, giving the company early international clinical validation alongside its domestic Serbian investor base.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a software-only solution designed for integration into existing manufacturer-agnostic imaging hardware, rather than requiring hospitals to purchase new equipment, substantially lowering the barrier to clinical adoption.
- Assembled a genuinely interdisciplinary founding team (physicists, programmers, radiologists) rather than a purely technical or purely clinical team, directly addressing the dual technical-and-medical expertise that spectral-CT-based oncology diagnostics software requires.
- Pursued clinical testing partnerships in the US and EU markets alongside regional clinics from an early stage, rather than validating only domestically first, building international credibility in parallel with local funding.
- Focused specifically on particle radiotherapy planning as the area of greatest technological impact, rather than positioning the software as a general diagnostic tool, sharpening its value proposition for a specific high-value clinical use case.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Vinaver Medical demonstrates that deep-tech medical software ventures can achieve meaningful international clinical traction even from smaller regional tech ecosystems like Serbia's, provided the founding team combines the specific interdisciplinary expertise (physics, radiology, software) the technology genuinely requires. Building the software to integrate with existing, manufacturer-agnostic imaging hardware rather than requiring new equipment purchases is also a broadly reusable adoption strategy for healthtech startups selling into cost- and capital-constrained hospital systems, where the barrier to trying a new software layer is far lower than the barrier to a full equipment upgrade.
