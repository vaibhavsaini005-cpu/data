## One-liner

Seluxit CEO Daniel Lux and the board decided on June 25, 2024 to file the Danish IoT company for bankruptcy after unsuccessful capital-raising efforts left it unable to meet obligations; Nasdaq removed its 9,632,961 shares from trading on July 1.

## What happened

Seluxit said its SmartMeter sales had stalled during the pandemic and that the important German market never recovered. Attempts to offset the decline through new geographies and products in energy-management systems and education technology developed too slowly, producing significant revenue declines and losses over the following two financial years.

Management tried to strengthen the capital base, but Seluxit said a low share price, inflation, rising interest rates, and delayed orders frustrated those efforts. Even with larger customer orders during the preceding six months and an improving pipeline, liquidity and cash generation could not cover obligations as they fell due. Lux and the board therefore chose bankruptcy on June 25, 2024. Nasdaq subsequently confirmed that the company had been declared bankrupt and removed its shares from trading on July 1.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Management tried to replace lost SmartMeter revenue with new products and new geographic markets, but sales cycles were too slow.
- Lux and the board pursued ways to reinforce Seluxit's capital base as liquidity deteriorated.
- On June 25, 2024, they concluded that cash flow could not meet due obligations and decided to file for bankruptcy.
- The measurable outcome was a declared bankruptcy and the removal of all 9,632,961 Seluxit shares from Nasdaq First North on July 1, 2024.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Seluxit shows how an apparently improving order pipeline can arrive too late to rescue a cash-constrained hardware-software business. New-market expansion and adjacent products did not mature fast enough to replace a damaged core market, while financing conditions narrowed management's remaining options.
