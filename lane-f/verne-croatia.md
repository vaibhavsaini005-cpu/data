## One-liner

Verne, a Croatian autonomous-vehicle startup spun out of Mate Rimac's Rimac Group, closed a Series A round bringing total funding to roughly €100M in July 2024, with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund making its first-ever technology investment outside the Kingdom through the round.

## What happened

Verne began in 2019 as an internal project called Project 3 Mobility (P3) within Rimac Group — the ecosystem of companies built by hypercar maker Bugatti Rimac's founder Mate Rimac — before being officially presented as an independent company in June 2024 at the Rimac Campus near Zagreb. The company builds fully autonomous, two-seat electric vehicles designed to operate as urban robotaxi services, complete with a consumer-facing app and dedicated hubs for charging, cleaning, and maintenance, with Zagreb planned as the first launch city.

The July 2024 Series A round brought total funding to approximately €100M, with investors including TASARU Mobility Investments (backed by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund), Rimac Automobili, Infinum, Kia Motors, SiteGround, and Neurone Technologies. Notably, this investment marked TASARU's first-ever technology-sector investment outside Saudi Arabia, a significant validation signal given the Public Investment Fund's scale and selectivity as one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Incubated the autonomous-vehicle project inside an existing, well-resourced corporate ecosystem (Rimac Group) for years before spinning it out independently, allowing access to automotive engineering expertise and capital without needing to build that infrastructure from scratch as a standalone startup.
- Attracted a major automotive strategic investor (Kia Motors) alongside sovereign wealth capital (TASARU/PIF), combining industry-specific technical validation with globally significant financial backing in the same round.
- Targeted the robotaxi/autonomous urban mobility category specifically — a capital-intensive, technically ambitious segment — leveraging Rimac's existing automotive engineering credibility to make the bet more credible to investors than an unproven founding team might achieve alone.
- Chose Zagreb, a smaller European capital, as the launch market rather than a larger, more competitive robotaxi market, likely reducing regulatory and competitive friction for an initial deployment.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Verne's raise shows how incubating an ambitious, capital-intensive hardware/mobility startup inside an established, credible corporate ecosystem (Rimac Group) before spinning out independently can accelerate access to both engineering talent and investor trust — a markedly different path from a standalone founding team building the same category from zero. The Saudi PIF's first technology investment outside the Kingdom going to a small Croatian startup is also a notable signal of how sovereign wealth funds are increasingly willing to back ambitious deep-tech mobility bets in smaller European markets when the underlying team and technical credibility (via Rimac's automotive pedigree) are strong enough.
