## One-liner

Natech Banking Solutions, a banking-technology platform based in Ioannina, Greece, secured over €28.1 million in Series B growth funding in June 2025 — split between €22.1M equity and €5.9M low-interest debt — to expand its modular, cloud-native banking infrastructure across the EU and support the launch of Snappi Bank, its joint venture with Piraeus Financial Holdings.

## What happened

Natech built modular, cloud-native banking platforms and Banking-as-a-Service (BaaS) tools that let banks and fintechs rapidly deploy customer-centric financial products without building core banking infrastructure from scratch — addressing a persistent bottleneck for financial institutions seeking to modernize legacy systems or launch new digital banking products quickly. Notably, the company is based in Ioannina, a regional Greek city rather than Athens, demonstrating that fintech infrastructure companies can build globally competitive products outside a country's primary capital-city tech hub.

The June 2025 Series B round, structured as a blend of €22.1 million equity and €5.9 million long-term, low-interest debt, was committed specifically ahead of the public launch of Snappi Bank — a joint venture between Natech and Piraeus Financial Holdings, one of Greece's major banking groups. This partnership with an established incumbent bank reflects a strategic path for banking-infrastructure startups: rather than solely selling technology to banks as an external vendor, Natech co-created a new banking product with a major incumbent, directly monetizing its infrastructure through equity participation in the resulting venture rather than software-licensing revenue alone.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built the company's headquarters and engineering base in Ioannina rather than Athens, demonstrating that Greece's regional cities can support globally competitive fintech infrastructure companies rather than requiring concentration in the capital.
- Structured the Series B as blended equity-and-debt financing rather than pure equity, likely optimizing capital cost across different funding needs (growth capital via equity, working capital via low-interest debt).
- Pursued a joint venture with an established incumbent bank (Piraeus Financial Holdings) to launch Snappi Bank, rather than solely licensing its platform to banks as an arms-length vendor, creating a deeper equity-aligned commercial relationship.
- Timed the funding round specifically to precede the public launch of Snappi Bank, ensuring capital was in place ahead of a major go-to-market milestone rather than raising reactively after launch.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Natech demonstrates that banking-infrastructure fintechs can deepen their commercial relationships with incumbent banks by co-creating new banking products as joint ventures, rather than remaining purely arms-length technology vendors — a model that aligns incentives more closely and can generate equity upside alongside licensing revenue. The company's regional Greek origin (Ioannina rather than Athens) also illustrates that fintech infrastructure businesses, which sell primarily to enterprise financial-institution customers rather than needing proximity to a large consumer market, can build globally competitive companies from secondary cities within smaller European startup ecosystems.
