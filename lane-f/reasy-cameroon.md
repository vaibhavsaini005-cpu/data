## One-liner

REasy, a Cameroonian fintech and logistics platform founded in 2023 by Mba Mathieu Wing, Richard Kue, and Stéphane Meng to simplify cross-border trade payments between African importers and Chinese suppliers, raised a $1.8M pre-seed round in October 2025 led by Ingressive Capital, Launch Africa, and 54 Collective.

## What happened

REasy — short for "Rapid and Easy" — built a fintech and logistics platform specifically addressing barriers facing African importers engaged in cross-border trade with China: high transaction costs, fraud risk, and limited access to trade finance. Rather than serving general cross-border payments broadly, the company focused specifically on the Africa-China trade corridor, a high-volume but historically underserved segment for African SME importers.

The October 2025 $1.8M pre-seed round, backed by Ingressive Capital, Launch Africa, and 54 Collective, followed earlier angel backing including a 32,824.42 EUR (21.5 million CFA) investment from the Cameroon Angels Network — at the time the network's largest investment, signaling growing local investor confidence in solutions addressing African trade inefficiencies. Notable individual investors in the pre-seed round included Christophe Chausson (Chausson Partners) and Mathias Léopoldie, founder of Julaya (another Francophone African fintech), reflecting cross-pollination of expertise from adjacent African fintech founders.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused specifically on the Africa-China trade corridor rather than general cross-border payments, targeting a high-volume, historically underserved segment where African SME importers faced particular pain points around cost, fraud, and financing access.
- Combined payments and logistics/shipping into one platform rather than solving only the payments piece, addressing the full trade workflow importers need to manage.
- Built local investor confidence first (Cameroon Angels Network's largest investment to date) before securing international pan-African VC backing (Ingressive Capital, Launch Africa, 54 Collective), sequencing capital sources from local validation to broader institutional support.
- Attracted a strategic angel investor who was himself a successful Francophone African fintech founder (Julaya's Mathias Léopoldie), bringing directly relevant sector experience alongside capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

REasy demonstrates that African fintechs solving for a specific, well-defined trade corridor (Africa-China imports) rather than general-purpose cross-border payments can find a clear wedge into a market with real, quantifiable pain points (cost, fraud, financing access) for importers. The sequencing of capital — building local angel network confidence before securing international VC backing — also illustrates a viable fundraising path for Francophone African startups in markets that receive comparatively less international VC attention than Anglophone hubs like Nigeria or Kenya.
