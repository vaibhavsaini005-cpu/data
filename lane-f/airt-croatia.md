## One-liner

Airt, a Zagreb-founded AI startup co-founded in 2019 by Davor Runje and Hajdi Ćenan, pivoted from its original financial-AI product to agentic AI and autonomous systems after early implementation challenges, culminating in its acquisition by health-tech company Synthpop in November 2025.

## What happened

Airt originally built AI technology targeting financial applications, working from a mission to make AI usable on selected data "in the simplest, easiest and quickest way." The company encountered implementation challenges scaling that initial financial-AI product — a common failure point for early AI startups whose technical capability outpaces a clear, monetizable enterprise use case. Rather than shutting down, Airt's founders redirected the company's technical foundation toward agentic AI and autonomous systems, a rapidly growing category as enterprises sought AI systems capable of executing multi-step tasks rather than only generating outputs for human review.

That pivot ultimately led to Airt's acquisition by Synthpop in November 2025. Synthpop, a health-tech company that had recently secured $15 million in funding to automate complex administrative processes in healthcare, acquired Airt to bring in its agentic AI and autonomous-systems technical capabilities — applying general-purpose agentic AI infrastructure to the specific, high-value problem of healthcare administrative automation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Recognized that the original financial-AI product faced implementation challenges limiting its path to scale, rather than continuing to invest further in a struggling initial direction.
- Redirected the company's core technical capabilities toward the emerging agentic AI and autonomous-systems category rather than shutting down after the first product's limitations became clear.
- Found an acquirer (Synthpop) whose specific commercial need — automating complex healthcare administrative processes — was a strong match for Airt's pivoted agentic AI capabilities, rather than seeking a generic technology-licensing outcome.
- Achieved an acquisition exit as the resolution to a product pivot, illustrating how a technically capable team can find an outcome for investors and the team even when the original product concept does not scale independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Airt's trajectory illustrates a specific pattern in AI startup pivots: when a promising underlying technical capability struggles to find product-market fit in its original application (financial AI, in this case), redirecting that same core technology toward an adjacent, faster-growing category (agentic AI) can create a viable path to an acquisition exit, even without a large independent commercial success. The deal also demonstrates how specialized vertical AI companies (Synthpop in healthcare administration) are increasingly acquiring general-purpose AI capability providers to accelerate their own roadmaps, rather than building comparable agentic AI infrastructure entirely in-house — a buy-versus-build dynamic that can provide exit paths for smaller AI teams with strong technical foundations but limited independent commercial traction.
