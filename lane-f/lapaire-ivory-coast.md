## One-liner

Ivory Coast-founded eyecare startup Lapaire raised a combined $6M across two 2024 rounds — a $3M raise led by Investisseurs & Partenaires in January and further funding from AfricInvest and Proparco in October — to expand affordable optical retail from its West African base into Senegal, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Tanzania.

## What happened

Founded in 2018 by Jérôme Lapaire, the company built an optical retail chain delivering affordable prescription eyewear and vision care across West and East Africa, a category with historically limited access outside major urban centers on the continent. By 2024, Lapaire already operated in Ivory Coast, Benin, Togo, Mali, Burkina Faso, and Uganda before pursuing two funding rounds in the same year to accelerate expansion.

The January round, a $3M raise led by impact investment fund Investisseurs & Partenaires (I&P) with participation from AAIC, FINCA Ventures, and Beyond Capital, funded continued expansion across existing markets. The October round — $2.5M approved by AfricInvest plus $488,000 from Proparco's Bridge by Digital Africa facility — specifically funded entry into three new markets: Senegal, DR Congo, and Tanzania, reflecting a deliberate geographic-expansion financing structure with two distinct capital sources for "deepen existing markets" versus "enter new markets."

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted a healthtech category (vision care) with clear, measurable unmet need across a large population, rather than a more saturated consumer or fintech vertical.
- Built a bricks-and-mortar retail network (physical optical stores) in a sector often assumed to require significant capital for real estate and inventory, rather than pursuing an online-only model — demonstrating investor confidence in physical retail infrastructure for this specific healthcare category.
- Sequenced two funding rounds within the same year with distinct purposes (deepening existing markets vs. entering new ones), allowing investors with different risk appetites (impact funds vs. development finance institutions) to participate at the stage matching their mandate.
- Attracted development finance institutions (Proparco, AfricInvest) alongside impact investors, signaling the company's positioning as measurable social-impact infrastructure, not just a commercial retail play.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lapaire demonstrates that healthcare-access startups addressing basic unmet needs (affordable vision care) can attract blended impact and development-finance capital even while operating a capital-intensive physical retail model, when the social impact case is clear and measurable. The dual-round financing structure — separating "strengthen the core" capital from "expand geography" capital — is a useful pattern for other physical-infrastructure healthtech startups in emerging markets seeking to match investor risk appetite to specific growth phases.
