## One-liner

Barcelona-based open banking infrastructure company Unnax, a licensed Electronic Money Institution holding a triple license from the Bank of Spain, was acquired by French fintech Powens for approximately €40M — announced in April 2023 and closed in 2024 — creating a combined open finance platform spanning Southern Europe and Latin America.

## What happened

Unnax built open banking and embedded finance infrastructure, holding a rare triple license from the Bank of Spain that let it operate as a licensed Electronic Money Institution across Spain and Latin American markets, harnessing banking infrastructure to simplify payments and streamline access to financial data through a unified API — a regulatory position that took significant time and cost to establish and became the company's core competitive asset. PSG Equity's investment in France's Powens paved the way for Powens and Unnax to formally announce their intent to combine in April 2023, with the acquisition completing in 2024 at a value of roughly €40M.

The combination brought together Powens' presence in France and broader Europe with Unnax's licensed infrastructure and market position in Spain and Latin America, creating a single open finance group with materially wider geographic reach than either company held independently — a consolidation move reflecting how capital-intensive and regulatorily complex it is to build licensed payment infrastructure market-by-market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Invested in obtaining a triple regulatory license from the Bank of Spain — a slow, resource-intensive process — which became the primary asset making Unnax an attractive acquisition target rather than merely a feature layer that could be replicated quickly by a competitor.
- Pursued an acquisition/consolidation exit rather than continuing to raise independent growth capital to expand geographically on its own, opting to combine complementary regional footprints (Spain/LatAm plus France/Europe) rather than compete for the same expansion.
- The deal was backed by a private equity firm (PSG Equity) rather than a strategic corporate acquirer, reflecting financial-sponsor consolidation logic in the fragmented European open banking infrastructure space.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Unnax's acquisition illustrates a common maturation pattern in regulated fintech infrastructure: once a company has secured a hard-to-replicate regulatory license, consolidation with a geographically complementary peer becomes a more capital-efficient path to scale than years of market-by-market license applications and expansion. It's a useful signal for other regionally licensed fintech infrastructure providers — the license itself, not just the software, is frequently the asset that drives acquisition value and consolidation logic in this space.
