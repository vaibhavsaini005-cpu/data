## One-liner

Ivorian fintech Julaya raised an additional $5M pre-Series A in September 2022 (bringing the round to $7M total) to become a digital banking partner for businesses across Francophone West Africa, digitizing B2B payments in a region still dominated by cash and manual bank transfers.

## What happened

Julaya built infrastructure to digitize business-to-business payments — payroll, supplier payments, and bulk disbursements — for companies operating in Francophone West Africa, a region where business payments still relied heavily on cash and manual bank processes despite consumer mobile money being relatively widespread. The company first raised $2M in a pre-Series A round in mid-2021, then extended that same round with an additional $5M in September 2022, bringing the total to $7M as it positioned itself as a banking partner for businesses rather than just a payments processor.

The extension round let Julaya expand its footprint across Francophone West Africa beyond its Ivory Coast base, targeting a segment — formal SME and corporate payment infrastructure — that had received less fintech investment attention than consumer-facing mobile money in the region.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted B2B payment digitization specifically, a less crowded segment than consumer mobile money, which already had entrenched players across Francophone Africa.
- Extended an existing pre-Series A round with a second $5M tranche in 2022 rather than immediately pursuing a new round, building on investor relationships already established.
- Positioned itself explicitly as a "banking partner" for businesses rather than a narrower payments tool, signaling ambition to own a broader piece of the business financial infrastructure stack.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Julaya shows that within Africa's broader fintech boom, differentiated positioning within an underserved sub-segment (formal business payments, as distinct from crowded consumer mobile money) can attract sustained investor interest across multiple rounds. Extending an existing round rather than resetting fundraising terms from scratch also reflects a capital-efficient way to scale within an established investor relationship during a period when new fundraises were becoming harder to close.
