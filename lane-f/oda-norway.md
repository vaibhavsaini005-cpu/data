## One-liner

SoftBank-backed Norwegian online grocery delivery startup Oda retreated from its broader Northern European expansion, laying off 150 staff and resetting focus back to just Norway and Sweden after 2023's funding environment made continued multi-country expansion unsustainable.

## What happened

Oda built an online supermarket delivery model in Norway, expanding into Germany and Sweden with SoftBank backing during the pandemic-era e-grocery investment wave. When financing conditions tightened through 2023, the company reversed course on its expansion strategy — pulling back from broader Northern European ambitions and laying off 150 employees as part of a formal reset to concentrate resources on its two strongest markets, Norway and Sweden.

The move reflected a wider pattern among venture-backed e-grocery startups globally: businesses that scaled delivery infrastructure and inventory across multiple countries during a period of cheap capital and elevated pandemic demand found that model unsustainable once both funding and consumer online-grocery demand normalized.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Expanded into Germany during the pandemic-era funding boom, betting on continued high online-grocery demand and capital availability.
- Reversed the multi-country expansion once 2023's funding conditions tightened, choosing retrenchment over continuing to fund unprofitable new markets.
- Concentrated remaining resources specifically on Norway and Sweden — its most established markets — rather than spreading thin across all prior territories.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Oda's pivot illustrates that pandemic-era demand spikes in categories like online grocery were not a reliable signal for permanent multi-country expansion — many operators, including Oda, mistook a temporary demand and funding environment for durable market conditions. The 2023-2024 retreat to core markets shows a disciplined response once that became clear: choosing to protect profitability in proven markets over continuing to subsidize growth in newer, less-established ones.
