## One-liner

Seoul-based rocket startup Unastella, founded and led by CEO Jae Park, raised a $24 million Series B — bringing total funding to $44 million — months after becoming the first Korean private company to launch its own rocket, UNA EXPRESS-I, from home soil.

## What happened

Jae Park, who previously worked on combustion systems for Korea's state-built Nuri orbital launch vehicle at the Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI), founded Unastella to build and commercially operate small-satellite launch vehicles from South Korea rather than relying on foreign launch providers. In May 2025, Unastella flew UNA EXPRESS-I from South Korean soil — a private-sector milestone launch for the country. On June 1, 2026, the company announced a $24 million Series B round led by Altos Ventures, with Korea Development Bank, Strong Ventures, Hana Ventures, KDB Capital, Woori Venture Partners and Samho Green Investment also participating, bringing Unastella's total funding to $44 million.

Park was explicit that Unastella's engineering choices are driven by commercial speed rather than technical maximalism: "We're not an R&D group trying to build the most impressive rocket. We're a commercial launch company trying to get to market fast." In line with that, Park chose an electric motor pump over a traditional turbopump for the rocket's propulsion system — a simpler, cheaper design that adds weight and reduces payload capacity, a deliberate tradeoff of maximum satellite-carrying capability for faster time-to-market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Leaving a role on Korea's national rocket program to build a private, commercial launch company**: Park applied direct KARI/Nuri combustion-systems experience to a company betting Korea could support a domestic private launch provider, not just a state one.
- **Choosing an electric motor pump over a turbopump**: a specific, named engineering tradeoff — simpler and cheaper, at the cost of payload capacity — made explicitly to prioritize speed to market over technical performance.
- **Sequencing a home-soil demonstration launch (UNA EXPRESS-I, May 2025) before the Series B raise**: Park used a flown vehicle, not just a design, as the proof point to bring in Series B investors a year later.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Unastella's path shows a founder converting narrow technical experience from a national space program into a commercial venture by deliberately under-optimizing on the metric (payload capacity) that publicly-funded rocket programs typically maximize, in exchange for speed. Sequencing a real, flown launch before raising a large round — rather than raising on simulations or ground tests alone — de-risked the Series B for investors and let Park make the "fast beats impressive" tradeoff explicit rather than implicit, which is a reusable playbook for deep-tech founders spinning out of state research institutes into commercial startups.
