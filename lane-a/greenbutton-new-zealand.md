## One-liner

GreenButton, a Wellington-founded "big compute" cloud startup (originally
InterGrid), was acquired by Microsoft in 2014 and its technology became part
of Azure's high-performance computing offering.

## What happened

Founded in 2006 by Scott Houston, formerly CTO of Weta Digital, the company
started as InterGrid before launching its GreenButton cloud service in 2010,
giving software vendors and enterprises a way to burst compute-intensive
workloads to the cloud without rewriting their applications. Customers
included Pixar (a cloud rendering service for RenderMan, announced 2012),
Boeing, GNS Science and oil explorer Stillwater Group, and by 2011 Microsoft
had named it Windows Azure ISV Partner of the Year. On May 2, 2014, Microsoft
announced it was acquiring GreenButton outright, with deal terms undisclosed,
to strengthen Azure's "Big Compute" capabilities. GreenButton's technology
was subsequently integrated into Azure and today underpins what operates as
Azure Batch.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Pivoting from a generic grid-computing tool (InterGrid) to a focused cloud
  "burst compute" product (GreenButton) aimed at ISVs with spiky
  high-performance workloads.
- Landing marquee reference customers (Pixar, Boeing) that gave a small New
  Zealand startup credibility with a large enterprise acquirer.
- Deepening the Microsoft Azure partnership (ISV Partner of the Year, 2011)
  years before the acquisition, which likely set up Microsoft as the natural
  buyer rather than a competitive sale process.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GreenButton is a clean case of a small, deep-tech infrastructure startup
building toward strategic acquisition by a hyperscaler rather than chasing
an independent scale-up path — going deep on a narrow, technically hard
problem (bursting HPC workloads to the cloud) and building a partnership
with the eventual acquirer over several years before the deal.
