## One-liner

Coda Payments, a Singapore-based fintech founded in 2011, became a unicorn in 2022 by solving one of gaming's least glamorous problems: collecting cash payments from gamers in emerging markets where credit cards don't reach.

## What happened

Coda Payments was founded in 2011 by Philippe Laval, a former telecom executive, who noticed that global game publishers — Tencent, Activision Blizzard, Riot Games, Krafton, and others — were leaving enormous revenue on the table in Southeast Asia, South Asia, and the Middle East because they could not collect payments from players who lacked credit cards or international bank accounts.

Coda built infrastructure to bridge that gap: a Merchant of Record platform that connects game publishers to 400+ local payment methods (e-wallets, over-the-counter bank transfers, convenience store cash payments, telco billing) across 70+ countries. Publishers embed Coda's checkout, and Coda handles the local tax compliance, currency conversion, fraud risk, and settlement — entirely invisible to the end player.

By 2022, Coda had processed payments for major global titles (Genshin Impact, PUBG Mobile, League of Legends, Call of Duty Mobile) across Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, India, and beyond. In April 2022, the company was reported to be raising a Series C round at a valuation above $2.5 billion, led by GIC (Singapore's sovereign wealth fund), Insight Partners, and Smash Ventures — cementing its unicorn status.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Merchant of Record model:** Rather than acting as a pure payment gateway (and leaving compliance and currency risk with publishers), Coda took on the MoR role — absorbing local regulatory complexity in exchange for higher take rates and stickier publisher relationships.
- **Gaming-only focus:** Coda resisted the temptation to expand into general e-commerce early, building deep integrations and trust with game publishers before eventually extending to streaming and digital content. The vertical focus is what made it credible to Tencent and Riot.
- **Southeast Asia first, then global:** Starting in markets where the payment gap was most acute gave Coda a beachhead before expanding to the Middle East and South Asia, where a similar infrastructure gap existed.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Coda is a textbook infrastructure play: it built boring-but-essential plumbing for an industry (gaming) whose players are visible but whose payment rails are not. The pattern — find where a high-value global industry is underserved in emerging markets, own the local compliance and payment complexity, become indispensable — is replicable across sectors. Coda also demonstrates that Singapore can produce globally competitive fintech infrastructure companies without being anchored to the Singapore domestic market.
