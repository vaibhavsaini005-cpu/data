## One-liner

Philippine Stock Exchange-listed Xurpas, a mobile content and games publisher, pivoted hard into blockchain in 2018 by spinning up a Singapore subsidiary to sell up to $100M in utility tokens for a "free internet" project.

## What happened

Xurpas Inc. built its business on mobile consumer content and value-added services for phone subscribers — casual games, airtime management, content development, and mobile marketing — alongside enterprise IT staffing and systems integration work for telcos. As the mobile-content business matured and crypto markets ran hot in early 2018, listed chair and CEO Nix (Nico Jose) Nolledo announced a strategic pivot: "today it is the blockchain."

The centerpiece was ODX Pte., a Xurpas subsidiary incorporated in Singapore, which planned to use blockchain to provide free, sponsored internet data access to emerging-market mobile consumers. ODX intended to raise up to $100 million by selling utility tokens in a token generation event, with backers including Bitcoin Foundation chairman Brock Pierce. Xurpas disclosed the venture to the Philippine Stock Exchange in filings through mid-2018 and said ODX would be the first of several blockchain projects layered on top of its existing mobile and enterprise IT businesses.

The 2018 crypto downturn undercut the token-sale model industry-wide, and Xurpas subsequently repositioned again toward enterprise IT services — systems integration, staff augmentation, and outsourced/custom software development, including blockchain-based application development work for enterprise clients rather than consumer token sales. The company remains listed on the PSE.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Riding the 2018 ICO wave**: Xurpas moved to launch a $100M utility-token sale for ODX at the height of the global ICO boom, betting blockchain-funded free internet access could scale across emerging markets.
- **Singapore subsidiary structure**: Housing the blockchain venture (ODX) in a separate Singapore entity rather than the PSE-listed parent limited direct disclosure/regulatory exposure for the core business.
- **Retreat to enterprise IT services**: When the token-sale model collapsed with the broader 2018 crypto crash, Xurpas fell back on its more defensible enterprise IT and systems-integration business rather than doubling down on consumer blockchain products.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Xurpas illustrates the risk of a publicly listed operating company bolting a speculative token-sale venture onto its core business during a market mania — the ODX token sale never delivered the free-internet product at scale, but the underlying mobile-content and IT-services businesses gave Xurpas a fallback that pure-play ICO startups from the same era didn't have. It's a useful contrast to failures where the entire company was the speculative bet.
