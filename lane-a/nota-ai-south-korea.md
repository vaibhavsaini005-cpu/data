## One-liner

Korean AI optimization company Nota, co-founded by CEO Myungsu Chae and CTO Tae-ho Kim, listed on the KOSDAQ on November 3, 2025 at a roughly $462 million valuation, becoming the first Korean company specializing in AI model compression to go public.

## What happened

Myungsu Chae and Tae-ho Kim founded Nota in 2015 to build NetsPresso, an automated AI model optimization platform that compresses AI models — through techniques like pruning and quantization — to run efficiently on edge devices and smaller hardware without sacrificing accuracy. The company built up a decade-long funding history from seed backing by Naver D2SF and Blue Point Partners, through a 2020 Series A (KRW 8 billion from LB Investment, Samsung SDS and LG CNS), a 2021 Series B (KRW 17.5 billion), and a 2024 Series C (KRW 53.2 billion from Stick Ventures, LB Investment, KDB Industrial Bank and Mirae Asset Securities), before pursuing an IPO through 2025.

Chae was explicit at each stage of the IPO process that the listing was a strategic, not just financial, decision. Filing in May 2025, he said the move would "accelerate our innovation capabilities and significantly boost our global market penetration." After securing fast-track preliminary approval in July, he framed it as proof of maturity: "Nota AI is more than just a promising startup — we're a globally recognized company with proven technology and business impact." At the listing itself on November 3, 2025, Chae summarized the decision's purpose: "This IPO is a crucial strategic decision that goes beyond mere capital procurement, serving instead as a definitive opportunity to earn the trust of the global market and establish a robust foundation for exponential future growth."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Committing a decade (2015-2025) to building a narrow technical specialty (AI model compression/optimization) before going public**: Chae and Kim built proprietary technology (NetsPresso) and enterprise credibility over 10 years rather than a fast-scale-then-exit approach.
- **Choosing an IPO over continued private funding rounds despite raising a large Series C ($53.2 KRW billion) just one year earlier**: Chae explicitly framed going public as a trust and credibility move for global expansion, not a response to a private-funding shortfall.
- **Pursuing a fast-track KOSDAQ listing process amid what Chae called "tightened listing requirements"**: used the fast-track approval itself as external validation of the company's technology and business fundamentals before the market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Nota's IPO shows a specific playbook for deep-tech AI infrastructure companies: spend a full decade building genuine technical differentiation and enterprise partnerships (Naver, Samsung SDS, LG CNS as investors) before treating a public listing as a credibility and global-expansion tool rather than a liquidity event. Chae's repeated framing of the IPO as strategic trust-building, not capital-raising, is notable in a market where South Korean tech IPOs faced tightened requirements in 2025 — suggesting that demonstrable, long-accumulated enterprise validation was what got Nota through fast-track approval where less-established companies might not have qualified.
