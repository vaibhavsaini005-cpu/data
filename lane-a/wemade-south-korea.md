## One-liner

Kosdaq-listed Korean gaming veteran Wemade pivoted its flagship MMORPG franchise into a play-to-earn blockchain empire built around its WEMIX token, only to see South Korea's top exchanges delist WEMIX in December 2022 over disclosure violations and the tax authority later demand $41 million.

## What happened

Wemade, a long-running Korean game developer best known for "The Legend of Mir" series, pivoted hard into blockchain gaming when it launched MIR4 globally on August 25, 2021, integrating an in-game token economy that let players mine "Darksteel" and convert it into the DRACO token. MIR4 became the first commercially successful title to fuse mainstream MMORPG gameplay with a blockchain economy, reportedly reaching over 1.4 million concurrent users and 20 million registered accounts within months, and Wemade used that momentum to push its broader WEMIX platform (originally launched in 2019) as a hub for other games. The pivot unraveled in late 2022: the Digital Asset Exchange Alliance (DAXA), representing South Korea's five largest crypto exchanges — Bithumb, Upbit, Coinone, Korbit, and Gopax — accused Wemade of filing false circulating-supply disclosures for WEMIX and delisted the token from four exchanges on December 8, 2022, wiping out more than 380 billion won (about $287.5 million) in market capitalization in the process. Wemade contested the delisting in court and continued expanding WEMIX3.0 as an independent EVM-compatible chain, but by January 2024 South Korea's National Tax Service issued the company a $41 million bill covering its crypto operations from 2019-2022, and prosecutors separately indicted a former Wemade CEO over allegedly fabricated WEMIX token data.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Betting the company's growth story on token-based gaming**: rather than treating WEMIX as a side experiment, Wemade made it central to its valuation and public narrative, tying a legacy game publisher's fortunes to a volatile, newly regulated asset class.
- **Disclosure practices lagging regulatory expectations**: DAXA's core complaint wasn't that WEMIX failed commercially but that Wemade's public circulating-supply figures didn't match reality — a governance failure specific to token issuers that traditional game publishers had never had to manage.
- **Continuing to double down post-delisting**: rather than retreating from crypto after the December 2022 delisting, Wemade pushed forward with WEMIX3.0's independent mainnet and further international blockchain-fund partnerships, accepting continued regulatory and tax exposure as the cost of staying in the space.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Wemade shows what happens when a traditional software company pivots into token issuance without building exchange-grade disclosure discipline: the underlying product (MIR4) was a genuine commercial hit, but the token-supply governance problem was severe enough to erase hundreds of millions in market value in a single day and invite a multi-year regulatory and tax reckoning. It's a pattern likely to recur anywhere a listed operating company tries to fund growth through a token whose issuance isn't held to the same reporting standard as its equity.
