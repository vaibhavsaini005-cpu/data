## One-liner

New Zealand's flagship health IT company listed on NZX and ASX in 2014 at NZ$600 million, posted persistent losses it could not reverse, and was taken private in 2021 via a founder-led buyout at a steep discount to its IPO price.

## What happened

Orion Health was founded in Auckland in 1993 by Ian McCrae, a New Zealand software engineer, to build clinical information systems for hospitals. By the 2000s, it had grown into one of the world's leading health information exchange (HIE) vendors — software that enables hospitals, GPs, labs, and insurers to share patient data across institutional boundaries. Customers included health systems in the U.S., U.K., Canada, and Australia. In November 2014, McCrae listed the company on both the NZX and ASX at NZ$5.70 per share, implying a market capitalisation of approximately NZ$590 million. The listing was intended to fund global expansion and product development. Post-IPO, Orion Health consistently missed profitability targets. Revenue grew slowly while operating losses mounted — driven by high R&D spending required to maintain competitive products across multiple international markets, and by the long sales cycles inherent to government and hospital procurement. COVID-19 compounded the pressure in 2020, delaying contract decisions and straining the customer pipeline. By early 2021, with the share price having fallen well below the NZ$5.70 IPO level, McCrae proposed a scheme of arrangement under which his family's trust would acquire all shares not already held by insiders. Minority shareholders approved the scheme, and the company delisted from both exchanges in 2021. Orion Health continues to operate as a private company.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2014 dual listing**: The NZX/ASX listing gave the company growth capital and a public profile but imposed quarterly scrutiny on a business with government-paced sales cycles — a structural mismatch. Healthcare IT deals often take 12–24 months to close; public market patience typically runs shorter.
- **Multi-market strategy**: Chasing customers simultaneously in the U.S., U.K., Australia, and NZ spread engineering and sales resources thin. A narrower geographic focus might have produced earlier profitability, but McCrae believed the HIE market was winner-takes-all globally.
- **Founder buyout over external sale**: McCrae chose to buy back the company himself rather than sell to a strategic acquirer (several large health IT companies could have been interested). This preserved operational independence and New Zealand ownership but delivered a poor outcome for public shareholders who had bought at or near the IPO price.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Orion Health is a canonical case of a technically strong, niche-market software company ill-suited to public markets at its revenue scale. Healthcare IT's long procurement cycles, high regulatory complexity, and relationship-driven sales make it difficult to produce the quarter-on-quarter growth trajectory that listed tech companies require. The pattern — undervalued on public markets, taken private, continues to operate — appears frequently in B2G (business-to-government) and clinical software: companies that have real customers and real recurring revenue but not the growth rate or margin profile that justifies a public listing. For New Zealand specifically, the story raises questions about the NZX's depth of capital for mid-stage tech companies in slow-growth enterprise sectors.
