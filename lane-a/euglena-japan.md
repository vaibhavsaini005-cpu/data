## One-liner

Euglena Co. started out selling algae-based health food and cosmetics, then spent over a decade and hundreds of millions of yen redirecting the same core technology into producing Japan's first domestically made renewable jet fuel.

## What happened

Mitsuru Izumo founded Euglena Co. in 2005 after encountering severe childhood malnutrition in Bangladesh in 1998 and identifying the nutrient-dense microalgae species Euglena gracilis as a potential food-security solution. The company achieved the world's first outdoor mass-cultivation of Euglena algae that same year and built its initial business around algae-derived health foods, supplements, and cosmetics, listing on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's Mothers growth-stock section in December 2012 and graduating to the TSE First Section in December 2014.

Starting around 2014, Euglena began redirecting the same cultivation and lipid-extraction technology toward an entirely different market: renewable biofuel, specifically aviation fuel, a sector with essentially no direct alternative to fossil kerosene at commercial scale. The company blended an early euglena-based biofuel to power shuttle buses in 2014, then built Japan's first biojet/biodiesel demonstration production plant in Yokohama, completed in November 2018, with founder Izumo declaring an ambition to "make Japan the leader in biofuel." After further development with partners including Chevron Lummus Global and Applied Research Associates, Euglena's fuel met the ASTM D7566 Annex 6 international jet-fuel standard. On June 4, 2021, a Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism-operated aircraft flew using euglena-derived renewable jet fuel — the world's first such flight — followed weeks later by a HondaJet flight from Kagoshima to Haneda on June 29, 2021, opening limited commercial availability of the fuel to private jet operators.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Reusing core biotech IP for an unrelated end market**: Rather than staying in food/cosmetics or launching a separate biofuel company, Euglena redeployed its existing algae-cultivation and lipid-extraction capability directly into aviation fuel, a market with different customers, regulations, and unit economics entirely.
- **Building physical production infrastructure before having a committed offtake market**: Completing a dedicated demonstration plant in 2018, years before its fuel reached ASTM certification and its first real flights in 2021, was a multi-year capital commitment made without certainty the fuel would ever be certifiable or scalable.
- **Targeting a symbolic "world's first" flight milestone**: Structuring the 2021 government and HondaJet flights as public firsts served to establish Euglena's technology as a credible, verified aviation fuel — a reputational and marketing turning point as much as a technical one.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Euglena shows a pivot pattern distinct from most startup pivots: it didn't abandon its original product line (algae food and cosmetics still exist commercially) so much as it grew a second, capital-intensive business vertical out of the same underlying deep-tech capability, betting that a genuinely hard-to-replicate biological production process could be worth more solving a bigger, less-served problem (decarbonizing aviation) than the one it started with. It's a useful counter-example to the common startup advice to stay narrowly focused — Euglena's moat was the algae cultivation science itself, which turned out to be portable across two very different end markets over a 15+ year horizon.
