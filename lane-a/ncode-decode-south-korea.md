## One-liner

Ncode, operator of Korean luxury pre-order platform Decode, filed for closure in March 2025 after burning through 23.5 billion won ($17M+) in venture funding without escaping a global luxury-market downturn.

## What happened

Founded in June 2015, Ncode Inc. built Decode (디코드), a pre-order platform that let Korean shoppers order luxury and designer fashion goods from overseas brands ahead of stock arriving — a model that reduced inventory risk and had reportedly found early traction not just in Korea but in Japan as well. Over six funding rounds, Ncode raised a cumulative 23.5 billion won from investors including Woori Investment, Shinhan Venture Investment, SV Investment, KT Investment, and Mirae Asset Venture Capital, and was ranked alongside Mustit and Trenbe as one of Korea's three major luxury resale/pre-order platforms.

The company could not sustain the model through a global downturn in luxury spending. Facing worsening profitability and intensifying competition among Korea's luxury platforms, Ncode filed for business closure in March 2025. A July 2025 report on Korean startups that shut down despite heavy investment named Ncode as one of the largest failures of the year by cumulative funding raised, alongside autonomous-driving startup SpringCloud, in a wave the article tied to a broader "growth ladder" (성장사다리) funding pipeline coming under strain.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pre-order model exposure to demand swings**: Because Decode's model depended on forecasting consumer demand for luxury goods months ahead of delivery, a downturn in luxury spending hit working capital and margins directly rather than allowing inventory to be discounted down gradually.
- **Crowded category**: Competing directly against Mustit and Trenbe in the same narrow luxury-platform niche meant no single player had pricing power to protect margins when the category cooled.
- **No pivot before the cash ran out**: Unlike peers that diversified into resale or authentication services, Decode's public record shows no reported strategic pivot before the March 2025 closure filing.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Decode is a cautionary case for single-category e-commerce platforms built around a macro tailwind (in this case, Korea's luxury-spending boom of the early 2020s). When that tailwind reversed, heavy prior VC backing (23.5B won across six rounds) was not enough to buy time for a pivot — the capital had already been deployed against the boom-era model. It reinforces a recurring lesson in this dataset: well-funded, single-thesis platforms in narrow luxury/resale niches are especially exposed when the macro cycle turns, because the business model itself, not just execution, depends on the trend continuing.
