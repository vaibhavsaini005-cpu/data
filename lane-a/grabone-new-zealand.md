## One-liner

New Zealand's long-running daily-deals site GrabOne collapsed into liquidation in October 2025, owing more than NZ$16.5 million and unable to refund unredeemed vouchers.

## What happened

Global Marketplace New Zealand Limited, the entity operating GrabOne, ceased trading and entered liquidation on 16 October 2025. Liquidators Daniel Stoneman and Neale Jackson of Calibre Partners cited funding constraints as the cause and immediately began a sales process for the company's remaining business and assets. Reporting put total debts at over $16.5 million, including a related-party creditor claim of roughly $9.3 million, unsecured creditor claims around $3.9 million, and preferential claims of $518,531 (covering $365,278 in employee entitlements and $153,253 owed to Inland Revenue).

## Key decisions / turning points

GrabOne had operated as one of New Zealand's best-known deal-voucher platforms for over a decade, bundling discounted experiences, products, and travel offers. The liquidators' statement that the company had "ceased trading" with no rescue financing in place points to a funding crunch that hit suddenly rather than a managed wind-down — the business stopped promoting deals and pulled its social media presence around the same time liquidation was announced.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Voucher-based marketplaces carry a structural liability: prepaid customer funds and unredeemed vouchers become a liquidation-time liability rather than protected escrow. When GrabOne ran out of funding, consumers holding vouchers were told refunds were "unlikely" and would have to individually chase merchants — a reminder that deal-voucher platforms concentrate float risk on the platform itself, with little downside protection for end users when the business fails.
