## One-liner

Singapore-founded vegan discovery and review app Abillion shut down in
March 2026 after founder/CEO Vikas Garg concluded the company could not
raise through a fundraising environment reshaped by AI-driven investor
caution and platform consolidation.

## What happened

Founded in Singapore in 2018 by Vikas Garg, Abillion built a social
review platform for plant-based products and restaurants, eventually
covering roughly 1.3 million businesses across 183 countries and raising
over $17M, including a $10M Series A in 2021. In the last week of March
2026, Garg announced the company was shutting down by the end of the
month, telling users the team had hit its "best metrics to date" but
"simply couldn't overcome the realities of the current fundraising
environment" — citing shifting investor appetite, geopolitics, a wave of
non-surviving startups in the space, and AI-driven pause among investors
around software/internet businesses competing with mega-platforms like
Amazon, Meta, and Google.

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2018: Vikas Garg founds Abillion in Singapore as a vegan/plant-based
  discovery and review app.
- 2021: Raises a $10M Series A, part of $17M+ total raised.
- Late March 2026: Garg decides to wind down the main app after
  concluding a new funding round wasn't reachable, framing it explicitly
  as a fundraising-environment failure rather than a product/metrics one.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Garg's own explanation is notable for what it isn't: the company hit its
best-ever metrics right before shutting down, so this wasn't a product-
market-fit failure — it was a funding-environment call by the founder
that continuing to chase capital in a market spooked by AI disruption and
consolidating around mega-platforms wasn't worth the team's remaining
runway.
