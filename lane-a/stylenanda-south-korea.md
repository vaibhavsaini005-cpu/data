## One-liner

Seoul streetwear blog turned makeup brand 3CE sold 100% of itself to L'Oréal for roughly $371M, becoming the French group's first-ever Korean acquisition.

## What happened

Kim So-hee started StyleNanda in 2004 as an online fashion shop built off her own street-style photo blog. The brand's cosmetics line, 3CE (3 Concept Eyes), launched later but grew to generate over 70% of company revenue by the mid-2010s, riding the global K-beauty wave alongside K-pop's rising cultural export. By 2017 the company reported turnover of roughly €127 million with close to 400 employees, operating flagship stores in Korea and Japan and retailing into Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.

On May 2, 2018, L'Oréal announced it would acquire 100% of Nanda Co., Ltd. (StyleNanda's parent) — its first-ever takeover of a Korean company. The deal, reported at roughly 400 billion won (~$371M), closed on June 20, 2018 after regulatory clearance. L'Oréal kept founder Kim So-hee involved and used the acquisition to fold 3CE into its luxury/consumer products push into Asian color cosmetics, with an explicit strategy of turning 3CE into a global brand.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Blog-to-brand sequencing**: StyleNanda built an owned audience (fashion blog) before monetizing it as e-commerce, then let the makeup sub-brand (3CE) eventually eclipse the original fashion business in revenue share.
- **Riding, not fighting, the K-beauty wave**: rather than building a Western-style distribution deal, StyleNanda let regional demand (Korea, Japan, Southeast Asia) pull growth before an acquirer came calling.
- **Clean full-sale exit**: Kim sold 100% of the company rather than taking a strategic minority investment, converting founder equity into a lump-sum outcome instead of chasing an independent IPO.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

StyleNanda is a rare fully-verified, large-multiple exit out of Korea's D2C/cosmetics scene, and a template for how a single visually-led blog audience can be sequenced into a product business a multinational will pay a nine-figure sum for. It's also a useful counterpoint to the many failure/pivot stories logged elsewhere in this lane: the same regional cultural-export tailwind (K-pop, K-beauty) that some startups rode into overexpansion trouble is the exact tailwind StyleNanda converted into a clean acquisition.
