## One-liner

Sendo, once Vietnam's #2 e-commerce marketplace which had burned roughly $130M in funding, exited the general e-commerce market entirely in April 2025 after its market share fell below 1%, then shut down its subsequent grocery-delivery pivot Sendo Farm on July 9, 2025.

## What happened

Sendo built one of Vietnam's earliest and largest e-commerce marketplaces, competing directly against Shopee and, more recently, TikTok Shop for market share in Southeast Asia's fast-growing but brutally competitive online retail market. Despite raising and deploying roughly $130M in funding over its operating history, Sendo's market share steadily eroded against better-capitalized regional rivals, and in April 2025 the company exited the general e-commerce marketplace business entirely after its share slipped below 1%.

Rather than closing outright, Sendo attempted a survival pivot into online grocery delivery under the Sendo Farm brand — a narrower, differentiated category less directly contested by Shopee and TikTok Shop's dominant general-merchandise marketplaces. That pivot also failed to gain sustainable traction: on July 9, 2025, Sendo Farm ceased operations as part of a broader restructuring, with the company citing efforts to "optimise resources, improve competitiveness, and streamline" its business portfolio. Customers with unused membership packages and wallet balances were offered refunds as part of the wind-down.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Competed head-on in Vietnam's general e-commerce marketplace category against Shopee (a well-capitalized, dominant regional platform) and TikTok Shop (a fast-growing new entrant backed by ByteDance's massive resources), a market structure that squeezed smaller, less-capitalized players.
- Chose to exit general e-commerce entirely (April 2025) rather than continuing to fight for shrinking share, freeing resources to attempt a narrower pivot into a less-contested category.
- Pivoted specifically into online grocery delivery (Sendo Farm) as a survival strategy, betting that a differentiated, less commoditized category could sustain the business where general marketplace competition had failed.
- Ultimately shut down the pivot as well within roughly three months, suggesting the underlying resource and competitive pressures that forced the original e-commerce exit were not resolved by the narrower repositioning.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sendo's trajectory — burning roughly $130M, losing its core e-commerce business to better-capitalized rivals, then failing at a rapid category pivot within months — illustrates how difficult it is for capital-constrained platforms to survive in markets dominated by deep-pocketed players (Shopee, TikTok Shop) simply by retreating to an adjacent category, if the underlying capital and competitive disadvantages that caused the original failure remain unaddressed. A defensive pivot executed under financial distress, rather than from a position of strength, often fails on a compressed timeline because it inherits the same resource constraints that forced the retreat in the first place.
