## One-liner

Tokyo-based business intelligence SaaS Uzabase paid $86M for US digital media outlet Quartz in 2018, then sold it back to its CEO at a $55M loss two years later, pivoting to double down on its core SPEEDA and NewsPicks products.

## What happened

Uzabase was founded in 2008 by Yusuke Umeda and Nobuyuki Kubota and built two profitable Japanese-market products: SPEEDA (enterprise business intelligence for M&A and strategy teams) and NewsPicks (social economic news curation). Riding post-IPO confidence after listing on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in 2018, Uzabase acquired Quartz from Atlantic Media in July 2018 for $86 million (with earnouts up to $110M), betting it could bring NewsPicks' model to an English-language global audience.

The thesis collapsed quickly. Quartz posted a $18.4M operating loss on $26.4M revenue in 2019. When COVID-19 hit in early 2020 and US advertisers slashed digital spending, Uzabase cut roughly 80 Quartz staff. In November 2020, Uzabase sold Quartz back to its co-founder and CEO Zach Seward in a management buyout. Uzabase booked a $55 million loss on the deal. CEO Umeda personally lent Quartz operating capital to keep it alive while Seward sought new investors. Uzabase redirected capital and management attention to SPEEDA and NewsPicks, both of which continued growing through the same period.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2018 acquisition at premium**: Paid $86M (up to $110M) for a US media brand already losing money, motivated by post-IPO international growth ambition and belief in editorial curation as a shared competency with NewsPicks.
- **2019 — slow restructure**: First full year under Uzabase ownership produced a $18.4M loss; management was slow to cut costs or pivot the ad-dependent revenue model.
- **March 2020 — COVID advertising collapse**: Quartz's US corporate ad base evaporated; Uzabase cut ~80 staff but by then the business was not viable.
- **November 2020 — management buyout**: Uzabase exits at a $55M net loss, effectively gifting operating capital to Seward's team while reclaiming focus on its core B2B SaaS products.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

The Quartz acquisition is a textbook case of a profitable niche B2B company overpaying for a loss-making consumer media brand in a foreign market, assuming product vision can substitute for distribution and local ad-sales infrastructure. Japanese tech companies have repeatedly made expensive US media/content acquisitions that stall on monetisation — the pattern recurs from DeNA's gaming acquisitions to SoftBank's media bets. The lesson: domestic product-market fit in B2B SaaS does not transfer to ad-supported English-language media. SPEEDA and NewsPicks thrived precisely because they sold subscriptions to captive Japanese corporate buyers; Quartz depended on volatile global advertising.
