## One-liner

Vntrip spent three years burning cash competing for Vietnamese leisure travelers against deep-pocketed foreign OTAs, then pivoted entirely to B2B corporate travel management software and reached break-even profitability by April 2022.

## What happened

Vntrip launched as a consumer-facing online travel agency (OTA), competing for Vietnamese hotel and flight bookings against much better-capitalized foreign platforms. Around 2018, after roughly three years of trying to establish a defensible position in that consumer segment, leadership concluded the B2C travel-booking business was structurally unprofitable in Vietnam: as CEO Lê Đắc Lâm put it, growth only continued as long as the company kept "burning money" on promotions, and stopped the moment marketing spend paused, because customers followed whichever platform offered the best deal that week rather than showing brand loyalty.

Rather than keep funding that acquisition treadmill, Vntrip pivoted to building the Vntrip Travel Management System (TMS), a B2B platform letting companies centralize corporate travel booking, approvals, and expense management in one system. The B2B model eliminated the constant paid-acquisition cost of the consumer business and offered materially better margins. By the time of a December 2022 CafeF profile, Vntrip TMS was serving roughly 1,500 corporate clients, including nearly 100 companies from Vietnam's VNR500 (top 500 largest enterprises) list — such as Thế Giới Di Động, Sabeco, FWD, and TH True Milk — managing several hundred billion VND in monthly corporate travel spend, and had reached break-even profitability in April 2022.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Recognizing B2C travel-booking as a subsidy race, not a moat**: leadership's own diagnosis was that customer loyalty in Vietnamese OTA was effectively zero — retention depended entirely on continuous promotional spending, which doesn't compound into a sustainable business.
- **Redirecting the same booking infrastructure at corporate buyers**: rather than building an entirely new product, Vntrip repointed its hotel/flight inventory and booking technology at a B2B buyer (finance/HR/admin teams) who cared about expense control and approval workflows, not discount-chasing.
- **Multi-year runway to break-even**: the pivot decision (around 2018) to profitability (April 2022) took roughly four years — a reminder that B2B pivots from consumer businesses are not instant fixes even when the underlying thesis is sound.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Vntrip is a useful counter-example to the more common "failed OTA" story in Southeast Asia: instead of shutting down after losing the consumer subsidy war, it recognized that its actual durable asset (travel inventory + booking tech) had a second buyer — corporate finance teams — who value predictability and expense control over the lowest possible price. The four-year gap between pivot decision and profitability is also a realistic data point against pivot narratives that imply a fast turnaround; B2B enterprise sales cycles and trust-building with VNR500-tier clients take years, not quarters.
