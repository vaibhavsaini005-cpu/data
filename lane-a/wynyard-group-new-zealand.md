## One-liner

Christchurch-based crime-fighting software maker Wynyard Group, once one of the NZX's most hyped tech listings, collapsed into liquidation in February 2017 after burning through investor cash and losing a key government contract.

## What happened

Wynyard Group listed on the New Zealand stock exchange in 2013 at $1.15 a share, selling intelligence and investigation software to police forces, government agencies, and financial institutions worldwide. The stock became a market darling, peaking at $3.12 in March 2014 on the promise of large international contracts. But the company never turned its revenue growth into profit, and one of its flagship deals — a three-year contract to supply New Zealand's Serious Fraud Office with an investigator product — was terminated early after the SFO lost confidence in the software following significant technical problems. Unable to secure emergency funding from UK lender Skipton Building Society, Wynyard went into voluntary administration in October 2016. By the time creditors voted on February 8, 2017 to put the company into liquidation, its shares — once worth $3.12 — were trading at just 21.5 cents.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Chasing marquee government contracts before the product was ready**: the Serious Fraud Office deal was meant to validate Wynyard's core investigator product internationally, but technical failures caused the SFO to cancel the contract a year early, damaging the company's credibility with other prospective government buyers.
- **Scaling sales and headcount ahead of sustainable revenue**: like many NZX growth-stock darlings of that era, Wynyard expanded its cost base on the strength of its share price and pipeline rather than proven recurring revenue, leaving it exposed when growth slowed.
- **Relying on a single emergency lender for survival**: when Skipton Building Society declined to extend further funding in late 2016, Wynyard had no fallback financing option and went straight into administration within weeks.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Wynyard is one of the clearest cautionary tales in New Zealand's small public-tech-listing history: a company can be a stock-market favorite on narrative and pipeline alone, but a single lost anchor contract combined with no committed lender of last resort can end it within months. It's also a reminder that government and law-enforcement software sales cycles carry outsized reputational risk — losing one flagship public-sector client can poison the well for the rest of the pipeline.
