## One-liner

Bunjang, Korea's largest C2C secondhand marketplace, grew from a roughly $363 million valuation in a November 2021 funding round to an approximately $520 million valuation in a February 2026 sale process, driven largely by cross-border K-pop merchandise and streetwear resale demand from U.S. buyers.

## What happened

Bunjang raised 83 billion won (about $70 million) in November 2021 at a valuation of up to 430 billion won (roughly $363 million), positioning it as one of Korea's top consumer-to-consumer resale platforms alongside categories like fashion, electronics, and collectibles. Rather than stall out as a domestic-only marketplace, Bunjang leaned into cross-border demand — particularly U.S. buyers importing K-pop merchandise and Korean streetwear — with North America eventually accounting for over half of its cross-border commerce (51.7%), well ahead of Europe (19.4%) and the rest of Asia (19.1%).

By February 2026, Bunjang's owner, private equity firm Praxis Capital Partners, launched a sale process valuing the company at around $520 million, with Citi advising and large Korean retailers such as Shinsegae Group reported as potential buyers. That represents roughly a 43% increase in valuation over the 2021 funding round, in a period when many Korean consumer-tech startups from the same 2021 funding vintage wrote down or shut down entirely.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Leaning into cross-border demand instead of staying domestic-only**: turning U.S. K-pop and streetwear fans into a majority (51.7%) of cross-border transaction volume gave Bunjang a growth vector that pure domestic Korean resale competitors didn't have.
- **Surviving the 2022-2023 consumer-tech funding pullback with valuation growth intact**: many Korean startups that raised at 2021-era valuations were later sold or wound down at a discount; Bunjang instead entered its 2026 sale process priced above its last primary round.
- **Attracting strategic retail buyers, not just financial ones**: Shinsegae Group's reported interest signals that a large incumbent retailer sees Bunjang's resale marketplace as a strategic asset worth acquiring outright, not merely a distressed asset to pick up cheaply.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Bunjang is a rare 2021-vintage Korean consumer startup whose valuation held up and grew through the subsequent funding downturn, and the mechanism is specific and replicable: a domestic C2C marketplace found an export demand niche (global K-pop fandom) that a purely local player wouldn't have captured, converting a niche cultural export into majority transaction volume. It's a useful counterpoint to the more common story of 2021-vintage unicorns being sold at steep discounts — here the export angle, not cost-cutting, is what's credited with the valuation growth into 2026.
