## One-liner

Tokyo robotics software company Mujin, co-founded and led by CEO Issei Takino, raised $233 million in a Series D first close led by NTT Group and Qatar Investment Authority, positioning MujinOS as a global standard for no-code factory and warehouse automation ahead of a planned IPO by 2030.

## What happened

Issei Takino co-founded Mujin in July 2011 with Rosen Diankov to build MujinOS, a robotics control platform that lets factories and warehouses deploy automation — robotic mobility, picking, storage and warehouse execution — without custom coding, using real-time digital twins and motion planning instead of manually programmed robot behavior. After 14 years of building the technology and customer base, Mujin announced on December 2, 2025 the first close of a Series D round totaling $233 million: $133 million in equity led by NTT Group and co-led by Qatar Investment Authority, with Mitsubishi HC Capital Realty and Salesforce Ventures also participating, plus $100 million in debt from Japanese financial institutions. A planned second close was set to add Silicon Valley's Pegasus Tech Ventures, Accenture and other investors.

Takino tied the raise directly to a specific strategic goal — establishing MujinOS as a global standard rather than a Japan-only product — and to a specific societal problem: "We will accelerate our global expansion through productization and strategic partnerships, with the aim of addressing the global labor shortage and the societal challenges it creates," adding that Mujin aims to become a company that can "compete and win" on the world stage. Reporting around the raise also noted Takino's plan to pursue a New York IPO by 2030, with the round explicitly framed as fuel for global expansion ahead of that listing.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Spending 14 years building coding-free robotics control software before scaling capital-intensive global expansion**: Takino prioritized deep technical maturity (digital twins, motion planning) over early scale.
- **Raising a blended equity-plus-debt Series D ($133M equity + $100M debt)** rather than equity alone, using Japanese financial institutions for debt financing specifically to fund global growth without excessive dilution.
- **Explicitly targeting a global standard-setting position and a U.S. IPO**, rather than staying a Japan-focused automation vendor — a deliberate scope expansion tied to labor-shortage-driven demand for automation worldwide, not just in Japan.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Mujin shows a long-horizon, technology-first path to a large late-stage round in industrial robotics: rather than raising aggressively early, the company spent over a decade proving MujinOS's no-code automation approach before combining strategic corporate investors (NTT, Qatar Investment Authority) with debt financing to fund global scale. Explicitly linking the raise to a societal driver (labor shortage) and a concrete future milestone (a planned U.S. IPO) gives outside observers a clear, falsifiable marker of what "success" means next for the company, rather than treating the funding round itself as the endpoint.
