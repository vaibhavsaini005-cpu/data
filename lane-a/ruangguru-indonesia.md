## One-liner

Indonesia's largest EdTech unicorn, flush with $150M from General Atlantic, laid off hundreds of employees in late 2022 as pandemic-era growth evaporated and the global EdTech sector contracted.

## What happened

Ruangguru ("Learning Room" in Indonesian) was founded in 2014 by Belva Devara and Iman Usman. It grew to become Indonesia's dominant online learning platform, serving millions of K-12 students with video lessons, live tutoring, and test-prep products. In 2020 the company raised $150 million from General Atlantic, crossing the $1 billion valuation mark — making it one of Indonesia's few EdTech unicorns.

Like most consumer EdTech companies globally, Ruangguru benefited enormously from school closures during COVID-19. When Indonesian schools reopened through 2021-2022, engagement and conversion rates normalised sharply downward. Rising interest rates globally dried up growth-stage capital, and investors began demanding profitability over growth. In November 2022, Ruangguru laid off an estimated 1,000+ employees — a significant portion of its workforce — citing the changed market environment. Founder Belva Devara acknowledged the layoffs publicly.

The company continued operating its core platform after the restructuring, but at substantially reduced scale and headcount compared to its 2021 peak.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **$150M raise at peak COVID enrollment**: Capital raised when engagement was artificially elevated by lockdowns — the unit economics at scale in a post-COVID market looked far weaker.
- **Headcount expansion during boom**: Ruangguru scaled staff rapidly to match pandemic-era demand, leaving a significant cost base when volumes normalised.
- **Public acknowledgment**: Co-founder Belva Devara took to LinkedIn to announce the layoffs, an unusually transparent move for Indonesian startups — helped preserve brand trust but did not change the financial trajectory.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ruangguru's arc mirrors the global EdTech cohort (Byju's, Unacademy, Udemy) that over-capitalised on COVID-driven demand without stress-testing against school reopening. Indonesia's K-12 EdTech market remains real and large, but the pandemic-era growth multiple proved misleading for fundraising purposes. The pattern: raise at COVID multiples → hire aggressively → normalize back to pre-COVID retention curves → restructure.
