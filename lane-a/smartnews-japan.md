## One-liner

Tokyo-based AI news aggregator that raised $230 M at a $2 B unicorn valuation in 2021, then laid off 40% of its US and China staff in January 2023 and retreated from its US expansion after Apple's ATT privacy change gutted its ad-based business model.

## What happened

SmartNews was founded in 2012 in Tokyo and built an AI-powered news curation app that became one of the most-downloaded news apps in Japan. Between 2018 and 2021 the company raised over $400 M in total, including a $230 M Series F in October 2021 that valued it at $2 B. The company made an aggressive push into the US market, hiring engineering, product, and editorial teams across San Francisco and New York. That push ran headlong into two concurrent headwinds: a broad digital advertising contraction in late 2022 and Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) framework, which drastically reduced the targeting signals available to ad-funded apps. On 12 January 2023, SmartNews announced layoffs of approximately 120 employees — about 40% of its US and China headcount — alongside planned reductions in Japan. The company explicitly cited "current economic conditions" and ATT's impact on its advertising revenue model.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **US market expansion bet:** Committed significant headcount and capital to the US market before the ad model was proven durable at scale outside Japan, where SmartNews had deep brand recognition and a loyal user base.
- **ATT exposure:** SmartNews's monetisation depended heavily on behavioural ad targeting. Apple's ATT rollout in 2021 removed the data layer its US ad business relied on, with no quick substitute available.
- **Retreat and refocus:** Rather than attempt a second US pivot, SmartNews shifted strategy toward Japan profitability — a classic "return to core market" move that preserved the company but abandoned the global ambition implicit in its $2 B valuation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SmartNews illustrates the asymmetry of Japan-to-US expansion for consumer media apps: a dominant position in Japan (strong brand, loyal user base, localised content relationships) does not translate automatically to the US, where news app competition, user acquisition costs, and platform policy risk are all materially higher. The ATT example is a broader lesson — single-platform dependency on Apple or Google advertising infrastructure creates systemic risk that any one company's engineering cannot offset.
