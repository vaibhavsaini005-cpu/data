## One-liner

Bangkok's Eko Communications spent a decade consolidating three separate SaaS products into a single parent brand, Amity, before re-betting the whole company on generative AI in 2023.

## What happened

Korawad Chearavanont founded Eko in 2012, while still in high school, as a Slack-style workplace messaging tool aimed at frontline and mobile-first workers in retail and hospitality — a segment underserved by Slack and Microsoft Teams. He dropped out of Columbia in 2015 to run it full-time, and later added a second product, Upstra, an application-development tool. Growth was slow: Eko took eight years to break even, though the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated demand as frontline businesses digitized communication, with Eko sales reportedly surging 50% even as the company cut costs 20%.

On June 30, 2020, Chearavanont acquired ConvoLab, a Thai chatbot provider, and folded all three products — Eko, Upstra, and ConvoLab — under one new parent company, Amity. Then, in early 2023, the company made a second, sharper pivot: rather than continuing as a general-purpose SaaS collaboration vendor, it re-focused primarily on building generative-AI applications (an enterprise chatbot platform, a voicebot platform, and AI features layered onto its legacy Eko collaboration tool) on top of its existing products. Amity Solutions was spun out from Amity Corporation in 2023 to pursue this GenAI strategy ahead of a planned listing on the Thai stock market, and raised a $60M Series C in July 2024 led by Insight Capital to fund it.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Multi-product consolidation (2020)**: Rather than running three separately branded SaaS tools, the founder merged them under one holding company, Amity, simplifying the go-to-market story.
- **GenAI re-bet (early 2023)**: With generative AI reshaping enterprise software, Amity chose to reposition its legacy collaboration and chatbot tools as AI-native products rather than compete as a plain messaging platform.
- **Spin-out for a public listing**: Separating Amity Solutions from the parent corporation in 2023 was explicitly framed as preparation for a Thai stock market IPO, tying the AI pivot to a capital-markets strategy.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Eko/Amity shows a slower, compounding kind of pivot rather than a single dramatic reversal: a decade of incremental product consolidation, followed by a sharp strategic re-focus when a new technology wave (generative AI) arrived. The eight years to breakeven also underscores how long enterprise SaaS in emerging Southeast Asian markets can take to reach sustainability compared to the "hypergrowth" model common in US SaaS narratives.
