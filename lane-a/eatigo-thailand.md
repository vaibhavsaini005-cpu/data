## One-liner

Bangkok-founded Eatigo turned off-peak restaurant discounts into a regional
booking platform, pulling in $10M+ from TripAdvisor after building a
dominant position in Thailand.

## What happened

Founded in Bangkok in 2013 by Judy Tan, Pumin Yuvacharuskul, Michael Cluzel
and Siddhanta Kothari, Eatigo built a reservation app around time-banded
discounts — deeper discounts for booking tables at off-peak hours — to help
restaurants fill seats outside their normal rush. It first won a dominant
share of online restaurant bookings in Thailand, then expanded into Hong
Kong, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines. By July 2018 the
platform had seated over 5 million diners across more than 4,000 partner
venues and been downloaded by over 1.5 million users, and it raised a
Series B extension of $10M+ from TripAdvisor and TheFork's parent, giving
it capital and distribution backing from two of the biggest names in
online travel and dining.

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2013: Launches in Bangkok with an off-peak discount booking model built
  specifically around Thai dining habits.
- 2017: Expands regionally (Malaysia, India) on the strength of its Thailand
  "monopoly" position in restaurant bookings.
- Jul 2018: Raises $10M+ from TripAdvisor/TheFork, validating the model to
  strategic travel and dining incumbents.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Eatigo didn't invent restaurant reservations — it won by localizing the
mechanic (steep off-peak discounts) to Southeast Asian dining behavior
first in a single market, then used that regional beachhead to raise from
global strategics rather than trying to go multi-country from day one.
