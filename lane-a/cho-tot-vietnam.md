## One-liner

Cho Tot, Vietnam's leading online classifieds marketplace, gave up standalone ownership in November 2019 when its parent, Telenor Group's 701Search, merged into Singapore's Carousell — folding Cho Tot into a pan-regional group that later raised $80 million from Naver in 2020 rather than Cho Tot ever raising or exiting independently as a Vietnamese company.

## What happened

Cho Tot (Vietnamese for "Good Market") built up a dominant position in Vietnam's online classifieds category — used goods, real estate, jobs, and vehicles — while owned by Telenor Group's regional classifieds arm, 701Search, which also controlled Mudah in Malaysia and OneKyat in Myanmar. Rather than Cho Tot continuing to grow as an independent Vietnamese company toward its own funding round or exit, Telenor instead merged 701Search into Singapore-based Carousell on November 21, 2019, in a deal that valued the combined Carousell group at more than $850 million and made Telenor its single largest minority shareholder at 32%. Cho Tot's ownership and strategic direction moved from a telecom-backed regional classifieds unit into a broader Southeast Asian consumer marketplace group overnight.

Under Carousell, Cho Tot continued to operate as the group's flagship Vietnam brand rather than as an independent entity. On September 24, 2020, Carousell announced an $80 million investment led by South Korean internet giant Naver, with participation from the Mirae Asset-Naver Asia Growth Fund and NH Investment & Securities, pushing Carousell's valuation above $900 million — money and strategic backing that flowed to the wider group, not to a standalone Cho Tot cap table. Carousell later restructured its Vietnam business further, spinning out a dedicated real estate vertical, Nha Tot, from the general Cho Tot classifieds site.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Telenor choosing consolidation over standalone scale-up**: Rather than backing Cho Tot to raise its own capital and compete as an independent Vietnamese platform, parent Telenor opted to merge its whole 701Search portfolio into Carousell, trading full control of a market-leading Vietnam asset for a large minority stake in a bigger regional group.
- **Cho Tot's brand persisting through ownership change**: Unlike many classifieds consolidations that involve migrating users to a new brand, Cho Tot kept its name and market position in Vietnam even as the corporate structure behind it changed twice (Telenor/701Search → Carousell, then later vertical spin-offs) — showing that brand continuity can be preserved through an M&A pivot when local market recognition is valuable enough.
- **Category vertical spin-off (Nha Tot) after the merger**: Splitting real estate listings into their own dedicated product after joining Carousell reflects a common post-merger pattern — using the resources of a larger parent to invest in deeper category specialization that a smaller standalone Cho Tot likely couldn't have funded alone.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Cho Tot illustrates a different kind of "pivot" than a product pivot: a market-leading local platform pivoting its ownership structure — moving from a single foreign telecom parent into a multi-country consumer marketplace group — to access more capital and cross-market resources than it could raise standalone. It's a reminder that in classifieds and marketplace categories, regional consolidation (Southeast Asia's Carousell rolling up country-specific leaders) is often a more common outcome than either outright failure or an independent IPO, and that a strong local brand can survive multiple ownership changes as long as its market position is defensible.
