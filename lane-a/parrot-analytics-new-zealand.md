## One-liner

Founded by Wared Seger while still at university in Auckland, Parrot Analytics turned a data-science approach to measuring audience "demand" (not just viewership) into the global standard content-valuation tool used by Hollywood studios and broadcasters, including New Zealand's own TVNZ.

## What happened

Parrot Analytics was built to solve a problem traditional TV ratings couldn't: as audiences fragmented across streaming platforms, piracy, social media, and word-of-mouth, conventional viewership panels stopped representing how in-demand a show actually was. The company's data-science team built "Demand Expressions," a methodology aggregating streaming, social, piracy, and search signals into a single comparable demand score for any piece of content, in any market.

Founder Wared Seger raised more than NZ$8 million (roughly US$4.8 million) in seed and pre-Series-A capital from New Zealand, Australian, and US investors to build the product, backed further by government co-investment funding via Creative Collective and support from New Zealand's innovation ecosystem. The company scaled its dataset to track more than 1.5 billion daily "expressions" of demand across 100+ languages and 200+ countries, and built an international R&D presence while landing clients across Hollywood, the UK, Europe, and Brazil. By 2021, Television New Zealand (TVNZ) — the country's own leading broadcaster — signed on as a client for global and local audience demand insights, a notable homecoming validation for a company that started as a university project in Auckland.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Reframing the metric, not just building a better panel**: instead of competing with Nielsen-style viewership panels on their own terms, Parrot Analytics invented a new unit (demand, not just views) that made cross-platform, cross-territory comparison possible for the first time.
- **Raising early and using government co-investment leverage**: the NZ$8M+ raised while the founder was still at university, stretched further by Creative Collective co-investment funding, let the company fund R&D and sales expansion in parallel rather than sequentially.
- **International-first go-to-market from a small home market**: rather than build out New Zealand first, Parrot Analytics pursued Hollywood, UK, and European media clients early, with the TVNZ deal coming years after the company was already globally established.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Parrot Analytics shows a repeatable small-market startup pattern: when the home market is too small to be a primary customer base, invent a category that only makes sense at global scale (cross-territory content demand comparison is meaningless with only NZ data) and sell into the biggest markets first. It also illustrates how government co-investment vehicles (Creative Collective) can meaningfully extend a young company's runway without diluting founder control the way an equivalent VC round might.
