## One-liner

Indonesia's largest online travel platform lost ~90% of its bookings when COVID-19 hit in 2020, cut 28% of its workforce, then pivoted from pure-play OTA to a lifestyle super-app — adding food delivery, insurance, and healthcare — to survive and eventually recover as a diversified consumer platform.

## What happened

Traveloka was founded in 2012 in Jakarta by Ferry Unardi (who dropped out of a Carnegie Mellon PhD programme), Derianto Kusuma, and Albert Zhang. The company pioneered mobile-first flight search in Indonesia at a time when most booking still happened through travel agents or desktop websites. It grew rapidly into Southeast Asia's dominant online travel agency (OTA) and achieved unicorn status in 2016 following a $350 million round that included Expedia Group.

By 2019 Traveloka operated across six SEA markets (Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore, Philippines), offered flights, hotels, rental cars, and experiences, and had accumulated over $1.5 billion in total funding from East Ventures, Sequoia India, JD.com, GIC, and others.

In March 2020, cross-border and domestic travel collapsed across Southeast Asia. Traveloka's booking volumes fell by more than 90%. In May 2020 the company disclosed it was cutting approximately 2,600 roles — 28% of its global headcount. To stabilise revenue, it rapidly built out non-travel verticals: **Traveloka Eats** (food delivery in select markets), **Traveloka Health** (telemedicine), and **Traveloka Protect** (insurance). The strategic intent was explicit: a "lifestyle super-app" that reduced dependence on discretionary travel spend.

In 2021, Traveloka explored a SPAC merger with Bridgetown Holdings (backed by Peter Thiel and Richard Li) to list on a US exchange, but the deal was not completed as SPAC market conditions deteriorated. As travel recovered in 2022–2023 the company's core OTA business rebounded strongly, and by 2023 management reported the company was approaching profitability.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2016**: Unicorn round ($350M, Expedia joining) locked in the pure-play OTA positioning just four years before travel would collapse.
- **May 2020**: Disclosed 28% workforce reduction — unusually transparent for a Southeast Asian private company; helped maintain creditor and investor confidence.
- **2020–2021**: Rapid launch of Health, Eats, and Protect verticals changed the product mix; none became dominant but collectively added a lower-volatility revenue base.
- **2021**: SPAC option not taken; staying private allowed Traveloka to restructure without the quarterly earnings pressure of a US public listing during COVID.
- **2022–2023**: Travel rebound validated the decision to protect the core brand rather than liquidating assets; Eats was quietly wound down as core travel recovered.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Traveloka demonstrates both the concentration risk of single-vertical consumer platforms and the optionality value of a strong mobile brand in an ecosystem where users will follow a trusted app into adjacent services. The COVID pivot was forced, not strategic, but it provided enough revenue diversification to outlast the crisis without a fire-sale exit. The SPAC path — seriously explored, ultimately rejected — is a useful case study in why market-timing a public listing matters as much as valuation at the point of the decision.
