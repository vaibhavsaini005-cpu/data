## One-liner

Indonesian B2B wholesale marketplace Ula raised $141M from Bezos Expeditions, Tencent, and Sequoia then shut down in early 2024 with roughly $50M still unspent — a rare "solvent shutdown" driven by inability to find a sustainable unit-economics model for serving small warungs.

## What happened

Ula was founded in 2020 by Nipun Mehra (ex-Flipkart) and co-founders to digitize FMCG (fast-moving consumer goods) distribution for Indonesia's roughly 3 million small neighborhood stores (warungs). The model: aggregate warung demand, buy wholesale from distributors, and deliver via owned logistics. At peak, the platform served over 100,000 warungs across Java.

The company raised $87M in a Series B in 2021 (led by Tencent, Bezos Expeditions, Prosus, and Sequoia India/Peak XV), reaching a post-money valuation reported at approximately $250M. Total capital raised exceeded $141M.

By late 2022, cracks appeared. Ula laid off ~134 employees (23% of headcount) in December 2022, citing the need to reach profitability. A second round of cuts — ~125 employees, ~30% of staff — followed in May 2023. The company then paused its core inventory-led distribution operations in late 2023, citing inability to achieve sustainable margins while holding physical stock. A pivot to an asset-light cross-border B2B model was explored but abandoned.

In early 2024, Ula formally closed operations and returned capital to investors — offering approximately 30 cents on the dollar (30% of invested capital). The company held approximately $50M in cash at shutdown, making the decision to return funds rather than burn through them an unusual and deliberate choice. Founder Mehra indicated plans to launch a new venture.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Inventory-led model in a fragmented market**: Owning inventory improved delivery reliability but compressed margins and tied up working capital. Pure-software B2B models (connecting warungs to existing distributors) proved more capital-efficient.
- **Two mass layoffs before shutdown**: Both rounds were framed as path-to-profitability moves, but the core economics never resolved.
- **Rational shutdown with cash remaining**: Returning ~30% capital rather than burning to zero preserved founder credibility and investor goodwill — a form of disciplined exit rarely seen in the region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ula's collapse tracks a broader pattern of B2B "next-billion" platforms in Southeast Asia that overestimated how quickly informal retail could be formalized. The warung market's informality (cash-based, trust-based, relationship-driven supply chains) made digital displacement harder than models built on Indian kiranas assumed. The "solvent shutdown" decision is particularly notable: with $50M in the bank, Ula chose to stop rather than pivot repeatedly — a signal that the founders assessed no viable path, even with more time and money.
