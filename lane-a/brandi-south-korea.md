## One-liner

Once ranked among Korea's "big three" fashion-resale platforms and flush with a $51 million round explicitly chasing unicorn status, Brandi's operator Newnex filed for court-supervised rehabilitation in September 2025 after revenue collapsed and the debts from its growth years became unpayable.

## What happened

Brandi was founded in 2014 by Seo Jeong-min, with its shopping app launching in July 2016 as a mobile marketplace connecting Dongdaemun wholesale fashion sellers directly to young female shoppers. It grew alongside rivals Ably and Zigzag into what Korean press dubbed the "big three" of MZ-generation fashion platforms, all built on the same playbook of aggregating small wholesale sellers behind a single app and competing hard on marketing spend to win shopper attention.

Riding investor enthusiasm for vertical-commerce apps, Brandi raised a Series C of roughly $51–55 million in July 2022 from the Korea Development Bank (KDB), LB Investment, Signite Partners, Kolon Investment and Neoplux — a round KED Global reported at the time was explicitly aimed at pushing the company toward unicorn status. That capital-intensive growth model — heavy customer-acquisition spending funded by external capital rather than margin — left Brandi exposed once funding markets tightened in late 2022 and consumer spending on fashion-resale apps cooled. By 2024, per Korean reporting, Newnex's revenue had fallen roughly 66% year-on-year to about 19.5 billion won, and the company's capital base had gone fully negative (complete capital impairment).

On September 16, 2025, Newnex — the operator of both Brandi and sister platform Hyber — filed for court-supervised corporate rehabilitation (Korea's equivalent of Chapter 11 reorganization) with the Seoul Rehabilitation Court. The company said it had already pursued cost cuts, restructuring and efforts to raise fresh investment, but could no longer cover fixed costs against liabilities built up in its earlier growth years, and framed the filing as an attempt to normalize the business under court supervision rather than liquidate it.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Raising a ~$51M Series C explicitly to chase "unicorn status" (July 2022).** Committing to a high-cash-burn growth model right as global and Korean funding markets began tightening locked in a cost structure the company could not later support on falling revenue.
- **Competing head-to-head with Ably and Zigzag in the same category rather than differentiating.** All three "big three" platforms grew on the same wholesale-aggregation, marketing-spend-led model, which meant they faced the same margin squeeze simultaneously — by late 2025, Korean coverage described the entire fashion-platform category as facing a "wave of bankruptcies," not just Brandi.
- **Filing for rehabilitation rather than liquidation (September 2025).** Management explicitly chose court-supervised restructuring, committing to keep post-filing vendor payments current, betting the underlying business is salvageable if the pre-2025 debt load is restructured.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Brandi is a caution about vertical-commerce startups that raise growth capital explicitly to chase a valuation milestone rather than unit economics: the same July 2022 round that signaled market confidence also locked in a cost base that, three years later, actual revenue (down 66% in a single year) couldn't support, ending in complete capital impairment. It's also a reminder that being one of a category's recognized leaders offers no protection when the whole category's underlying model is under pressure — Brandi didn't fail because it lost to Ably or Zigzag, it faced the same funding-driven growth trap they did, at roughly the same time.
