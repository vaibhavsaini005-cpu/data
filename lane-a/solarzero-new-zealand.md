## One-liner

SolarZero, New Zealand's largest residential solar-subscription provider with 40% market share, was put into liquidation without warning by its majority owner BlackRock in November 2024, just two years after a US$250 million buy-in meant to scale it nationally.

## What happened

SolarZero's roots trace back to Thermocell, a solar water-heating company, which evolved into New Zealand's first solar subscription service — letting households get rooftop solar and battery storage for a monthly fee instead of a large upfront purchase. The subscription model, paired with a proprietary smart-battery network the company marketed as Australasia's largest virtual power plant (VPP), let SolarZero grow to roughly 15,000 customers and about 40% of the national residential solar market by mid-2024.

In November 2022, BlackRock Real Assets acquired SolarZero through its BlackRock Climate Infrastructure vehicle, initially paying around $110 million and pledging to invest up to US$1 billion in new solar and battery installations across New Zealand — eventually putting in roughly $250 million combined. Public co-investor New Zealand Green Investment Finance (NZGIF) also had money in the structure. Earlier in 2024, SolarZero raised US$195 million in debt financing using a "split-series" structure that separated customer-subscription cash flows from the battery/VPP business — a sign the company was already leaning hard on debt to fund growth.

On November 26, 2024, BlackRock abruptly placed SolarZero into liquidation, citing "unsustainable operating losses and liquidity constraints." Staff learned of the shutdown with immediate effect — reporting they were not paid holiday pay or notice — and roughly $4-5 million was left owing to staff and contractors. The decision, made just weeks before Christmas with no advance warning to employees or the public co-investor, triggered an Auckland staff protest and public scrutiny of NZGIF's oversight role in the deal.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **BlackRock's 2022 buy-in at ~$110M, scaling to ~$250M total**: turned a subscription-model solar company into a debt-and-infrastructure-financed bet that needed continuous capital to keep installing.
- **The 2024 "split-series" $195M debt raise**: financing growth by further leveraging the business, rather than reaching sustainable unit economics, left the company exposed when losses didn't turn around.
- **BlackRock's sudden, unilateral liquidation decision**: a controlling financial investor pulling the plug without restructuring negotiations or advance notice to co-investors, staff, or the ~15,000 subscribed customers.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SolarZero shows how a capital-intensive subscription hardware business (upfront installation cost, monthly revenue recovery over years) is acutely exposed to its financial backer's patience: market leadership (40% share, 15,000 customers) did not protect it once its majority owner decided the losses were unsustainable. It's also a cautionary case for public-private cleantech financing — NZGIF's involvement as a public co-investor meant the company's collapse became a public accountability question, not just a private-equity write-off.
