## One-liner

LawVu, founded in Christchurch in 2016 by Sam Kidd and Rupert Crosbie, built the first true legal workspace SaaS for in-house legal teams and raised a $12M Series A in 2021 from Bessemer Venture Partners — the prestigious Silicon Valley firm's first-ever investment in New Zealand.

## What happened

Sam Kidd and Rupert Crosbie founded LawVu in 2016 after both had spent years watching in-house legal departments operate from spreadsheets, email threads, and disconnected point solutions — matter tracking in one tool, contract management in another, spend management in a third, with no unified dashboard for the General Counsel. The thesis was straightforward: legal operations was the last major enterprise function to lack a purpose-built system of record.

The founders bootstrapped through early product development in Christchurch, signing their first paying customers across New Zealand before expanding to Australian enterprise clients. The product covered the core workflows of an in-house legal team: matter and task management, contract lifecycle management (drafting, review, e-signature), outside counsel billing and spend management, and reporting dashboards for board-level legal risk visibility.

By 2020, LawVu had grown to serve clients including NZ Rugby, Tower Insurance, and a number of Australian ASX-listed companies. The product-market fit was clearer than most: legal departments that switched from spreadsheets reported significant reduction in administrative overhead and better visibility into legal spend, which in large companies runs into millions annually.

In July 2021, Bessemer Venture Partners led a $12M Series A — marking the renowned Silicon Valley VC's first investment in a New Zealand company. The Bessemer imprimatur carried specific significance: the firm's portfolio includes Shopify, LinkedIn, Twitch, Pinterest, and Intercom, and their legal/compliance sector thesis gave LawVu a direct pathway into North American enterprise sales. The round accelerated hiring and fueled expansion into the US and UK markets, where in-house legal team sizes are substantially larger than Australasian counterparts.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Christchurch base, global product**: Building in Christchurch (New Zealand's second city) rather than Auckland or Sydney kept burn rates low enough to bootstrap to meaningful revenue before raising — arriving at a Series A with real traction rather than a pitch deck.
- **In-house focus, not law firm focus**: Most legal tech before LawVu targeted law firms. Choosing in-house legal as the primary buyer gave access to a buyer with a single procurement decision-maker (the GC) and a clear ROI case tied to headcount reduction and spend visibility — dramatically shorter sales cycles than law firm software.
- **Bessemer alignment (2021)**: Choosing a lead investor with an existing legal sector thesis and North American distribution relationships accelerated the US go-to-market by roughly 12-18 months compared to raising from a generalist regional fund.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

LawVu is a textbook example of the "last major enterprise function without a SaaS system of record" opportunity — a pattern that has repeatedly produced category-defining companies (Workday for HR, Veeva for life sciences compliance, Procore for construction). Identifying that in-house legal was still operating on spreadsheets, then building for that specific buyer's workflow rather than for lawyers in general, is the kind of narrow-but-deep ICP (ideal customer profile) focus that allows a bootstrapped founding team to reach product-market fit before raising. The Bessemer outcome vindicates the New Zealand SaaS model: if the product is genuinely category-defining, geography does not prevent accessing top-tier global capital.
