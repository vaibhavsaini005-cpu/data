## One-liner

BASE Inc rode Japan's pandemic e-commerce boom to a 2019 IPO, then had to force its free shop-builder user base onto paid plans in April 2022 when the boom faded — shrinking its core segment before embedded-fintech diversification pulled the company back to revenue growth by 2025.

## What happened

Yuta Tsuruoka founded BASE in 2012 after his mother, who ran a small shop in rural Japan, mentioned she wanted an online store but didn't know how to build one. The flagship "BASE" shop-creation service launched in November 2012 and grew on a free-to-start, commission-based model, eventually reaching roughly 900,000 registered merchant shops. BASE listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Mothers market on October 25, 2019, raising about $26 million.

The COVID-19 pandemic supercharged demand: a wave of first-time sellers opened BASE shops in 2020–2021 under the free model. As Japan reopened and that tailwind faded, BASE made a structural pivot in April 2022, introducing a mandatory monthly paid plan for shop owners instead of relying purely on free signups plus transaction commissions. The transition hurt in the short run — the core BASE shop-creation segment's sales fell 11.0% year-over-year and its segment loss widened. By February 8, 2023, when BASE gave full-year guidance for the fiscal year ending December 2023, it forecast a net loss of about ¥1.9 billion — worse than the prior year in absolute terms, but better than the roughly ¥2.19 billion loss analysts had modeled, signaling the paid-plan transition was landing better than feared.

Alongside the monetization pivot, BASE leaned into embedded fintech products — PAY.JP (a payment API for developers) and YELL BANK (merchant cash advances against future sales) — to diversify revenue beyond the increasingly commoditized shop-builder product. By the first half of 2025, net sales were up 24.8% year-over-year to ¥9.14 billion, though profit attributable to owners was roughly flat at ¥446 million, reflecting a company still converting post-pivot growth into durable profit.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **April 2022 monetization pivot**: moving free users onto mandatory paid plans traded near-term segment revenue and user churn for healthier long-term unit economics — a bet that showed up first as a metrics decline, not a win.
- **Diversifying into embedded finance**: PAY.JP and YELL BANK reduced dependence on the core shop-builder product, which had become table stakes against Shopify-style competitors in Japan.
- **Accepting a wider loss forecast (Feb 2023) rather than chasing growth-at-all-costs**: management prioritized guiding to a credible, beatable number over projecting a premature turnaround.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

BASE is a textbook case of the post-pandemic freemium reset: platforms that scaled on a pandemic-driven wave of free-tier signups had to retrain their user base to pay once the organic tailwind disappeared, and that retraining shows up in the numbers as contraction before it shows up as sustainable growth. The lesson generalizes to any commission- or freemium-based SaaS business — a monetization pivot forced by a cooling market often looks like decline for several quarters before the healthier unit economics compound into recovery.
