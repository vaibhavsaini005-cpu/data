## One-liner

Bangkok-based agritech startup using satellite imagery, AI crop monitoring, and digital credit scoring to serve unbanked smallholder rice and corn farmers across Thailand and Pakistan, raising $10M+ from global impact investors.

## What happened

Ricult was founded around 2016 in Thailand by a team targeting smallholder farmers — a segment largely excluded from formal credit markets and reliable market price information. The platform bundled three services: satellite-based crop monitoring (assessing yields and field health remotely without farm visits), a digital marketplace connecting farmers directly to buyers, and a credit scoring engine that used agronomic data to underwrite loans for farmers without conventional credit histories. Early backing came from 500 Tuktuks, Wavemaker, Chanwanich Group, dtac Accelerate, and the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation (seed stage). A $1.85M seed round in 2018 validated the social impact thesis. The company raised $2M in 2020 and then $3.5M from Sojitz Corporation (Japan) and elea Foundation (Switzerland) in 2021. Bualuang Ventures (Bangkok Bank) and Krungsri Finnovate joined as strategic investors, with a $3.3M Series A following in 2022. By 2024, total funding exceeded $10M. Ricult expanded into Pakistan, applying the satellite and credit model to South Asian smallholder agriculture.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Satellite + credit bundling**: Integrating remote sensing data with loan origination was the core differentiation — it gave lenders objective risk visibility into fields with no prior credit data history, replacing the subjective farm-visit model.
- **Bank JV participation**: Bualuang Ventures and Krungsri Finnovate investing signalled that Thai commercial banks saw Ricult as distribution infrastructure for agricultural credit rather than a competitor threat.
- **Pakistan expansion**: Replicating the Thai model in Pakistan confirmed that the agritech platform was geography-agnostic at the smallholder crop layer, not just a Thailand-specific tool.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ricult demonstrates how agritech can unlock formal financial services for previously unbanked smallholders by substituting objective satellite and agronomic data for subjective credit assessments. Thailand's context — where >40% of the labour force has historically worked in agriculture — is replicable across other rice-producing economies in Southeast and South Asia. The Gates Foundation's early involvement also illustrates how development-capital can de-risk impact-tech bets at the seed stage in ways that purely commercial VC typically cannot, enabling a startup to reach the scale and track record needed to attract institutional and bank-backed follow-on capital.
