## One-liner

Bangkok-based insurtech Sunday built Thailand's first full-stack, AI-driven non-life insurance platform and rode that model to a $45M Series B, tripling its total funding to $75M within four years of launch.

## What happened

Sunday was co-founded by Cindy Kua in August 2017 as a fully integrated data-and-technology platform for real-time, customized, affordable non-life insurance in Thailand — a market historically dominated by standardized products sold through traditional agent networks. Rather than reselling incumbents' policies, Sunday built its own underwriting stack, using AI for risk assessment, fraud detection, and claims automation, and sold motor, health, travel, and warranty products directly to consumers and corporate clients.

The company's funding trajectory tracked its expansion: a $10M Series A led by Vertex Ventures SEA & India, a $9M pre-Series B in September 2020 led by SCB 10X (with Vertex, Quona Capital, and LINE Ventures also participating), and then a $45M Series B in September 2021 backed by Tencent, SCB 10X, Vertex Growth, Vertex Ventures SEA & India, Quona Capital, Aflac Ventures, and Z Venture Capital — bringing total funding to $75M. Sunday used the round to expand its full-stack insurance and employee-benefits platform into Indonesia alongside Thailand, eventually serving more than 1,500 corporate clients.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building the insurance stack, not just distribution**: Sunday chose to become a full-stack insurer rather than a marketplace/broker layered on top of incumbents, giving it control over pricing, underwriting, and claims — higher execution risk but a stronger long-term moat.
- **AI baked in from day one**: fraud detection and claims automation were built into core operations early rather than bolted on later, letting Sunday compete on speed and accuracy simultaneously — normally framed as a trade-off in insurance.
- **Regional expansion timed to the raise**: the Series B was used to enter Indonesia rather than just deepen the Thai market, diversifying regulatory and demand risk.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sunday is a useful counterpoint to Southeast Asia's insurtech-as-marketplace pattern (most regional players resell incumbent policies with a better UX layer). Owning the underwriting stack is capital- and talent-intensive but converts data advantages directly into pricing and claims-speed advantages that a pure distribution player can't replicate. The steady step-up in round sizes (Series A → pre-B → B) also shows a company de-risking with investors incrementally rather than chasing one large raise — useful for founders in markets where local insurance regulation makes rapid scaling harder to underwrite.
