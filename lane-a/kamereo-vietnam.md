## One-liner

Japanese entrepreneur Taku Tanaka left a Credit Suisse career and a COO role at Vietnam's Pizza 4P's chain to found KAMEREO in 2018, building a B2B platform that lets restaurants and institutional kitchens order fresh produce and ingredients the way consumers order on an app — growing to over 3,000 business customers and raising a $7.8 million Series B from a roster of Japanese financial institutions to push into Hanoi and build cross-country refrigerated logistics.

## What happened

Tanaka's path into Vietnamese foodtech ran through the industry rather than around it: after Credit Suisse, he became chief operating officer at Pizza 4P's, one of Vietnam's best-known pizza chains, which put him inside the operational reality of sourcing fresh ingredients reliably for a multi-location restaurant business. KAMEREO, founded in 2018, productized that pain point: a tech-powered B2B platform connecting restaurants, supermarkets, convenience stores, factories, schools and hospitals directly to fresh-produce suppliers through a website or app, replacing the informal, relationship-based wholesale purchasing that had been the default.

The company's funding trajectory tracked a market description from SMBC Venture Capital's Motohiro Takeuchi, who called Tanaka "one of Vietnam's most promising Japanese entrepreneurs" while highlighting how he led the company through COVID-19 lockdowns rather than pausing through them. A $4.6 million Series A closed in mid-2021, backed by CPF Group (the food distribution arm of Thai conglomerate Charoen Pokphand), Quest Ventures and Genesia Ventures — notably, a round assembled and growing (15% month-over-month) during pandemic mobility restrictions that shut down much of the restaurant sector KAMEREO served, rather than after the disruption passed.

By September 2024 the company had grown to serve over 3,000 business customers inside a Vietnamese food-service market estimated at $100 billion with 5%+ annual growth, and closed a $7.8 million Series B — bringing total funding past $15 million over six years — from Sumitomo Corporation, Inspire, SMBC Venture Capital, Mitsubishi UFJ Capital and Reazon Holdings, alongside individual investors. The round's backers are almost entirely Japanese financial and trading institutions, a direct reflection of Tanaka's own background and networks converting into capital access that a purely local or Western-backed founder might not have had the same access to.

The capital funded genuine physical infrastructure rather than just software: a daily refrigerated transport system connecting northern and southern Vietnam, a December 2024 expansion into Hanoi to complement the original Ho Chi Minh City base (the two cities together representing over 50% of Vietnam's GDP), and planned logistics and sales operations in central Vietnam to connect the two existing hubs into one continuous network. The company also launched a marketplace initiative, partnering with Japanese retailer GYOMU JAPAN to list roughly 450 products from Gyomu Super directly on the KAMEREO platform — extending the business from pure logistics into a broader sourcing marketplace.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Founding from direct operational experience inside the customer's business, not from outside observation.** Running procurement as Pizza 4P's COO gave Tanaka firsthand knowledge of exactly where the existing wholesale supply chain broke down, which is a materially stronger starting position than researching the pain point as an outsider.
- **Raising and growing through COVID-19 rather than retreating.** Closing a Series A and posting 15% monthly growth during the exact period when restaurant demand collapsed suggests the platform's value proposition — reliable, lower-friction sourcing — became more urgent, not less, when supply chains were under stress.
- **Building refrigerated logistics infrastructure in-house rather than outsourcing cold-chain to a third party.** A daily north-south refrigerated transport system is a capital-intensive, operationally complex asset to own, but it is also a durable moat that a pure software marketplace would lack.
- **Sequencing geographic expansion to the two highest-GDP cities first, then bridging them.** Hanoi was added only after Ho Chi Minh City was established, and the central-Vietnam investment is explicitly framed as connecting the two existing hubs — expansion following the logistics network's own logic rather than opportunistic city-by-city growth.
- **Converting founder nationality into an investor network.** The Series B's investor list is dominated by Japanese banks and trading houses — a direct capital-access advantage traceable to Tanaka's own background, deployed deliberately as the company scaled.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

KAMEREO is a strong example of a founder's prior operating role inside the target customer's business functioning as product research no outsider could replicate as cheaply. Tanaka did not have to interview restaurant owners to discover their sourcing frustrations — he had been the executive responsible for solving that exact problem at scale for one of Vietnam's largest restaurant chains. For founders considering a B2B platform play, the KAMEREO path argues for treating a senior operating role inside the target industry as a legitimate and underused form of founder-market fit, worth more than months of customer discovery interviews from the outside.

The second lesson concerns owning infrastructure versus owning only software in a logistics-dependent business. KAMEREO's refrigerated transport network is expensive and operationally demanding to build, but it is also what makes the company more than a listings marketplace — it is the thing a well-funded software competitor could not simply replicate by building a nicer app. In categories where the physical supply chain is the actual bottleneck, the durable advantage tends to sit in the infrastructure layer, not the interface.
