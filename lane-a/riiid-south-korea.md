## One-liner

Riiid, the Seoul-based AI tutoring startup that raised $175 million from SoftBank Vision Fund 2 on the strength of its Santa TOEIC app, pivoted sharply from direct-to-consumer English test prep to a B2B white-label AI education platform after failing to replicate its Korean traction in the US market and facing mounting pressure to demonstrate a path to profitability.

## What happened

Founded in 2014 by CEO Yunju Jang, Riiid built its reputation on Santa — an AI-powered mobile application that used machine learning to personalize preparation for the TOEIC English proficiency test, which is taken by millions of Korean professionals and students annually to demonstrate English competency for employment. The app's approach was genuinely novel for its time: rather than delivering fixed study plans, Santa used an adaptive algorithm to identify each learner's weak points, predict score outcomes, and serve targeted practice problems. By the late 2010s, Santa had become the dominant TOEIC prep platform in Korea and had expanded into Japan, where TOEIC is similarly ubiquitous in corporate hiring.

The company's June 2021 Series D — $175 million from SoftBank Vision Fund 2 — reflected peak edtech enthusiasm during the COVID-19 remote-learning boom and positioned Riiid as a potential global AI education infrastructure play. The pitch extended well beyond TOEIC: the company argued its underlying adaptive learning engine could power any standardized test preparation, with the US market (SAT, LSAT, GMAT) as the obvious next target.

US expansion proved much harder than the Korean and Japanese successes suggested. American test-prep was a saturated market with entrenched incumbents (Kaplan, Princeton Review), low consumer willingness to pay for AI features over familiar brands, and TOEIC's near-total irrelevance to the US domestic market. Santa's brand had no foothold in a country where TOEIC is rarely taken. By 2022-2023, with SoftBank's own portfolio under pressure and a broader edtech downturn following COVID-era demand normalization, Riiid shifted its public positioning to B2B: licensing its adaptive AI engine to educational institutions, corporate training programs, and other edtech companies rather than continuing to build its own consumer brand.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Concentration in TOEIC**: Santa's success was partly a function of TOEIC's structural importance in Korean and Japanese hiring — a captive, motivated user base with a very specific, measurable outcome to optimize for. No equivalent single-exam market existed in the US at the scale required to replicate the model.
- **$175M raise at peak edtech valuations**: The SoftBank round gave Riiid resources to pursue the US push but also anchored the company to a valuation that assumed US-scale revenue, creating pressure to fund a market-entry playbook that didn't fit the underlying product-market dynamics.
- **B2B pivot as capital preservation**: The shift to licensing the AI engine rather than owning the consumer relationship traded long-term margin upside for near-term revenue visibility and dramatically reduced the sales motion cost — a rational response to market conditions but a significant narrowing of the original platform ambition.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Riiid is a clean case study in the limits of geographic product-market fit transfer: an AI product that works because it optimizes a specific, government-standardized, employment-gating credential (TOEIC) cannot simply be redeployed in a market where that credential doesn't exist. The lesson is that "adaptive learning AI" was a technology description, not a market, and the actual market was "Korean/Japanese test anxiety around a specific high-stakes exam." For investors, the Riiid trajectory illustrates how edtech companies that raised at COVID multiples faced a particularly difficult path: reduced to choosing between burning through raised capital on US consumer expansion or pivoting to B2B at much lower multiples — neither of which returned the implied value of a SoftBank Vision Fund 2 check.
