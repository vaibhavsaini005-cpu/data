## One-liner

Thailand's largest crypto exchange became the country's first crypto unicorn when SCBx agreed to buy 51% for $537M — then the deal collapsed three months later after Thai SEC investigations into governance and listing irregularities.

## What happened

Bitkub was founded in 2018 by Jirayut "Top" Sritalomlarp after he returned to Thailand from Silicon Valley. It grew rapidly to become Thailand's dominant crypto exchange, controlling an estimated 70%+ of the local market.

In December 2021, SCBx — the holding company of Siam Commercial Bank, Thailand's third-largest bank — announced it would acquire a 51% stake in Bitkub for 17.85 billion baht (~$537M at then-prevailing rates), placing Bitkub's valuation at approximately $1.05 billion and marking it as Thailand's newest tech unicorn.

Within weeks of the announcement, the Thailand Securities and Exchange Commission (Thai SEC) launched enforcement actions against Bitkub, citing unlicensed securities offerings (specifically the KUB utility token) and compliance failures. The regulatory cloud was enough to make SCBx walk away. In March 2022, SCBx formally terminated the acquisition agreement, citing unresolved regulatory risks, and received a refund of its deposit.

Bitkub remained independent and continued operating. It subsequently settled with the Thai SEC on the KUB token matter and received formal licensing. Jirayut remained CEO. The company did not collapse but lost its unicorn valuation narrative and the strategic backing of Thailand's largest financial institution.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **KUB token launch without full securities clearance**: The utility token was offered before regulatory frameworks in Thailand were fully established — a calculated risk that became the deal-breaker.
- **SCBx's 3-month diligence window**: The Thai SEC actions emerged during diligence, suggesting the compliance gaps were known pre-announcement but not resolved before signing.
- **Post-deal independence**: Rather than seek another strategic acquirer, Jirayut focused on regulatory normalisation and continued operations, preserving Bitkub's market leadership.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Bitkub illustrates the "compliance-as-deal-risk" dynamic common in regulated-sector acquisitions: the target's valuation depends on regulatory clearance that hadn't been secured before the M&A announcement. The deal's collapse also signalled that Thailand's SEC was willing to enforce aggressively even against the country's fintech champions. Thai banks' fintech M&A strategies became more cautious afterward.
