## One-liner

Under CEO Johnny Widodo, Indonesian P2P lender Ringan voluntarily surrendered its lending license to regulator OJK on April 24, 2025 and wound down while still solvent, choosing an orderly exit over burning through its remaining Rp 16.8 billion cash reserve.

## What happened

Ringan launched as an Indonesian peer-to-peer lending app offering unsecured cash loans up to Rp 20 million with 3-12 month terms, built on AI-driven eKYC and automated underwriting powered by OneConnect, the fintech arm of backer Ping An Insurance Group (via Lufax Holdings). Founding CEO Yudhono Rawis, a 16-year veteran of KPMG Indonesia's M&A practice, led the company until departing for Binance in May 2022. Johnny Widodo — previously at OVO, OLX Indonesia, and TaniHub — took over as CEO in March 2023. At its peak Ringan had disbursed Rp 50 billion to 5,000-10,000 borrowers with a 1-million-user waitlist, but that volume was far below the Rp 1 trillion+ disbursed by scaled competitors, while the company carried a Tier-1 cost base of roughly 100 employees in a premium Jakarta HQ. As sector-wide loan defaults rose (90-day past-due rates climbing toward 4.33%) and Indonesia's OJK tightened minimum capital requirements to Rp 12.5 billion by July 2025, management and shareholders concluded in early 2025 that continued operations would produce only sustained, unrecoverable losses. The company requested OJK revoke its license, which OJK did on April 24, 2025 (announced publicly May 6); shareholders approved formal dissolution on July 24, 2025, and the liquidation team published its interim balance sheet on October 30, 2025, showing Rp 16.8 billion in cash against just Rp 1.0 billion in liabilities and zero outstanding obligations to lenders.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **The corporate parent's decision to exit while still solvent** rather than deploy remaining capital chasing scale against much larger, better-capitalized P2P lending competitors.
- **Requesting voluntary license revocation from OJK** rather than waiting for regulatory action or running the license down to expiry, giving the company control over the wind-down timeline.
- **Prioritizing a clean, fully-repaid exit to lenders** (zero outstanding obligations at liquidation) over an attempted last-ditch pivot or fire-sale of the loan book.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ringan is an unusually clean counterexample to the typical startup-failure story: a company with cash still in the bank and no obligations owed to its lenders chose to shut down anyway, because its underlying unit economics (a premium cost base supporting a fraction of scaled competitors' loan volume) made the business structurally unviable regardless of remaining runway. It's a reminder that "still solvent" and "worth continuing" are different questions — and that corporate-venture-backed startups (here, backed by Ping An/Lufax) can have a cleaner, faster path to an orderly wind-down than founder-led startups without a well-capitalized parent willing to accept the loss.
