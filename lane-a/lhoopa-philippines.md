## One-liner

Founded in 2018 as a real estate brokerage, Manila-based proptech Lhoopa closed a $80M equity-and-debt round in mid-2024 — one of Southeast Asia's largest raises that year — to scale affordable-housing construction across the Philippines.

## What happened

Lhoopa started in 2018 as a real estate brokerage before rebuilding itself as a data- and AI-driven proptech platform: it identifies underserved neighborhoods with development potential, then coordinates a network of roughly 100 local contractors and more than 4,000 realtors to build and sell affordable homes at scale, aiming to close the Philippines' large affordable-housing backlog.

In May 2024, the company closed a $12.5M tranche of its Series B from Wavemaker Partners, Patamar Capital, and NataRock Partners. Two months later, in July 2024, Lhoopa announced it had expanded that round into an $80M package — $20M in equity co-led by the World Bank's International Finance Corporation (IFC) and Wavemaker Partners (with Pavilion Capital, 10X Group, Concentric Equity Partners, UAE-based Mirath Investments, Black Kite Capital, Patamar Capital, and Tekton Ventures also participating), plus $60M in debt from the Asian Development Bank, the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation, and fintech lender Lendable.

By the time of the July announcement, Lhoopa had sold more than 2,500 homes across 58 Philippine cities and grown its team from 30 to 95 employees, with a stated three-year goal of delivering 15,000+ additional homes. Founder Marc-Olivier Caillot and President/Co-Founder Sabrina Tan positioned the raise as fuel for that expansion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Brokerage-to-platform pivot (2018 onward)**: Rather than staying a pure real estate broker, Lhoopa rebuilt around a tech-and-data layer to identify viable land and coordinate a fragmented network of small local contractors — turning a manual, low-margin business into a scalable development pipeline.
- **Blending equity and development-finance debt**: Structuring $60M of the $80M round as debt from institutions like IFC, ADB, and DFC — rather than chasing an all-equity mega-round — let Lhoopa access capital sized for real-asset construction without excessive dilution, a financing pattern common to infrastructure-adjacent proptech but rare among typical VC-backed startups.
- **Sequencing the raise in two public steps**: Announcing the $12.5M Series B tranche in May 2024, then the expanded $80M package in July, gave Lhoopa two rounds of market validation and press momentum rather than one single announcement.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lhoopa demonstrates a financing pattern under-used by most startups tackling physical, capital-intensive problems (housing, in this case): pairing a modest venture-equity check with a much larger layer of development-finance debt from institutions like IFC and ADB, whose mandates are explicitly aligned with the startup's social outcome (affordable housing). For founders in infrastructure, housing, or hard-asset sectors in emerging markets, this is a more repeatable path to scale than trying to raise the entire capital stack from venture investors alone — the debt providers are underwriting the real asset, not just the team.
