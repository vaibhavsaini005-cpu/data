## One-liner
First Circle built a short-term lending platform for Philippine SMEs shut out of bank credit, and grew it from a $2.5M seed into a $26M Series B in three years.

## What happened
First Circle was founded by Irish duo Patrick Lynch (CEO, previously at CompareAsia Group) and Tony Ennis (CTO, previously at WebSummit) to address a specific gap: the Philippines has no formal, widely-used SME credit-scoring system, so small businesses — which make up 99.6% of Philippine businesses, employ 65% of the workforce, and generate 35% of GDP — routinely couldn't get working-capital loans from traditional banks. First Circle built short-term, transaction-based lending aimed at that underserved segment. After an initial $2.5 million raise, the company announced a $26 million Series B on October 2, 2018, led by Venturra Capital with participation from Insignia Ventures Partners, Silverhorn Investment Advisors, and Tryb Group, earmarked for regional expansion beyond the Philippines.

## Key decisions / turning points
- Targeting SME working-capital lending specifically, rather than consumer credit, in a market with no reliable credit bureau infrastructure to underwrite against.
- Building underwriting fast enough to approve loans in hours rather than the weeks typical of Philippine bank lending, which became the product's core differentiator.
- Raising a $26M Series B in 2018 explicitly to fund regional expansion beyond a single-country base, rather than staying Philippines-only.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)
In markets that lack formal credit infrastructure, the winning fintech wedge is often not a flashier product but faster, more usable underwriting built around alternative signals — First Circle's edge was speed and access, not novelty, which is a repeatable playbook for SME lending in other underbanked Southeast Asian markets.
