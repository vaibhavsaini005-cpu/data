## One-liner

Wellington-based challenger electricity retailer that gave NZ consumers live wholesale spot prices — a decade of growth ended when fuel major Ampol sold the brand to incumbent Meridian Energy for NZ$70M, discontinuing it entirely and leaving 100 staff redundant.

## What happened

Richard Wills and Steve O'Connor founded Flick Electric in 2014 with a structurally simple but commercially risky idea: pass the real-time wholesale spot price of electricity directly to consumers, cutting out the margin the "gentailers" (generator-retailers) built into fixed tariffs. Customers accepted price volatility in exchange for transparency and lower average bills.

The model won fans early. Flick grew to ~41,000 customers and was named Consumer NZ Energy Retailer of the Year. In August 2018, NZ fuel retailer Z Energy acquired a majority stake, backing Flick to scale and challenge the incumbent duopoly.

The vulnerability always lurked in the wholesale market. When spot prices surged — notably during the 2021 dry-year hydro shortage — customers on spot-pass-through plans faced bill shock. Flick stopped accepting new customers in 2021, citing unaffordable wholesale conditions. Z Energy itself was acquired by Australian fuel company Ampol, which subsequently decided to exit NZ retail electricity entirely. In May 2025, Ampol sold Flick's customer book, hedge positions, and brand to Meridian Energy — one of the very gentailers Flick was founded to disrupt — for NZ$70 million. Meridian absorbed the ~41,000 connections (18% market share boost) and discontinued the Flick brand. All ~100 Flick staff were made redundant.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Spot-price model (2014):** radical consumer transparency, but left both company and customers exposed to wholesale volatility — a structural bet that held in normal years and broke in dry ones.
- **Z Energy acquisition (2018):** provided capital and distribution but embedded Flick inside a fuel company with no long-term electricity generation assets — misaligned with the model's hedging needs.
- **Pause on new customers (2021):** correct risk management in the short term, but signalled the model's limits and froze growth at a critical juncture.
- **Ampol exit → Meridian sale (2025):** the challenger's customer base was ultimately most valuable to the incumbent as a bolt-on, not as proof of a new retail paradigm.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Flick is a clean case of a disruptive retail model that works at normal market conditions but lacks the generation hedging assets to absorb tail-risk events. Pure pass-through price models are structurally dependent on stable wholesale inputs; without vertical integration or deep hedging, one bad season can destroy customer trust and growth momentum. The irony — sold to Meridian, the gentailer archetype it was designed to replace — underscores that challengers in capital-intensive regulated markets often create value for incumbents rather than for themselves.
