## One-liner

Once-aspiring unicorn Mesh Korea, operator of two-wheeler delivery platform Vroong, collapsed into court receivership after a CEO integrity scandal and cash burn, then was absorbed by beverage maker hy (formerly Korea Yakult) at a fraction of its former value.

## What happened

Mesh Korea built Vroong into one of South Korea's largest two-wheeled last-mile delivery networks, and was targeting unicorn status (over 1 trillion won valuation) as of January 2022. Two things broke that trajectory. First, founder-CEO Yoo Jung-beom was publicly exposed for fabricating his academic and career background, an integrity scandal that KED Global's reporting flagged as part of a broader pattern of Korean startup-CEO ethics failures shaking investor confidence. Second, the company had been aggressively expanding into money-losing verticals — early-morning delivery and grocery shipping — that it could not sustain as fundraising dried up.

By October 2022 Mesh Korea had let go of roughly 100 employees and shut down those loss-making units. Unable to raise a rescue round, the company filed for court receivership in late November 2022. In February 2023, hy (the company formerly known as Korea Yakult) stepped in with an 80 billion won paid-in capital increase via third-party share allocation, taking a 66.7% controlling stake as part of its strategy to become a general distribution company using Vroong's delivery infrastructure. The deal valued Mesh Korea at roughly a tenth of the up-to-800-billion-won valuation it had carried at its peak.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Overexpansion into thin-margin verticals**: early-morning and grocery delivery burned cash faster than the core two-wheeler logistics business could support.
- **Founder credibility collapse**: the CEO's fabricated credentials became public at the worst possible moment — mid-fundraise, in a tightening 2022 venture market — and accelerated investor withdrawal.
- **Distress sale over shutdown**: rather than liquidate, Mesh Korea's court receivership process led to a strategic buyer (hy) acquiring control, preserving the Vroong brand and operations under new ownership.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Mesh Korea shows how a single credibility shock can compound an already-fragile cash position — the CEO scandal didn't cause the cash crunch, but it foreclosed the rescue financing that might have bought time to fix it. It's also a case study in strategic acquirers using court receivership as an entry point: hy didn't buy a healthy company, it bought infrastructure (Vroong's rider network and delivery tech) at a distressed price to accelerate its own pivot into distribution. For founders, it's a reminder that personal-background misrepresentation is treated by the market as a solvency-relevant risk, not just a PR problem.
