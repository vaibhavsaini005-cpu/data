## One-liner

Four Auckland University friends built a simple PDF-annotation tool called Notable, rebranded it as Kami once it found traction in classrooms, then rode a 500%-month-on-month COVID growth spike into an 11-million-user global edtech platform.

## What happened

Hengjie Wang, Jordan Thoms, Alliv Samson, and Bob met at Auckland University and, starting in 2012, built what launched as "Notable Inc." — a tool for annotating and marking up PDFs. In 2013 the company took its first outside investment and initially tried selling the product to New Zealand universities; that market grew too slowly, so the founders pivoted after about a year to target American K-12 and higher-ed institutions moving coursework onto Chromebooks, tweaking the product around real-time document annotation for classrooms rather than general-purpose PDF markup. Google Workspace integration followed in 2014, deepening the classroom fit.

By 2016 the product had reached 1 million users and the company rebranded from Notable to Kami, alongside a first-time appearance at the ISTE education-technology conference in Philadelphia and a Google Classroom integration — the rebrand marked the shift from "PDF annotation tool" to "digital classroom platform" as the company's actual identity. Growth was steady but unspectacular until COVID-19 hit: North American lockdowns in early 2020 drove 500% month-on-month user growth as roughly four million new users signed up in weeks, pushing Kami's global user base past 11 million by mid-2020. The company responded by offering free subscriptions to every school worldwide, including a push to reach all 2,500 schools in New Zealand (860+ were already using it), while staying headquartered in New Zealand with about 31 staff at the time and plans to hire 20 more locally. Kami later took investment from Boston Ventures Investment Partners (2024) and now operates with a US commercial base while its co-founders continue to lead the company from New Zealand.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pivoting from NZ universities to US K-12/higher-ed (circa 2013)**: The founders abandoned a slow-growing domestic university market for a much larger, faster-moving US school market willing to adopt Chromebook-native tools.
- **Rebranding from Notable to Kami at 1 million users (2016)**: Rather than staying "a PDF tool that schools happened to use," the company renamed itself to match what it had actually become — a classroom collaboration platform — right as it hit ISTE and shipped Google Classroom integration.
- **Turning a pandemic shock into a permanent user base**: Offering free access broadly during COVID lockdowns converted a temporary crisis spike into lasting adoption, rather than letting usage collapse back down once the acute emergency passed.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kami's arc is a clean example of product-market pivot preceding, not following, a growth rebrand: the founders didn't rebrand to chase a new market, they rebranded once the market they'd already pivoted into (US classrooms) had organically redefined what the product was for. It also shows a small New Zealand-based team riding a global demand shock (COVID remote learning) without relocating headquarters, using free-access distribution during the crisis window to lock in scale that persisted well after lockdowns ended.
