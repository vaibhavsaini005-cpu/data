## One-liner

Rabbit LINE Pay, Thailand's e-wallet joint venture between Thai transit-card operator Rabbit and Japan's LINE, was bought out of its original ownership by LINE MAN Wongnai and LINE Thailand in September 2023 and rebranded simply as "LINE Pay" the following month.

## What happened

Rabbit LINE Pay launched as a joint venture combining Rabbit's BTS SkyTrain-linked stored-value card business with LINE's messaging-app payment rails, letting users top up, transfer money, and pay for transit, dining, and online shopping from inside the LINE app. In August–September 2023, LINE MAN Wongnai — the Thai food-delivery and marketplace "unicorn" — together with LINE Company (Thailand) acquired Rabbit LINE Pay's shares from its previous owners, RabbitPay System Company Limited and Advanced mPAY Company Limited, with LINE MAN Wongnai taking the majority stake; deal terms were not disclosed. LINE MAN Wongnai's CEO, Yod Chinsupakul, framed the move as bringing payments infrastructure directly into its own ecosystem of food delivery, LINE SHOPPING, and its merchant network rather than depending on an externally owned wallet. On October 18, 2023, the company completed a rebrand from "Rabbit LINE Pay" to "LINE Pay (Thailand) Company Limited," with LMWN's own announcement stressing that user-facing services — top-ups, transfers, payments — continued without interruption through the ownership and name change.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Original joint-venture structure split ownership between a transit-card operator and a messaging platform**: this gave Rabbit LINE Pay early distribution through both BTS ridership and LINE's massive Thai user base, but also meant neither parent could fully integrate it into a single commerce ecosystem.
- **LINE MAN Wongnai buying out the payments layer it depended on**: rather than continuing to rely on a jointly-owned, arms-length wallet provider, Thailand's food-delivery leader chose to internalize payments infrastructure directly, a classic vertical-integration move once a platform's transaction volume is large enough to justify owning the rails.
- **Rebranding immediately after acquisition, not years later**: dropping "Rabbit" from the name within about six weeks of the ownership change signaled a clean break from the legacy joint-venture identity while explicitly reassuring users that the underlying service was uninterrupted.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Rabbit LINE Pay's ownership change illustrates how fintech infrastructure built as a neutral joint venture can eventually get absorbed by the commerce platform that generates most of its transaction volume — a pivot driven by strategic control rather than financial distress. For other markets, it's a reminder that a payments product's "success" metric isn't just user adoption; it's also whether that adoption ends up captured by a single downstream platform that decides owning the rails outright beats sharing them.
