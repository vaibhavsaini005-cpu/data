## One-liner

Philippine climate-tech company Lumio Solar, founded by CEO Rey Sunglao, raised a $900,000 pre-seed round led by 100x100 to scale plug-and-play solar appliances as an alternative to rooftop solar installations for Filipino households and small businesses.

## What happened

Rey Sunglao founded Lumio Solar around a specific product bet: rather than competing in the capital-intensive, installation-heavy rooftop solar market, the company builds plug-and-play solar appliances — solar-powered fans, lights, freezers and portable power stations — designed as lower-cost, no-installation alternatives that bring solar power directly into households and small businesses. Sunglao framed this as a deliberate redefinition of where solar technology belongs: "The future of solar isn't just panels on rooftops. It also belongs in household and commercial products to bring reliable, affordable energy to power everyday life around the world."

On September 1, 2026, Lumio Solar announced a $900,000 pre-seed round led by 100x100, earmarked specifically for building out distribution networks, regional hub operations, and after-sales service infrastructure across Central Luzon and Metro Manila, with a stated plan to expand next into provincial and archipelagic markets across the wider Philippines — a geography where centralized grid power and traditional rooftop installation both face structural difficulty.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building appliance-form-factor solar products instead of installed rooftop systems**: Sunglao targeted the installation and upfront-cost barriers that limit rooftop solar adoption among lower-income and small-business customers specifically.
- **Sequencing geographic expansion from Central Luzon and Metro Manila into archipelagic provinces**: prioritized building distribution and after-sales infrastructure in accessible, denser markets before tackling the logistical complexity of the Philippines' island geography.
- **Raising a small, targeted pre-seed round earmarked for distribution and service infrastructure specifically**, rather than product R&D — signaling the core product was already built and the immediate constraint was go-to-market reach.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lumio Solar's approach shows a specific way to de-risk solar adoption in geographically fragmented, lower-income markets: move the unit of sale from an installed system (with financing, permitting and installation friction) to a plug-and-play appliance a household or small business can buy and use immediately. For an archipelagic country like the Philippines, where grid reliability and rooftop installation logistics vary sharply by region, sequencing expansion by distribution-network maturity rather than by population size alone is a distribution-first strategy uncommon among solar hardware startups, which more often lead with product capability claims.
