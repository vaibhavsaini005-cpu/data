## One-liner

Tridge, South Korea's first agricultural product and food trading unicorn backed by SoftBank Group and once valued at 3.6 trillion won ($2.6B), reported impaired capital at the end of 2023 after three straight years of losses and pivoted its business purpose toward data services while cutting jobs.

## What happened

Tridge built an agricultural product and food trading platform, becoming South Korea's first agtech unicorn with SoftBank Group as a strategic investor and a peak valuation of 3.6 trillion won. Despite this milestone status, the company logged 115.5 billion won in cumulative operating losses over three consecutive years, and by the end of 2023 reported impaired capital — a balance sheet state where total capital falls below the par value of issued stock, a serious distress signal in Korean corporate finance.

The company's difficulty raising fresh funding in South Korea's broadly contracted 2024 startup environment (68 VC-backed startups closed in the first half of 2024 alone, up from 54 the year before) compounded the pressure: existing investor DS Asset Management, which had put 50 billion won into Tridge in 2022, marked its stake down to nil. In response, Tridge slashed jobs and formally changed its business purpose from agricultural trading toward data services, pivoting its core model rather than continuing to absorb losses on the original trading platform.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Scaled to unicorn valuation and status as South Korea's first agtech unicorn based on trading-platform growth metrics, without the underlying business achieving operating profitability across multiple years.
- Reported impaired capital rather than continuing to obscure the balance sheet distress, forcing a visible reckoning with investors and the market rather than a quiet wind-down of losses.
- Formally pivoted its registered business purpose to data services — leveraging the transaction and market data it had accumulated as an agricultural trading platform — rather than attempting to fix the original trading business's unit economics directly.
- Cut jobs alongside the pivot, right-sizing the organization to match a leaner, data-focused business model rather than the broader trading-platform operation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tridge's distress-driven pivot from agricultural trading to data services illustrates a common late-stage recovery pattern: a company that accumulated significant proprietary data through its original (unprofitable) core business can sometimes monetize that data asset directly, once continuing the original operating model becomes financially unsustainable. The impaired-capital disclosure and investor markdown to nil (DS Asset Management) also show how sharply South Korea's tightened 2023-2024 funding environment forced previously unicorn-status companies into visible financial distress rather than allowing them to quietly extend runway through additional private rounds.
