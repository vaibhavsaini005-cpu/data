## One-liner

Carousell grew from a mobile-first secondhand marketplace founded by three NUS students in 2012 to Singapore's first consumer-internet unicorn in September 2021, reaching a $1.1 billion valuation on the back of a post-pandemic recommerce boom across Southeast Asia and Hong Kong.

## What happened

Quek Siu Rui, Lucas Ngoo, and Marcus Tan founded Carousell in 2012 while students at the National University of Singapore, launching it as a simple iOS app for selling secondhand goods using only smartphone photos. The mobile-first, photo-tap-list experience was deliberately frictionless compared to incumbent classifieds sites (like Gumtree, Mudah.my, and OLX). Y Combinator admitted the team in 2013 and the app grew virally across Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaysia, and the Philippines.

Rather than trying to build a transactional platform immediately, Carousell spent years on trust and community — verifying listings, introducing buyer-seller chat, and adding optional escrow — before layering in advertising revenue from professional sellers. The company expanded to Malaysia, Indonesia, the Philippines, Taiwan, and Hong Kong through a mix of organic growth and two acquisitions: Malaysia's iCar Asia and Hong Kong's OLX businesses absorbed as part of an OLX Group partnership.

The inflection came during COVID-19 lockdowns (2020–2021), when secondhand buying and selling surged globally as consumers decluttered, sought bargains, and embraced sustainability. Carousell's gross merchandise value and user engagement jumped across all markets. In September 2021, South Korean PE firm STIC Investments led a $100 million funding round that pushed Carousell's valuation to $1.1 billion, making it Singapore's newest tech unicorn.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Mobile-first simplicity**: At launch in 2012, most classifieds sites were desktop-native and required lengthy form-based listings. Carousell's three-step phone-photo flow reduced listing time to under 30 seconds and captured a mobile-first generation.
- **YC network and early validation**: YC's 2013 cohort gave the team credibility with Southeast Asian investors who were otherwise cautious about consumer internet at the time.
- **Recommerce positioning**: As sustainability became mainstream in SEA, Carousell leaned into the "reduce, reuse, resell" narrative rather than purely "cheap deals," attracting a higher-NPS user cohort.
- **OLX partnership**: Instead of competing head-to-head in markets like Indonesia and the Philippines where OLX had existing traction, Carousell absorbed OLX's operations under a regional deal, acquiring market share without a costly land-grab.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Carousell demonstrates that a classifieds/marketplace company can win in emerging markets not by out-spending incumbents but by re-imagining the UX for mobile-first users. Its decade-long path from YC to unicorn, without any single blowout funding round, is also notable in a SEA ecosystem dominated by SoftBank-backed hypergrowth companies. The recommerce sector Carousell pioneered is now one of the fastest-growing globally — a market Carousell helped create rather than just entered.
