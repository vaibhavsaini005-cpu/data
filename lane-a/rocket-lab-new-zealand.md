## One-liner

Auckland-founded Rocket Lab went from a garage rocketry project to a Nasdaq-listed company generating $600M in annual revenue and the third most frequent orbital launch provider on Earth.

## What happened

Peter Beck founded Rocket Lab in June 2006 in Auckland, New Zealand, with the goal of building small, low-cost orbital rockets to serve the smallsat launch market that larger providers ignored. The company spent over a decade developing its Electron rocket and its own battery-powered Rutherford engine, additively manufactured to cut cost and lead time, before achieving its first orbital launch in 2018 from a purpose-built private launch site on New Zealand's Māhia Peninsula.

Rocket Lab confirmed in March 2021 that it would go public via a SPAC merger with Vector Acquisition Corporation, and the deal closed on August 25, 2021, valuing the combined company at roughly $4.1 billion and listing it on the Nasdaq under ticker RKLB, with existing Rocket Lab shareholders retaining about 82% of the equity. The listing made instant millionaires of roughly 150 staff who held equity.

Since listing, Rocket Lab expanded well beyond its original small-launch niche into spacecraft components, satellite manufacturing, and its own medium-lift Neutron rocket under development — while continuing regular Electron launches. By 2025 the company was generating around $600 million in annual revenue and ranked as the third most frequent orbital launch operator globally, trailing only SpaceX and the Chinese government's launch program.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Founding a private orbital launch site**: Building Launch Complex 1 on Māhia Peninsula rather than relying on booked slots at government ranges gave Rocket Lab launch-cadence control that most small launch competitors lacked.
- **Vertical integration into engines and materials**: In-house development of the Rutherford engine and heavy use of 3D-printed components let the company cut per-unit cost far below traditional aerospace manufacturing.
- **SPAC listing timed to raise growth capital**: Going public via SPAC merger in 2021, at the height of space-sector investor enthusiasm, funded expansion into satellite manufacturing and the larger Neutron program without waiting for a traditional IPO cycle.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Rocket Lab is one of the clearest non-US, non-India success stories in hard-tech/deep-tech venture building: a country with no prior orbital launch industry produced a global top-three launch provider by owning the full stack — engine, rocket, launch site, and eventually spacecraft components — rather than being a component supplier to incumbents. It also shows a SPAC listing can be used deliberately as a growth-capital vehicle for capital-intensive hardware businesses, not just as an exit ramp, when the underlying operating business (regular, reliable launches) was already proven before the public listing.
