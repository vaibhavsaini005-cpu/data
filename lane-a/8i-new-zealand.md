## One-liner

8i, a New Zealand-founded volumetric-capture startup that raised $27M to build consumer celebrity holograms and AR apps, laid off half its staff in late 2017 and re-emerged under a new CEO as a B2B volumetric video technology licensor.

## What happened

8i was founded in Wellington, New Zealand in May 2014 by Linc Gasking, Eugene d'Eon, Sebastian Marino, and Joshua Feast to build software that captures, compresses, and recreates 3D "holograms" of real people, expanding to Los Angeles as it grew. The company raised a $27 million round led by Time Warner in early 2017 to push a consumer-facing vision: celebrity holograms and an AR app called Holo that let users drop volumetric figures into their own videos.

On December 15, 2017, 8i announced it would lay off roughly half of its 70-person staff, citing a need to "align teams and resources with important core technology initiatives" and acknowledging the company was "challenging assumptions about where the AR/VR market is headed" — an implicit admission that the consumer AR/hologram bet wasn't converting into a sustainable business. Hayes Mackaman took over as CEO in October 2018 and relaunched the company in 2019, moving away from consumer apps toward licensing 8i's core volumetric-video capture technology directly to enterprises and telecoms. By November 2020, that repositioning was concrete: 8i announced a partnership with camera maker IO Industries aimed at advancing volumetric video capture for enterprise and broadcast customers, alongside disclosed deals with SoftBank, LGU+, China Telecom, and others.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Consumer holograms as the original wedge**: 8i's early identity and funding pitch centered on flashy consumer use cases — celebrity holograms and an AR selfie-style app — rather than the enterprise capture technology underneath it.
- **December 2017 layoffs as the forcing function**: cutting half the team was the visible signal that the consumer strategy wasn't working, well before any public repositioning was announced.
- **Leadership change preceding the pivot**: the actual strategic shift toward enterprise/B2B volumetric video licensing followed a new CEO (Hayes Mackaman, October 2018), not a change made by the founding team under the original consumer thesis.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

8i's path — flashy consumer application, funding round, layoffs, new CEO, quiet pivot to selling the underlying technology as B2B infrastructure — is a common recovery pattern for deep-tech startups that raise on a consumer story their core IP doesn't actually need. The volumetric-capture technology survived and found buyers; the celebrity-hologram business it was originally wrapped in did not.
