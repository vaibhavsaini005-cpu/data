## One-liner

Auckland chatbot maker Ambit AI, co-founded by CEO Tim Warren, stopped trading in May 2024 after ChatGPT's release let customers assume they could get equivalent AI for free, and was formally placed in liquidation in June 2026 after defaulting on a $467,429 Callaghan Innovation government loan.

## What happened

Tim Warren co-founded Ambit AI in Auckland in 2017 with Josh Comrie and Gareth Cronin to build customer service chatbots for enterprise clients, including a chatbot named "Scout" built for Southern Cross Travel Insurance. The company grew to an annual revenue run rate of $2.5 million by 2022. Warren told NZ Herald's Tech Insider that OpenAI's ChatGPT release in November 2022 "froze" the market for Ambit's product: enterprise customers increasingly believed "they could get equivalent products for free or cheaper," undermining the commercial case for Ambit's purpose-built, paid chatbot platform. Ambit stopped trading in May 2024.

The company had taken a pandemic-era loan of $467,429 from Crown innovation agency Callaghan Innovation, and after stopping trading it defaulted on that loan. Liquidators Adele Hicks and Stephanie Jeffreys of Grant Thornton were appointed, with the liquidation formally commencing in June 2026; their initial report is dated June 12, 2026. Two days before that report, on June 10, 2026, the company changed its name from Ambit AI to Bambi Tech.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building a vertical, paid chatbot product for enterprise customers**: a bet that specialized, purpose-built conversational AI would command a premium over generic tools — a bet that held until general-purpose LLMs became publicly available.
- **Continuing to operate on the existing product/pricing model after ChatGPT's November 2022 release** rather than immediately repositioning: Warren's own account attributes the commercial freeze directly to that specific market event, not a gradual decline.
- **Taking on a Callaghan Innovation government loan during the pandemic era**, which became a fixed liability that outlasted the revenue the company needed to service it once demand froze.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ambit AI is a direct case study in platform-shift risk for vertical AI startups: a profitable, purpose-built product (reaching $2.5M ARR) can lose its commercial rationale almost overnight when a general-purpose competitor (ChatGPT) resets customer expectations about what "AI features" should cost, even if the incumbent's product is technically still differentiated. It also illustrates a second-order failure mode — a government innovation loan taken during good years becomes a forcing function for formal liquidation once revenue can no longer service it, turning a quiet wind-down into a public default.
