## One-liner

Zed, a Philippines-based credit startup founded by husband-and-wife team Danielle Cojuangco-Abraham and Steve Abraham, raised a $16.5M Series A led by Silicon Valley's Accel in December 2025, using AI-driven underwriting to give young Filipino professionals credit access traditional banks overlook.

## What happened

Zed built a credit product specifically targeting young Filipino professionals who are frequently excluded from traditional bank credit due to thin or nonexistent formal credit histories — a large underserved segment across the Philippines and broader Asia-Pacific region. Rather than relying on conventional credit scores, the company underwrites customers using AI models that assess current income, transaction data, and other alternative data sources, addressing the structural credit-access gap that leaves creditworthy young professionals unable to qualify through traditional scoring methods.

The December 2025 $16.5M Series A was led by Accel — the Palo Alto-based venture firm known for early backing of Facebook, Spotify, and Slack — bringing Zed's total funding to $22.5M. The company reported a 500% increase in monthly transaction volume since early 2025, a growth signal that likely underpinned Accel's decision to lead the round and reflects strong underlying demand for the alternative-underwriting credit product among its target demographic.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built underwriting around alternative data (current income, transaction history) rather than traditional credit scores, directly targeting the structural reason young professionals are excluded from conventional bank credit.
- Founded by a husband-and-wife team, an operating structure less common among VC-backed fintech founding teams, which the company and press coverage explicitly highlighted as part of its narrative.
- Attracted a marquee Silicon Valley investor (Accel) for a Philippines-focused credit product, signaling international VC confidence in Southeast Asian fintech infrastructure targeting underserved consumer segments specifically.
- Demonstrated a 500% year-to-date increase in monthly transaction volume ahead of the raise, using concrete usage-growth metrics rather than purely projected demand to support the funding round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Zed demonstrates that AI-driven alternative-data underwriting can unlock meaningful venture capital interest — including from top-tier Silicon Valley firms not traditionally focused on Southeast Asia — when it directly targets a well-defined, structurally underserved credit segment (young professionals without traditional credit histories) rather than competing broadly against incumbent banks. Strong, quantifiable usage growth (500% transaction volume increase) ahead of a raise remains a powerful signal for attracting international investors into markets they might otherwise view as higher-risk or less familiar.
