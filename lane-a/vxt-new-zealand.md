## One-liner

VXT, a Christchurch-founded voicemail-transcription app, pivoted into a VoIP phone system for law firms and grew into a fast-scaling legal-tech business, raising $2.5 million NZD at a $45 million valuation with Silicon Valley backing.

## What happened

VXT was founded by University of Canterbury students Luke Campbell and Lucy Turner, launching in October 2019 as a consumer (B2C) app that transcribed voicemails to text. The company joined Vodafone's Xone startup accelerator that December and raised an initial $600,000 in September 2020. In March 2021 it introduced VXT Call, a call-recording and transcription feature aimed at professionals rather than consumers — and the team noticed the business-facing call product generated 50–100x more revenue per customer than the original voicemail transcription service.

That signal drove a full pivot: VXT re-engineered itself as a B2B VoIP phone system built specifically for legal firms and recruitment professionals, automatically logging billable call time into a firm's practice management software. It raised a $1.8 million NZD pre-Series A round in April 2024 (led by existing investor GD1, with Startmate, Phase One, and new investor J Zac Stein), and by February 2024 had reached $1.3 million in annual recurring revenue across more than 200 legal firms. Growth accelerated from there: VXT hit $2.5 million NZD in ARR during 2024 and raised a further $2.5 million NZD round in early 2025 at a $45 million valuation, with participation from Silicon Valley firm Alpine VC — while guiding toward tripling ARR to $7.5 million NZD by the end of 2025. The original consumer voicemail-transcription product, VXT Voicemail, was discontinued in 2025 as the company committed fully to the B2B legal/recruitment vertical.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Followed the revenue signal, not the original product vision.** Rather than optimizing the B2C voicemail app, the founders reallocated toward the B2B call product the moment they saw it monetizing 50–100x better per customer.
- **Picked a narrow, underserved vertical (legal firms) instead of staying horizontal.** Focusing on time-tracking and billable-hours integration for lawyers gave VXT a defensible wedge that generic VoIP providers didn't specifically solve for.
- **Killed the legacy product on a deadline.** Discontinuing VXT Voicemail in 2025 rather than running both products signaled full commitment of engineering and support resources to the now-proven B2B model.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

VXT is a clean example of a consumer-to-business pivot driven by unit economics rather than market hype: the founders let per-customer revenue data — not a top-down strategy exercise — tell them which product to build the company around, then had the discipline to eventually sunset the original product once the new one was clearly outperforming it.
