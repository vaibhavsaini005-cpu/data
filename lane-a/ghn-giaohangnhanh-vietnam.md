## One-liner

GHN (Giaohangnhanh, "Fast Delivery" in Vietnamese) grew from a Ho Chi Minh City courier startup in 2012 to Vietnam's largest e-commerce logistics network, handling over one million parcels a day and raising $100M from Tiger Global in 2021.

## What happened

Nguyễn Trần Phi and co-founders launched GHN in 2012 in Ho Chi Minh City to solve a straightforward problem: Vietnam's e-commerce boom had no reliable last-mile infrastructure outside the state-owned postal system (Vietnam Post), which was too slow and opaque for online retailers. GHN built its own fleet, warehouse network, and tracking technology from scratch.

By 2018 GHN was handling millions of orders per month for Shopee, Lazada, Tiki, and thousands of independent merchants. The company built a nationwide network reaching 63 provinces with same-day delivery in major cities — a logistics feat in a country where traffic infrastructure varies wildly by geography.

In September 2021, GHN raised $100M in a funding round led by Tiger Global and SoftBank Ventures Korea, joined by ADB Ventures and existing backers. By then GHN was processing over one million parcels per day and was profitable on a unit-economics basis. The company had become the default logistics layer for Vietnam e-commerce, competing directly with Giao Hang Tiet Kiem (GHTK) and J&T Express for market leadership.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2012–2016**: Bootstrapped growth focused on Ho Chi Minh City; built proprietary tracking technology and driver network rather than relying on third-party couriers.
- **2018**: Nationalised network to all 63 provinces, enabling retailers to reach rural Vietnam — the geography that state postal services served badly and other startups avoided.
- **2019–2020**: COVID-19 accelerates e-commerce penetration; GHN volume doubles as offline retail closes and consumers shift online.
- **2021**: $100M round from Tiger Global and SoftBank Ventures Korea validates GHN as the infrastructure layer for Vietnam's e-commerce market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GHN demonstrates how emerging-market logistics gaps create defensible businesses if solved at the infrastructure layer. Vietnam's e-commerce growth was gated by delivery reliability, not consumer demand — once GHN proved tracking, on-time rates, and cash-on-delivery (the dominant payment method in Vietnam), it became a natural monopoly partner for every online retailer. The lesson: in markets where payment and delivery infrastructure is immature, the company that solves the pipe rather than the marketplace often captures more durable value.
