## One-liner

Skinetiq co-founder and Executive Chairman Bui Ngoc Anh agreed to sell a 75% stake in the Ho Chi Minh City D2C skincare company to Indian consumer-goods conglomerate Marico for roughly Rs 350 crore, announced February 10, 2026.

## What happened

Bui Ngoc Anh, a serial entrepreneur, and Hannah Nguyen, a Vietnamese beauty blogger and TikTok influencer with over 1.5 million followers, co-founded Skinetiq in Ho Chi Minh City on January 9, 2020. The company built a digital-first skincare business around its own Candid brand plus exclusive Vietnam distribution rights for Murad luxury clinical skincare, scaling primarily through e-commerce and social commerce to roughly Rs 152 crore ($18M) in annual revenue by 2025. On February 10, 2026, Marico Limited, through subsidiary Marico South-East Asia Corporation, announced an agreement to acquire a 75% equity stake in Skinetiq at a valuation of approximately Rs 350 crore ($42M). Marico MD and CEO Saugata Gupta said Vietnam "remains a priority market for us" and the deal "positions us to invest ahead of the curve."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Bui Ngoc Anh choosing a majority sale to a strategic conglomerate (Marico) rather than raising further independent venture capital**, trading full ownership for "platform, expertise, and resources," in his own words, to fund the company's next growth phase.
- **Pairing an owned brand (Candid) with exclusive distribution rights for an established international label (Murad)** rather than competing purely as an owned-brand D2C player, a hybrid model that made the business attractive to a strategic acquirer looking for both brand IP and distribution infrastructure.
- **Marico's decision to enter Vietnam's Gen-Z beauty market via acquisition rather than organic launch**, buying an already-scaled local operator instead of building distribution from scratch.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Skinetiq shows a founder converting social-media-driven brand building (via co-founder Hannah Nguyen's influencer following) and a hybrid owned-brand-plus-distribution model into an attractive acquisition target for a multinational conglomerate looking to enter Southeast Asian beauty markets. It's a pattern for emerging-market D2C founders: pairing your own IP with distribution rights for an established international brand can make a mid-sized business (Rs 150 crore revenue) exit-ready years before it would otherwise reach the scale multinational acquirers typically look for.
