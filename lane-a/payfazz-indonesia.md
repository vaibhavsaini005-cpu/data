## One-liner

Payfazz, the Jakarta agent-banking network that put unbanked Indonesians in front of a nationwide network of small-shop "agents" running the Payfazz app, pivoted from a standalone agent-network startup into the anchor business of Fazz Financial Group via a 2021 merger with Singapore's Xfers, then absorbed a 2023 restructuring that cut jobs across the merged group to refocus on payments, credit and stablecoins.

## What happened

Payfazz was founded in Jakarta in 2016 by Hendra Kwik, Jefriyanto Winata and Ricky Winata to solve a specific distribution problem: most of Indonesia's population outside the big cities is unbanked or underbanked, but almost every neighborhood has a small shop (warung). Payfazz recruited these shopkeepers as "agents" who used the Payfazz app to offer basic banking-style services — deposits, withdrawals, bill payments, remittances — to customers without a bank account, becoming, in the company's framing, the first Indonesian startup accepted into Y Combinator (2017). It raised a $21 million Series A in September 2018 led by Tiger Global, and a $53 million Series B in 2020, building a network the company said covered hundreds of thousands of agents.

On 3–4 March 2021, Payfazz announced a $30 million investment into Xfers, a Singapore-based payments infrastructure company, to jointly form a new holding entity, Fazz Financial Group. Rather than remaining a single-country agent-banking app, Payfazz became the Indonesia-facing consumer arm of a regional group, with Xfers serving as its B2B and payments-infrastructure arm across Southeast Asia. Payfazz co-founder and CEO Hendra Kwik became group CEO of Fazz, with Xfers co-founder Tianwei Lu as deputy CEO — explicitly positioning the combined group to compete with financial products embedded in super-apps like Gojek and Grab.

The pivot did not insulate the group from the 2022–2023 regional tech downturn. On 1 March 2023, Fazz confirmed a "business restructuring exercise" that terminated an undisclosed number of employees across its regional offices, telling press the move was meant to protect the group's financial position and redirect focus toward its "core strengths" of payments, credit and stablecoins — a narrower mandate than the original Fazz vision of broad regional financial-inclusion infrastructure.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Choosing an agent-network distribution model over a pure app (2016).** By routing services through existing small shopkeepers rather than requiring smartphone-only self-service, Payfazz could reach unbanked customers who wouldn't otherwise download a fintech app, which became its core differentiator through 2020.
- **Merging with Xfers to form Fazz Financial Group (announced 3 March 2021).** This converted Payfazz from a single-market consumer product into part of a two-sided regional group — consumer agent network in Indonesia, B2B payments rails via Xfers in Singapore — trading some independence for regional reach and cross-selling.
- **Refocusing on "core strengths" during the March 2023 restructuring.** Naming payments, credit and stablecoins specifically was a retreat from the broader financial-inclusion platform ambitions of 2021, prioritizing the parts of the business closest to revenue over expansion.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Payfazz is a clear example of a hard-mode-distribution startup (agent networks in unbanked rural areas are slow and capital-intensive to build) using an in-kind merger, rather than a straight acquisition or fresh equity round, to buy itself regional infrastructure and a second revenue engine just as regional venture funding was tightening. The 2023 restructuring shows the limits of that move: combining two cost bases without combining into a profitable one still required layoffs when growth capital dried up, and the language of the retreat — narrowing to "core strengths" — is the same signal seen across the region's 2022–2023 fintech consolidation wave.
