## One-liner

Tokyo-headquartered Kakao Piccoma pulled its webtoon and manga app out of France just two years after launch, retreating from Europe to refocus resources on its dominant Japan business.

## What happened

Kakao Piccoma Corp, the Japan-based digital comics subsidiary of South Korea's Kakao Corporation, runs Piccoma — the top-grossing manga/webtoon app in Japan, which logged a record ¥100 billion ($641M) in annual transaction volume in 2023. Riding that domestic success, Kakao Piccoma expanded into Europe, launching a French-market version of the app in 2022 (with European operations tracing back to March 2021) to capture French readers' well-documented appetite for Japanese manga.

The French bet did not translate. On May 13, 2024, the company announced it would wind down all Piccoma operations in France, with the app shutting down completely by September 30, 2024. Kakao Piccoma cited a slowdown in the growth rate of France's comics market as the direct trigger, while industry observers pointed to deeper structural issues: French readers' strong preference for physical manga volumes over digital, and local regulations that protect print publishers and booksellers from digital-platform competition. Insiders also flagged a lack of localization and cooperation with local publishers as a contributing failure mode.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Expansion without a physical-to-digital bridge**: Piccoma's model (pay-per-chapter digital access) worked in Japan's mobile-first reading culture but ran into a French market still anchored to physical book purchases.
- **Two-year runway before retreat**: Rather than iterating indefinitely on a underperforming market, Kakao Piccoma cut the French operation cleanly within roughly two years of full launch.
- **Refocus on core strength**: Capital and product attention were redirected back to Japan (and subsequently the US), where Piccoma's transaction volume kept climbing even as Europe was wound down — the classic "retreat to the fortress" pivot.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A platform's product-market fit is not portable by default, even for a category leader. Piccoma's chapter-based micropayment model exploited Japan's specific reading habits and app-store spending norms; France's print-first culture and publisher-protective regulation neutralized that same model. The pivot illustrates a common pattern for Asian consumer-tech platforms eyeing Western expansion: test fast, and be willing to fully exit rather than let a loss-making regional unit drag on the core business's economics.
