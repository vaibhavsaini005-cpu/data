## One-liner

Singapore's first buy-now-pay-later startup, launched in February 2018, grew to 3,000+ merchant partners across Singapore, Malaysia, and Hong Kong before being acquired by Temasek-backed ShopBack in November 2021 — folding into a broader rewards and payments super-platform.

## What happened

Stuart Thornton, an ex-Worldpay executive who had lived in Singapore since 2008, co-founded Hoolah in February 2018 with Arvin Singh, Daniel Pieper, and Jason Van. The proposition was straightforward: let consumers split any online purchase into three equal zero-interest monthly instalments, with merchants paying a transaction fee for every completed order — the same economics Afterpay had proved in Australia, applied to APAC's under-penetrated BNPL market.

Hoolah closed a Series A of $6.67M from Allectus Capital in October 2018 and expanded rapidly across Singapore, Malaysia, and Hong Kong, signing 3,000+ merchant partners in fashion, lifestyle, and travel verticals. The company positioned itself as the APAC-first alternative to the Australian BNPL incumbents (Afterpay, Zip) that were beginning to enter the region.

In November 2021, Temasek-backed ShopBack — Singapore's dominant cashback and shopping rewards platform — acquired Hoolah for undisclosed terms (cash and stock). The deal closed alongside ShopBack's own US$40M Temasek-led fundraise. Post-acquisition, Hoolah's BNPL product continued operating under its own brand for a transition period, before being integrated into the ShopBack app's checkout layer.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Merchant-fee model (not consumer fees):** matched global BNPL norms but meant unit economics were dependent on average order value and merchant margins — verticals like travel and electronics carried much better economics than fashion.
- **Three-market footprint by 2021:** proved cross-border replicability, making Hoolah attractive to acquirers who wanted instant merchant networks rather than years of partnership-building.
- **ShopBack fit:** ShopBack had purchase-intent traffic (cashback users already mid-funnel); Hoolah had checkout financing rails. The combination was strategically coherent — ShopBack could offer both cash-back and BNPL at checkout without redirecting users to a third party.
- **BNPL consolidation wave:** 2021–2022 saw global BNPL M&A compress the sector (Afterpay to Square, Paidy to PayPal, Hoolah to ShopBack). Regional pure-plays were absorbed rather than scaling to compete with the global entrants.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Hoolah's exit reflects the classic BNPL consolidation pattern: standalone BNPL is a feature, not a company, once distribution platforms decide to build or buy checkout financing. The strategic moat was not the instalment mechanic (easily replicable) but the merchant network — and that network was worth more to a platform with superior consumer traffic than to Hoolah running it independently. Regional fintech startups in commoditising payment rails should build toward a network or data asset the acquirer cannot efficiently replicate, rather than assuming independent scale is achievable before the platform giants move.
