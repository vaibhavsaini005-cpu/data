## One-liner

KFit, a Singapore-based fitness-class subscription startup founded by ex-Groupon Asia executive Joel Neoh, pivoted into online-to-offline deals platform Fave by acquiring Groupon's Malaysia, Indonesia, and Singapore operations in 2016–2017, and was ultimately acquired by India's Pine Labs for $45 million in April 2021.

## What happened

Joel Neoh, who had led Groupon's Asia-Pacific operations after Groupon acquired his earlier startup GroupsMore, launched KFit in 2015 as a pay-per-class fitness subscription app. Rather than staying narrowly focused on fitness, KFit repositioned itself as a broader online-to-offline (O2O) commerce platform, rebranding as Fave. It executed this pivot by acquiring Groupon's struggling regional units outright: Groupon Malaysia in November 2016, Groupon Indonesia shortly after, and Groupon Singapore in March 2017 — folding each market's merchant base and deal inventory directly into the Fave app rather than building demand from scratch. This gave Fave an instant footprint of restaurants, spas, fitness studios and hotels across multiple Southeast Asian markets under one brand.

Fave went on to build merchant-side payments and loyalty tools — letting offline merchants accept QR-code digital payments while automatically issuing customers cashback and loyalty points redeemable at that same merchant — evolving from a deals marketplace into merchant fintech infrastructure. Having raised more than $32 million as an independent company, Fave was acquired by Indian merchant-commerce platform Pine Labs for approximately $45 million in April 2021, with co-founder and CEO Joel Neoh staying on to lead the business, including a push to expand Fave's cashback/payments model into India within 3,700 cities.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Acquiring incumbents instead of competing with them**: buying Groupon's Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore operations gave Fave immediate merchant supply and consumer recognition in three markets simultaneously, compressing years of organic build-up into a single deal wave.
- **Widening from "fitness deals" to general O2O commerce**: recognizing that the KFit fitness-subscription niche was too narrow to build a regional platform business, and repositioning around merchant payments/loyalty broadened the addressable market substantially.
- **Selling into a strategic acquirer (Pine Labs) rather than raising another venture round**: this gave Fave's payments and rewards technology distribution into Pine Labs' much larger India merchant network instead of continuing to fund regional expansion independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Fave/KFit is a clear example of a startup abandoning its original product wedge (fitness subscriptions) once it became clear the real opportunity was platform-level O2O commerce infrastructure — and pursuing that pivot through consolidation (buying a struggling incumbent's regional units) rather than slow organic growth. It also shows a viable outcome path for regional Southeast Asian platforms that don't reach unicorn scale independently: a strategic trade sale to a larger player (Pine Labs) looking to buy distribution and product capability rather than build it in-house.
