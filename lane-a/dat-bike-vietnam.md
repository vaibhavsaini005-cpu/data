## One-liner

Dat Bike, founded in Ho Chi Minh City in 2019, has become Vietnam's leading homegrown electric motorcycle brand by out-pricing Chinese imports on performance while pitching local assembly as a national-pride story — raising over $51M and quintupling production capacity by 2024.

## What happened

Son Nguyen returned to Vietnam from the US in 2019 and founded Dat Bike with a thesis that Vietnamese consumers — who ride motorbikes for nearly all daily transport — would switch to electric if the product matched petrol performance and the charging infrastructure problem was addressed upfront. Vietnam has over 70 million registered motorbikes, making it one of the world's largest two-wheeler markets.

The company manufactured its Weaver series domestically in Vietnam, deliberately positioning against the flood of cheaper Chinese electric bikes entering the market by emphasising local quality control and stronger range. This resonated: Gojek partnered with Dat Bike for a ride-hailing pilot in 2023. The company raised $22M in a Series B in September 2025 and a further $4M from Thien Viet Securities in March 2026, bringing total funding to approximately $51M. Production capacity scaled fivefold in 2024 across expanded showrooms in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, and Da Nang.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Local assembly focus**: Choosing to manufacture in Vietnam (rather than import from China and rebrand) gave Dat Bike a nationalist narrative and better quality control — at the cost of higher unit economics that were partially offset by premium pricing.
- **Performance-first positioning**: Launching at 120km/h top speed and 100km range before cheaper alternatives had those specs made early adopters loyal advocates, building word-of-mouth that preceded paid marketing.
- **Ride-hailing partnership (2023)**: The Gojek pilot validated the B2B fleet use case and provided a recurring demand floor independent of retail consumers.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Dat Bike demonstrates that in markets where one transportation mode dominates (motorcycle-first Vietnam), electrification can succeed faster than in multi-modal markets by targeting a single high-frequency use case. The playbook — local manufacturing, premium specs at accessible prices, B2B fleet anchor — differs from both the imported-EV model and the mass-subsidy model, and is replicable in other Southeast Asian motorcycle-dominant markets (Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand).
