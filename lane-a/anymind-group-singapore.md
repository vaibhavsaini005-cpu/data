## One-liner

A Singapore-founded ad-tech startup that renamed itself, moved its
headquarters to Tokyo, and rebuilt itself as a full-stack "commerce
enablement" platform — then took that platform public on the Tokyo Stock
Exchange in 2023.

## What happened

The company started life in April 2016 in Singapore as AdAsia Holdings,
founded by Kosuke Sogo and Otohiko Kozutsumi as a digital advertising
technology business. In January 2018 it rebranded as AnyMind Group,
signaling a shift from a single ad-tech product toward a broader platform
strategy spanning influencer marketing, e-commerce enablement, HR tech, and
brand supply-chain tools for SMEs and creators across Asia. In 2019 the
company relocated its headquarters from Singapore to Tokyo, positioning
itself to raise Japanese growth capital and tap Japan's public markets
later, while keeping regional operations across Southeast Asia (it now runs
19 offices across 13 markets).

Before going public, AnyMind had raised roughly US$91.7 million from
investors including JAFCO Asia, LINE, and Mirai Creation Fund. The company
reported a 54% compound annual revenue growth rate from 2017 to 2022, with
fiscal 2022 revenue of ¥24,790 million and guidance of ¥32,744 million for
fiscal 2023. On February 28, 2023, the Tokyo Stock Exchange approved
AnyMind's listing on its Growth Market; the IPO — 885,300 new shares plus an
overallotment and a 1,804,200-share secondary offering, priced at ¥1,000 per
share — completed on March 29, 2023, under ticker 5027.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Renaming and re-platforming in 2018**, two years after founding, rather
  than staying a single-product ad-tech vendor. AdAsia → AnyMind Group
  wasn't cosmetic — it tracked a real shift into bundling influencer
  marketing, e-commerce, and workforce tools under one platform aimed at
  brands and creators who'd otherwise need several vendors.
- **Moving HQ from Singapore to Tokyo in 2019**, years before any listing
  was public knowledge. This was a deliberate bet on Japan as both a capital
  source (JAFCO, LINE, Mirai Creation Fund all Japan-linked) and an eventual
  listing venue, while the operating business stayed genuinely pan-Asian.
- **Choosing the Tokyo Stock Exchange Growth Market over a later-stage
  private round or a US listing.** Going public in March 2023 — a difficult
  window for tech IPOs globally — suggests the company prioritized
  Japan-market access and an earlier public listing over waiting for
  friendlier global conditions.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

AnyMind's path — found in one Southeast Asian hub, rebrand into a platform
business, relocate HQ to the country where the growth capital and the
public market actually are, then list there — is a reusable playbook for
regional SaaS/adtech companies that don't have a natural home market large
enough to IPO in on its own. The lesson for founders building pan-regional
businesses in Southeast Asia: the country you're founded in, the country
your capital comes from, and the country you eventually list in don't have
to be the same, and treating HQ location as a strategic lever (not just
where the founders happened to start) can open a public-market exit that
wouldn't otherwise exist.
