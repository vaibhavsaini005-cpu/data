## One-liner

Being AI Limited, the first AI company listed on the NZX, shut down its flagship Project Treehouse AI-agent marketplace on 16 May 2025 and accepted the immediate resignation of co-founder and CEO David McDonald.

## What happened

David McDonald co-founded Being AI (alongside Katherine Allsopp-Smith) as an AI consulting and ventures business, and took it public in April 2024 via a reverse listing into NZX-listed shell Ascension Capital — the first AI company to list on the exchange. McDonald stayed on as CEO to lead development of the company's central bet, Project Treehouse, an AI agent marketplace, even after stepping down from the board in March 2025. On 16 May 2025 the Being AI board formally closed Project Treehouse, citing both the growing dominance of large players (it specifically flagged Google's moves into AI agent standards) and the company's inability to raise the capital the project needed. The board accepted immediate resignations from McDonald, CTO Nicolas Fourrier, and two other staff supporting the project. As part of the wind-down, Being AI agreed to buy back 11.9 million shares held by 2384 Limited Partnership, an entity associated with McDonald, for nil consideration. Acting CEO Paul Forno took over to run the company's remaining consultancy business.

## Key decisions / turning points

- April 2024: McDonald takes Being AI public via reverse listing, betting the company's public identity on Project Treehouse.
- March 2025: McDonald resigns as a director but keeps the CEO title specifically to keep leading Project Treehouse.
- 16 May 2025: Board chair Michael Stiassny states the capital required "was far, far greater than predicted" and that the project was "unsuited to being a listed company entity" — the board closes it and McDonald resigns as CEO the same day.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A founder can survive losing a board seat but not losing the product the company was built around: McDonald kept his CEO title purely to protect Project Treehouse, and when the board pulled funding from that one initiative, his role ended with it. It's also a case of "first mover on an exchange" not translating into staying power — being the first AI company on the NZX didn't buy Being AI enough runway once a large incumbent (Google) began setting the terms of the exact market it was trying to enter.
