## One-liner

Thai payment gateway Omise chased crypto riches with the OmiseGO ICO (raising $25 million in 2017), pivoted back to payments as the token collapsed, rebranded to OPN in 2022, and by 2025 had reversed course again — returning to the Omise name as an AI-powered regional payments platform valued at unicorn status.

## What happened

Omise was founded in 2013 in Bangkok by Jun Hasegawa (Japanese) and Ezra Don Harinsut to build Southeast Asia's missing payment infrastructure — a Stripe-equivalent for the region. The company built a developer-friendly payment gateway deployed in Thailand, Japan, and Singapore. In 2017, riding the crypto boom, Hasegawa raised $25 million through an initial coin offering (ICO) for OmiseGO, a blockchain-based payments network; the OmiseGO token briefly reached a market cap of over $2 billion. As crypto markets crashed in 2018–2019, the OmiseGO token lost the vast majority of its value. Omise divested from the OmiseGO project in December 2020, cutting its blockchain bet. In mid-2022, the company rebranded the core payments business to OPN, reflecting its expansion beyond Thailand into a multi-market platform. In 2025, OPN reversed course again and restored the Omise name, positioning itself as an AI-powered embedded-finance provider with unicorn-level valuation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **OmiseGO ICO (2017)**: Raised $25M and created what became a top-20 cryptocurrency by market cap — a massive distraction from the core payments product.
- **Crypto divestment (December 2020)**: Pragmatic exit from blockchain after the OmiseGO token lost ~95% of peak value; refocused on licensed payments.
- **Rebrand to OPN (2022)**: Renamed to signal a shift from a developer tool to an enterprise-grade, multi-country platform.
- **Reversion to Omise (2025)**: Returned to the original brand with AI-payments positioning, suggesting the OPN rebrand created more confusion than value.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Omise is a rare case of a Southeast Asian fintech that survived a full crypto cycle — hyped, crashed, divested — and returned to its core payments business stronger. The trajectory illustrates both the danger of chasing token-economy distractions and the resilience available to companies with solid underlying infrastructure. The multiple rebrands also show the reputational cost of chasing narratives rather than maintaining product identity.
