## One-liner

Indonesian employment platform Pintarnya, co-founded by Henry Hendrawan, Ghirish Pokardas and Nelly Nurmalasari, raised a $16.7 million Series A after combining blue-collar job matching with asset-backed micro-lending, reaching profitability targets by pairing hiring with responsible-borrowing tools.

## What happened

Pintarnya was founded in 2022 by Henry Hendrawan, Ghirish Pokardas and Nelly Nurmalasari to address two problems blue-collar Indonesian workers face together rather than separately: finding stable work, and borrowing money without falling into predatory lending. Hendrawan described the founding thesis directly: the company exists to solve "earning enough and borrowing responsibly." Pintarnya built an AI-driven job-matching engine to speed up hiring for full-time and gig roles, and layered on asset-backed loans — collateralized by items such as gold, electronics or vehicles — sized to what a borrower could reasonably repay rather than to maximize loan value.

By August 2025, the platform had grown to serve more than 10 million job-seekers and 40,000 employers, with revenue up nearly fivefold year-over-year and break-even expected by the end of the year. On August 24, 2025, Pintarnya announced a $16.7 million Series A led by Square Peg, with existing investors Vertex Venture Southeast Asia & India and East Ventures also participating, to scale both the job-matching and lending sides of the platform together.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Bundling job matching with lending instead of building either as a standalone product**: Hendrawan and co-founders treated income instability and predatory borrowing as one connected problem for blue-collar workers, not two separate markets.
- **Sizing loans to affordability rather than collateral value**: a specific underwriting choice — "safer, healthier lending options designed around what they can reasonably afford" — that traded maximum loan size for lower default risk and worker trust.
- **Raising Series A only after nearly 5x revenue growth and a visible path to break-even**, rather than earlier on growth metrics alone, giving Square Peg and existing backers profitability evidence, not just usage numbers, to underwrite the round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Pintarnya's growth shows that in large blue-collar labor markets like Indonesia's, bundling adjacent financial pain points (income + credit access) into one product can outperform single-purpose fintech or job-matching apps, because the two problems compound for the same user. Raising capital only after demonstrating a credible break-even timeline — rather than purely on top-line growth — is also a pattern worth noting for founders in markets where 2024-2025 saw sharply reduced Indonesian VC funding: profitability signals, not just scale, were what unlocked a Series A in a tightening market.
