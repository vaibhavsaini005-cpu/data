## One-liner

Netbank, a BSP-regulated banking-as-a-service platform founded by Gus Poston, grew assets 1,600% since early 2022 and hit profitability with 88% year-on-year revenue growth before closing a Series B led by Singapore's Altara Ventures in April 2026.

## What happened

Netbank operates as a fully white-labelled, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)-regulated banking infrastructure platform, giving fintech companies, digital lenders, marketplaces, and non-bank networks like pawnshops and microfinance institutions the ability to offer bank accounts, payments, lending, and card products without obtaining their own banking license. Founder Gus Poston built the company around a problem he saw repeatedly in the Philippine fintech scene: younger companies kept "hitting the same wall" on licensing, settlement, compliance, and integrations — infrastructure too costly and slow for most startups to build themselves.

By the time of its Series B, announced April 21, 2026, Netbank had grown its balance sheet 1,600% from early 2022 levels to more than $18 million in assets, formed partnerships with Wise and Mastercard, and — in December 2025 — launched a Cards-as-a-Service offering built with card-issuing platform Thredd. Past clients cited include Akulaku Philippines and Sprout Solutions. The company reported reaching profitability and 88% year-on-year revenue growth in its fiscal year 2025, ahead of raising new capital, and used the round to continue building out its embedded-finance product suite with an eye toward international expansion beyond the Philippines. The Series B was led by Singapore-based Altara Ventures, with earlier backers BEENEXT, Oak Drive Ventures, Kaya Founders, Boleh Ventures, and January Capital all returning.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building for BSP-regulated compliance from the start** rather than operating in a gray area, letting Netbank sell licensed infrastructure as its core product to companies that couldn't obtain their own banking license quickly.
- **Reaching profitability before raising Series B**, giving the company negotiating leverage and a growth story (88% YoY revenue, 1,600% asset growth) independent of needing the capital to survive.
- **Expanding the product line into Cards-as-a-Service (Dec 2025) ahead of the raise**, broadening the platform beyond core banking rails to capture more of each client's stack.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Netbank's trajectory is a counter-example to the region's more common story of Philippine fintechs growing on subsidized user acquisition and burning toward a down round: it scaled a regulated, compliance-heavy infrastructure layer specifically because compliance friction was the market gap, and it timed its raise after profitability rather than before. That ordering — proving the unit economics first, then using growth capital to expand product surface area — is the throughline worth extracting for infrastructure-layer fintechs in emerging markets.
