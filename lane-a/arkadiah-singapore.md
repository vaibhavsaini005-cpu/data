*Classification note: this is a **founder-level domain pivot between ventures**, not a within-company product pivot. Arkadiah was founded as a nature-tech company in late 2023 and has not changed its own business model. The pivot documented here is Reuben Lai's redirection of seven years of fintech operating experience into climate tech — which is how both the company's own sources and Founders Forum frame it ("From Fintech to Forests"). Readers wanting within-company pivots should treat this entry accordingly.*

## One-liner

Reuben Lai built Grab Financial Group from scratch into a regional fintech unicorn, then left financial inclusion entirely to co-found Arkadiah in Singapore in late 2023 — applying the same playbook to a different problem, and reaching 5 million-plus hectares monitored across 16 countries and 40-plus projects before signing a five-year forest-carbon partnership with palm-oil giant Golden Agri-Resources in February 2026.

## What happened

At Grab, Lai built Grab Financial Group from nothing: launching payments and lending products, securing a digital banking licence, and growing the business into one of the region's leading fintech platforms. The mission was financial inclusion for millions of Southeast Asians, and by any reasonable measure he had won — the business became a unicorn.

He left anyway. His stated reason was not dissatisfaction with fintech but a judgement about where leverage now sat: "The urgency and visible impact of climate change drove us to act," paired with a specific quantitative thesis — "Research shows nature-based solutions can contribute up to 30% of the global climate solution."

What makes this a genuine transfer of expertise rather than a career change is that Lai diagnosed the new domain the way a fintech operator would. He identified the barriers to land restoration as limited capital, manual processes and complex regulations — which is almost exactly the barrier list for financial inclusion, and precisely the set of problems that technology plus capital-markets plumbing is suited to attack. The domain changed; the problem shape did not.

Arkadiah, co-founded in late 2023 with Lai as CEO, uses AI to fuse LiDAR, satellite imagery and ground truthing into 3D digital twins of landscapes, producing precise and verifiable carbon data with traceability. The operational contrast with incumbent practice is stark: traditional project developers measure forest carbon with tape measures and check progress every five years, while Arkadiah's digitisation gives landowners and corporates quarterly visibility. It sells digital monitoring, reporting and verification (DMRV) to landowners, project developers, corporates and governments.

The team was built for the hybrid problem rather than for either discipline alone — ecologists, technologists and geospatial experts, with international staff from India, Germany, Sweden and the Netherlands alongside local specialists. Co-founder Gerry Ong, Head of Geospatial, brings three decades of experience.

By April 2025 Arkadiah was monitoring more than 5 million hectares across 16 countries and supporting over 40 projects. Among them is Brújula Verde, a Colombian carbon project into which GenZero and Trafigura invested a combined US$100 million — meaning Arkadiah's measurement layer sits underneath institutional-scale capital.

In February 2026 the company signed a five-year agreement with Golden Agri-Resources to advance high-integrity forest carbon measurement for Southeast Asia, creating 3D digital twins of forest areas trackable over time to produce carbon sequestration data. Partnering with one of the region's largest agribusinesses puts the technology directly into the land base where the measurement problem is most contested.

Lai was explicit about why the company is headquartered in Singapore: "Singapore is a powerhouse in finance, tech, and talent," and "I see Singapore as a test bed for solutions that can address global climate challenges." EDB connected Arkadiah to the Singapore Carbon Market Alliance and the Earth Observation Initiative. His stated ambition: "We aim to become the largest nature tech platform driving reforestation and agroforestry across Asia and beyond."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Leaving a unicorn he had built, at the top, for an unproven category.** The pivot was not forced by failure. Lai walked away from a won position because he judged the marginal impact of his next seven years to be higher elsewhere.
- **Diagnosing climate restoration in fintech terms — capital, manual process, regulation.** This is the move that made his experience transferable. Had he framed the problem as ecological, his background would have been largely irrelevant.
- **Selling measurement rather than carbon projects.** Arkadiah supplies the verification layer to landowners, developers, corporates and governments instead of developing and selling credits itself. That makes it neutral infrastructure for a market whose central credibility problem is precisely that measurement has been self-reported.
- **Co-founding with three decades of geospatial expertise (Gerry Ong) rather than hiring it later.** Domain credibility was built into the founding team, compensating directly for the founder's own lack of ecological background.
- **Attacking the measurement *frequency* gap, not just accuracy.** Moving the industry from five-year tape-measure checks to quarterly digital visibility is the specific wedge — a change in cadence that makes carbon data financeable.
- **Partnering with Golden Agri-Resources, a palm-oil major.** Working with large agribusiness rather than against it puts high-integrity measurement where the land and the scrutiny both are.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

The transferable lesson is about what actually carries across a domain change. Lai's fintech knowledge — payments rails, lending products, a banking licence — was worthless in forestry. What carried was the diagnostic instinct: recognising that a market stalls when capital cannot underwrite something because the data is manual, infrequent and unverifiable. That is the same bottleneck in financial inclusion and in nature restoration, and it is solved the same way. Founders considering a domain switch should test whether the *problem shape* repeats, not whether the industry knowledge does; and if it does, hire the domain expertise into the founding team rather than trying to acquire it personally.

The second pattern is choosing to be the measurement layer in a market with a trust deficit. Voluntary carbon markets have been repeatedly damaged by credits whose claimed sequestration could not be independently verified. In that condition, the most durable position is not issuing credits but being the party everyone relies on to verify them — which is why Arkadiah can sell simultaneously to landowners, developers, corporates and governments whose interests otherwise conflict. When a market's core problem is credibility, neutral infrastructure beats participation.
