## One-liner

Timely, founded in Dunedin in 2011, built appointment and business management software for 50,000+ beauty professionals across 90 countries and was acquired by US-based EverCommerce for ~NZ$135M (~USD$95M) in July 2021.

## What happened

Ryan Baker, Andrew Schofield, and Will Berger founded Timely in Dunedin, New Zealand in 2011, targeting the fragmented spa and salon market. Their cloud-based platform handled appointment booking, staff scheduling, point-of-sale, inventory, and client communications — a full business management stack for SME beauty and wellness businesses.

Despite being based in one of the world's most geographically remote startup ecosystems, Timely achieved global distribution without a formal Series A/B funding path. By the time of acquisition, the platform served more than 50,000 beauty professionals in over 90 countries and processed more than 30 million appointments per year.

In May 2021, EverCommerce — a US-based service commerce platform backed by Silver Lake — announced the acquisition. The deal completed on July 8, 2021, for aggregate consideration of approximately USD$95M (NZ$135M). Timely joined EverCommerce's suite of vertical SaaS products spanning fitness, home services, and healthcare alongside beauty.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Vertical depth over breadth**: Timely chose to own one vertical (beauty/wellness SMEs) completely rather than build a generic scheduling tool, enabling deep product-market fit with a sticky, renewals-driven revenue base.
- **Remote-first from day one**: Building in Dunedin rather than Auckland forced distributed habits early, which translated into a globally distributed customer acquisition model.
- **Bootstrap-to-acquisition**: Rather than chasing venture capital, Timely grew to product-market fit organically, which preserved optionality and made it an attractive acquisition target without dilution constraints from multiple VC rounds.
- **EverCommerce as exit route**: The acquirer's playbook is to buy profitable vertical SaaS businesses and cross-sell across its portfolio — Timely's clean revenue quality made it a textbook fit.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Timely is a strong counter-example to the assumption that meaningful B2B SaaS exits require Silicon Valley capital or proximity. New Zealand's small domestic market forced Timely to think globally from the outset. The acquisition demonstrates that vertical SaaS companies with high retention in SME markets can command premium multiples even without unicorn trajectories, provided the revenue is clean and the product deeply embedded in daily workflows.
