## One-liner

Publons, a Wellington-founded platform letting academics get verifiable credit for peer review work, grew into the dominant player in its niche and was acquired by citation-data giant Clarivate Analytics in 2017, later being folded directly into Web of Science.

## What happened

Publons was co-founded in Wellington, New Zealand by Andrew Preston and Daniel Johnston around the belief that peer review — a huge, unpaid, and largely invisible part of academic labor — deserved to be tracked and credited like any other scholarly output. The platform let researchers log and verify their peer-review and editorial work for journals, building profiles that made an otherwise anonymous contribution visible to employers, funders, and tenure committees. Within about five years, Publons had built what Clarivate's June 1, 2017 acquisition announcement called "the largest and most trustworthy reviewer discovery tool in the world," serving 8 of the top 10 academic publishers and more than 150,000 reviewers on the platform. On June 1, 2017, Clarivate Analytics — owner of the Web of Science citation database — announced it was acquiring Publons to create what it described as "the definitive publisher-independent platform for accelerating research through peer review," with Clarivate CEO Jay Nadler framing it as extending support across the full research lifecycle from ideation through assessment. Both co-founders and the full Publons team joined Clarivate as part of the deal, keeping Publons' New Zealand base intact while expanding its Wellington and London offices. Publons was later merged directly into the Web of Science product in 2022, cementing peer-review tracking as a standard part of the world's dominant citation infrastructure rather than remaining a standalone niche tool.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Solving an unglamorous but universal problem**: rather than building another publishing or citation tool, Publons targeted the specific pain point of uncredited peer-review labor, which every academic publisher and researcher had a stake in fixing.
- **Winning publisher-side distribution before an exit**: reaching 8 of the top 10 publishers gave Publons the kind of entrenched, multi-sided network position that made it a natural acquisition target for infrastructure players like Clarivate rather than a competitor to be out-built.
- **Accepting integration over independence**: letting Publons be absorbed into Web of Science by 2022, rather than preserving it as a separate brand indefinitely, matched the founders' original goal — peer-review credit becoming a default expectation — more than standalone survival would have.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Publons shows a small New Zealand-founded startup building genuine global category leadership by picking a structurally under-served niche (peer-review recognition) adjacent to a much larger incumbent industry (academic publishing and citation data), rather than competing head-on with that industry. The acquisition by Clarivate, and the later full merge into Web of Science, is the clean version of the outcome such a niche-first strategy is built for: becoming infrastructure inside a bigger system rather than staying a bolt-on tool beside it.
