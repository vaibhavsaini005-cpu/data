## One-liner

aCommerce, Southeast Asia's largest e-commerce enablement company with $119M in institutional backing, received Stock Exchange of Thailand IPO approval in June 2022 but pulled the listing indefinitely months later citing "macroeconomic uncertainties" — becoming a marker for the abrupt closure of SEA's 2021–22 tech IPO window.

## What happened

Founded in 2013 in Bangkok, aCommerce built an end-to-end e-commerce outsourcing platform for global brands entering Southeast Asia. Its services covered performance marketing, channel management, web store operations, order fulfilment, warehousing, and last-mile logistics — effectively the full back-office stack a consumer brand would need to sell on Lazada, Shopee, or its own D2C site across five markets (Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines). Clients included Samsung, Unilever, Nestlé, L'Oréal, and Philips; by 2022 the company served more than 260 brands and employed over 1,200 people.

Having raised $119 million in institutional funding, aCommerce filed for a listing on the Stock Exchange of Thailand (SET) main board in December 2021, positioning itself as the first major Thai tech company to test public markets. The SET formally approved the IPO in June 2022, granting a deadline of March 2023 to complete the listing, with 35% of shares offered to the public.

The IPO was cancelled. As global equity markets contracted through H2 2022 and tech valuations reset — particularly for growth-stage companies without clear near-term profitability — aCommerce withdrew the offering, citing "macroeconomic uncertainties." The company then pivoted toward an operational efficiency and margin-improvement programme rather than continued pursuit of the growth narrative required for a public listing. By April 2024, the Group CEO confirmed aCommerce was still targeting a SET listing but had pushed the timeline to 2025 or beyond, stating the company was "on the path to profitability."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **SET listing over offshore exchanges**: Choosing Bangkok over Nasdaq or the Singapore Exchange was positioned as a cost-effective, natural-fit decision for a SEA-centric business. But Bangkok's tech IPO market had no proven comps, making the valuation expectation especially fragile when global sentiment shifted.
- **Delaying rather than pricing down**: aCommerce chose to pull the listing rather than proceed at a materially reduced valuation. This preserved the growth narrative for future investors but left its earliest backers (some in since 2013) without a liquidity event for at least another three years.
- **Pivot to profitability**: Post-delay, the company restructured internal operations and reduced discretionary spend. The shift from "IPO growth story" to "path to profitability" is a meaningful strategic reorientation — brand-servicing revenue is more stable than marketplace GMV but doesn't command the same tech multiples that justified the pre-IPO raise trajectory.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

aCommerce is representative of dozens of Asia-Pacific technology companies that found themselves in the IPO approval window between 2021–22 but watched the market close before they could list. Its case also highlights a structural challenge for e-commerce enablement businesses: brand-service revenue is stickier and more defensible than consumer marketplace revenue, but it is inherently capped by client marketing budgets and doesn't generate the hockey-stick growth curves that drive premium tech IPO multiples. Companies that built their funding stack on "tech multiple" expectations at the Series B and C then struggled to reconcile those valuations with "service business" pricing when public markets reset. Thailand's IPO market remains thin for tech companies, meaning any future listing will still depend substantially on global, not local, investor appetite.
