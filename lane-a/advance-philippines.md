## One-liner

Manila-based Advance, launched in 2018, raised a $16M pre-Series A in March 2023 to scale its earned-wage-access platform, which was already serving employees at roughly 200 Philippine companies.

## What happened

Advance built a salary-advance and financial-wellness platform aimed at the large population of underserved, often unbanked or underbanked, employees in the Philippines — letting workers draw against wages they'd already earned rather than take on high-interest informal debt between pay cycles. By March 2023, the company had built enough traction with employer partners (around 200 companies at the time) to close a $16M pre-Series A round.

The raise came during a period when overall Southeast Asian fintech funding was contracting, making Advance's ability to close a meaningful round on the strength of its employer-distribution model and usage metrics a notable signal of durable product-market fit rather than momentum-driven funding.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused on the earned-wage-access niche rather than broader consumer lending, directly targeting the gap between paydays that pushes underbanked workers toward informal credit.
- Distributed through employer partnerships (B2B2C) rather than direct-to-consumer acquisition, giving it a lower-cost, higher-trust channel into its target user base.
- Closed a meaningful pre-Series A in March 2023, during a broader funding contraction in the region, on the strength of existing employer traction.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Advance's raise during a funding winter illustrates that narrowly targeted fintech products solving a concrete, recurring pain point (the gap between paydays) can still raise when growth-at-all-costs consumer fintech struggles to. The B2B2C distribution model — selling through employers rather than acquiring individual users directly — kept acquisition costs low enough to demonstrate real unit economics even as investor risk appetite fell.
