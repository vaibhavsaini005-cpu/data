## One-liner

Vietnamese commercial-and-industrial solar developer CME Solar, founded by CEO Chung Dieu Tuan, secured a $20 million investment from the Emerging Africa & Asia Infrastructure Fund (EAAIF) to expand its rooftop solar pipeline to more than 260MWp as Vietnam tightened rules favoring C&I solar.

## What happened

Chung Dieu Tuan left a role at the Vietnam Oman Investment Fund after nearly a decade in renewable energy to found CME Solar in July 2018, targeting a specific niche: rooftop solar for commercial and industrial (C&I) manufacturing clients rather than utility-scale or residential solar. The company built a client base of over 100 companies across roughly 20 industries, including landmark projects at Tan Son Nhat International Airport, the Adidas R&D Center and Hwaseung Vina, and offtake relationships with multinationals like Foxconn and LG.

On January 6, 2025, the Emerging Africa & Asia Infrastructure Fund announced a $20 million investment in CME Solar, following earlier financing from responsAbility climate funds that had already brought the company's total raised capital to roughly $32 million by 2023. Chung Dieu Tuan tied the new investment directly to a specific regulatory shift: "The investment from EAAIF marks a pivotal moment for us, enabling us to further strengthen our position in light of the recent government regulation aimed at stimulating the development of the commercial and industrial solar energy sector." The capital is earmarked to grow CME Solar's project pipeline past 260MWp.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Leaving an investment-fund role to found a company focused narrowly on C&I rooftop solar**: Chung Dieu Tuan applied nearly a decade of renewable-energy investment experience to a specific segment (manufacturer-facing rooftop solar) rather than broader utility-scale generation.
- **Building offtake relationships with large manufacturing multinationals (Foxconn, LG, Adidas) before scaling financing**: gave CME Solar demonstrated, creditworthy customers that made it a bankable borrower for climate-focused infrastructure funds.
- **Timing the EAAIF raise to a specific regulatory change** favoring C&I solar development, rather than raising opportunistically — Tuan explicitly frames the capital as a response to a named policy shift, not just growth capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

CME Solar shows a repeatable model for emerging-market climate infrastructure: pick a narrow, creditworthy customer segment (multinational manufacturers with sustainability mandates) rather than competing for utility-scale contracts, and use each project with a known offtaker (Foxconn, LG) as proof to unlock progressively larger infrastructure-fund financing. Explicitly linking a funding round to a specific regulatory tailwind, rather than presenting it as generic growth capital, is also a pattern that signals founders reading policy shifts as fundable moments rather than passive market conditions.
