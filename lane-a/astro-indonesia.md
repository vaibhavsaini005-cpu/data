## One-liner

After nearly a year of failed fundraising in a post-eFishery funding
drought, Astro co-founder and CEO Vincent Tjendra accepted a down round led
by Amazon rather than shut the 15-minute grocery delivery startup down.

## What happened

Astro, the Jakarta-based 15-minute grocery delivery startup co-founded in
2021 by Vincent Tjendra and Marcella Moniaga, spent most of 2025 trying and
failing to raise fresh capital as Indonesian VC funding collapsed in the
wake of the eFishery fraud scandal. In September 2025, Amazon.com led a
$51.9m round into Astro — the largest single equity check any Indonesian
startup received that year — but at a valuation roughly 30% below Astro's
previous round.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Facing a genuine risk of running out of capital after a year of "meeting
  after meeting" rejections, Tjendra chose to accept a materially lower
  valuation rather than keep holding out for a flat or up round, or wind
  the company down.
- Tjendra publicly reframed the down round as a survival tactic rather than
  a defeat: "We're building for the long term. A down round is just one
  blip."
- The deal came specifically from Amazon (a strategic investor) rather than
  a traditional VC, at a moment when Indonesian VCs had become broadly
  risk-averse after the eFishery scandal — a deliberate pivot toward
  strategic rather than financial capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A concrete, named-founder example of how Indonesia's post-eFishery funding
crunch forced operators to take valuation cuts to survive, and how a
strategic investor (rather than VC) became the fundraising path of last
resort for a startup that still had a real, growing business.
