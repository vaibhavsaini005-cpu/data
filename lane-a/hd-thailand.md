## One-liner

Thai healthcare marketplace HD, co-founded and led by CEO Sheji Ho, brought in pharma giant Merck (MSD) as a strategic investor in a $7.8 million round after MSD identified HD as the largest online seller of its HPV vaccines in Southeast Asia.

## What happened

Sheji Ho co-founded HD (with Raya Ten Chantaramungkorn) to run two connected healthcare businesses in Southeast Asia: HDmall, an online marketplace for outpatient services like checkups, tests, teleconsultations and vaccinations, and HDcare, which connects patients with surgeons to use underutilized hospital and clinic operating-room capacity for elective surgeries at 15-20% lower cost — an "Airbnb for surgeries" model. By early 2025, HD had raised $18 million in cumulative funding across multiple rounds, including a 2023 raise and an April 2024 Series A.

On February 12, 2025, TechCrunch reported that MSD (Merck's non-US name), the manufacturer of major HPV vaccines, had invested in HD as part of a $7.8 million round. Ho explained the specific commercial logic behind Merck's interest: "MSD, which produces the HPV vaccines, reached out to [us] because we were already selling a lot of HPV vaccines online that were being administered at the hospitals and clinics we work with." He added that HD offers "the largest number [of] vaccines online in the markets" it serves. The capital was earmarked to enhance HD's marketplace and to further build out its AI technology.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building two connected but distinct products (HDmall and HDcare) rather than one general marketplace**: separated routine outpatient services from elective surgery capacity-sharing, targeting different cost and utilization problems within the same provider network.
- **Scaling vaccine distribution volume specifically, rather than treating it as one SKU among many**: Ho's account shows HD had already become MSD's largest online vaccine channel in its markets before MSD ever invested — the commercial relationship preceded the funding decision.
- **Accepting a strategic pharma investor (Merck/MSD) into the cap table** rather than raising only from generalist VCs, aligning HD's marketplace growth directly with a major vaccine manufacturer's distribution interests.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

HD's raise shows a healthcare marketplace converting operational scale in one specific category (vaccine sales volume) into a strategic investment relationship with the manufacturer whose product it was already distributing at scale — the commercial proof came first, and the investment followed as a natural extension rather than a speculative bet. This is a repeatable pattern for vertical marketplaces in regulated categories: dominate a measurable, high-value transaction type within the marketplace, and let that data become the basis for a strategic (not just financial) investor relationship.
