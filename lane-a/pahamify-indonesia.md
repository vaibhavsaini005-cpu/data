## One-liner

A YC-backed Indonesian test-prep edtech that grew rapidly on COVID-era demand shut down in June 2022, unable to sustain unit economics once in-person schooling resumed — part of a wave that also took down Zenius that same year.

## What happened

Pahamify was founded in 2018 by Rousyan Fikri (CEO) and co-founders as a video-based learning platform helping Indonesian high school students prepare for the national university entrance exam (SBMPTN/UTBK). The company was accepted into Y Combinator's W20 batch and received seed funding from YC (~US$150,000), followed by a Series A from Shunwei Capital. Total funding raised was approximately $8–10 million.

The platform offered short-form video lectures, practice tests, and AI-driven personalisation to help students navigate one of the world's most competitive university admissions systems — Indonesia's state university spots are highly limited relative to the applicant pool. During the COVID-19 school closures of 2020–2021, Pahamify saw strong user growth as students shifted to online preparation tools.

On June 28, 2022, Pahamify announced through its official social accounts that it was ceasing operations. The announcement followed a round of mass layoffs disclosed earlier that month. The company cited an unsustainable business model after the post-COVID return to in-person schooling eroded digital engagement. The shutdown came just weeks after Zenius — Pahamify's larger and better-funded rival in the same test-prep vertical — had also halted operations, underlining that the problem was structural to the segment rather than specific to one company.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **COVID dependency**: Pahamify, like most Indonesian edtech, experienced artificial demand inflation during remote schooling that was not structurally retained once schools reopened. The user cohorts acquired during lockdown churned heavily.
- **Exam-cycle seasonality**: University entrance prep is inherently seasonal (students use it for one 6-month cycle) and single-purpose — retention and upsell paths are extremely limited, making LTV hard to sustain.
- **Crowded vertical**: Pahamify competed against Zenius (better funded, older brand), Ruangguru (dominant platform, already diversified beyond test prep — also in this ledger), and international platforms. The differentiation was insufficient to command the market share needed for viable unit economics.
- **Shunwei Capital concentration risk**: Shunwei is a Chinese VC; the geopolitical scrutiny on Chinese-backed Indonesian tech investments in 2021–2022 may have constrained the company's ability to raise a bridge round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Pahamify's failure is a textbook illustration of the "borrowed demand" trap in edtech: pandemic-era growth was real but not durable. The test-prep subsegment is particularly vulnerable because it has a natural end-point per customer (pass/fail the exam), high seasonality, and commoditisation pressure from free YouTube content and state-provided study materials. Indonesian edtech's 2022 crash — Zenius and Pahamify both shutting within weeks — suggests the segment was fundamentally over-capitalised relative to sustainable demand, and that COVID metrics should have been heavily discounted in underwriting subsequent rounds.
