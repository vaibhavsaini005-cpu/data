## One-liner

Tokyo-listed payments and app-monetization company Metaps swung from a ¥4.05 billion annual profit to a ¥1.59 billion loss after a subsidiary's data breach exposed 2.8 million credit card records, ending its run as a public company via a management buyout at a discount to its former highs.

## What happened

Metaps built its business across two divisions: an ad-tech/app-monetization arm (its original product) and a payments/fintech arm. In the fiscal year ending December 2022, a subsidiary suffered an unauthorized access incident that exposed roughly 2.8 million customers' credit card records. Remediation, notification, and related incident costs came to approximately ¥2.136 billion. Even though the finance division grew revenue 5.5% and the marketing division grew 4.0% that year, the breach costs were large enough to flip the company from a ¥4.054 billion profit in the prior period to a ¥1.592 billion loss. On February 13, 2023, Metaps was placed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's supervised-issue list pending a management buyout, and representative Yuichiro Yamazaki set up an acquisition vehicle, Odessa12, to take the company private via tender offer at ¥889/share (about a 41% premium to the pre-announcement closing price of ¥627, though still a modest valuation for a company that had earlier traded much higher). The tender completed and Metaps delisted from the Tokyo Stock Exchange in mid-2023, later merging into Odessa12, which was renamed Metaps Holdings.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Diversifying into payments without hardening security in step**: the fintech pivot grew revenue, but the breach happened in the subsidiary handling that same payments business — the unit driving growth was also the point of failure.
- **Absorbing the breach cost in one fiscal year rather than smoothing it**: booking the full ¥2.1B+ in incident costs immediately turned an otherwise profitable year into a loss, which made the stock a target for a going-private transaction rather than a turnaround story.
- **Choosing an MBO over a public turnaround**: leadership opted to take the company private at a premium to the crashed share price rather than attempt a multi-year public rebuild of investor trust after the breach.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Metaps shows how a single security incident in a fast-growing fintech subsidiary can erase a full year of consolidated profit and force a public company off the exchange — the breach cost here (~¥2.1B) was larger than the entire prior-year profit. It's also a case where "success" (revenue growth in both divisions) and "failure" (a going-private exit at a discounted valuation) happened in the same fiscal year, driven entirely by a security lapse rather than a demand or product problem.
