## One-liner

KoinWorks, once one of Indonesia's best-funded P2P lending and SME neobank startups, went from 2022 workforce cuts to a full regulatory crisis by late 2024 — after its KoinP2P lending subsidiary paid out 365 billion rupiah against loans backed by fraudulent borrower data — and by 2026 its founders had been detained by prosecutors in a 600-billion-rupiah credit corruption case.

## What happened

Founded in 2016 by Benedicto Haryono and Willy Arifin, KoinWorks built a peer-to-peer lending platform for Indonesian SMEs and individuals, and expanded in April 2022 into full digital banking with "KoinWorks Neo," offering working-capital loans, payments, and financial reporting tools. The expansion came at a cost: in November 2022, the company laid off 70 employees — about 8% of its workforce — as part of an internal reorganization, an early signal of strain as Southeast Asian fintech funding cooled.

The bigger crisis emerged two years later. KoinWorks' lending subsidiary, PT Lunaria Annua Teknologi (operating as KoinP2P), disbursed roughly 365 billion rupiah in loans against borrower identity documents that turned out to be fraudulent, and the borrower subsequently defaulted, leaving KoinP2P unable to make scheduled payments to its own lenders. Indonesia's financial regulator OJK announced strict supervision of the entity on 21 November 2024, requiring on-site inspections and a capital injection commitment from the controlling shareholder to keep servicing lenders. The situation escalated further by May 2026, when Indonesian prosecutors detained KoinWorks' founders and other senior executives in connection with an alleged 600-billion-rupiah credit-disbursement corruption case, prompting OJK to open a special examination of the company.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Expanding from pure P2P lending into full-service digital banking (KoinWorks Neo, April 2022) increased balance-sheet and compliance complexity right before the 2022 funding downturn forced the first layoffs.
- Disbursing roughly 365 billion rupiah against loans backed by fraudulent KTP (national ID) data exposed a fundamental breakdown in KoinP2P's borrower-verification and underwriting controls, not just a bad macro environment.
- OJK's November 2024 decision to impose strict supervision — rather than allow a private workout with lenders — turned a liquidity problem into a public regulatory event that likely accelerated scrutiny leading to the 2026 corruption case.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

KoinWorks illustrates how Indonesian fintech lenders can fail in stages rather than all at once: a 2022 cost-cutting layoff that looked like routine downturn discipline was followed, two years later, by a underwriting-fraud-driven liquidity crisis, and finally by criminal charges against the founders themselves — echoing the same pattern (early workforce cuts as an early warning sign of deeper governance failure) seen in other Indonesian fintech and agritech collapses of the same period. For SME lending platforms specifically, it underscores that borrower-identity fraud at the underwriting stage is an existential risk, not a routine credit-loss cost, once it's large enough to break payments to a platform's own retail lenders.
