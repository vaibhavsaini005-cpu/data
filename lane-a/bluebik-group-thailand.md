## One-liner

A Thai digital-transformation consultancy founded in 2013 grew from a small-cap listing on Thailand's junior board into a main-board Stock Exchange of Thailand company chasing a spot in the SET100 index.

## What happened

Bluebik Group was registered in Thailand on September 12, 2013 as a strategy and digital-technology consulting firm, later expanding into AI-led enterprise transformation work with offices reaching the UK, India, and Vietnam and a headcount of over 1,000 tech consultants. It first went public on Thailand's Market for Alternative Investment (mai) on September 16, 2021, at an IPO price of 18.00 baht.

By 2025 the company had grown enough — including reporting a 38% year-on-year increase in net profit to 146 million baht in H1 2025 — to qualify for and pursue a move to the Stock Exchange of Thailand's main board. On July 22, 2025, Bluebik Group (ticker BBIK) began trading on the SET's Information & Communication Technology sector after transferring up from the mai board. Management framed the move as giving the company access to a broader base of institutional and foreign investors to fund further growth via M&A, joint ventures, and government-project bids, with a stated ambition to join the SET100 index within three years.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **mai-to-SET graduation as a growth signal**: Moving from the junior "Market for Alternative Investment" to the SET main board is a formal milestone in Thai capital markets, typically requiring a company to clear higher market-cap, profitability, and governance thresholds — Bluebik treated it as a deliberate funding-access strategy, not just a listing formality.
- **Service-line expansion beyond core consulting**: Growth was driven partly by broadening from strategy/digital consulting into AI-enterprise transformation work and regional offices (UK, India, Vietnam), diversifying revenue beyond the original Thai consulting base.
- **Using the uplisting to target index inclusion**: Bluebik explicitly tied the SET move to a three-year plan to reach the SET100 index, using the listing change itself as a lever for its next growth phase rather than an end state.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Bluebik is a useful counterpoint to Southeast Asia's more commonly logged fintech/e-commerce success stories: it's a professional-services business (digital consulting) that built genuine profitability and used domestic capital markets — not venture funding rounds — as its primary growth-financing tool, graduating through Thailand's two-tier listing system as a proxy for scaling maturity. It's a reminder that "success" in this region's tech economy isn't only unicorn funding rounds; a profitable, bootstrapped-to-IPO consultancy can be just as legitimate a growth story.
