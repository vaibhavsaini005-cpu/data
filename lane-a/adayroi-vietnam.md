## One-liner

Vingroup shut down Adayroi, once one of Vietnam's largest e-commerce sites, after it fell far behind Shopee, Lazada, Tiki and Sendo, folding its remnants into the VinID loyalty app as the conglomerate exited retail entirely.

## What happened

Adayroi launched in 2014 as Vingroup's e-commerce arm, backed by the conglomerate's deep pockets and its VinMart/VinMart+ retail network for fulfillment and cross-promotion. Despite that backing, it never built a durable lead: by Q3 2019 its monthly web traffic in Vietnam was around 6.4 million users, well behind homegrown rivals Sendo and Tiki and regional players Shopee and Lazada, according to iPrice data cited in coverage of the shutdown.

On December 17, 2019, Vingroup ceased Adayroi's operations effective 6:00 pm, merging its customer data and functions into VinID, the group's e-payments and loyalty app. The move came alongside a much larger announcement: Vingroup was exiting retail altogether, also shutting its VinPro electronics chain and divesting VinMart/VinMart+ to Masan Group, to refocus the conglomerate on technology and industrial manufacturing (VinFast, VinSmart).

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Conglomerate backing wasn't enough**: Adayroi had capital and a retail network most startups would envy, but still lost the e-commerce race to leaner, more focused competitors.
- **Folded into VinID rather than sold**: Unlike a clean divestiture, Adayroi's data and customer base were absorbed into Vingroup's loyalty app, suggesting the asset had more value as a data source than as a standalone marketplace.
- **Bundled with a full retail exit**: The shutdown wasn't an isolated e-commerce failure — it was part of Vingroup abandoning retail entirely to concentrate capital on VinFast and VinSmart, a sign the parent had decided e-commerce didn't fit its long-term strategy regardless of Adayroi's individual trajectory.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Adayroi shows that conglomerate backing and fulfillment infrastructure don't substitute for e-commerce-specific execution — Sendo and Tiki, both leaner and more focused, outpaced a well-capitalized incumbent. It's also a case of a parent company cutting a business unit not because it was catastrophically broken, but because it stopped fitting a reprioritized corporate strategy (Vingroup betting its future on VinFast/VinSmart instead).
