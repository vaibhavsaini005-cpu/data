## One-liner

GoTyme Bank, a joint venture between the Philippines' Gokongwei-led Tyme Group and South African neobank Tyme, grew from launch to roughly 8 million customers within about three years, becoming one of the country's fastest-scaling digital banks.

## What happened

GoTyme Bank launched in the Philippines in 2022 as a partnership between Tyme Group (backer of South Africa's TymeBank) and the Gokongwei family's JG Summit conglomerate, combining Tyme's digital-banking technology stack with Gokongwei's retail distribution (kiosks placed inside Robinsons malls and stores). The bank differentiated with fast, largely paperless account opening via physical kiosks plus an app-first product suite.

Growth was rapid by regional standards: GoTyme reported reaching 2 million customers within 14 months of launch (per JG Summit's January 2024 release), and by late 2024 was tracking to exceed 5.3 million customers, later reporting close to 8 million customers and roughly ₱40 billion in deposits, alongside a seven-fold year-on-year increase in income compared with 2023. By the end of 2024 the bank ranked Top 2 among all Philippine banks in Visa debit gross transaction value and monthly app downloads. The bank posted net losses in both 2023 (₱2.47 billion) and 2024 (₱3.44 billion) — expected given its early-stage expansion spend — and has stated it is targeting profitability by 2027 while continuing to prioritize customer growth over near-term earnings.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Distribution via an existing retail footprint**: routing account acquisition through Robinsons kiosks gave GoTyme a physical onboarding channel most neobank competitors lacked, solving the typical digital-bank cold-start distribution problem.
- **Importing a proven technology partner**: rather than building core banking tech from scratch, the JV imported Tyme Group's already-validated South African stack, cutting time-to-market.
- **Growth-over-profitability sequencing**: leadership has explicitly chosen to keep spending into customer acquisition and product breadth (deposits, payments, lending) ahead of turning a profit, betting that scale economics arrive later.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GoTyme is a useful counterpoint to fintech "profitability now" narratives: its investors are underwriting multi-year losses on the thesis that market-share capture in a large underbanked population (the Philippines) compounds into durable advantage once scale hits. The kiosk-based onboarding is also a transferable pattern for emerging-market digital banks — physical retail partnerships can substitute for the marketing spend pure-digital challengers need to build trust and awareness from zero.
