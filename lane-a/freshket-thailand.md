## One-liner

Freshket built a B2B fresh food marketplace for Thai restaurants, then COVID shuttered its entire customer base overnight — forcing a pivot that ultimately proved the underlying infrastructure was more valuable than the original use case.

## What happened

Freshket was founded in Bangkok in 2017 by Pongsatorn Vanathanophas and Teerawat Thammasaeng. The core insight was structural: Thai restaurants and food businesses sourced fresh ingredients through a fragmented chain of wet-market wholesalers, each taking a margin and introducing a variable in quality and reliability. Freshket built a mobile app that let restaurant operators order produce, meat, and seafood directly from verified suppliers, with next-day delivery, digital receipts, and a transparent price index.

Traction was solid through 2018-2019. Restaurant owners valued predictable pricing and the elimination of early-morning wet-market runs. Freshket raised a Series A from PTTEP (PTT Exploration and Production) and expanded its supplier network across Bangkok and into regional cities.

COVID-19 hit in early 2020 and was existentially threatening: Thailand's government-ordered restaurant closures reduced Freshket's core customer base to near zero overnight. Rather than waiting for restaurants to reopen, the team made an aggressive pivot — turning the same cold-chain logistics and supplier network toward cloud kitchens, meal-kit producers, catering companies, and food manufacturers who were still operating or scaling rapidly as home delivery surged. Freshket also added a direct-to-consumer channel for bulk buyers (households stocking up during lockdowns) and began onboarding suppliers in categories beyond fresh produce, including packaged goods and shelf-stable ingredients.

The pivot worked. By the time Thailand's restaurant sector recovered in 2021-2022, Freshket had a more diversified customer base and had validated that its infrastructure had multiple addressable markets beyond dine-in restaurants. In November 2021 it raised a $26M Series B led by TPVGP (a joint venture between True Digital Group and PTTEP), with participation from SCB 10X (Siam Commercial Bank's venture arm) and others. The company scaled nationally and began exploring international expansion in Southeast Asia.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Supply-chain infrastructure over pure marketplace:** Freshket owned cold-chain logistics rather than acting as a pure software broker. This made the pre-COVID build costlier but meant the pivot to new customer segments was possible without rebuilding from scratch.
- **Pivoting customers, not product:** Rather than changing the core product, Freshket shifted which customers it served — cloud kitchens and food manufacturers rather than dine-in restaurants — using the same underlying delivery and supplier infrastructure.
- **Timing of Series B:** Raising post-COVID-recovery with a demonstrated multi-segment customer base gave Freshket a significantly stronger narrative (and likely better terms) than it would have had in mid-2020.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Freshket is a useful example of supply-chain infrastructure being the durable asset when the initial customer segment evaporates. Startups that build proprietary logistics or procurement infrastructure often discover, under duress, that the infrastructure is more valuable than the initial use case because it can be repointed. The broader pattern — COVID forcing food-supply startups to discover adjacent customer segments they might never have explored under normal conditions — repeated across Southeast Asia, turning existential crises into unexpected pivots that improved unit economics and diversified revenue.
