## One-liner

JobHopin, a Ho Chi Minh City-based AI recruitment platform that had raised a Series A round and served clients including Techcombank, SAP, and Deloitte APAC, drastically scaled down from over 200 employees to a fully remote skeleton team of 15 in mid-2024 after failing to close a Series B round, while pivoting its focus toward the banking, financial services, and insurance sector.

## What happened

JobHopin built an AI-powered recruitment and HR technology platform in Vietnam, growing to serve major enterprise clients such as Techcombank, SAP, Deloitte APAC, Krungthai Bank, and Avanto. The company successfully raised a Series A round and built a workforce exceeding 200 employees at its peak, positioning itself as one of Vietnam's more prominent HR tech startups during the region's earlier funding boom.

When JobHopin failed to close a Series B round and ceased fundraising efforts from around October 2023, the company responded not with an outright shutdown but with a severe operational contraction — cutting staff from over 200 to just 15 people, all switched to fully remote work, and closing its physical office to reduce expenses. Rather than abandoning its recruitment technology entirely, the company pivoted its commercial focus specifically toward the banking, financial services, and insurance (BFSI) sector, betting that large financial institutions' recruitment needs would provide a more stable revenue base than its prior broader market focus — though the long sales cycles and extended payment terms typical of BFSI clients subsequently created new cash flow challenges for the leaner organization.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Scaled aggressively during the earlier funding boom, growing past 200 employees on the assumption that Series B capital would follow the company's Series A success.
- Chose severe cost-cutting (a roughly 92% staff reduction) and full office closure over shutting down entirely when Series B fundraising failed, preserving the company as a going concern for existing clients.
- Pivoted commercial focus toward the BFSI sector specifically, betting on large financial institutions as more durable clients than the broader recruitment market it had previously served.
- Retained fully remote operations post-contraction rather than maintaining physical office infrastructure, permanently restructuring its cost base around a much smaller core team.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

JobHopin's near-shutdown-but-not-quite trajectory illustrates a distinct failure mode between clean shutdown and successful pivot: companies that scaled aggressively on the assumption of continued venture funding can be forced into extreme operational contraction when later rounds fail to materialize, yet survive by cutting to a sustainable core rather than closing outright. The company's subsequent pivot toward BFSI clients also demonstrates a risk inherent in chasing "more stable" enterprise segments during a downturn: sectors perceived as safer, like banking and financial services, often carry structural drawbacks (long sales cycles, extended payment terms) that can undermine the very cash-flow stability a struggling, leaner company most needs.
