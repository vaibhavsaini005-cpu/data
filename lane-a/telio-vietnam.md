## One-liner

Telio, a Vietnamese B2B e-commerce platform connecting small traditional retailers with brands and wholesalers, shut down in November 2024 and formally dissolved in December 2024 after burning cash faster than it could raise fresh capital, despite still generating $2.5-3M in monthly revenue.

## What happened

Telio built a centralized B2B marketplace linking Vietnam's fragmented network of small "mom-and-pop" grocery and FMCG retailers directly to brands and wholesalers, digitizing a supply chain that had traditionally run through multiple layers of informal distributors. The company raised a total of $64M across its funding history and, at its peak, was considered one of Vietnam's most prominent B2B e-commerce players.

By late 2024, despite maintaining monthly revenue of $2.5-3M, Telio was losing roughly $1.4M per month and had exhausted its cash reserves. Unable to raise a follow-on round or find a buyout partner in a Vietnamese venture market that had sharply contracted since 2022, the company ceased operations in November 2024 and was officially dissolved the following month, laying off approximately 400 employees including an engineering team based in India.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a high-volume, thin-margin logistics and distribution business (B2B grocery) that required sustained access to growth capital to reach profitability — a model especially exposed when late-stage funding dried up.
- Continued operating and scaling revenue even as unit economics stayed underwater, rather than restructuring toward profitability earlier when the funding environment first tightened in 2022-2023.
- Could not secure a strategic buyer or bridge financing in time, unlike some peers that found acquirers — the absence of an exit option turned a cash crunch directly into a full shutdown.
- Founder pivoted almost immediately after the shutdown into an unrelated venture (bootstrapping an AAA game development studio), illustrating a clean break rather than an attempted internal pivot of Telio itself.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Telio's collapse illustrates that revenue scale alone does not protect a capital-intensive logistics/distribution startup once external financing disappears — a company can be generating millions in monthly revenue and still die from cash-flow mechanics if its unit economics were never fixed. It's a specific warning for B2B distribution and logistics plays in emerging markets: these models often require continuous capital injections to fund inventory, credit, and last-mile delivery infrastructure, making them unusually vulnerable to funding-market contractions compared to asset-light software businesses.
