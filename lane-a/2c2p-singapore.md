## One-liner

A Myanmar-born programmer's failed Bangkok mobile-games startup pivoted into payment security, relocated to Singapore to chase institutional backing, and nearly two decades later was bought by Ant Group in one of Southeast Asia's largest fintech exits.

## What happened

Aung Kyaw Moe, a Myanmar-born computer programmer who had settled in Bangkok, founded 2C2P in Thailand in 2003 originally to build mobile games — but the mobile-game market wasn't there yet, and the company had to find another way to survive. Aung, a trained programmer with an MBA from Bangkok's Sasin Graduate School of Business, redirected the company toward payment security instead, building a product that let banks secure credit-card transactions, and licensed it first to Thai banks and then progressively across the region.

The company relocated its headquarters to Singapore in 2007, and by 2009 had moved fully to seek support from the Singapore government and access a deeper pool of venture investors — a deliberate trade of its Bangkok origin story for institutional credibility and capital, which brought in backers including Amun Capital, GMO Venture Partners and Digital Media Partners starting with its first seed investment that year. Over the following decade 2C2P built out a full payment-gateway platform spanning Southeast Asia, expanding its merchant network and payment-method coverage across markets including Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore.

That build-out culminated in April 2022, when Ant Group — Alibaba's financial affiliate — acquired a majority, controlling stake in 2C2P in a deal DealStreetAsia valued at more than $590 million, one of the largest fintech exits in the region. As part of the deal, 2C2P's merchant network was connected to Ant's Alipay+ cross-border payment service, extending 2C2P's roughly 250 existing local payment options with additional e-wallets and cross-border rails. Aung Kyaw Moe remained with the company for several more years before stepping down as CEO in April 2025 after 22 years, handing the Group CEO role to Worachat Luxkanalode.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pivoting from mobile games to payment security (2003).** The original product had no market yet; redirecting the same technical team toward a real, immediate bank need (transaction security) is what kept the company alive past its first year.
- **Relocating headquarters from Bangkok to Singapore (2007-2009).** Moving before the company had regional scale, specifically to access Singapore government support and a deeper venture-investor base, was a bet that institutional access mattered more than staying close to its founding market — a bet that paid off in its 2009 seed round and later funding.
- **Selling majority control to Ant Group rather than staying independent or pursuing an IPO (April 2022).** Taking a strategic acquirer's capital and distribution (Alipay+ integration) over continued independence converted two decades of regional payment-infrastructure building into one of Southeast Asia's largest fintech exits.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

2C2P is a pattern recognizable to founders who launch into the wrong market first: **the original idea (mobile games) failing isn't necessarily fatal if the underlying capability (payment-security engineering) can be redirected toward a need that already has paying customers.** It's also a case study in trading founding-market roots for institutional access early — relocating to Singapore in 2007, years before the company had outgrown Bangkok, was a deliberate structural bet on capital access that shaped everything that followed, including the eventual Ant Group exit.
