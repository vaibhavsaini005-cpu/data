## One-liner

SoftBank Ventures-backed Propzy, Vietnam's best-funded proptech startup with over $37M raised, wound down entirely in September 2022 after a 50%+ staff cut and a business-model restructuring failed to secure fresh capital.

## What happened

Propzy built a real-estate transaction platform in Vietnam, closing a $25M Series A in 2020 led by Gaw Capital and SoftBank Ventures Asia — the largest funding round for a Vietnamese proptech company at the time. As the pandemic dragged on, the company laid off more than half its staff in mid-2022 and dissolved the legal entity tied to its direct-sales business as it attempted to restructure around a leaner model.

The restructuring wasn't enough. The founder cited the prolonged pandemic, global financial market instability, and macro shocks from the Russia-Ukraine war as contributing factors, but the critical failure was an inability to raise further funding in a tightening capital environment. Propzy ceased all operations on September 12, 2022, after six years.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Raised a record $25M Series A in 2020, scaling aggressively into a capital-intensive, asset-touching real estate model.
- Cut over 50% of staff and dissolved a legal entity mid-2022 as a first restructuring attempt rather than shutting down immediately.
- Ultimately could not convert the leaner cost base into a fundable story once the 2022 funding winter hit Southeast Asia broadly.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Propzy shows that a mid-restructuring pivot — cutting costs and narrowing scope — can still fail if it comes too late relative to the fundraising cycle. The company had already scaled on the assumption of continued capital access; when that access disappeared broadly across Vietnam's ecosystem (funding fell 82% in H1 2023 versus H1 2022), no amount of internal restructuring could substitute for the capital the model needed to operate.
