## One-liner

Partly, a Christchurch-founded startup that standardizes vehicle parts data for marketplaces like eBay and Shopify, went from a $1.7 million pre-seed in 2020 to a $21 million Series A in December 2022 and a $50 million round at a $500 million valuation by mid-2026 as it pushed into the US collision-repair market.

## What happened

Partly was founded in 2020 by Levi Fawcett, a former Rocket Lab engineer who had worked on hardware simulations, along with co-founders who saw that buying vehicle parts globally was bottlenecked by messy, non-standardized supplier and OEM data. Rather than building a consumer-facing marketplace itself, Partly built a backend data layer — a structured, standardized database of vehicle parts spanning 91% of vehicles from 58 manufacturers — that it licenses to existing platforms like eBay and Shopify, letting a buyer enter a license plate and get compatible parts automatically instead of relying on a human parts interpreter.

The company raised NZ$1.7 million in 2020 and a NZ$3.7 million pre-Series A in 2021 to prove the model, then closed a $21 million Series A in December 2022 led by Octopus Ventures, with participation from Shasta Ventures, Square Peg, Blackbird, Ten13, and individual backers including Figma CEO Dylan Field and Rocket Lab CEO Peter Beck. At that point Partly's database covered more than 50 million parts from over 20,000 suppliers and OEMs and was powering roughly $150 million in annual order volume, with about 50 staff and plans to double headcount through 2023. By June 2026, Partly had grown further, raising $50 million at a $500 million valuation and building "Interpreter," a foundation model trained specifically on vehicle-parts data (technical diagrams, damage photos, repair descriptions), as it opened a US operation in Austin, Texas to target the roughly $100 billion US collision-repair market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Selling infrastructure, not a marketplace**: By building the data layer that powers other companies' existing marketplaces (eBay, Shopify) instead of competing head-on with a new consumer marketplace, Partly avoided the cold-start problem of having to bootstrap both buyers and sellers itself.
- **Founder credibility from Rocket Lab**: Levi Fawcett's background at Rocket Lab — itself a marquee New Zealand deep-tech success story already in this dataset — helped attract backers like Rocket Lab CEO Peter Beck as an individual investor, reinforcing Partly's credibility in a hardware-adjacent, unglamorous B2B category.
- **Staged, metrics-first fundraising into a foundation-model pivot**: Partly didn't jump straight to an AI narrative — it built years of structured parts data first, then repositioned that data asset as training fuel for a purpose-built foundation model ("Interpreter") once large language model tooling matured, using its data moat to justify entering the far larger and more competitive US market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Partly shows how a small-market (New Zealand) startup can win a global, unglamorous B2B data category by focusing on infrastructure rather than a flashy consumer product — and how a genuinely proprietary, hard-to-replicate dataset (standardized vehicle parts across dozens of manufacturers) becomes even more valuable once it can be repurposed as training data for an AI model. It's also a pattern seen elsewhere in NZ's startup scene: deep technical founders (often with aerospace/hardware backgrounds, as with Rocket Lab alumni) building picks-and-shovels businesses that scale globally from a small home market.
