## One-liner

Ho Chi Minh City proptech Homebase, which raised $30M to help Vietnamese buyers get on the property ladder via rent-to-own and mortgage assistance, pivoted into HR tech as the country's real estate market stayed stagnant.

## What happened

Homebase, founded by JunYuan Tan and Phillip An, launched in Vietnam to tackle a specific affordability problem: banks were reluctant to issue mortgages to first-time buyers with thin credit histories, so Homebase built rent-to-own and co-investment products that let buyers move in while building equity over time. The model attracted institutional backing — a $30M raise led by Partech in November 2021 — on the thesis that Vietnam's urbanizing middle class needed alternative paths to homeownership.

That thesis ran into a multi-year real estate downturn. Vietnam's property market tightened sharply from 2022 onward as credit controls, bond-market jitters, and stalled project approvals froze transaction volume nationwide. With fewer homes moving and financing partners pulling back, Homebase's core rent-to-own unit economics stopped working. By early 2025, the company's founder had redirected the team and technology toward an HR tech product, a sector with faster sales cycles and less dependence on real estate transaction volume.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Raising into a single-country, single-asset-class bet**: The $30M Series A was sized for aggressive expansion of a model that depended entirely on Vietnam's housing transaction volume staying healthy.
- **Reading the downturn as structural, not cyclical**: Rather than waiting out the property slump, the founding team reallocated the company's engineering and operations capacity to a new market.
- **Adjacent-market pivot over shutdown**: HR tech reuses some of the same enterprise sales and ops muscle Homebase built serving property developers and corporate landlords, letting the company preserve its team instead of winding down.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Homebase is a case study in single-market proptech's exposure to macro real estate cycles: a well-funded, well-designed affordability product can be undone entirely by a national credit tightening it has no control over. It also illustrates a common Southeast Asian startup survival pattern — pivoting into a structurally different but adjacent vertical (HR tech) rather than either forcing the original model through a dead market or shutting down outright, preserving team and infrastructure investment even as the original value proposition became unviable.
