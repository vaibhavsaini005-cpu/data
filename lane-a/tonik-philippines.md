## One-liner

Tonik launched in February 2021 as the Philippines' first standalone digital bank — holding BSP Digital Banking License No. 001 — and by Q1 2026 became the first such institution in the country to achieve sustained profitability, validating a credit-led strategy at a time most neobanks worldwide remained loss-making.

## What happened

Tonik Digital Bank was incorporated and launched in the Philippines in February 2021, targeting the roughly 70% of adult Filipinos who were unbanked or underserved by traditional lenders. CEO Greg Krasnov, a serial fintech entrepreneur, had previously built a consumer lending platform in Vietnam, and brought that credit-first philosophy to Tonik from day one.

In June 2021, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) granted Tonik Digital Banking License No. 001 — the first standalone digital banking license ever issued in the Philippines. Within one month of its March 2021 public launch, Tonik had collected over 1 billion pesos (~US$20M) in retail deposits, a record for any new Philippine bank.

Where most neobanks globally competed on payment features and user-growth metrics — chasing scale before revenue — Tonik deliberately built a credit-led institution. Krasnov's reasoning: "Revenue on a loan client is 20x higher than on a payment client." Tonik focused on personal loans, store installment financing, and employer-channel lending, funding those loans with retail deposits gathered at competitive interest rates through its app.

By April 2026, the results were measurable: Tonik's loan portfolio stood at $110 million (2.3x year-over-year growth), its annualized revenue run-rate exceeded $60M (99% from lending), and its net interest margin and lending return of 51% and 25% respectively were the highest in Philippine banking. Its loan-to-deposit ratio of 82% was the highest among Philippine digital banks. In Q1 2026, Tonik generated positive cash net income — becoming the first standalone Philippine digital bank to achieve sustained profitability.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Credit-led from launch (2021)**: Deliberately choosing lending over payments as the core product avoided the unit-economics trap that crippled most global neobanks (high CAC, low ARPU, years to profitability). Tonik's deposits gathered to fund loans, not as a standalone product.
- **BSP license first (2021)**: Securing License No. 001 gave Tonik the full regulatory infrastructure to offer deposits, make loans, and operate as a real bank — not a payments startup wearing a bank's clothing.
- **Employer-channel lending**: Partnering with employers to offer salary-deduction loans to their staff created a low-default, captive lending channel — reducing credit risk compared to open-market consumer lending.
- **Deposit rates as a growth lever**: Offering market-leading deposit rates through the app attracted retail savers who became the funding base for the loan book — a self-reinforcing flywheel.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tonik is the rare Southeast Asian neobank case where the conventional wisdom ("grow users first, monetize later") was explicitly rejected and replaced with "profitable unit economics first, then scale." The Philippines' large unbanked population created a market where credit inclusion, not payment disruption, was the actual gap. Tonik's Q1 2026 profitability milestone — while global peers in Europe and Latin America still burned cash at scale — validates the credit-led model for emerging markets where formal lending penetration is structurally low.
