## One-liner

Moka built Indonesia's leading mobile point-of-sale platform for 40,000 restaurants and shops across 200 cities, then was acquired by Gojek for ~$130M in April 2020 — a strategic exit that turned an independent POS company into the backbone of Gojek's offline merchant ecosystem.

## What happened

Moka was founded in Jakarta in 2014 as a mobile POS application targeting the underserved small-and-medium enterprise (SME) segment in Indonesia. Traditional cash registers were expensive and siloed; Moka ran on an iPad or Android tablet and bundled inventory management, sales reporting, bookkeeping, and digital payment acceptance into a single subscription. By 2020 it had 40,000 merchant clients operating across 200 Indonesian cities — a footprint spanning coffee shops, restaurants, and retail outlets.

The company raised funding from East Ventures, Sequoia Capital India (now Peak XV), and others. Its positioning was similar to Square in the US — using hardware-light software to digitize the cash transaction layer — but tailored to Indonesia's fragmented, cash-heavy retail environment.

Discussions with Gojek reportedly began in 2019. In April 2020 — in the opening weeks of Indonesia's COVID-19 lockdowns — Gojek formally announced the acquisition for approximately $130M. The timing was deliberate: with offline commerce collapsing, Gojek needed a way to pull its 500,000+ GoFood restaurant partners online faster. Moka's POS infrastructure, which already sat in those merchants' premises, was the missing link.

Post-acquisition, Moka retained its brand and operated as an independent entity within the Gojek ecosystem. The integration enabled Gojek's GoFood delivery orders to flow directly into the Moka POS, closing the offline-to-online gap without requiring merchants to adopt an entirely new system.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **iPad-first POS in 2014**: Betting on tablet hardware that was still premium in Indonesia meant Moka targeted slightly more established SMEs (coffee shops, mid-size restaurants) rather than street-level warungs — a focus that made its merchant base high-value enough to attract Gojek's interest.
- **Integration with payment rails**: Adding GoPay and other digital payment acceptance to the POS system made Moka a node in Gojek's financial ecosystem before the acquisition, lowering integration risk.
- **COVID-19 accelerator**: The pandemic compressed Gojek's timeline for acquiring offline merchant infrastructure — a deal that might have taken another year of negotiation closed in weeks as both sides saw the urgency of connecting offline supply (merchants) with online demand (delivery orders).
- **Independent brand retention post-acquisition**: Moka continued operating under its own brand, preserving merchant relationships and trust rather than forcing an abrupt rebrand into Gojek's product suite.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Moka's exit is an example of a "strategic acqui-hire at scale": Gojek wasn't primarily buying the team or the technology but the installed base of 40,000 merchants and the trust relationships that came with it. In Southeast Asian super-app competition, controlling the offline merchant touchpoint is a structural advantage — whoever owns the POS terminal owns the transaction data and the upsell surface. Moka's acquisition gave Gojek a hardware-presence at the point of sale that pure software integrations could not replicate quickly.
