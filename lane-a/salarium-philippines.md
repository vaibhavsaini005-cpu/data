## One-liner

Philippine payroll and HR fintech Salarium suspended operations "indefinitely" on December 31, 2023, after months of customer complaints culminated in frozen funds, an unexplained system incident, and the deletion of client data.

## What happened

Salarium built payroll, HR, and disbursement software for Philippine businesses, including SALPay, an e-wallet and salary-disbursement facility used by employers to pay staff. Starting around August 2023, clients began complaining on social media that they could no longer access funds held in the platform, and SALPay intermittently stopped processing disbursements. The company gave no clear public explanation over the following months.

On December 18, 2023, an unspecified "incident" further disrupted the system. Rather than resolving it, Salarium announced on December 31, 2023 that it was suspending operations indefinitely, with no timeline for restoring service. The company said that, due to a lack of operational staff, all customer data held in its systems would be deleted and inaccessible during the shutdown — leaving businesses unable to retrieve payroll records or recover employee funds still sitting in the platform. Founder and CEO were reported to be unreachable through the company's own support channels, with users describing being "ghosted."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Silence over disclosure**: Salarium never publicly detailed the nature of the December 18 incident (technical failure, security breach, or insolvency), which deepened customer distrust rather than containing it.
- **Data deletion during shutdown**: Announcing that customer data would be deleted while operations were suspended converted a service outage into a full data-loss event for client businesses still owed payroll funds.
- **No wind-down plan for held funds**: Unlike an orderly shutdown with a payout timeline, Salarium's suspension left businesses and employees with money stuck in the system and no stated recovery process.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Salarium is a cautionary case for fintechs that combine software-as-a-service with custody of client money: when a payroll or e-wallet platform holds employer funds destined for employee salaries, an operational failure doesn't just interrupt a workflow — it strands wages. The prolonged period between the first customer complaints (mid-2023) and the eventual shutdown (Dec 2023–Jan 2024) also illustrates how silence and slow-drip service degradation can be more damaging to trust than a clean, fast failure would have been.
