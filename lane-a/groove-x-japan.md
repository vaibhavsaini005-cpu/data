## One-liner

GROOVE X, the SoftBank-Pepper-alumni robotics startup behind the companion robot Lovot, never reached profitability on venture and state-fund capital — so in 2022 its state-backed investor exited entirely and ZOZO founder Yusaku Maezawa's fund bought out the whole company.

## What happened

GROOVE X was founded in 2015 by Kaname Hayashi, a former SoftBank Robotics engineer who had worked on the humanoid robot Pepper. The company built Lovot ("love" + "robot"), a wheeled, penguin-shaped companion robot with no practical utility functions — no chores, no assistant tasks — designed purely to simulate affection and companionship, unveiled to the Japanese market in 2019. GROOVE X raised roughly ¥8 billion (~$67.6M) across multiple funding rounds from investors including Toyota Motor Corporation and the state-backed fund INCJ (Innovation Network Corporation of Japan).

By 2022, GROOVE X was still not profitable. On March 15, 2022, INCJ sold its entire stake to a fund operated by Yusaku Maezawa, the ZOZOTOWN founder and billionaire best known for chartering a SpaceX flight around the Moon. INCJ declined to disclose the sale price. Maezawa's fund completed acquisition of 100% of GROOVE X's outstanding shares the following month, April 2022, taking the company fully private under his personal ownership rather than a traditional VC or strategic-acquirer exit. Maezawa said he was drawn to the idea that a robot with no practical function could still "make people feel happy," particularly given pandemic-era isolation.

GROOVE X's own financial disclosures for the fiscal year ending October 2023 show the pivot in ownership did not immediately produce profitability: net loss narrowed slightly from ¥2.73 billion to ¥2.68 billion, against an accumulated deficit of ¥8.55 billion, even as the company reported sales growth of over 300% year-on-year — a company with real commercial traction that still could not fund itself without a single deep-pocketed backer.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **State fund as early backer (INCJ)**: Japan's government-linked innovation fund backing a consumer hardware bet on emotional companionship robots reflects Japan's broader industrial-policy push into robotics, not typical VC return-seeking.
- **INCJ's full exit (March 2022)**: Rather than continue funding an unprofitable seven-year-old hardware startup, the state fund cashed out completely, effectively signaling the traditional funding path had run its course.
- **Single-owner buyout instead of IPO or trade sale**: Maezawa's fund taking 100% ownership is an unusual exit structure — not an acquisition by a strategic competitor or a public listing, but a wealthy individual patron absorbing the whole company.
- **Growth without profitability persisting post-acquisition**: FY2023 results (300%+ revenue growth, yet still an ¥2.68B annual loss) show the new ownership structure bought time and capital, not an immediate fix to the unit economics.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GROOVE X illustrates a distinct Japanese exit pattern for capital-intensive consumer hardware startups that don't fit a normal VC-return timeline: state-linked funds provide patient early capital, then exit to a single ultra-wealthy individual willing to run the company as a passion project rather than force an IPO or distressed sale. It's a useful counterpoint to the more common Silicon Valley "profitability or shutdown" binary — GROOVE X kept scaling revenue and building product (LOVOT 3.0 shipped in 2024) years after its official backers gave up on a conventional return.
