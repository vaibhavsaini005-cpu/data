## One-liner

Japan's Zaif exchange lost ~¥7 billion (~$60M) in a September 2018 hack, forcing operator Tech Bureau to sell a majority stake to Fisco and ultimately shut down entirely.

## What happened

Tech Bureau Corp, an Osaka-based company, operated the Zaif cryptocurrency exchange. On September 14, 2018, between 17:00 and 19:00 local time, attackers gained unauthorised access to Zaif's hot wallets and drained 5,966 Bitcoin, an undisclosed amount of Monacoin, and Bitcoin Cash. The breach went undetected for three days. Total losses were estimated at ¥6.7 billion (~$60M), split between ¥4.5B in customer funds and ¥2.2B in company assets.

The hack was only the second major crypto-exchange breach in Japan after Coincheck lost $530M in NEM tokens earlier in 2018. Japan's Financial Services Agency (FSA) had already issued Tech Bureau a business-improvement order months prior, citing weak internal controls.

Fisco Digital Assets Group, a publicly listed Japanese firm, agreed to inject ¥5 billion and acquire a majority of Tech Bureau stock to fund customer compensation. Zaif's exchange business was transferred to Fisco. Tech Bureau subsequently wound down operations entirely after completing the restitution.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pre-existing regulatory red flags ignored**: The FSA had flagged Tech Bureau's controls before the hack; the company did not act with sufficient urgency.
- **Delayed detection (3 days)**: The gap between the breach (Sep 14) and discovery (Sep 17) allowed maximum asset drainage and complicated forensics.
- **Forced acquisition as lifeline**: Without Fisco's ¥5B bailout, customer compensation would have been impossible, highlighting how thinly capitalised crypto exchanges remained their own systemic risk.
- **Operator shutdown**: Unlike Coincheck (which survived and was acquired by Monex), Tech Bureau itself ceased operations — the exchange lived on under Fisco but the original entity did not.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Zaif is a canonical example of the "regulatory warning → inaction → catastrophic breach" arc common in early crypto exchanges. Japan's FSA subsequently tightened its Virtual Currency Exchange Act requirements and mandated cold-wallet segregation standards. The episode illustrates how small-to-mid-tier exchanges that survive early regulatory scrutiny on paper can still fail catastrophically when internal security culture lags behind licence compliance.
