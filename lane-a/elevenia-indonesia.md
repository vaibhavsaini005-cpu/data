## One-liner

Elevenia, once Indonesia's third-largest e-commerce marketplace, quietly shut down on December 1, 2022, after losing a decade-long cash-burn war to Tokopedia, Shopee, and Lazada.

## What happened

Elevenia launched in 2014 as a joint venture between Indonesian telecom operator XL Axiata and South Korean e-commerce firm SK Planet (PT XL Planet), riding SK Planet's experience running Korea's 11st marketplace. At launch it quickly became one of Indonesia's largest online shopping platforms by sales volume, competing directly with Tokopedia and the newly-arriving Lazada.

The venture's ownership shifted twice: XL Axiata and SK Planet exited their stakes in August 2017, and Indonesian conglomerate Salim Group acquired the platform later that year. Despite the change in backing, Elevenia could not keep pace once Shopee entered the market and, together with Tokopedia and Lazada, escalated an aggressive cash-burning subsidy war for buyers and sellers. Elevenia's market share and relevance steadily eroded through the late 2010s. The company ultimately shut down its services on December 1, 2022, announcing the closure quietly via its own website rather than through social media or a press push — a low-key ending for a platform that had once ranked among Indonesia's top three marketplaces.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Ownership churn without a clear strategic anchor**: two ownership changes in three years (SK Planet/XL Axiata exit, then Salim Group) left the platform without the kind of sustained, patient capital that Tokopedia and Shopee's backers provided.
- **Failure to match subsidy intensity**: Indonesia's e-commerce market from 2016 onward was won largely through capital-intensive subsidies and logistics investment; Elevenia's owners did not match the burn rate of Shopee's Sea Group or Alibaba-backed Lazada.
- **Quiet wind-down instead of a sale or pivot**: unlike some peers that pivoted format or sought acquisition, Elevenia simply ceased operations, suggesting no buyer was found for the remaining business.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Elevenia is a textbook casualty of Southeast Asia's marketplace subsidy wars: being an early, credible player was not sufficient once better-capitalized regional platforms (Shopee, Lazada) and a strong local incumbent (Tokopedia) normalized loss-leading pricing and free shipping as the cost of customer acquisition. It also illustrates a distribution-plus-conglomerate JV model (telco + e-commerce specialist) that can provide an early user base but struggles to survive when true competition requires multi-year capital commitments larger than either parent is willing to keep funding.
