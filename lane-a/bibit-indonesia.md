## One-liner

Bibit, a mutual-fund and bond robo-advisor app launched in Indonesia in 2019 by investment platform Stockbit, grew from its first funding rounds to more than 8 million active users, with first-time and millennial investors making up around 90% of its base, by raising $30 million in 2021 and $80 million led by Singapore's GIC in 2022.

## What happened

Bibit was launched in 2019 by Stockbit, an Indonesian stock-trading and investment community platform, to solve a specific access problem: most Indonesians had never invested because opening a mutual fund or bond account was paperwork-heavy and confusing, so Bibit built a robo-advisory product that asks users a short risk-profile questionnaire and then auto-allocates their money across mutual funds and government bonds. The product found strong product-market fit with first-time retail investors — CEO Sigit Kouwagam reported more than 1 million new first-time investors registered on the app in the year leading up to January 2021 — and Bibit raised a $30 million round that month with Sequoia Capital India joining existing backers East Ventures, EV Growth, AC Ventures and 500 Startups. Growth continued through 2021, and in May 2022 Bibit closed a larger round of more than $80 million led by Singapore's sovereign wealth fund GIC, with participation from Prosus Ventures and existing investors, to expand its product and user base further. By the mid-2020s, Bibit and its parent Stockbit together reported more than 8 million active users, with millennials and first-time investors continuing to make up roughly 90% of Bibit's base — evidence the platform succeeded at its original goal of pulling a first-time-investor audience into formal capital markets rather than just capturing existing sophisticated traders.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building a robo-advisor specifically for first-time, non-expert investors rather than competing for existing active traders**: this positioning let Bibit tap an almost entirely unaddressed segment of the Indonesian population instead of fighting incumbents for a smaller pool of already-active investors.
- **Operating as a product under an existing platform (Stockbit) rather than a fully standalone company**: this gave Bibit distribution and infrastructure support from Stockbit's existing trading-community user base while it built out its own product.
- **Raising a large, sovereign-wealth-fund-led round (GIC, 2022) at a point of user-growth momentum rather than earlier**: sequencing the largest raise after demonstrating strong first-time-investor adoption (1M+ new users in the prior year) let Bibit raise on proven traction rather than speculative growth projections.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Bibit is a straightforward financial-inclusion success story: a simplified, questionnaire-driven robo-advisor converted a largely non-investing population into active mutual-fund and bond holders at meaningful scale, validated by follow-on institutional capital from a sovereign wealth fund. The pattern — strip a complex financial product down to its simplest usable form, target the segment with zero existing habit rather than the segment already served by incumbents — is a repeatable playbook for wealthtech expansion into other underserved Southeast Asian retail-investment markets.
