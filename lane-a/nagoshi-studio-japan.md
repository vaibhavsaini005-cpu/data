## One-liner

Nagoshi Studio co-founder and director Daisuke Sato quietly confirmed the Tokyo studio's closure by updating his public bio to "ex-Nagoshi Studio, Inc." on June 17, 2026, three months after sole backer NetEase decided not to fund the additional $44.4 million needed to finish the studio's debut title.

## What happened

Toshihiro Nagoshi, creator of Sega's Yakuza franchise, founded Nagoshi Studio in Tokyo with NetEase as its sole financial backer, working on a debut title, Gang of Dragon — an original action game starring actor Ma Dong-seok and set in Tokyo's Kabukicho district, teased at The Game Awards in December 2025. Employees were told on March 6, 2026 that NetEase had decided to stop funding the studio from May, after concluding that completing Gang of Dragon would require roughly ¥7 billion (about $44.4 million) more than NetEase was willing to commit. Neither NetEase nor the studio issued a public statement after that. By May 2026 the studio's website had gone offline and its YouTube channel had been deleted; its Tokyo office was later listed as permanently closed on Google Maps. On June 17, 2026, co-founder and director Daisuke Sato updated his social media bio to "ex-Nagoshi Studio, Inc.," and Nagoshi himself appeared the same day in a Famitsu industry feature with no studio affiliation listed — the closest thing to a confirmation either founder gave, with no formal cancellation ever announced.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **NetEase's March 6, 2026 decision to stop funding from May**, after judging the extra ¥7 billion ($44.4M) needed to finish Gang of Dragon not worth committing, cutting off the studio's only source of capital.
- **The studio's choice to go dark rather than announce anything** — no press release, an offline website, and a deleted YouTube channel, rather than a public wind-down statement.
- **Daisuke Sato's June 17, 2026 bio change to "ex-Nagoshi Studio, Inc."**, the first and only concrete confirmation from a named studio principal that the studio had ended, coinciding with Nagoshi's own studio-less byline in Famitsu the same day.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Nagoshi Studio shows how exposed even a celebrated, founder-led creative studio can be when it depends on a single foreign publisher for 100% of its capital: once NetEase's own cost-cutting in gaming made the incremental $44.4 million not worth it, the studio had no fallback financing and no leverage, despite Nagoshi's pedigree. It's also a pattern worth naming on its own — the "silent unwind," where a studio's end is never formally announced but has to be pieced together from a founder's bio edit, a dead website, and a magazine byline, rather than from any press statement.
