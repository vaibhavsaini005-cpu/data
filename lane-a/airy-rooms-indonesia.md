## One-liner

Airy Rooms, a Traveloka-affiliated Indonesian budget-hotel aggregator that had built a network of roughly 2,000 properties since its 2015 founding, became the first Indonesian hospitality startup to permanently shut down in the COVID-19 pandemic, closing for good at the end of May 2020.

## What happened

Founded in 2015, Airy Rooms built a network of around 2,000 budget hotel properties across Indonesia, totaling roughly 30,000 rooms, using the same aggregator-and-standardization model that budget hotel chains like OYO used elsewhere in Asia — partnering with independent hoteliers rather than owning property. The company was affiliated with Traveloka, Indonesia's dominant online travel platform. When COVID-19 travel restrictions hit Indonesia in early 2020, Airy's occupancy collapsed, especially in tourist-heavy destinations like Bali. On May 8, 2020, The Jakarta Post reported the company was closing permanently, citing what CEO Louis Alfonso Kodoatie described as a "significant technical decline and a reduction in human resources," after Airy had already cut roughly 70% of its staff. DealStreetAsia's May 15, 2020 follow-up confirmed Airy would stop serving all of its hotel partners after May 31, 2020, making it the first Indonesian hospitality startup to cease operations entirely during the pandemic, rather than following peers into furloughs, layoffs, or pay cuts while continuing to operate.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Choosing full shutdown over managed contraction**: while competitors responded to the pandemic with furloughs and pay cuts, Airy's leadership chose to wind the business down completely rather than attempt to survive at reduced scale, a decision the company framed as a response to how deep its occupancy and staffing losses already were.
- **Depending on tourist-destination occupancy**: heavy reliance on markets like Bali left Airy disproportionately exposed once international and domestic leisure travel stopped almost entirely, compared to hospitality models less tied to tourism-specific demand.
- **Operating as an asset-light aggregator in a demand shock**: the aggregator model that let Airy scale quickly during growth (no property ownership, fast partner onboarding) offered no cushion — with no owned real estate or long-term contracts, there was no floor under revenue when partner properties simply stopped taking guests.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Airy Rooms illustrates how an asset-light marketplace model, which is often what makes a hospitality startup fast to scale, can also make it fast to unwind when demand disappears completely: there's no owned inventory or fixed contracts to fall back on, only partner relationships that dissolve the moment underlying demand does. Being "first to shut down" rather than first to furlough is also a useful signal of how thin the operating margins already were before the external shock — the pandemic exposed a fragility that likely predated it, rather than creating it from scratch.
