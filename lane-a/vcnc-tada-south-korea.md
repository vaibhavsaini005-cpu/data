## One-liner

VCNC built Between, one of Asia's most popular couples apps, then was acquired by car-sharing giant SoCar in 2018 and pivoted hard into ride-hailing with Tada — enduring taxi-union battles and regulatory threats before being acquired a second time by Toss in 2021.

## What happened

VCNC was founded in 2011 and launched Between, a private social app designed exclusively for couples to share photos, notes, and memories. Between spread rapidly across South Korea and internationally in Asia, accumulating millions of couples-users. The app was one of the first successful examples of a hyper-niche social network — a vertical social product that worked because of its specificity rather than despite it.

In July 2018, Lee Jae-woong, CEO of Korean car-sharing platform SoCar, acquired VCNC. SoCar's rationale was to use VCNC's engineering team and product culture to build a ride-hailing offering that could challenge KakaoTaxi, which had come to dominate the Korean market. VCNC launched Tada in October 2018 — not as a taxi service, but as a licensed 11-seat van rental service, which at the time existed in a regulatory grey zone outside the taxi industry's jurisdiction.

Tada's van-based approach proved popular with users seeking a premium, silent, professional ride experience. However, Korean taxi unions mounted fierce protest campaigns and lobbied for the service to be outlawed. In early 2020, the Korean government passed the Passenger Transport Service Act amendment that effectively made Tada's model illegal, forcing VCNC to discontinue its van service and pivot again — this time to a licensed taxi brokerage model.

In October 2021, Viva Republica (the company behind Toss, South Korea's fintech super-app) announced an MOU to acquire a 60% stake in VCNC from SoCar, integrating Tada's ride-hailing capabilities into Toss's broader financial super-app strategy. In 2023, Tada began merger proceedings with i.M, another ride-hailing app, aimed at creating South Korea's largest van platform.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Between → ride-hailing pivot (2018):** A couples-app company reinventing itself as a mobility platform is unusual; SoCar's acquisition was the forcing function rather than VCNC's own strategic choice.
- **Van-service regulatory arbitrage (2018–2020):** Using licensed van rentals to avoid taxi regulations worked as a wedge but invited regulatory backlash that ultimately destroyed the product model.
- **Toss acquisition (2021):** Positioning Tada inside a fintech super-app mirrors the regional pattern of mobility being bundled into broader financial services (Grab, Gojek), suggesting the end state for ride-hailing in Asia is as a feature, not a standalone business.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

VCNC/Tada illustrates the regulatory ceiling that confronts ride-hailing disruptors in heavily incumbency-protected markets like South Korea's taxi industry. It also shows that a successful product (Between) built an engineering culture capable of entirely different adjacent bets. The company's double acquisition arc — SoCar then Toss — reflects a broader Asian pattern where strong product teams become assets that larger platforms absorb to accelerate their own roadmaps rather than letting the product compete standalone.
