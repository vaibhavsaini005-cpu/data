## One-liner

Manila-based fintech Salmon, which operates a licensed bank in the Philippines, completed a $30M Series A-2 round in October 2024 led by the International Finance Corporation and Abu Dhabi's Lunate, targeting more than 50 million Filipino consumers underserved by legacy banks.

## What happened

Salmon built consumer lending and financial services products through a licensed banking operation in the Philippines, directly addressing the country's severe underbanking problem — the company explicitly targets over 50 million Filipino consumers who lack adequate access to legacy banking services. Operating as a licensed bank rather than an unregulated lending app gave Salmon regulatory legitimacy and product breadth unavailable to fintechs operating purely through partnership or lending-license structures.

The October 2024 Series A-2 round, which brought the company's cumulative Series A funding (across an earlier $25M Series A-1 extension and this $30M Series A-2) into a substantially oversubscribed multi-tranche raise, saw the International Finance Corporation (IFC) and Abu Dhabi's Lunate both commit amounts exceeding their pro-rata allocation rights — a signal of unusually strong investor conviction beyond standard follow-on participation. The round also included ADQ (Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth fund) and Singapore-based private equity fund Northstar Group in the earlier tranche, reflecting substantial Gulf and Southeast Asian institutional capital interest in Philippine financial inclusion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Pursued and obtained a full banking license rather than operating as a lending-app fintech under a partner bank, gaining broader product and regulatory latitude at the cost of a more complex, slower path to launch.
- Structured its Series A as multiple oversubscribed tranches (A-1 extension, then A-2) rather than a single large round, allowing investor demand to compound across rounds and signal escalating conviction to the market.
- Attracted development-finance institution capital (IFC) alongside sovereign wealth fund investment (Lunate, ADQ) — a blend of impact-oriented and purely financial capital sources — reflecting the dual investment thesis of financial inclusion impact and commercial returns.
- Explicitly targeted the underbanked segment (50 million+ Filipinos) as its core addressable market rather than competing for the smaller, already-banked population, aligning product design with the country's largest unmet financial-services need.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Salmon demonstrates that pursuing a full banking license, despite the added regulatory complexity, can become a durable competitive advantage for fintechs targeting large underbanked populations in emerging Southeast Asian markets — the license itself becomes a moat that lending-app-only competitors can't easily replicate. The oversubscribed, multi-tranche funding structure and blended development-finance/sovereign-wealth investor base also show how financial-inclusion fintechs in large underbanked markets can access both impact-oriented and purely commercial capital pools simultaneously when the addressable market size and regulatory positioning are strong enough.
