## One-liner

Christchurch-founded AI meeting-notes startup Contented raised NZD $4.1m to
push into the UK and US after co-founders Hannah Hardy-Jones and Lucy Pink
grew the company to real revenue on their own first.

## What happened

Contented, founded in 2023 by Hannah Hardy-Jones and Lucy Pink, builds an AI
tool that captures conversations from meetings, video calls and in-person
discussions and turns them into documentation and business insights via
customisable templates. Rather than raising early, the co-founders grew the
company from a handful of customers to over NZD $1.4m in annual recurring
revenue and 200+ customers globally (spanning SMEs, wealth management,
healthcare, government and enterprise) before taking outside capital. In
February 2026 they closed an oversubscribed NZD $4.1m seed round led by
Altered Capital, with Shearwater Capital (Australia), K1W1, Icehouse
Ventures, Aspire NZ Seed Fund, Phase One Ventures and Exhort Ventures also
participating.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Hardy-Jones and Pink chose to bootstrap Contented to meaningful recurring
  revenue and a global customer base before running a raise, rather than
  raising on an early prototype.
- Both founders had already run their own companies before Contented (Pink
  founded ethical-fashion startup Onyou and is a self-taught developer;
  Hardy-Jones founded mental-health app The Kite Program), which shaped the
  decision to prioritise proven traction over an early capital cushion.
- With the round closed, the founders are using it specifically to expand
  headcount (from ~5 to ~15 people) and push into the UK and US markets,
  rather than deepening the New Zealand/Australia base first.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A small-market (NZ) B2B AI startup used demonstrated revenue and customer
traction, not a pitch deck, as its leverage in fundraising — and only then
used the capital to internationalise. It's a concrete, recent counter-example
to "raise early, grow into it" playbooks common in AI SaaS.
