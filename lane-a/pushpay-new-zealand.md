## One-liner

New Zealand-founded Pushpay built a dominant giving and church management platform serving over 14,000 US and Australian churches, went public on the NZX in 2016, grew to NZ$300 million in annual revenue, and was taken private in May 2023 by US firm Sixth Street Partners and Australian PE firm BGH Capital for NZ$1.6 billion — one of New Zealand's largest tech exits.

## What happened

Pushpay was co-founded in Auckland in 2011 by Chris Heaslip and Eliot Crowther, initially as a general-purpose digital payment platform. The founders pivoted quickly to the US faith-sector market after discovering that churches — which rely on congregational giving but were still passing physical collection plates — were ideal customers for a recurring-donation digital platform. The niche turned out to be far larger than it appeared from New Zealand: the US church economy is enormous, and a software-first giving platform with donor management tools, messaging, and CRM-like features had no dominant incumbent.

Pushpay listed on the NZX in 2016 (ticker: PPH) and also secured an ASX listing, giving it access to capital markets in both countries while maintaining its NZ headquarters. The company grew by acquiring Church Community Builder (CCB) in 2019 for $87.5 million, adding comprehensive church management software to its giving platform — a strategic product expansion that increased switching costs significantly. Revenue grew consistently, reaching NZ$238 million in FY2022 (March year-end), with strong US dollar denominaton because virtually all revenue came from US and Australian faith communities.

By 2022 Pushpay had attracted attention from private equity. Negotiations began with Sixth Street Partners (a US alternatives firm with $75B AUM) and BGH Capital (an Australian PE firm) who together held ~20% of Pushpay shares. After an initial bid was rejected by Pushpay's board as undervaluing the company, an increased offer of NZ$1.42 per share — implying ~NZ$1.6 billion total — was accepted. Shareholders voted 85% in favour on 27 April 2023, the High Court granted final orders on 8 May 2023, trading ceased on 10 May 2023, and the scheme was implemented on 19 May 2023. Pushpay was simultaneously delisted from both the NZX and ASX.

## Key decisions / turning points

- The 2011 pivot from general payments to faith-sector giving was the foundational bet that unlocked a large, underpenetrated, and sticky niche with very low customer acquisition cost relative to lifetime value (churches rarely churn software vendors).
- The CCB acquisition in 2019 transformed Pushpay from a payment processor into a full church management platform — meaningfully raising switching costs and average revenue per customer.
- The dual NZX/ASX listing gave a New Zealand company access to capital on two exchanges while its revenue was almost entirely USD-denominated — useful for reducing currency mismatch risk in capital raises.
- The board's rejection of the initial PE bid and extraction of a higher final price (achieved through shareholder pressure and an independent adviser report) added materially to the exit value for New Zealand retail investors who held the stock.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Pushpay is one of the clearest examples of vertical SaaS done right from a small-market starting point: founders who identified a massive underserved niche (US faith sector giving), built defensible product around it (recurring giving + management software), and grew to a billion-dollar exit without ever being a consumer-facing brand known outside their sector. The New Zealand exit pathway — NZX listing → institutional PE take-private — is a template other NZ tech founders have studied closely as an alternative to the US IPO route. The $1.6B exit is one of the largest in New Zealand tech history.
