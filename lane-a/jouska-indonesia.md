## One-liner
Jouska built a following as a social-media financial advisory brand for young Indonesian investors, then collapsed in 2020 when regulators found it was managing client money without a license.

## What happened
Jouska marketed itself on Instagram and Twitter as an educational financial-planning service for millennials, but in practice it also directed clients' brokerage accounts and traded stocks on their behalf. Clients said Jouska concentrated their portfolios in low-quality, thinly-traded stocks — most notably PT Sentral Mitra Informatika (ticker LUCK) — and that losses on those positions exceeded 70-80%, with clients reporting they were discouraged or blocked from selling as the price fell. Indonesia's Financial Services Authority (OJK) investigated and found Jouska was registered only as an education-services company, not as a licensed investment manager or securities firm, meaning it had no legal basis to manage client funds or sell investment products at all.

## Key decisions / turning points
- Building the business on discretionary trading of client accounts while operating under an education-only registration, rather than obtaining an investment-manager license.
- Concentrating multiple clients' portfolios in the same illiquid small-cap stock, which amplified losses once the stock dropped.
- On July 24, 2020, OJK ordered Jouska and two affiliated firms (PT Mahesa Strategis Indonesia and PT Amarta Investa Indonesia) to cease operations and blocked their websites, apps, and social accounts.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)
A consumer-facing "financial education" brand quietly crossing into unlicensed portfolio management is a recurring failure mode in fintech-adjacent markets with fast-growing retail-investor bases and slower-moving regulators — the brand can scale on social media faster than a regulator can catch the underlying legal gap, but when it's caught the collapse is total and immediate (same-day shutdown, not a gradual wind-down).
