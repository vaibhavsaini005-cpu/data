## One-liner

Indonesian startup-news outlet DailySocial.id laid off its entire editorial staff in April 2024 and relaunched founder Rama Mamuaya's company as DiscoveryShift, an AI-driven business consultancy.

## What happened

DailySocial.id had operated for over a decade as one of Indonesia's best-known technology and startup-ecosystem media outlets, later also running an associated early-stage fund, DS/X Ventures. In late April 2024, CEO and founder Rama Mamuaya confirmed the company had terminated its entire staff as part of a "transition," while stating the news site itself would keep publishing content in some form rather than disappearing outright.

Within days, the restructuring was formalized as a full pivot: the company relaunched as DiscoveryShift, an AI-powered research and consultancy business helping corporations, startups, and SMEs identify AI opportunities and build AI capabilities internally. By the time of the relaunch, DiscoveryShift already listed 112 client organizations and said it was operating out of both Indonesia and Singapore, positioning the new venture as a regional consultancy rather than a domestic media property.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Full staff exit before relaunch**: rather than a gradual editorial wind-down, DailySocial terminated its entire workforce first, then announced the new business direction — a higher-risk sequencing that generated a period of ambiguity (and negative press) about whether the outlet was shutting down entirely.
- **Leveraging an existing brand/network for a different business**: founder Rama Mamuaya used a decade of relationships built through media coverage and DS/X Ventures to seed DiscoveryShift's initial client base (112 organizations) rather than starting cold in consultancy.
- **Regional over domestic framing**: positioning the new entity across Indonesia and Singapore from the outset signaled a deliberate move away from being a purely Indonesian media brand.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

DailySocial's pivot illustrates a specific mid-2020s pattern: legacy tech-media and content businesses facing structurally declining ad/subscription economics converting their accumulated industry relationships and credibility into paid AI-advisory services instead. The abrupt "lay off everyone, then reveal the new business" sequencing also shows how a pivot can be misread publicly as a shutdown if the new direction isn't communicated simultaneously with the layoffs.
