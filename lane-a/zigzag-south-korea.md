## One-liner

Zigzag, a women's fashion aggregation app built by Korean startup Croquis Inc., was acquired by Kakao Corp for $900 million in 2021 and folded into a new subsidiary, KakaoStyle — which then swung to an overall operating loss in 2023 from new-business spending before returning to profit in 2024.

## What happened

Croquis Inc., founded in 2015 by CEO Seo Jung-hoon (also reported as Seo Sang-yong in some Korean coverage), built Zigzag into Korea's leading fashion-discovery app for women in their 20s, aggregating listings from thousands of small independent shopping-mall sellers rather than holding inventory itself. By the end of 2020 the app had more than 3 million monthly users and Croquis reported roughly $35 million in revenue. On April 15, 2021, Kakao Corp acquired Croquis for $900 million as part of a strategy to build out its commerce business against Naver and eBay Korea, merging its existing Kakao Commerce style division with Croquis to launch a new subsidiary, KakaoStyle, on July 1, 2021 — with Seo Jung-hoon staying on as CEO of the combined entity. The transition was not immediately smooth financially: KakaoStyle posted an overall operating loss of about 19.8 billion won in 2023, driven largely by investment in new businesses such as its secondhand-fashion service Posty, even as the core Zigzag platform itself turned an operating profit that year for the first time since 2019 after a cost-structure overhaul. By 2024, group-level performance caught up — KakaoStyle posted a 5.8 billion won operating profit for the year, widening from 2.2 billion won in the prior year, alongside continued user growth (Zigzag's monthly active users were up 38.1% year-over-year as of March 2025).

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Selling to a platform giant instead of staying independent**: founder Seo Jung-hoon took the $900 million Kakao acquisition in 2021 rather than continuing to scale Croquis independently, trading founder control for distribution and capital inside Kakao's commerce ecosystem.
- **Investing in new adjacent businesses (like Posty) while the core app was still stabilizing margins**: this widened KakaoStyle's group-level losses in 2023 even as the flagship Zigzag product itself became profitable — a deliberate bet on future growth lines over near-term consolidated profitability.
- **Running a cost-structure overhaul at the product level (Zigzag) in parallel with group-level expansion spending**: the split outcome — Zigzag profitable in 2023, KakaoStyle overall not — shows the company managed the core asset and the new-bets portfolio on different timelines rather than freezing growth spend to hit a single P&L number.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Zigzag is a case of an independent-startup-to-platform-subsidiary pivot that looks messy in the near term (a large parent-level loss two years after a headline nine-figure acquisition) but resolves into a coherent growth story once the timelines are disaggregated: the core acquired product reached sustainable profitability on its own cost structure before the parent's broader bet-driven spending caught up. For founders evaluating an acquisition versus staying independent, and for observers reading a subsidiary's post-acquisition financials, the lesson is that a consolidated loss figure can mask a healthy core business subsidizing earlier-stage bets — the two need to be read separately to judge whether the acquisition is working.
