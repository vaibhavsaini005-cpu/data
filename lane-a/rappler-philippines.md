## One-liner

Co-founder and CEO Maria Ressa chose to keep Rappler operating and fight a 2018 SEC shutdown order through the courts rather than fold, and on 23 July 2024 the Court of Appeals ruled the order "illegal" and ordered Rappler's licence restored — a win the Philippine Supreme Court made final in early 2026.

## What happened

The Philippine SEC first moved to revoke Rappler's certificate of incorporation in 2018, alleging the news site had violated constitutional restrictions on foreign ownership of media through a 2015 investment structure involving depositary rights sold to the US-based Omidyar Network (Omidyar later transferred its stake to local managers specifically to remove that exposure). The SEC affirmed the shutdown order on 28-29 June 2022, in the final days of the Duterte administration. Rather than comply, Ressa kept Rappler publishing throughout the multi-year appeal, alongside separate cyberlibel and tax cases against her personally. On 23 July 2024, the Court of Appeals ruled the SEC had acted with "grave abuse of discretion" and ordered Rappler's certificate of incorporation restored; the decision was released to media on 9 August 2024, when Ressa told a press conference in the Rappler newsroom, "It's a day of celebration, it's a day of hope," while acknowledging the parallel legal risk to her personally: "I'm not going to let it stop me from doing my job." The SEC continued to contest the ruling, but the Court of Appeals rejected its motion for reconsideration in July 2025, and the Philippine Supreme Court closed the case entirely in early 2026 after the Solicitor General withdrew its final challenge.

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2018-2022: Ressa opts to keep Rappler operating and litigate the SEC's shutdown order rather than shut down or restructure around it.
- 23 July 2024 (released 9 August 2024): Court of Appeals overturns the SEC order and restores Rappler's licence; Ressa publicly frames it as vindication rather than relief and states the parallel personal legal risk won't change how she runs the newsroom.
- 2025-2026: Court of Appeals rejects the SEC's reconsideration bid; Supreme Court closes the underlying case for good.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Rappler's survival wasn't a product decision or a funding decision — it was a founder choosing to keep publishing through a multi-year existential legal threat instead of settling, restructuring ownership defensively, or winding down preemptively. That choice only paid off because the underlying legal challenge was eventually found to have no merit; it's a specific, dated instance of the difference between a company that survives regulatory pressure by outlasting a bad-faith order versus one that survives by capitulating to it.
