## One-liner

Japan's largest fashion marketplace abandoned its flagship ZOZOSUIT custom-fit initiative after a costly failed pivot, then sold founder Yusaku Maezawa's controlling stake to SoftBank's Yahoo Japan.

## What happened

ZOZO Inc (formerly Start Today) runs ZOZOtown, Japan's dominant online fashion platform. In late 2017, founder Yusaku Maezawa announced ZOZOSUIT — a Lycra bodysuit covered in markers that users would photograph to generate precise body measurements, enabling a made-to-measure clothing line sold under the ZOZO brand. Over 1 million suits were shipped free globally in 2018.

The concept collapsed in execution. The measurement app was unreliable on many devices; custom orders were slow, expensive, and plagued by fit errors. The ZOZO-brand clothing line generated only modest revenue. By August 2019 the company quietly wound down all custom-fit operations, recording a write-down of roughly ¥7.7 billion (~$73M).

Simultaneously, Maezawa — who had been publicly distracted by his MoonShot art project and SpaceX moon trip plans — sold his 37.4% stake in ZOZO to SoftBank Group for approximately ¥400 billion, giving SoftBank control. Yahoo Japan (a SoftBank entity) formally absorbed ZOZO into its e-commerce portfolio. ZOZO continued operating ZOZOtown under new ownership but without its founder's vision or the custom-fit experiment.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **ZOZOSUIT go-big-or-go-home bet**: Shipping 1M+ suits for free was a massive CAC gamble. The measurement tech was not production-ready when the campaign launched.
- **Founder distraction**: Maezawa's public moonshot project and lavish spending (buying a Basquiat for $110M) signalled to investors that ZOZO was not his primary focus.
- **Clean exit**: Selling to SoftBank/Yahoo Japan preserved ZOZOtown's market position while eliminating the drag of the failed custom-clothing unit.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

ZOZO is a case study in the "moon-shoot" company-within-a-company failing while the core remains healthy. Hardware-dependent measurement tools in fashion have repeatedly struggled with consumer behavior variance (lighting, pose, device camera). The failure mirrors other body-measurement startups (e.g., True&Co, MTailor) that found the cold-start problem severe. The pivot here was founder exit + operational refocus, not technology reinvention.
