## One-liner

Lunit, a Seoul-based medical-imaging AI company co-founded in 2013, went public on South Korea's KOSDAQ in July 2022, surging 29.87% on debut, and followed with a $150M secondary raise in late 2023 as its cancer-detection AI scaled to 600+ healthcare sites in 40+ countries.

## What happened

Lunit was co-founded in 2013 in Seoul, building AI models to analyze medical imaging for cancer detection and treatment response prediction, under CEO Brandon Suh. Its two flagship product lines are Lunit INSIGHT (AI analysis of chest X-rays and mammograms for cancer/abnormality screening) and Lunit SCOPE (an AI biomarker platform predicting immunotherapy response from tissue data). The company built out FDA clearances and partnerships with major imaging OEMs including GE Healthcare, Philips, and FujiFilm, plus a biomarker partnership with Guardant Health, while investing over half its headcount — including more than 12 full-time medical doctors — into R&D.

On July 21, 2022, Lunit listed on KOSDAQ (ticker A32813), pricing 1,215,800 shares at KRW 30,000 (~$23) and closing its first day up 29.87% at the KRW 40,000 upper-limit price (~$30.50), raising roughly KRW 36.4 billion (~$28M) earmarked for further AI R&D and global expansion. By March 2022 (pre-IPO), Lunit INSIGHT was already deployed across roughly 600 medical facilities in more than 40 countries, processing around 4 million chest X-rays a year in South Korea alone. The company returned to capital markets in November 2023 with a secondary offering that drew over 100% investor subscription, raising $150M in new capital to fund continued global scale-up.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Betting on regulatory-grade clinical AI over a consumer or B2B-lite model**: Lunit invested heavily in FDA clearances and clinical-grade validation from early on, a slower and more expensive path than shipping a lighter diagnostic tool, but one that opened enterprise partnerships with GE, Philips, and FujiFilm.
- **Going public in Seoul rather than staying private or pursuing a US listing**: The KOSDAQ IPO gave Lunit non-dilutive-feeling growth capital and public-market visibility in its home market, and the listing's strong debut validated the model to future partners and customers.
- **Following the IPO with a second capital raise rather than resting on the listing**: The November 2023 $150M secondary offering, oversubscribed, shows Lunit treating the IPO as a funding milestone within a longer scale-up rather than an exit event.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lunit demonstrates that a deep-tech, regulation-heavy AI company can reach a public-market success outcome from a non-US base by combining clinical rigor (FDA clearances, physician-staffed R&D) with distribution partnerships instead of trying to go direct-to-hospital alone. The oversubscribed follow-on raise a year after IPO is the more telling signal than the debut pop itself — it shows sustained investor conviction once the market could see real deployment numbers (600+ sites, 40+ countries) rather than just a listing-day story. For healthtech founders outside the US, it's a concrete counter-example to the assumption that regulatory-grade medical AI requires a US-centric go-to-market to scale.
