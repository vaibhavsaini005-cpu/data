## One-liner

Facing a tightening funding environment, Indonesian e-commerce enabler Sirclo cut 8% of its roughly 1,371-person workforce in November 2022 and refocused its business units toward larger enterprise clients rather than long-tail MSMEs.

## What happened

Sirclo built its business helping Indonesian merchants — from small MSMEs to large brands — set up and run online stores, spanning website-building tools, omnichannel commerce, and enterprise e-commerce enablement services. As Southeast Asia's 2022 funding winter set in, the company undertook a restructuring that cut about 160 roles, or 8% of staff.

Founder and CEO Brian Marshal framed the move publicly as a deliberate strategic reset rather than a pure cost-cutting exercise, saying Sirclo had gone through "a series of internal evaluations" and would make "significant changes, especially in the aspect of business focus, to ensure the sustainability of the company." The restructuring touched all of SIRCLO Group's business lines, with the company signaling it would concentrate more of its resources on serving large-scale and enterprise clients — a higher-margin, more defensible segment than the fragmented MSME tier it had also historically served.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Layoffs paired with an explicit strategy statement**: Sirclo's leadership used the November 2022 restructuring announcement to publicly commit to a business-focus shift, rather than presenting the cuts as isolated cost management.
- **Weighting enterprise over MSME**: Serving thousands of small merchants generates volume but thin, hard-to-collect margins in a downturn; enterprise clients offered more predictable, larger contracts — a segment-prioritization call typical of e-commerce-enablement startups once cheap growth capital dries up.
- **Timing relative to the funding cycle**: The cut came as Southeast Asian venture funding contracted sharply through 2022, making runway extension via headcount reduction a precondition for pursuing the longer, slower enterprise sales motion.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sirclo's move illustrates a common pivot pattern among e-commerce-enablement and SaaS-for-SMB companies when funding tightens: retreat from the long tail of small, low-margin customers toward a smaller number of larger, stickier accounts. This trades near-term revenue diversity for unit economics that don't depend on continuous cheap capital to subsidize acquisition and support costs across thousands of small merchants. For founders serving both SMB and enterprise tiers, a downturn is often the forcing function that finally makes explicit which segment the business can actually sustain on its own margins.
