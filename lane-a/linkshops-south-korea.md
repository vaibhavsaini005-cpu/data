## One-liner

Once considered a potential unicorn, Korean e-commerce startup Linkshops declared bankruptcy in March 2024 after burning through ₩15.4 billion (~$11.4M) in funding, squeezed between falling post-pandemic demand and cheaper Chinese competitors.

## What happened

Linkshops was a South Korean e-commerce startup that had drawn investment from a group of backers including US venture capital firm Altos Ventures Management, raising a total of ₩15.4 billion (~$11.4M) and building a reputation as one of the country's future-unicorn candidates. In March 2024, the company ceased operations and filed for bankruptcy.

According to KED Global's reporting, Linkshops' collapse was driven by two compounding pressures: e-commerce demand cooling off sharply from its pandemic-era peak, and intensifying price competition from large-scale Chinese e-commerce platforms offering steep discounts to Korean consumers. Linkshops was cited as one of several Korean e-commerce startups forced to shut down in this period, alongside a broader contraction in the sector — Series A funding for Korean e-commerce startups fell to roughly ₩30.6 billion between October 2023 and March 2024, about one-third of the total raised in the same window a year earlier, while the number of VC deals in the sector dropped from 22 to 9.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Scaling into a maturing category**: Linkshops built out its business assuming continuation of pandemic-level e-commerce growth rates, leaving it overexposed when demand normalized downward.
- **No defensible moat against price competition**: without a differentiated position (e.g. proprietary logistics, brand exclusivity, or a defensible niche), Linkshops was directly exposed to Chinese platforms competing on price alone — a fight capital-constrained domestic startups are poorly positioned to win.
- **Funding drought timing**: the shutdown landed exactly as Korean e-commerce VC funding cratered (down roughly two-thirds year over year), cutting off the option to raise a bridge round through the downturn.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Linkshops is a textbook case of a category-wide macro shift (Chinese cross-border e-commerce platforms undercutting domestic players on price) turning a "future unicorn" narrative into a bankruptcy within about a year. It illustrates why growth-stage e-commerce startups need a cost or differentiation moat that survives a price war, not just execution speed during a demand upswing — and why "unicorn candidate" framing in press coverage is a lagging signal, not a predictor of resilience once market conditions reverse.
