## One-liner

Aruna digitized Indonesia's fragmented seafood supply chain — connecting 50,000+ fishermen directly to domestic restaurants and international buyers — and raised a $35 M Series B in 2022 to vertically integrate the logistics and processing layer.

## What happened

Indonesia is the world's second-largest fishing nation, yet its fishermen historically earned as little as 15–20 % of the final consumer price, with the remainder absorbed by a multi-tier system of middlemen, transporters, and wholesalers.

Aruna was founded in 2016 by Farid Naufal Aslam, Indraka Fadhlillah, and Utari Octavianty with a simple premise: eliminate as many intermediary layers as possible using a mobile marketplace and direct-to-buyer logistics. The platform registers fishermen, provides them with a price-discovery tool and buyer connections, arranges chilled/frozen logistics, and increasingly handles export paperwork for international buyers.

By 2022 the company had onboarded more than 50,000 fishing households across 22 Indonesian provinces, processed over 11,000 tonnes of seafood, and attracted institutional attention. In January 2022 Aruna closed a $35 M Series B led by East Ventures Growth Fund, Vertex Ventures SEA, SBI Investment, and Prosus Ventures, among others. The round funded expansion into processing infrastructure (cold-storage and filleting facilities) and working-capital financing products for fishermen.

## Key decisions / turning points

1. **Supply-side first** — Rather than building demand before supply, the team registered and educated fishermen first, establishing trust that became a durable moat. Demand from restaurants and exporters followed once supply quality was consistent.
2. **Embedded financial services** — Offering purchase-order financing and short-term working-capital loans to fishermen gave Aruna revenue diversification and deepened fisherman stickiness beyond the marketplace itself.
3. **Vertical integration via processing** — Acquiring and building processing facilities shifted Aruna from a pure marketplace (thin margins) to a value-added processor and exporter (higher margins, quality control).
4. **Export orientation** — Targeting international buyers (Japan, USA, EU) allowed Aruna to capture USD/EUR pricing while sourcing in IDR, creating a natural FX hedge.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Aruna is a textbook example of supply-chain digitization in a fragmented emerging-market primary industry. Its success hinges on a structural insight that is replicable: where information asymmetry depresses producer prices most, a digital coordinator that builds producer trust first — then sells aggregated, reliable supply to buyers — can capture durable margin. The embedded financial-services layer also illustrates how agriculture-adjacent marketplaces can monetize beyond transaction fees once they have producer trust.
