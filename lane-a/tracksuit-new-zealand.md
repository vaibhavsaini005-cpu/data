## One-liner

New Zealand-founded brand-tracking SaaS Tracksuit raised a $20.5M Series A at a $142.9M valuation in February 2024, then followed it with a $42M round in 2025 to fund US and UK expansion.

## What happened

Tracksuit, founded in 2021, built a subscription brand-tracking tool that surveys thousands of consumers per week so brands can monitor awareness, perception, and competitive positioning without commissioning expensive one-off market research studies of the kind traditionally sold by Kantar, Ipsos, Nielsen, and YouGov. By early 2024 the company was already tracking around 4,000 brands across New Zealand, Australia, the UK, Canada, and the US, and counted brands like Arnott's, GHD, and Bondi Sands as customers.

On February 28, 2024, Tracksuit announced a $20.5 million Series A led by Silicon Valley firms Altos Ventures and Footwork, with existing investors Blackbird, Icehouse Ventures, Ascential, and Shasta Ventures also participating — its second external raise, taking total funding to roughly $27.8 million and pushing its valuation to $142.9 million on about $9.5 million in annual recurring revenue. The company said the capital would fund further expansion into the US and UK markets. By 2025 Tracksuit had raised a further ~$42 million round to continue that international push.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Category creation over feature competition**: rather than compete head-on with incumbent research firms on methodology, Tracksuit positioned itself as an always-on, self-serve alternative aimed at marketing teams who previously went without continuous brand tracking at all.
- **Early international benchmarking**: tracking brands across five English-speaking markets (NZ, Australia, UK, Canada, US) from a relatively early stage gave it a multi-market data asset to sell into US/UK expansion.
- **Sequential fundraising tied to expansion milestones**: the Series A was explicitly earmarked for US/UK growth, and the subsequent 2025 round continued funding that same geographic expansion rather than a pivot in product.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tracksuit shows a New Zealand-headquartered B2B SaaS company using a "modernize a slow, expensive incumbent workflow" wedge (traditional brand-tracking research) to attract Silicon Valley Series A capital, rather than needing a US relocation to do so. It's a useful counterpoint to the more common narrative that ANZ-founded SaaS companies must redomicile to raise from top-tier US VCs.
