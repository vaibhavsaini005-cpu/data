## One-liner

Vietnamese restaurant-discovery pioneer Foody sold 82% of itself to Singapore's Sea Group in 2017, and its food-delivery arm Now was fully absorbed and renamed ShopeeFood in 2021, erasing the original independent brand.

## What happened

Foody Corporation launched in 2012 as one of Vietnam's first restaurant discovery and review platforms, later adding an in-app food ordering and delivery service under the Now brand. Garena (Sea's earlier name) backed Foody's Series B, and Tiger Global supplied Series C funding in mid-2015. In July 2017, Sea Ltd — then preparing for its NYSE listing — acquired an 82% stake in Foody for approximately $64 million, folding it into its growing Vietnamese consumer ecosystem alongside Shopee.

Sea ran Now as a semi-independent brand for four more years before deciding to fully unify its Vietnam consumer apps. On August 18, 2021, Now was renamed ShopeeFood, following the earlier rename of e-wallet AirPay to ShopeePay. CEO Dang Hoang Minh framed the move as building "a robust integrated ecosystem with Shopee and ShopeePay" rather than running Foody/Now as a standalone product. The original Foody name and app were discontinued as a consumer-facing brand.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Selling majority control to Sea (2017)** rather than staying independent or raising further venture rounds, trading autonomy for the resources of a regional platform.
- **Running Now as a separate brand for 4 years post-acquisition** before folding it in, suggesting Sea initially valued Foody's local brand equity before deciding full integration mattered more.
- **The 2021 rebrand to ShopeeFood** coincided with AirPay's rebrand to ShopeePay, a deliberate platform-wide unification move rather than a Foody-specific decision.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Foody's trajectory shows a common outcome for category-pioneer startups acquired by regional platform conglomerates: the acquired brand survives for years as a semi-independent unit before being absorbed once the acquirer decides ecosystem consistency outweighs preserving local brand recognition. The founders and original brand identity effectively exit even though the underlying product and market position continue under new ownership.
