## One-liner

Auckland deep-tech startup Atomic Tessellator, founded by former Google engineer Alain Richardt, raised $13 million in seed funding at a $50 million valuation to commercialize Vireon, an AI-designed, rare-earth-free permanent magnet material meant to break dependence on China's rare-earth supply chain.

## What happened

Alain Richardt, a former Google engineer, founded Atomic Tessellator to build a "digital laboratory" combining quantum physics simulation and AI to design new materials for defence, semiconductor and aerospace applications — compressing a materials-discovery process that traditionally takes years down to days, and doing with a six-person team work Richardt says would otherwise require roughly 30 scientists. The company's flagship result, a material called Vireon, is an AI-designed alternative to samarium (used in high-temperature permanent magnets) synthesized entirely from elements mined in Australia, positioned to outperform existing rare-earth-based magnets while sidestepping China's dominance of the global rare-earth supply chain.

On April 17, 2026, Outset Ventures announced its participation in Atomic Tessellator's seed round; by the time NZ Herald covered the round on June 25, 2026, the total raised was reported at $13 million at a $50 million valuation, led by London-based Crane Venture Partners, with In-Q-Tel (the US intelligence community's venture arm), New Zealand's Icehouse Ventures, Global From Day One and Outset Ventures also participating. Richardt described the compression in R&D timelines as the company's core technical bet, and separately framed investor and customer interest in geopolitical terms: the rare-earth supply problem is one that "investors and clients recognise... is a problem that is at the national security level."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Applying AI/quantum-simulation to materials discovery specifically for rare-earth alternatives**, rather than a general-purpose materials-AI platform: Richardt targeted a narrow, high-value, geopolitically urgent problem (samarium-magnet substitution) as the company's first commercial proof point.
- **Sourcing Vireon's inputs exclusively from Australian-mined elements**: a specific supply-chain design choice aimed directly at reducing dependence on Chinese rare-earth processing, not just a materials-science choice.
- **Raising from a mix of a European VC (Crane), a US intelligence-linked fund (In-Q-Tel) and New Zealand VCs**: Richardt assembled investors whose interests span commercial deep-tech returns and national-security-adjacent supply chain resilience, widening the fundable narrative beyond a typical materials startup.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Atomic Tessellator shows a small deep-tech team using AI to compress a traditionally slow, capital-intensive R&D process (new materials discovery) into a fundable, fast-moving venture, and picking a first application (rare-earth-free magnets) chosen explicitly for its geopolitical salience rather than just its market size. Framing a technical breakthrough around supply-chain security — attracting a CIA-linked fund alongside conventional VCs — is a specific and repeatable positioning move for deep-tech founders in small countries competing for global-scale capital.
