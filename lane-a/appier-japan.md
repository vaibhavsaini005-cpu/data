## One-liner

Appier, a Tokyo-based AI company co-founded by Taiwanese engineers, listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in March 2021, raising $271 million and becoming the first Taiwanese company to IPO in Japan since Trend Micro in 1998.

## What happened

Appier was founded in 2012 by Chih-Han Yu, along with co-founders Ji-Ru Yu and Winnie Lee, all alumni of Harvard and MIT with AI research backgrounds. Initially focused on gaming AI (predicting player behaviour), the company pivoted early to marketing analytics — building AI tools that help brands acquire, engage, and convert customers across digital channels.

The company's four core products (CrossX for customer acquisition, AIQUA for engagement, AiDeal for purchase-intent prediction, and AIXON as a data science platform) attracted clients including Panasonic, Kao, Suntory, and hundreds of other enterprise brands across Asia.

Backed by Sequoia Capital India (via Sequoia Capital Global), LINE Corp, SoftBank Ventures Asia, and DeNA, Appier listed on the TSE Mothers Section (ticker: 4180) on March 30, 2021, at ¥1,600 per share. Shares surged 37% in early trading before closing up 19% at ¥1,900 on debut day. The IPO raised approximately ¥29.5 billion (~$271M).

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pivot from gaming AI to MarTech (2013–2014):** Recognising that enterprise marketing budgets were far larger than gaming, the team reoriented from B2C game AI to B2B marketing intelligence — a move that widened the total addressable market dramatically.
- **Japan as home base over Silicon Valley:** Yu deliberately chose Tokyo over the Bay Area, arguing that proximity to Japanese enterprise clients and a less crowded VC ecosystem let Appier build trust faster. The TSE listing reinforced this Japan-first identity.
- **Acquisitions to round out the stack:** Appier acquired Glasp (social media analytics) and Quintly before IPO, expanding the product portfolio to include social and data science capabilities.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Appier demonstrates that AI companies built in Asia — not just in the US — can reach public markets with enterprise-grade technology. The pivot from a narrow gaming AI niche to broad MarTech infrastructure is a textbook example of using an initial vertical as a proving ground before expanding to a larger adjacent market. The TSE listing also signals that Tokyo is a viable exit venue for Pan-Asian AI companies, not just a stepping stone to Nasdaq.
