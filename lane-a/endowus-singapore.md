## One-liner

Endowus built Singapore's first digital advisor licensed to invest CPF (mandatory retirement savings) and SRS funds, then used that regulatory edge to scale into a multi-hundred-million-dollar, fee-only wealth platform across Singapore and Hong Kong.

## What happened

Endowus launched as a fee-only robo-advisor distinguishing itself from competitors by being the first digital wealth platform approved to invest Singaporeans' CPF (Central Provident Fund) and SRS (Supplementary Retirement Scheme) monies — pools of money previously locked into a narrow set of low-yield, bank-distributed options. That regulatory access gave Endowus a defensible wedge into a mass-market savings pool competitors couldn't easily reach.

The company raised a Series A first close of S$23 million in June 2021, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners with SoftBank's participation, followed by further funding rounds. In August 2023, it raised an additional US$35 million from global banks UBS, MUFG, and Citi Ventures alongside existing investors and several Asian billionaire family backers, at a time when many wealthtech and fintech peers globally were cutting staff or shutting down amid the 2022-2023 funding downturn. The round funded expansion of Endowus's core Singapore and Hong Kong markets and cemented its position as one of the region's largest independent digital wealth advisors, with client assets later surpassing US$10 billion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Regulatory-access-as-moat**: Securing approval to manage CPF/SRS funds gave Endowus a distribution channel competitors without that license couldn't replicate, rather than competing purely on app UX or fees.
- **Fee-only positioning**: Explicitly avoiding commission-based revenue (common among bank-distributed funds in Singapore) built trust that helped attract institutional investors like UBS and Citi Ventures as backers, not just users.
- **Raising counter-cyclically**: Closing a US$35 million round in August 2023, during a period when global wealthtech funding had cooled sharply, signaled investor confidence that Endowus's unit economics and growth were real, not hype-driven.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Endowus shows how a regional fintech can win by finding a specific regulatory gap (locked-up retirement savings with poor default options) rather than competing head-on with global robo-advisors on generic features. Its ability to keep raising capital from strategic banking investors during a fintech funding drought is also a useful counter-example to the many wealthtech failures/pivots elsewhere in this dataset — disciplined, fee-only unit economics and a genuine distribution advantage can outlast a downturn that kills less-differentiated competitors.
