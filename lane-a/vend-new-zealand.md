## One-liner

Auckland-founded Vend built cloud-based point-of-sale software for independent retailers and was acquired by Lightspeed Commerce for C$350 million in 2021 — one of New Zealand's largest tech exits at the time.

## What happened

Vend was founded in 2010 by Vaughan Rowsell in Auckland, New Zealand, with the thesis that iPad-era cloud software could replace the clunky on-premise POS systems choking independent retailers. The product was simple: a browser-based POS with inventory management, customer loyalty, and reporting that synced across devices in real time. Vend was one of the first cloud POS systems globally and launched in an era when most retailers still ran Windows XP-era desktop software.

The company raised over NZ$100 million across multiple rounds — including investors like Valar Ventures (Peter Thiel's fund) and Square Peg Capital — and expanded from New Zealand to Australia, the UK, the US, and Canada. By 2020, Vend served more than 25,000 retailers in over 140 countries with ~300 employees. In March 2021, Montreal-based Lightspeed Commerce (TSX/NYSE: LSPD) acquired Vend for C$350 million (~NZ$380 million), integrating it into a growing suite of hospitality and retail commerce tools. The deal was Lightspeed's largest acquisition to that point and validated Vend's decade of profitable product-led growth in a sector few VCs considered "sexy."

## Key decisions / turning points

- Launching as one of the first cloud-native POS systems gave Vend a multi-year head start before Shopify, Square, and others entered retail POS seriously.
- Geographic expansion from NZ → AU → UK → US sequenced by English-language market familiarity minimized localization friction early on.
- Staying in the SMB/independent retailer segment (rather than chasing enterprise) kept churn manageable and allowed the team to iterate tightly on product-market fit.
- The Lightspeed acquisition came at a premium that reflected Vend's strong recurring-revenue base and Lightspeed's strategy to consolidate SMB commerce tooling.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Vend is a textbook example of a "boring vertical SaaS done well" exit: not a moonshot, not a category creator, but a durable product solving a real pain for a large underserved market. New Zealand's small domestic market forced Vend to be international from year two, which paradoxically accelerated product quality — they had to work across currencies, tax regimes, and payment networks far earlier than a US-focused startup would. The C$350M exit also illustrates that Pacific-region B2B SaaS can reach meaningful scale without being US-based, a signal that later influenced NZ/Australia's startup ecosystem funding appetite.
