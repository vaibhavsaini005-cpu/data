## One-liner

Singapore/Jakarta-based tech-news outlet Tech in Asia shut down its Bahasa Indonesia platform and cut 18% of staff in mid-2025, less than 18 months after being acquired by Singapore Press Holdings' SPH Media.

## What happened

Tech in Asia was founded on August 1, 2010 by Willis Wee (originally as Penn Olson) and grew into one of Southeast Asia's best-known tech and startup news outlets, backed early on by Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin (2015) and a $6.6M round led by Hanwha Investment and Securities (2017). It is headquartered across Singapore and Jakarta. In January 2024, the company was acquired by SPH Media, Singapore's national newspaper publisher.

On July 2, 2025, CEO Willis Wee announced the company would shut down its Bahasa Indonesia-language platform by July 15, 2025 and lay off approximately 18% of total staff, citing an unsustainable cost structure amid declining media ad revenues across Southeast Asia. Wee described the move as "cutting off an arm to save the body," stressing it did not reflect on the departing team's quality, and said affected employees would receive severance aligned with local regulations plus transition support. The company retained its English-language international edition covering Indonesia's tech sector and kept a local team for conference operations.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Sale to a legacy publisher (2024)**: Being folded into SPH Media gave Tech in Asia a larger corporate backer, but didn't insulate the Indonesian operation from cost pressure the following year.
- **Language-market retreat, not full exit**: Rather than closing entirely, leadership chose to preserve the English edition and event/conference business while cutting the highest-cost, most localized product line.
- **Public, named justification**: Wee's direct, on-record explanation ("cost structure isn't sustainable") is unusually transparent for a media pivot in the region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tech in Asia's retrenchment is a reminder that being acquired by a larger, well-capitalized parent doesn't guarantee a business unit survives intact — SPH Media's backing didn't prevent an 18% headcount cut 18 months later. It also illustrates a recurring Southeast Asian media pattern: pan-regional outlets scaling into multiple local-language editions during boom years, then retreating to a leaner, English-first core once ad revenue and startup-ecosystem coverage volume (their core subject matter) contracts.
