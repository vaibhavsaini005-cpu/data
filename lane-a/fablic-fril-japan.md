## One-liner

Japan's first C2C fashion marketplace app, FRIL, grew to 5 million downloads before being acquired by Rakuten in September 2016 as the company sought to compete with the rapidly rising Mercari.

## What happened

Fablic Inc. launched FRIL in September 2012, making it Japan's first consumer-to-consumer marketplace app focused exclusively on fashion and beauty. The app targeted women in their late teens and 20s and built its community before Mercari — which would go on to dominate the category — had even launched its own flea market product.

By July 2014, FRIL had surpassed 1.5 million downloads and was processing over $5 million in monthly transaction value, prompting Cookpad, Colopl, and Jafco to lead a $10 million funding round. By 2016 cumulative downloads had crossed 5 million.

In September 2016, Rakuten announced it was acquiring 100% of Fablic for an undisclosed sum, making it a wholly-owned subsidiary. Rakuten's motivation was to fold FRIL into its existing Rakuma platform to challenge Mercari's dominance in the domestic C2C market. The full corporate merger was completed in July 2018.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Fashion-only focus (2012):** Betting on a narrow, underserved vertical rather than horizontal flea-market positioning gave FRIL early credibility with its core demographic and allowed it to build community before Mercari arrived.
- **$10M raise from strategic investors (2014):** Bringing in Cookpad (food-tech) and Colopl (mobile gaming) rather than pure financial VCs signalled belief in network-effect businesses and gave Fablic operational credibility.
- **Rakuten acquisition (2016):** Rather than competing head-on with a better-capitalised Mercari, Fablic accepted Rakuten's offer, giving both parties an exit path — Rakuten got a proven user base, and Fablic's team got resources it couldn't have raised independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Fablic illustrates the "fast follower squeeze" dynamic common in Asian mobile markets: being first in a category is valuable only until a well-funded second mover (Mercari) enters. For early-stage marketplaces, an acquisition by a platform giant is often the rational outcome once the category leader establishes flywheel momentum. FRIL's story is also a reminder that vertical focus (fashion-only) can build defensible community, even if it ultimately caps total addressable market relative to a horizontal rival.
