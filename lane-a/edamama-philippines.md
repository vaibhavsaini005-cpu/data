## One-liner

Philippine parenting e-commerce platform edamama, co-founded by Bela Gupta D'Souza and Nishant D'Souza, closed a $35 million Series A+ round led by Ayala's ACTIVE Fund to fund a deliberate pivot from pure online retail into a nationwide offline store rollout.

## What happened

Bela Gupta D'Souza and Nishant D'Souza founded edamama in 2020 as an online-to-offline (O2O) platform for Filipino parents, combining e-commerce with community and content resources; by late 2023 it had delivered more than 3.5 million products to Filipino families. After piloting pop-up retail locations at Robinsons Magnolia and Robinsons Manila malls, the founders committed to a specific, capital-intensive next step: building a permanent, nationwide offline store network, starting with a store at Ayala Vertis North before the end of 2023 and further expansion planned nationwide the following year.

To fund that shift, edamama closed a Series A+ round of over $35 million (announced November 20-21, 2023), led by the Ayala Corporation Technology Innovation Venture (ACTIVE) Fund, with existing investors Kickstart Ventures, Gentree Fund and InnoVen Capital participating alongside new investor GS Group, a South Korean retail conglomerate. Co-founder Nishant D'Souza tied the capital directly to the offline pivot: "This funding will accelerate our offline roll-out and private label product development, providing even more value and accessibility to our customers wherever they choose to be."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Piloting offline pop-up stores before committing capital to permanent retail locations**: the founders tested the O2O thesis at Robinsons Magnolia and Robinsons Manila before scaling, rather than opening stores speculatively.
- **Raising a large ($35M) Series A+ specifically earmarked for offline expansion and private-label products**, rather than treating the online platform as the sole growth engine going forward.
- **Bringing in a strategic new investor (GS Group, a South Korean retail conglomerate) alongside existing VCs**: added retail-specific operating expertise and capital to a round explicitly built around a store-network build-out, not just general growth capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

edamama's raise shows a specific, sequenced approach to an online-to-offline pivot: validate the physical retail thesis with low-commitment pop-ups, then raise a large round explicitly earmarked for permanent stores and private-label products, and bring in a strategic retail investor rather than only financial VCs. For e-commerce startups in emerging markets where digital-only retail has structural limits (logistics costs, trust, discovery), this sequencing — pilot, then a dedicated capital raise, then scale — is a lower-risk way to fund a business-model expansion than committing to stores before proving offline demand.
