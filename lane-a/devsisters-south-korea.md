## One-liner

South Korean gaming studio that pivoted its decade-old Cookie Run casual runner into a global RPG franchise — Cookie Run: Kingdom — generating a fivefold revenue jump in 2021 after years of stagnation.

## What happened

Devsisters was founded in 2007 in Seoul and listed on KOSDAQ in 2013 (ticker 194480). The company's first major hit came when Cookie Run launched on Kakao's gaming platform in 2013, quickly becoming one of the most-downloaded games in Korea. The game was a side-scrolling endless runner with simple mechanics — accessible but limited in monetisation ceiling.

For several years after the initial Cookie Run success, Devsisters struggled to convert franchise momentum into a scalable business. Cookie Run: OvenBreak (2016, standalone global version) built an international fanbase but did not generate the revenue needed to sustain a growing studio. The core problem: the endless-runner genre had a hard ceiling on ARPU, and the Cookie Run universe had accumulated loyal fans without a deeper gameplay hook to monetise them.

The pivot decision was to rebuild the franchise from the ground up as an RPG city-builder. Cookie Run: Kingdom launched in South Korea in June 2019 for soft testing and globally on January 19, 2021. The RPG format — guilds, base-building, character collection — unlocked dramatically higher willingness to pay. By mid-2021 Cookie Run: Kingdom had reached No. 1 in the turn-based RPG category globally, surpassing Dragon Ball Z Dokkan Battle and Marvel Strike Force. The game accumulated 150 million+ downloads globally. Devsisters reported ₩369 billion (~$290M) in operating revenue in 2021, a fivefold increase over 2020, and the company's market cap temporarily reached over 2 trillion Korean won.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Genre reinvention, not sequel**: Devsisters did not make Cookie Run 2. They rebuilt the IP as a fundamentally different game genre (RPG vs. runner) targeting a higher-spending player type.
- **IP moat recognised early**: Before building Kingdom they invested in deepening the Cookie Run lore, character roster, and fan community — making the RPG launch feel like a reward to an existing fanbase rather than a new product.
- **Global-first day-one launch**: Cookie Run: Kingdom launched in 178 countries simultaneously rather than rolling out market by market. This created simultaneous social media momentum across English, Korean, and Japanese communities.
- **US market expansion Sept 2021**: A concentrated US push in Q3 2021 — including influencer campaigns and App Store featuring — drove a second revenue spike that sustained the 2021 annual figures.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Devsisters demonstrates a viable **franchise IP pivot**: when a casual game builds strong character IP but hits a genre monetisation ceiling, rebuilding the same universe in a higher-engagement genre can unlock a step-change in economics. The lesson is about recognising what you actually own — in Devsisters' case, beloved cookie characters, not a runner game mechanic — and pivoting to a format that monetises the owned asset better. The same pattern has played out globally (Angry Birds → Angry Birds 2/RPG/go-kart), but Devsisters executed it with unusually high fidelity to the original fanbase.
