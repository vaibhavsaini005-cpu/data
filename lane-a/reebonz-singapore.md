## One-liner

Singapore's once-celebrated luxury marketplace Reebonz filed for creditors' voluntary liquidation in September 2021, ending a 12-year run after accumulating S$65 million in liabilities, a failed Nasdaq stint, and mounting trust issues with sellers and buyers alike.

## What happened

Founded in 2009 by brothers Samuel Lim and Daniel Lim, Reebonz positioned itself as Southeast Asia's go-to destination for new and pre-owned luxury goods — handbags, watches, jewellery — targeting aspirational consumers in Singapore, Malaysia, Australia, and beyond. At its peak it claimed millions of registered users and had raised capital to fuel regional expansion.

In March 2019, Reebonz made the rare move of listing on the Nasdaq via an IPO, raising around US$23 million. The listing was short-lived: by August 2020 — just 17 months after listing — Reebonz was delisted after failing to maintain the minimum US$1 share price for more than 30 consecutive days.

Post-delisting, the company's troubles accelerated. Sellers on the platform began reporting unpaid proceeds, with outstanding debts to 11 sellers alone exceeding S$30,000 by August 2021. Buyers simultaneously complained of receiving counterfeit or substandard goods. In September 2021, co-founder Samuel Lim filed notices informing creditors that Reebonz "cannot by reason of its liabilities continue its business," initiating creditors' voluntary liquidation. Total reported liabilities came to approximately S$65 million, the majority owed to financial institutions.

In December 2021, LiveCommerceEntertainment (LCE) acquired the Reebonz.com brand assets and relaunched the website under a live-commerce format.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Nasdaq IPO (2019):** Going public on a US exchange for a Southeast Asian luxury resale platform was an unusual strategic bet; the capital raise was modest but the regulatory burden and investor scrutiny amplified every operational problem.
- **Authenticity failures:** Allowing counterfeit or misrepresented goods onto the platform destroyed the trust premium that a luxury marketplace lives or dies by — once buyers doubted authenticity, price compression and volume collapse followed.
- **Seller payment defaults (2021):** Withholding payouts to consignment sellers — the supply side of the marketplace — compounded the trust crisis and accelerated the public collapse.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Reebonz is a cautionary case for luxury resale platforms: the trust premium in this category is fragile and non-recoverable once lost. A marketplace that cannot guarantee authenticity or timely seller payouts will face supplier withdrawal before buyer churn, collapsing the inventory base that sustains any transaction volume. The premature public listing also illustrates the pressure that investor capital structures can place on a company that hasn't yet built sustainable unit economics.
