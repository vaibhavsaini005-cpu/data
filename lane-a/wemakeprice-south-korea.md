## One-liner

WeMakePrice, a South Korean e-commerce marketplace founded in 2010 that had raised a cumulative $486.65M, was declared bankrupt by the Seoul Bankruptcy Court in November 2025, roughly 16 months after a liquidity crisis at parent company Qoo10 Group left hundreds of thousands of buyers and sellers unpaid.

## What happened

WeMakePrice operated as an online marketplace connecting merchants with consumers in South Korea for over a decade before Singapore-headquartered Qoo10 Group acquired it in April 2023, adding it to a portfolio that already included rival marketplace TMON (acquired August 2022). Qoo10's aggressive acquisition strategy across multiple Korean e-commerce platforms concentrated financial risk under one parent company's balance sheet.

The crisis became public in early July 2024 when merchants on both WeMakePrice and TMON noticed delayed payments; Qoo10 initially attributed this to a payment-system glitch, but the problem persisted and both platforms filed for court receivership on July 29, 2024. The resulting damage was severe: delayed payments and refunds affected roughly 520,000 people — about 470,000 buyers and 56,000 sellers — with estimated losses around 1.5 trillion Korean won. WeMakePrice attempted to find a buyer or merger partner during rehabilitation proceedings but failed; the court terminated rehabilitation in September 2025, and the Seoul Bankruptcy Court formally declared the company bankrupt in November 2025, appointing a bankruptcy trustee to oversee asset liquidation and creditor claims.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Qoo10 Group's strategy of acquiring multiple competing Korean e-commerce platforms (TMON in 2022, WeMakePrice in 2023) concentrated liquidity risk across its portfolio, so that a cash crunch at the parent level cascaded into simultaneous failures at two previously separate, independently-operating businesses.
- Qoo10's initial public explanation (a "payment system glitch") for delayed merchant payments obscured the underlying liquidity crisis, delaying merchants' and regulators' recognition of the problem's true severity.
- The company pursued a merger-or-acquisition path during court receivership rather than an independent restructuring, but was unable to find a buyer willing to take on the business — a search failure that directly led the court to terminate rehabilitation.
- The South Korean government stepped in with a bailout/support fund for affected merchants rather than for the platforms themselves, reflecting a policy choice to protect the marketplace ecosystem's smaller participants over the failing platform operators.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

WeMakePrice's collapse illustrates how aggressive multi-platform acquisition strategies in e-commerce can create hidden systemic risk: when a parent holding company over-extends by acquiring several competing marketplaces, a liquidity crisis at the parent level can simultaneously take down what were previously separate, independently-viable businesses. It also demonstrates that in marketplace businesses, a payments-trust breakdown (delayed merchant payouts, delayed buyer refunds) can trigger an existential crisis far faster than typical operating losses — once merchants and buyers lose confidence in a platform's ability to pay them, no rehabilitation plan may be sufficient to restore that trust or attract a buyer.
