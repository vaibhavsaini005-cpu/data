## One-liner

Vietnam's homegrown e-commerce champion Tiki collapsed from a near-$1 billion valuation in 2021 to under $10 million by 2024, outgunned by Shopee and unable to pivot fast enough to content-driven commerce.

## What happened

Tiki was founded in 2010 by Vietnamese-American entrepreneur Son Tran as an Amazon-inspired online bookstore in Ho Chi Minh City. It diversified into electronics and general merchandise, raised $258 million from investors including JD.com, AIC Capital, and Mirae Asset, and reached a valuation approaching $1 billion in 2021. But the company could not close the gap on Shopee (backed by Sea Limited), which dominated Vietnam's e-commerce market through aggressive subsidies, a superior logistics network, and the rise of TikTok Shop's video-commerce format. Tiki's total revenue fell 7% in FY2022 while operating losses jumped 39%. Founder and CEO Tran Ngoc Thai Son resigned in November 2023. VNG Corporation, one of Vietnam's largest tech conglomerates and an early Tiki investor, exited its stake. By 2024, Tiki's implied valuation had dropped to under $10 million — a fall of more than 99% from its peak — stunning regional investors and marking one of Southeast Asia's starkest startup declines.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **JD.com partnership (2017)**: Strategic investment from China's JD.com brought logistics know-how but not enough capital to match Shopee's subsidy war.
- **$258M funding round (2021)**: Reached near-unicorn status but the capital was insufficient against a Shopee with virtually unlimited Sea/Tencent backing.
- **Failure to pivot to live commerce**: TikTok Shop's video-first model captured Vietnamese shoppers; Tiki's text-and-image catalogue format lost relevance rapidly.
- **CEO exit November 2023**: Founder Son stepped down as losses mounted, casting doubt on the planned overseas IPO that never materialised.
- **VNG exit (2024–2025)**: One of Vietnam's largest tech firms quietly exited, signalling the investor consensus that recovery was unlikely.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tiki's collapse illustrates the limits of geographic "local champion" moats in open Southeast Asian digital markets. When a well-capitalised platform giant (Shopee) enters with structural advantages — logistics, cross-border inventory, and a gaming/entertainment ecosystem — local incumbents that cannot match spending or innovate their commerce format face an existential squeeze. The sub-$10M implosion from a near-unicorn is one of the region's most dramatic valuation collapses.
