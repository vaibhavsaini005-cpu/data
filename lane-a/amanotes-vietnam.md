## One-liner

Amanotes, founded in Ho Chi Minh City in 2014, became the world's #1 music mobile game publisher by downloads — amassing 2.7 billion downloads globally — without raising significant VC capital, by running a high-volume, rapid-iteration portfolio publishing model in an underserved genre.

## What happened

Amanotes was founded in 2014 in Ho Chi Minh City. Its founders chose music-rhythm mobile games as a niche: at the time, app stores were crowded with generic puzzle and action games, but tap-rhythm and music experiences were thin. Rather than betting on a single prestige title, Amanotes built a factory model — publish many lightweight games quickly, measure organic retention, and double down on winners.

Its breakout app was Magic Tiles 3, a piano-tap game that climbed to the Top 20 Free Games globally on both iOS and Google Play. "Tiles Hop" became another chart-topper. From 2018 to 2022, Amanotes' total download portfolio grew at over 20% per year. In 2020 — turbocharged by COVID-19 stay-at-home behaviour — revenue grew 100% year-on-year. By late 2022 the company had crossed 2.7 billion total downloads, making it the most-downloaded Southeast Asian app publisher and the global #1 in the music game category.

Amanotes achieved this without a large external funding round, relying instead on ad revenue from its portfolio and reinvesting into new titles — a rarity in a region where startups typically raise aggressively.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2014** — Founded in Ho Chi Minh City; chose music/rhythm as a niche rather than competing in generic hyper-casual segments.
- **2018–2020** — Rapid-iteration publishing model yields multiple global chart-toppers; Magic Tiles 3 and Tiles Hop enter Top 20 free games globally.
- **2020** — Named among world's top mobile app publishers by download volume; revenue doubles on COVID-driven engagement spike.
- **2022** — 2.7B total downloads milestone; rebranded as an "interactive music company" to signal ambitions beyond passive tap-rhythm games into participatory music creation and social features.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Amanotes demonstrates that emerging-market studios can own a global genre niche without Western-scale VC funding. The key ingredients: niche focus (music games when no major publisher owned that space), portfolio breadth (many bets rather than one), low cost base (HCMC engineering talent), and organic growth over paid acquisition. It follows the playbook of French publishers Voodoo and Ketchapp — but from Vietnam, and in a music genre those companies overlooked.
