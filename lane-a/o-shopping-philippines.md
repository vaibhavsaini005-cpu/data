## One-liner

A home-shopping TV joint venture between ABS-CBN and South Korea's CJ ENM shut down entirely in November 2020 after years of losses, becoming one of several ABS-CBN-linked casualties of the network's 2020 franchise expiration and the pandemic.

## What happened

O Shopping launched on October 14, 2013 as ACJ O Shopping Corporation, a joint venture between Philippine media giant ABS-CBN Corporation and the commerce division of South Korea's CJ ENM. ABS-CBN put in an initial ₱200 million to seed the channel, which sold products via a dedicated pay-TV shopping format, later moving onto BEAM TV (2014–2018) and then a standalone channel on ABS-CBN TV Plus from July 2018. By 2016 the channel was generating roughly ₱2.2 million in daily sales (about ₱60 million monthly), but the business never became reliably profitable.

Two shocks in 2020 ended it. On April 21, 2020, O Shopping stopped broadcasting amid COVID-19 lockdowns. Then on May 5, 2020, its parent network ABS-CBN went off air after Congress failed to renew its broadcast franchise, cutting off O Shopping's main distribution platform. On July 8, 2020, ABS-CBN announced the venture would close by year's end, citing that "the past two years have been challenging" with mounting financial losses; CJ ENM opted to fully exit the business rather than restructure. O Shopping ceased all operations on November 1, 2020.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Distribution dependency**: O Shopping's entire sales model depended on ABS-CBN's broadcast platforms; when the franchise was denied, the venture had no independent distribution channel to fall back on.
- **Slow path to profitability**: Despite seven years of operation and measurable daily sales, the channel apparently never cleared a sustainable margin, making it an easy cut once its parent network was under existential pressure of its own.
- **Clean joint-venture wind-down**: Rather than one partner buying out the other, both ABS-CBN and CJ ENM chose full closure — a sign neither saw a viable path forward independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

O Shopping is a case study in platform-dependency risk for joint ventures built entirely on a partner's distribution infrastructure: the underlying TV-shopping business model wasn't independently doomed, but it had no way to survive its host network's regulatory collapse. It's also a reminder that cross-border JVs (Philippines + South Korea) can be unwound just as quickly as they're formed when the local partner's core business is disrupted by a factor — here, a government franchise decision — entirely outside the venture's control.
