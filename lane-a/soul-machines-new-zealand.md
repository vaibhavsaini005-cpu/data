## One-liner

Auckland-based Soul Machines built AI-powered "Digital People" for enterprise customer service, raised ~US$225 million from investors including Horizons Ventures, and collapsed into receivership on February 5, 2026 — owing at least NZ$19.6 million with only NZ$12 million cash left.

## What happened

Soul Machines was founded in 2016 in Auckland by Greg Cross (serial entrepreneur) and Dr. Mark Sagar (an Academy Award-winning visual effects scientist, former Weta Digital supervisor on Avatar and King Kong). The company's core technology was "Digital People" — emotionally responsive, AI-powered humanoid avatars intended to replace human agents in customer-facing roles: banking chatbots, healthcare virtual assistants, retail concierges. Early demos were striking; the avatars could express facial micro-emotions and respond to visual cues in real time.

Funding arrived quickly. An early Series A of US$7.5M came from Horizons Ventures (Li Ka-shing's family office), followed by a US$40M round in March 2021. In February 2023, the company raised US$70M in a Series B1 round, bringing total lifetime capital to approximately US$225M (NZ equivalent). Clients included large financial institutions, automotive brands, and health organizations.

The collapse came faster than the build. CEO Greg Cross departed in September 2023. Co-founder Dr. Mark Sagar resigned as a director in June 2024. The rise of commoditized generative AI (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini) severely undercut Soul Machines' value proposition: enterprise customers could now bolt a chat API onto their own interface at a fraction of the cost. Without a defensible moat, the company's high burn rate became fatal. KPMG was appointed as receiver on February 5, 2026. At that point, out of ~US$225M raised, only approximately NZ$12M (~US$7M) remained.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Proprietary "Biological AI" bet**: Soul Machines built custom neural simulation tech rather than adopting the emerging LLM stack; when LLMs commoditized the conversational layer, their differentiation became a technical curiosity rather than a market advantage.
- **Dual-founder departures**: Cross leaving (Sept 2023) and Sagar departing (June 2024) — both within ~9 months — removed the company's primary evangelists at exactly the moment when enterprise re-signing decisions depended on executive trust.
- **$225M raised, $12M left**: The ratio (95% of capital consumed) suggests a sustained high burn rate that was never matched by revenue, not a sudden external shock.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Soul Machines is a textbook example of the "demo ≠ business" failure mode in AI startups: the technology was visually compelling and press-friendly but required expensive customization per client and faced commoditization from below. It also illustrates the New Zealand deep-tech paradox — world-class research talent, strong early investment, but a small domestic market that forces reliance on enterprise deals in the US/Europe where sales cycles are long and decision-makers are conservative. The receivership, happening just as the broader generative AI gold rush accelerates, underscores that proximity to a hot category is not protective if the product is expensive and replaceable.
