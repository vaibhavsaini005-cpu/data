## One-liner

Beyeu.com, a Vietnamese e-commerce site for mother-and-baby products launched in 2013 alongside sister sites lamdieu.com and foreva.com, shut down in late 2015 after prolonged losses, despite operating in one of Vietnam's fastest-growing retail categories.

## What happened

Beyeu.com launched in 2013 as part of a trio of vertical e-commerce sites — alongside lamdieu.com (cosmetics/lingerie) and foreva.com — targeting Vietnam's children's products market, a segment estimated at roughly $2.5 billion a year in sales. The site sold toys, baby gear, and related products in a market where a handful of well-funded horizontal platforms (Tiki, Sendo, Lazada) and vertical specialists were competing hard for the same buyers.

By late 2015, Beyeu's backers pulled funding after sustained losses, and the site closed. Its own administrators summed up the failure bluntly at the time: "E-commerce requires lots of money" — a signal that the business had underestimated the capital intensity of customer acquisition and logistics in a category with thin margins and high fulfillment costs (diapers, formula, and toys are bulky, low-margin, and price-sensitive). Beyeu's closure was reported alongside two other Vietnamese e-commerce shutdowns from the same period, Deca.vn and Lingo, as part of a broader wave of consolidation in Vietnam's e-commerce sector even as overall online retail revenue kept growing (reported at $4.07 billion in 2015).

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Launching as a narrow vertical in a capital-intensive category**: Mother-and-baby products carry high logistics costs (bulky, fragile, needs cold/careful handling for some SKUs) and thin margins, making the category unusually expensive to scale compared to lighter verticals.
- **Running three sister sites in parallel** (Beyeu, lamdieu.com, foreva.com) split marketing and operational spend across multiple brands rather than concentrating capital behind one, at a time when larger horizontal players were already outspending most verticals on customer acquisition.
- **No pivot before shutdown**: Rather than narrowing scope or merging with a horizontal platform, the company wound down operations once losses became unsustainable.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Beyeu is a clean example of a category-correct, timing-correct startup that still failed on capital structure: it targeted a genuinely large and growing market (Vietnam's children's products category) but underestimated how much sustained capital a logistics-heavy vertical e-commerce play needs to outlast horizontal competitors with deeper pockets. It's also a useful counterpoint to survivorship-biased narratives about Vietnam's e-commerce boom — the same 2015-2016 window that produced durable winners like Tiki and Sendo also produced a wave of vertical shutdowns (Beyeu, Deca, Lingo) in categories that looked promising on market-size slides alone.
