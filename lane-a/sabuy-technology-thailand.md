## One-liner

Sabuy Technology, a once fast-growing Thai vending-machine-and-fintech conglomerate listed on the Stock Exchange of Thailand, collapsed into a going-concern crisis in 2024–2025 after auditors flagged a 215-million-baht asset and inventory loss, goodwill overstatement, and a 3,183% surge in annual losses — triggering a debt default spiral and lawsuits against its own former directors.

## What happened

Sabuy Technology built a sprawling business around unmanned vending machines, coin/bill-payment kiosks, and fintech services across Thailand, going public on the SET and expanding aggressively through acquisitions. That expansion masked deteriorating fundamentals: in its 2024 financial statements, auditors issued an unqualified opinion with emphasis of matters, flagging a 215-million-baht loss of assets and inventory, an incomplete fair-value assessment of the company's locker-rental business (where goodwill represented 71% of the purchase price), and going-concern doubts tied to massive losses. The company reported a 2024 net loss of 6,238 million baht — up 3,183% year over year — with accumulated deficits of 8,152 million baht, up 1,003%. The SET publicly flagged the filing and directed investors to scrutinize it, and the stock plunged 20% to THB 0.32 per share on the news (27 March 2025).

Underneath the accounting crisis was a debt crisis: Sabuy faced default on outstanding debentures that risked triggering a cross-default across roughly 3,795 million baht of additional debt. At a bondholder meeting on 20 November 2024, creditors rejected a proposed waiver that would have restructured the debt and extended maturities by three years, leaving the company without an agreed path to avoid default. Sabuy's own internal fact-finding review concluded that past operations may not have complied with applicable laws and regulations, and the company responded by filing roughly 2.6 billion baht in lawsuits against former directors and a debtor, and sought a probe from Thailand's Department of Special Investigation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Aggressive acquisition-led growth (vending, lockers, fintech) built up goodwill-heavy balance sheet exposure — the locker business alone carried goodwill equal to 71% of its purchase price, which proved impossible to fully justify once fair-valued.
- Bondholders' 20 November 2024 rejection of the debt-restructuring waiver removed the main negotiated off-ramp from default, escalating the situation from an accounting problem to an active credit crisis.
- Management's decision to sue former directors and a debtor for 2.6 billion baht, and to request a DSI probe, signaled the company's own admission that the underlying losses stemmed from prior-management conduct, not just market conditions.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sabuy's unraveling is a textbook case of how acquisition-fueled growth on a public listing can outrun governance capacity: goodwill and inventory figures went unchallenged until an audit emphasis-of-matter forced disclosure, at which point losses, debt covenants, and creditor confidence all broke down within the same reporting cycle. For a market-intelligence dataset, it's a useful counterweight to venture-funded startup failure narratives — this is a listed, audited, publicly traded company, and the same fraud/oversight failure modes (inflated goodwill, undisclosed asset losses, board-level malfeasance) still played out, just with SET disclosure rules and bondholder votes as the forcing mechanism instead of a term sheet running out.
