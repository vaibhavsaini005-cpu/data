## One-liner

GravityLabs, a Seoul-founded healthtech startup behind the gamified wellness app Moneywalk (5M+ cumulative users, ~$25M ARR), raised a $17M Series A in December 2025 led by Goodwater Capital and SBVA, bringing total funding to $25M as it plans US expansion.

## What happened

GravityLabs, founded in 2022 by medical-school-dropout-turned-CEO Woonyeon Kim, built Moneywalk, a gamification-based healthcare app using rewards, challenges, and community features to encourage healthier daily habits. The company describes its core differentiator as "behavior-change technology" — AI-driven analysis of user activity patterns, sleep rhythms, and reward responses used to personalize missions and feedback that reinforce long-term habit formation, rather than a generic fitness-tracking approach.

By the time of its December 2025 Series A, Moneywalk had reached roughly 1.3 million monthly active users and surpassed 5 million cumulative users globally across Korea, the US, and Japan, with annual recurring revenue approaching $25 million run with a lean 20-person team. The $17M round, led by Goodwater Capital and SBVA (formerly SoftBank Ventures Asia) with participation from Atinum Investment, NAVER, DSC Investment, and Bass Ventures, brought total funding to $25M. The capital is earmarked specifically for establishing a US office, relocating key Korean personnel, and recruiting local talent across product, data, growth, and partnerships functions to accelerate the company's US market push.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built the product around AI-driven behavior-change mechanics (analyzing sleep, activity, and reward-response patterns) rather than simple activity tracking, differentiating from commodity fitness-app competition through personalization depth.
- Achieved substantial user scale (5M+ cumulative, 1.3M monthly active) and revenue (~$25M ARR) with a notably lean 20-person team, demonstrating unusually high revenue-per-employee efficiency ahead of its Series A raise.
- Sequenced international expansion by establishing initial user bases across Korea, the US, and Japan organically before committing dedicated capital and relocated personnel specifically to a US office buildout.
- Attracted a funding syndicate blending Korean and international investors (Goodwater Capital, SBVA, NAVER) rather than relying solely on domestic Korean capital, positioning the round to directly support US market entry with investors experienced in that transition.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GravityLabs demonstrates that Korean healthtech startups can achieve substantial global user scale and revenue efficiency (5M+ users, ~$25M ARR on a 20-person team) before committing dedicated capital to international expansion, using organic multi-market traction as validation before the formal go-to-market investment. Relocating key domestic personnel to lead a new market's launch — rather than hiring an entirely new local team from scratch — is also a notable execution pattern: it preserves institutional product and company knowledge during international expansion while still building out local market expertise around that transplanted core team.
