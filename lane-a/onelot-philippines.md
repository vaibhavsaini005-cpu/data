## One-liner

Manila-based fintech OneLot, co-founded and led by CEO Harm-Julian Schumacher, raised a $3.3 million seed round — the Philippines' largest of 2025 — to scale its AI-underwritten inventory financing platform for independent used-car dealers.

## What happened

Harm-Julian Schumacher, who grew up around his family's Mercedes-Benz dealership in Germany, co-founded OneLot with Tommy Campos and Subramaniam Srinivasan to solve a specific financing gap: independent used-car dealers in the Philippines had no fast, affordable way to borrow against inventory, relying instead on slow, collateral-heavy bank processes or informal lenders. OneLot built an AI-driven underwriting engine combined with vehicle appraisal to issue secured, collateralized inventory loans to dealers within hours rather than weeks, alongside software tools for sourcing, inventory management and sales.

After roughly a year and a half of operation — during which OneLot issued more than $7 million in loans to over 150 dealerships nationwide — the company closed a $3.3 million seed round on November 10, 2025, co-led by Accion Ventures and 468 Capital, with participation from Everywhere Ventures, Seedstars, returning investor Crestone Venture Capital, Kaya Founders, and Philippine fintech angels Georg Steiger (BillEase) and Jojo Malolos (PayMongo). Schumacher framed the raise as a continuation rather than a pivot: "With this new funding round, we're doubling down on our mission to become the leading financing platform for used car dealers."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Targeting a narrow, underserved niche (used-car dealer inventory financing) instead of consumer auto loans**: Schumacher's dealership background pointed him toward the dealer-side financing gap rather than the more crowded consumer car-loan market.
- **Building AI-driven underwriting to compress approval time from weeks to hours**: a specific technical bet that speed of credit access, not just credit availability, was the binding constraint for dealers.
- **Raising a seed round to scale after proving unit economics with $7M+ in loans issued**, rather than raising larger amounts earlier — Schumacher and the founding team chose to prove the lending model at a smaller scale (150+ dealerships) before taking on the largest seed round in the Philippines for 2025.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

OneLot's raise shows a working playbook for fintech-lending startups in underbanked Southeast Asian markets: pick one narrow, capital-intensive niche where incumbents (banks) are structurally slow, build underwriting technology specifically to remove that friction, and prove the loan book's performance at modest scale before raising a large round. The round being the Philippines' largest seed of 2025 — in a year when regional VC funding was broadly tightening — also suggests investors were still willing to back founder-led, niche B2B lending plays with demonstrated repayment data, even as more generalized consumer fintech and e-commerce funding slowed.
