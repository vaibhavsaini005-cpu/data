## One-liner

Be Group, the homegrown Vietnamese ride-hailing app "be," launched in December 2018 explicitly rejecting the super-app model its rivals were chasing, then reversed course to bundle deliveries, groceries, insurance, telecom, and financial services into the same app anyway.

## What happened

Be Group's app "be" went live in Vietnam in December 2018 as a domestic challenger to Grab, initially positioning itself as an open, mobility-as-a-service (MaaS) platform that would build partnerships with other companies rather than own every service vertically — a deliberate contrast to the all-in-one super-app strategy Grab and Gojek were running elsewhere in Southeast Asia. Over the following years that positioning gave way: Be Group progressively folded deliveries, online groceries, insurance, telecom service bundles, and financial services directly into the "be" app, arriving at essentially the same bundled super-app structure it had originally set itself apart from.

By September 2022, when Be Group announced a $60 million loan from Deutsche Bank to fund further expansion, the company was competing head-to-head with Grab on Grab's own turf — holding an estimated 30-40% ride-hailing market share in Hanoi and 25-35% in Ho Chi Minh City — with CEO Vu Hoang Yen framing the loan as fuel for continued multi-service growth rather than a return to a narrower mobility focus.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Launch positioning as MaaS, not super-app**: Be Group's initial pitch was an "open platform" that partnered rather than vertically integrated — a bet that this would be a differentiator against Grab.
- **Gradual reversal into bundling**: rather than a single announced pivot, Be Group added delivery, grocery, insurance, telecom, and financial products one at a time until the app converged on the super-app model it had originally avoided.
- **Financing the converged strategy**: the 2022 Deutsche Bank loan shows the pivot wasn't just product positioning — it required real capital to fund the multi-service build-out and compete directly with Grab's scale.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Be Group's trajectory shows how much gravitational pull the super-app model exerts in Southeast Asian mobility markets: a company can launch with an explicit strategic rejection of that model and still converge on it within a few years once user expectations (and competitor behavior) make single-service apps a growth ceiling. The lesson isn't that the original strategy was wrong — it won share in a very Grab-dominated market — but that differentiated positioning has a shelf life when the entire market's users, and eventually the company's own investors, expect the bundle.
