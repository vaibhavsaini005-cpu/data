## One-liner

Indonesian fashion e-commerce platform Sorabel, backed by MNC Media and Sinar Mas, liquidated in July 2020 after the Covid-19 pandemic wiped out the cash it needed to keep operating.

## What happened

Launched in 2014 as Sale Stock and later rebranded to Sorabel, the company ran an online fashion marketplace aimed at young female shoppers in Southeast Asia, differentiated by a "try-first-pay-later" model and an automated chatbot stylist. It raised a Series B+ round led by Meranti ASEAN Growth Fund and a Series C from investors including Kejora Ventures and Ncore Ventures, totalling roughly $27 million across four rounds, and expanded regionally with a Philippines subsidiary, Yabel.

The expansion did not hold. Yabel was shut down in February 2020, and co-founders Lingga Madu and Ariza Novianti had already left the company by October 2019. When Covid-19 hit discretionary fashion spending in early 2020, Sorabel could not raise a bridge round. Management told staff by internal email that it "did its best to save the business, but had to go through liquidation," and the company ceased operations and laid off its remaining team by July 30, 2020.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Regional expansion into the Philippines (Yabel)** before the core Indonesian business was durably profitable, adding cash burn without a matching revenue base.
- **Founder departure in October 2019**, roughly nine months before the shutdown, left the company without its original leadership heading into the pandemic shock.
- **No bridge financing found in early 2020** — unlike some peers that secured emergency runway, Sorabel's investors did not step in once Covid-19 hit discretionary spending.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sorabel is a clear case of a venture-backed retail startup whose unit economics depended on continuous new funding rather than underlying profitability; when the pandemic simultaneously cut off consumer demand and investor appetite, there was no cushion left. It also illustrates the risk of premature regional expansion (the Yabel subsidiary) diluting focus and cash before a home market is secured.
