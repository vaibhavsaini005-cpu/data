## One-liner

F88, Vietnam's largest pawnshop-finance chain, was raided by police in March 2023 over alleged extortionate debt-collection practices and overcharging, then overhauled its operations and became the first Vietnamese pawnbroking company to list on the stock exchange, trading on UPCoM from August 2025.

## What happened

F88 Investment JSC built the country's largest network of modernized, storefront-based pawnshops, lending against collateral like motorbike registration papers to Vietnam's largely unbanked population and small businesses, and had grown into an alternative-finance leader in a market where the new-generation pawnshop subsegment grew at a reported 53% CAGR from 2019–2024. That growth trajectory hit a serious setback on March 6, 2023, when hundreds of police raided F88's Ho Chi Minh City headquarters in Go Vap District following public complaints about "administrative violations," with local press reporting the company had used extortion-style methods in debt collection and that some of its roughly 830 shops had overcharged customers with extra fees pushing effective interest well beyond the stated pawn-loan rate. Follow-up inspections hit 26 more branches across Can Tho City and Tien Giang province in the Mekong Delta, with police in Tien Giang finding branches had failed to keep required business records and financial reports. F88 continued operating through the investigation, and by 2024 the company was pursuing a roughly $1 billion valuation with a plan to go public; that plan slipped past the original target but on May 6, 2025, F88 Investment Joint Stock Company was formally recognized as a public company, and on August 8, 2025, it listed 8.26 million shares on the UPCoM exchange at a reference price of about $24 per share — the highest per-share price of any company listed on Vietnam's three exchanges at the time, and the first pawnbroking company to trade publicly in the country. By May 2026 F88 reported operating 972 branches nationwide.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Continuing to operate and pursue formalization (an IPO) rather than retreating after the 2023 raids**: F88 treated the police investigation as a compliance problem to fix rather than an existential threat, keeping its branch network running while working toward public-company status.
- **Pursuing a public listing to convert an industry associated with stigma and predatory-lending complaints into a regulated, disclosure-bound entity**: going public forces ongoing financial transparency that a private pawnbroker can otherwise avoid, which doubles as a trust-rebuilding move after the 2023 scrutiny.
- **Scaling branch count (830 shops at the time of the raids to 972 by 2026) through and after the investigation**: rather than shrinking the network defensively, F88 kept expanding physical footprint, betting that demand from unbanked borrowers would outlast the reputational hit.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

F88 shows how a consumer-finance business built on a stigmatized, lightly-regulated lending model (pawnbroking) can survive a serious regulatory and reputational crisis — police raids and public "extortion" allegations — by leaning into formalization rather than retreating from it, ultimately using a stock exchange listing as the mechanism to prove legitimacy. It's a useful counter-example to the assumption that a police raid is a near-terminal event for a fintech-adjacent lender: the outcome depended heavily on whether the underlying unit economics and demand were sound once questionable collection practices were reformed.
