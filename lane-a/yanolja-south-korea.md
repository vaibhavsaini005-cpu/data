## One-liner

Yanolja began as a booking site for South Korea's budget "love motel" segment — a market the mainstream travel industry refused to touch — then pivoted into a full-stack travel super-app and B2B hotel-SaaS vendor that raised $1.7 billion from SoftBank Vision Fund 2 at an $8.5 billion valuation, becoming the country's third tech unicorn.

## What happened

Founded in 2005 by Lee Su-jin, a former part-time hostel employee, Yanolja (Korean for "Hey, wanna play?") started by listing and ranking budget yeogwan (cheap inns) and motel-style accommodation — a category dominated by cash transactions and walk-in guests, largely invisible to mainstream OTAs like booking.com. The niche was unglamorous but enormous: tens of millions of Koreans used budget accommodation regularly, and nobody was building software for it.

Between 2015 and 2019, Yanolja steadily expanded from accommodation aggregation to a broader leisure and activity booking platform (theme parks, sports, leisure venues), then launched Y FLUX — a cloud PMS/POS system for accommodation operators — positioning the company as both a consumer OTA and a B2B SaaS provider for the hospitality industry. This vertical integration gave Yanolja proprietary data on the supply side, improving its consumer product's availability and pricing accuracy.

In July 2021, SoftBank Vision Fund 2 invested $1.7 billion, valuing the company at approximately $8.5 billion. Yanolja used the capital to acquire assets internationally, expand Y FLUX into Southeast Asia and the Middle East, and build an international B2B sales force. However, the 2022 global tech downturn hit growth-stage multiples hard; Yanolja postponed a planned Nasdaq IPO and cut approximately 160 employees in late 2022. The company re-engaged IPO advisors in 2024 targeting a NYSE listing.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Starting in the unglamorous segment**: By building in the Korean budget-motel niche, Yanolja faced zero competition from global OTAs and was able to achieve near-monopoly supply coverage before expanding upmarket — a classic "start in a niche others find embarrassing" playbook.
- **Vertical integration into supply-side SaaS**: Launching Y FLUX turned Yanolja from a marketplace into an operating infrastructure provider; properties running Y FLUX were naturally listed on the Yanolja consumer platform and shared data that couldn't be replicated by competitors.
- **SoftBank cheque and international overreach**: The $1.7B at peak-2021 valuations committed the company to a global expansion pace it struggled to sustain after interest rates rose; the missed IPO window and workforce reduction showed that even a genuine category leader can over-lever on bull-market capital.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Yanolja's first decade is one of the purest examples of a geographic/demographic niche pivot in consumer tech: a real market that incumbents dismissed because of its associations (budget, cash-only, informal) became the beachhead for a multi-billion-dollar platform. Its post-2021 trajectory is an equally clear lesson in the risk of using Vision Fund-scale capital to compress a globalization timeline that a more methodical expansion might have achieved safely.
