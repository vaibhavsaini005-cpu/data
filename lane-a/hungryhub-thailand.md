## One-liner

Hungry Hub, a Bangkok reservation-based dining platform founded in 2014, pivoted from pure in-restaurant reservations to a delivery and staycation-package model ("Hungry@Home") when COVID-19 shut down dine-in demand in 2020, then used that adaptability to later expand into Singapore in 2025.

## What happened

Hungry Hub launched in 2014 under founder Surasit Sachdev as a restaurant reservation system, later adding fixed-price "All You Can Eat" packages in 2017 after noticing diners consistently blew past group dining budgets. The business model depended on in-person dining traffic across its restaurant and hotel partner network.

When COVID-19 lockdowns eliminated dine-in demand in 2020, Hungry Hub pivoted its core offering to "Hungry@Home," a delivery-based service bringing curated set menus from partner restaurants directly to customers, and layered on "Staycation Packages" with hotels in Bangkok, Pattaya, and Hua Hin to capture the shift toward domestic, at-home leisure spending while international travel was halted. The pivot kept the company's restaurant-and-hotel partner network intact through a period when pure reservation volume would have collapsed to near zero.

Having survived the pandemic by diversifying delivery and staycation revenue, the company returned to expanding its original reservation/dining-experience model post-pandemic, forming partnerships with Klook, KKday, and Google in 2024, working with Michelin-starred restaurants, and expanding into Singapore with 100+ restaurant partners in 2025.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Shifted from an exclusively in-restaurant, reservation-based model to a delivery and staycation-package model within the 2020 COVID-19 lockdown period, rather than pausing operations.
- Retained and repurposed its existing restaurant and hotel partner network for the new delivery/staycation offering instead of building new partnerships from scratch.
- Returned to and re-invested in the original dining-experience model once conditions normalized, rather than treating the pandemic pivot as a permanent business model change.
- Used the resilience built during the pivot period as a springboard for international expansion into Singapore in 2025.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Hungry Hub is a case of a temporary, survival-driven pivot rather than a permanent one: the company changed its delivery mechanism to match a demand shock (COVID-19 lockdowns) while preserving its underlying partner relationships, then reverted to and scaled its original core model once the shock passed. It illustrates that a pivot doesn't have to be a one-way door — reversibility, when the original demand returns, can be as valuable as the pivot itself.
