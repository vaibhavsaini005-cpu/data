## One-liner

Vietnam's homegrown ride-hailing app GoViet, launched as Gojek's local joint-venture entity to compete with Grab, was dissolved as a separate brand and folded directly into the unified Gojek app in August 2020.

## What happened

GoViet launched in Vietnam in 2018 as a locally run, Vietnamese-branded operation built with backing and technology from Indonesian super-app Gojek, mirroring the "GET" playbook Gojek used in Thailand. It grew into the number-two ride-hailing and food-delivery player in Vietnam behind Grab, running its own separate app, brand identity and local leadership under co-founder and COO Phung Tuan Duc.

On July 3, 2020, the company announced GoViet would be merged into a single, globally unified Gojek app and rebranded as "Gojek Vietnam" — ending GoViet as an independent brand. The transition rolled out starting August 17, 2020 (coinciding with Indonesian Independence Day), when Vietnam and Thailand's local apps (GoViet and GET) were retired in favor of the single Gojek app already used in Indonesia and Singapore. Phung Tuan Duc was named CEO of the newly unified Gojek Vietnam entity. Gojek framed the move as fixing a fragmented user experience — travelers and regional users previously had to download a different, separately branded app in each country.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Launching as a distinct local brand to win trust fast**: GoViet's Vietnamese identity (separate from Gojek's Indonesian brand) was a deliberate early localization bet to compete credibly against Grab's already-established regional presence.
- **Sacrificing brand independence for platform unification**: once GoViet had proven the market and built a driver/user base, Gojek chose consolidated global brand consistency over preserving the local brand equity GoViet had built up over two years.
- **Retaining local leadership through the transition**: keeping Phung Tuan Duc as CEO of the merged entity preserved continuity and local market knowledge even as the consumer-facing brand disappeared.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GoViet is a clean example of a regional super-app absorbing a successful local joint-venture brand once it has served its market-entry purpose — the underlying business didn't fail, but the standalone identity did, as part of a deliberate platform-unification strategy. It's a useful contrast to acquisitions driven by distress: here the "end" of GoViet as a brand was a sign of Gojek's confidence in the Vietnamese market, not a retreat from it.
