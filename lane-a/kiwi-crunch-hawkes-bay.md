## One-liner

New Zealand's second-largest apple grower and exporter, Kiwi Crunch Hawke's Bay, was put into voluntary administration by its director, Wade Steven Glass, on 7 July 2026, after a subsidiary was liquidated by the High Court over roughly NZ$19 million in unpaid tax.

## What happened

Kiwi Crunch Hawke's Bay ran around 450 hectares of orchards across Hawke's Bay and Central Otago, making it the country's second-largest apple grower and exporter. Its sole shareholding included Hastings-based Crasborn Fresh Harvest Ltd, which the High Court in Auckland ordered into liquidation over approximately NZ$19 million owed to Inland Revenue in unpaid taxes and penalties. That liquidation forced director Wade Steven Glass to place six related entities — Kiwi Crunch Hawke's Bay, Kiwi Crunch Exports, Kiwi Crunch Orchards, Kiwi Crunch Farms, Kiwi Crunch Nurseries, and KC IP — into voluntary administration on 7 July 2026. Gareth Hoole of Ecovis was appointed administrator. The Hawke's Bay Fruitgrowers' Association publicly flagged the risk of job losses across the group's orchard workforce.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Director Wade Steven Glass structured Crasborn Fresh Harvest as a subsidiary wholly owned by Kiwi Crunch Hawke's Bay, tying the parent's fate to that one entity's tax position.
- Letting the Inland Revenue debt reach ~NZ$19 million rather than resolving it before court action forced the High Court's hand on Crasborn's liquidation.
- Glass's response was to place the parent and four sister entities into voluntary administration rather than let each be picked off individually by creditors — a single, coordinated group-wide filing.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A single subsidiary's unresolved tax liability can cascade into the collapse of an entire group structure, even one big enough to be a country's #2 player in its sector — a reminder that group liability exposure through shared directorship and shareholding is a structural risk, not just a compliance footnote.
