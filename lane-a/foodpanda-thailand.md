## One-liner

After 13 straight years of losses totalling more than 13 billion baht, Delivery Hero pulled foodpanda out of Thailand entirely in May 2025 rather than keep funding an unprofitable position in one of Southeast Asia's most crowded food-delivery markets.

## What happened

foodpanda launched in Thailand in 2012 as one of the country's earliest food-delivery platforms, eventually competing against Grab, LINE MAN Wongnai, and Robinhood in a market with thin margins and heavy discounting. Despite more than a decade of operation, the Thai unit never turned a profit: it posted a loss of 522 million baht on 3.84 billion baht of revenue in 2023 alone, and accumulated losses of roughly 13.36 billion baht between 2019 and 2023.

On April 23, 2025, Delivery Hero (foodpanda's German parent) announced it would exit Thailand completely, with operations ceasing on May 23, 2025. The company framed the move as a "strategic realignment" to focus resources on "more profitable Asian markets," rather than continuing to subsidize a market position that current conditions no longer justified holding. The shutdown affected riders, merchant partners, and customers who had to migrate to competing platforms with little notice.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Thirteen years without a path to profitability**: unlike a startup burning venture money in year two or three, foodpanda Thailand kept losing money for over a decade before its parent pulled the plug — a sign of how entrenched discount-driven competition had become in the category.
- **Portfolio triage by a multinational parent**: this wasn't a standalone startup failing outright; Delivery Hero made a capital-allocation decision to cut a chronically unprofitable market to protect group-level margins, a pattern also seen in its other Southeast Asian exits.
- **Abrupt full exit rather than a scaled-down presence**: Delivery Hero chose a clean withdrawal over further restructuring, ceasing all Thai operations within a month of announcing the decision.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

foodpanda Thailand illustrates that "real, named company, checkable source" failures aren't limited to VC-funded startups running out of runway — multinational-backed local units can also be quietly and then abruptly wound down once a parent's global profitability targets stop tolerating a loss-making market. It's also a caution against reading longevity as validation: 13 years of continuous operation didn't mean the underlying unit economics ever worked, and food delivery's structurally thin margins in Southeast Asia continue to force even well-capitalized players to retreat market by market.
