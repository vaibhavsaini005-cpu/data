## One-liner

Bangkok ticketing platform Eventpop, founded in 2015 and backed early by Tao Kae Noi billionaire Ittipat Peeradechapan, was acquired by payments group OPN in 2021 to chase e-commerce during COVID-19, then bought back by co-founder Pattaraporn Bodhisuwan in November 2023 to return the company to its original events-and-ticketing focus.

## What happened

Eventpop launched in Bangkok in 2015, building ticketing, access-control, and cashless-payment tools for event organizers, and by 2017 had raised a Series A of over $2 million from investors including InVent (a subsidiary of telecom operator AIS's parent) and Kasikorn Bank's Beacon fund, with backing from Tao Kae Noi seaweed-snack billionaire Ittipat "Tob" Peeradechapan. In 2021, at the height of pandemic-driven event shutdowns, Eventpop was acquired by OPN (formerly SYNQA/Omise) as the company explored pivoting toward broader e-commerce solutions while live events were impossible to run.

That pivot didn't stick. Effective November 30, 2023 (announced December 1, 2023), co-founder Pattaraporn Bodhisuwan, together with angel investors, reacquired Eventpop from OPN for an undisclosed amount. The company described the buyback as a "pivotal moment" that let it "realign with its original mission under renewed leadership," and said it was moving from a loss the prior year back toward profitability, with plans to expand from pure ticketing into adjacent lifestyle services using its existing customer and event data.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Selling into OPN during COVID-19**: with live events halted, Eventpop's founders chose acquisition by a payments group over trying to independently survive a demand collapse, betting a larger parent could fund an e-commerce pivot.
- **The e-commerce pivot inside OPN didn't stick**: rather than staying diversified, the business drifted from Eventpop's original ticketing strength during its time under OPN ownership.
- **Buying the company back**: the founder-led reacquisition in November 2023 was an explicit bet that Eventpop's core events/ticketing identity, not the e-commerce detour, was the more valuable business to run independently.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Eventpop's round-trip — founder-led startup, acquired for a crisis-driven pivot, then bought back by its own founder to undo that pivot — is a concrete example of a startup's "pivot" not being a single permanent decision but something that can be reversed once the external shock (COVID-19 event shutdowns) passes. It's a useful counterpoint to pivot stories elsewhere in this dataset that stick permanently: here, the original business model turned out to be the right one, just poorly timed.
