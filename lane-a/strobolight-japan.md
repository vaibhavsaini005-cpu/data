## One-liner

Tokyo gardening-tech startup Strobolight, founded and run by CEO Hidehiko Ishitsuka, filed for bankruptcy in October 2024 after raising over ¥1 billion from VCs but never turning the business profitable.

## What happened

Hidehiko Ishitsuka founded Strobolight in 2012 as an ad-sales and promotions company, then redirected it in 2014 toward gardening and horticulture IT services, building the "LOVE GREEN" gardening media site and later "MIDOLAS," a landscaping renovation service offering end-to-end design-to-construction work. The company raised more than ¥1 billion across multiple third-party share allotments from venture capital investors, and in 2020 was profiled by a major Japanese business magazine as one of the country's promising startups to watch, with roughly 40 staff and offices in Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka and Fukuoka.

Despite that visibility, the business never reached profitability — running annual operating losses in the hundreds of millions of yen against revenue in a similar range — and venture funding for the sector dried up after the pandemic. Strobolight cut costs, carried out a capital reduction for tax purposes, and sold its core LOVE GREEN media business in September 2023 to raise cash. In March 2024, its remaining funding partner told the company it could not provide further support, and with no alternative financing secured, Strobolight filed for bankruptcy; the Tokyo District Court issued the bankruptcy commencement decision on October 30, 2024.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pivoting from ad-sales to gardening IT in 2014**: Ishitsuka redirected the company into a niche vertical (horticulture) that was easier to build early media traction in but proved structurally difficult to monetize at scale.
- **Selling the core LOVE GREEN media asset in September 2023 to buy runway**: a defensive move that generated cash but stripped the company of the audience asset most of its brand value depended on.
- **Continuing to seek VC support through multiple funding rounds rather than restructuring toward profitability earlier**: kept the company alive through repeated capital increases, but left it fully dependent on investor goodwill that evaporated once its main backer withdrew in March 2024.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Strobolight shows how a startup can accumulate real brand visibility (media coverage, a well-known gardening publication, a "promising startup" magazine feature) without that translating into a monetizable core business — the company never closed the gap between audience and revenue across a decade of operation. It's also a clear example of single-funder dependency risk: once its primary VC backer decided in March 2024 it would not continue supporting the company, there was no other capital source to fall back on, converting one investor's decision into the company's bankruptcy filing seven months later.
