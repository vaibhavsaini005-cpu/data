## One-liner

Three Vietnamese scientists who had built their careers researching genetics and cancer biology in the United States returned to found Gene Solutions in Ho Chi Minh City in 2017, priced a non-invasive prenatal test at a fraction of the prevailing market rate to make it reachable for Vietnamese families, and by 2026 were processing hundreds of thousands of tests a year while preparing a Hong Kong IPO at a targeted valuation around $800 million.

## What happened

Gene Solutions was established in 2017 by Dr. Nguyen Hoai Nghia, Dr. Nguyen Huu Nguyen and Dr. Giang Hoa — three Vietnamese scientists with years of research experience in the United States across genetics, cancer biology and biotechnology. The problem they returned to solve was access rather than science: advanced genetic testing existed, but was not reachably priced or locally available in Vietnam and much of Southeast Asia.

Their flagship product is triSure, a non-invasive prenatal test (NIPT) — a blood test for pregnant women that screens for common fetal chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome. The decisive commercial choice was price. The company introduced its NIPT at a fraction of the prevailing market price, which converted a test that had been a premium procedure into something a mass Vietnamese market could use. In diagnostics this is not merely a discount; below a certain price point a screening test can be recommended as a default rather than an exception, which changes the addressable volume by orders of magnitude.

The volume figures show that this worked. The company processed more than 650,000 tests in 2025, and is targeting four million tests per year by the end of the decade. Mekong Capital invested $21 million to help scale operations.

By mid-2026 Gene Solutions was running two financing tracks at once. It expected to complete a $50 million Series C by the end of the third quarter of 2026, and separately was preparing a Hong Kong share offering to raise approximately $100 million, with trading targeted between the second and third quarters of 2027 at a valuation of around $800 million depending on market conditions. The IPO plan carried a specific operational requirement attached to it: the company aimed to generate about $90 million of revenue by the end of 2026 in order to meet Hong Kong's listing revenue threshold, against a longer-term goal of roughly $350 million in annual revenue by the end of the decade.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Returning from US research careers to build in Vietnam rather than publishing or joining a Western diagnostics firm.** The founders' credential was American research experience; the opportunity they identified was the gap between what that research made possible and what was actually available at home.
- **Pricing the NIPT at a fraction of the prevailing market rate.** This is the central decision in the whole file. It traded margin per test for a change in category — from premium procedure to routine screening — and the 650,000-plus annual test volume is the direct consequence.
- **Choosing a screening test for a universal life event as the wedge product.** Prenatal screening applies to every pregnancy, recurs with the population rather than with a purchase decision, and is recommended by clinicians rather than chosen by consumers. It is close to the most volume-stable entry point available in diagnostics.
- **Listing in Hong Kong rather than domestically.** The choice imports a stricter disclosure and revenue regime, which is what makes the $90 million revenue target a hard operational deadline rather than an ambition — the company deliberately subordinated its 2026 operating plan to an external listing requirement.
- **Running a $50M private Series C and a ~$100M IPO in parallel.** The Series C funds the growth needed to clear the listing threshold that the IPO depends on, sequencing two raises so that the first qualifies the company for the second.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Gene Solutions is a strong case for price as the product decision rather than a go-to-market afterthought. The underlying science of non-invasive prenatal testing was not proprietary to them; what they changed was what it cost, and that single move redefined who the test was for. Pricing a diagnostic low enough that clinicians can recommend it by default converts a company from a vendor of a premium service into infrastructure for a national health behaviour — and the volume that follows is not a marketing outcome but a structural one.

The second pattern is the returnee-scientist founding team as a specific and repeatable advantage in emerging-market healthtech. The founders' years in US labs gave them technical credibility that local competitors could not easily claim and a clear view of the gap between the global state of the art and domestic availability. That combination — frontier knowledge plus home-market access and cost structure — is one of the few durable edges available when the technology itself is not exclusive.

Finally, the IPO sequencing is instructive for any founder considering a foreign listing. By targeting Hong Kong, Gene Solutions accepted a revenue threshold as a binding constraint and then raised a private round specifically to clear it. That inverts the usual relationship between operating plan and fundraising: the listing requirement became the operating target, and the Series C became the means of hitting it. It works, but it means the company's 2026 priorities were effectively set by an exchange's listing rules.

*Status note: as of the sources here, both the $50M Series C and the Hong Kong listing were stated plans and targets rather than completed events. The documented achievement is the test volume, the Mekong Capital investment, and the pricing strategy that produced them.*
