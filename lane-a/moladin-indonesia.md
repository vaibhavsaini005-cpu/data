## One-liner

Moladin raised $137M across two rounds in a single year (2022) to become Indonesia's full-stack used-car ecosystem, then laid off 360 employees — 11% of its workforce — in February 2023 after over-hiring by 567% in twelve months.

## What happened

Moladin was founded in Jakarta around 2020–2021 by former Gojek and OLX executives to digitise Indonesia's fragmented used-car dealer network. The pitch: aggregate thousands of independent "mom-and-pop" dealers onto a platform offering inventory financing, digital tools, and a consumer marketplace.

The company raised a **$42M Series A** co-led by Sequoia Capital India and Northstar Group in early 2022, then rapidly followed with a **$95M Series B** led by DST Global in May 2022 — $137M in total within roughly four months. Between May 2021 and May 2022, headcount grew by **567%**, far outpacing revenue growth.

By early 2023 the global funding winter and rising interest rates had tightened Indonesia's tech capital markets. Moladin announced in February 2023 that it was cutting 360 roles — citing "sustainability" — and restructuring its dealer-facing and consumer divisions. The layoff came less than 10 months after the Series B closed.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Dual-round blitz (early/mid 2022)**: raising two large rounds in rapid succession forced the company to deploy capital faster than its operational maturity could absorb — the 567% headcount spike was the visible symptom.
- **Full-stack ambition**: Moladin tried to own inventory financing, logistics, dealer tooling, and the consumer-facing marketplace simultaneously; each required separate expertise and burn.
- **2023 restructuring**: the February 2023 layoff was framed as rightsizing, but it came after less than a year at peak staffing, indicating the hiring plan was disconnected from sustainable unit economics.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Moladin is a textbook case of the 2021–2022 "raise as much as you can, hire fast, figure out profitability later" playbook colliding with 2023 reality. The used-car vertical in Southeast Asia is capital-intensive and requires deep dealer trust built over years; the speed of expansion meant relationships and systems hadn't matured when the funding environment changed. The pattern recurred across multiple Indonesian startups (Pahamify, Fabelio, TaniHub) that scaled headcount ahead of unit economics in the same window.
