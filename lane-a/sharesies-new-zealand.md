## One-liner

Wellington-founded Sharesies turned the New Zealand investing market on its head by letting anyone invest from NZ$1, growing to 500,000+ customers — roughly one in ten New Zealand adults — within five years of launch.

## What happened

Sharesies was founded in Wellington in 2017 by Brooke Roberts, Sonya Williams, Leighton Roberts, and Cara Williams. The premise was that investing in shares and funds had been structurally inaccessible to most New Zealanders: minimum investment thresholds, high brokerage fees, and perceived complexity kept retail investing the preserve of the already-wealthy. Sharesies built a mobile-first platform where anyone could buy fractional shares in New Zealand, US, and Australian listed companies plus managed funds, starting from as little as NZ$1 per transaction.

The platform launched publicly in 2019 after a regulatory journey to obtain a managed investment scheme manager licence from the Financial Markets Authority (FMA). Customer growth accelerated sharply during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, when low interest rates and enforced savings pushed a new cohort of New Zealanders toward equity markets for the first time. By the time Sharesies closed a NZD $50 million (roughly USD $35M) Series B in June 2021 — one of New Zealand's larger consumer fintech rounds — it had well over 300,000 customers.

Expansion to Australia launched in September 2021. By early 2022 the platform had passed 500,000 total customers across New Zealand and Australia, a remarkable figure given New Zealand's total adult population of roughly 3.5 million. Funds under management exceeded NZD $1 billion. The platform had by that point expanded its product suite to include US exchange access, a Kids Accounts feature for under-18 investing, and a savings product, while maintaining its founding principle of no minimum investment.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Fractional shares as the core unlock**: The decision to allow sub-$1 investments made the platform accessible to students, low-income earners, and first-time investors — demographics that traditional brokers ignored — while also enabling dollar-cost averaging at very small amounts.
- **FMA regulatory engagement from the start**: Rather than operating in a grey zone, founders engaged the FMA early and secured a formal licence, which took longer but gave the platform credibility that later proved important for consumer trust and institutional partnerships.
- **COVID-19 as an unexpected tailwind**: The 2020 lockdowns coincided with Sharesies' post-launch growth phase; the combination of enforced savings and near-zero bank deposit rates drove first-time investors to the platform in volume the team had not projected.
- **Kids Accounts as a retention and referral mechanism**: The product allowing parents to invest on behalf of children under 18 — a regulatory novelty requiring FMA approval — proved to be a high-retention user segment and drove significant word-of-mouth among families.
- **Australia expansion before profitability**: The decision to expand to Australia in 2021 while still growing the NZ base was a deliberate "land and expand" bet on the total addressable market, funded by the Series B.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Sharesies is the clearest New Zealand example of a fintech that democratised a historically gatekept market by attacking minimum thresholds and price. Its trajectory — modest NZ market, FMA licence, pandemic tailwind, Series B, cross-Tasman expansion — follows a pattern common in small-country fintech startups that use their home market as a proving ground before attacking a larger neighbour (Australia, in this case). The platform's 500,000-customer milestone is also a useful benchmark: at roughly 14% adult penetration of New Zealand in five years, it is one of the highest consumer fintech penetration rates achieved by any startup in an English-speaking country outside the United States.
