## One-liner

Vuong Le Vinh Nhan, chairman of HVA Group and creator of the ONUS crypto exchange, was arrested in Vietnam on March 24, 2026, after authorities alleged his platform issued fraudulent tokens and manipulated prices to mobilize billions of dollars from investors since 2018.

## What happened

Vuong Le Vinh Nhan (born 1984, Can Tho) built ONUS into a fast-growing Vietnamese crypto exchange and "super-app" offering trading, staking and token issuance, under his company HVA Group, and issued a family of tokens including VNDC, ONUS and HNG starting in 2018. Vietnamese authorities opened an investigation on March 23, 2026 alleging that Nhan and associates created and promoted these tokens using misleading marketing to inflate their perceived value, then used wash trading — artificially generated buy/sell activity — to fabricate supply-and-demand signals and prop up prices, ultimately mobilizing billions of US dollars from investors.

On March 24, 2026, prosecutors issued detention and prosecution decisions against Nhan along with associates including Tran Quang Chien (technical operations) and Ngo Thi Thao (director of HANAGOLD, a linked jewelry company used in the scheme). Authorities conducted coordinated raids across multiple Vietnamese cities, summoned more than 140 people for questioning, and seized computers, phones and documents. Seven individuals were charged under Article 290 of Vietnam's penal code (appropriating property via computer/telecommunications networks) and one additional suspect was charged with money laundering under Article 324. The ONUS platform itself experienced an outage that temporarily blocked users from accessing their funds around the time of the arrests.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building a token-issuance business alongside the exchange itself (VNDC, ONUS, HNG)**: gave Nhan's group direct control over the assets being traded on its own platform, creating the structural conditions authorities allege were used for price manipulation.
- **Using wash trading to fabricate market activity**: rather than growing through organic trading volume, prosecutors allege the group manufactured artificial supply-and-demand signals to make the tokens appear more liquid and valuable than they were.
- **Vietnamese authorities' decision to launch a coordinated, multi-city investigation in March 2026**: converted years of alleged token manipulation into a criminal case within days, freezing the platform and its founder's operations almost overnight.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

ONUS is a case study in the structural conflict of interest at the center of many crypto "super-apps": when the same company both issues a token and operates the exchange it trades on, the incentive and the means to manipulate that token's price sit in the same hands. The multi-year gap between the alleged start of the scheme (2018) and the 2026 crackdown also illustrates how long token-based fraud can operate in a market with limited real-time oversight before a coordinated regulatory action — rather than a market correction — becomes the actual end of the business.
