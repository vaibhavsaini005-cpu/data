## One-liner

Singapore's dominant real estate marketplace listed on NYSE via SPAC in March 2022 at a $1.8 B valuation, then was taken private by Swedish PE firm EQT at $1.1 B in December 2024 — a successful exit for founders and early backers despite the valuation markdown.

## What happened

PropertyGuru was founded in 2007 by Jani Rautiainen and Steve Melhuish as a real estate listings platform for Singapore. It expanded across Southeast Asia (Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia) and became the dominant portal in Singapore and Vietnam. KKR and TPG became major backers and together held 56% of ordinary shares before exit. In March 2022, the company completed a SPAC merger with Bridgetown 2 Holdings (backed by Peter Thiel and Richard Li) and listed on the New York Stock Exchange at a valuation of approximately $1.8 B. Post-listing, the share price struggled amid broader tech-stock sell-offs. In July 2024, Swedish private equity firm EQT announced an all-cash acquisition at $6.70 per share, valuing PropertyGuru at $1.1 B — a 39% discount to the SPAC merger price but still a successful liquidity event. The acquisition completed on 13 December 2024; PropertyGuru delisted from NYSE and returned to private ownership.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **SPAC route over traditional IPO (2022):** Allowed a faster listing at a peak valuation, but exposed the company to post-merger stock price volatility with less stabilisation than a traditional bookbuild would provide.
- **Broad Southeast Asia footprint:** Operating in five markets gave PropertyGuru defensible regional scale but required sustained investment in local teams, complicating the path to profitability.
- **EQT buyout (2024):** Rather than pursue further public-market fundraising, the board accepted PE acquisition — recognising that continued operation as a small-cap NYSE issuer brought cost (compliance, investor-relations overhead) without proportionate capital benefits.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

PropertyGuru's arc — SPAC listing at peak → valuation compression → PE take-private — became a common pattern for Southeast Asian tech companies that listed in 2021–2022. The EQT exit was commercially successful but highlighted how SPAC valuations rarely hold in post-enthusiasm markets. For regional proptech, the key insight is that classifieds-style portals need either network-effect lock-in at massive scale or recurring monetisation (software/SaaS to agents) to justify public-market multiples.
