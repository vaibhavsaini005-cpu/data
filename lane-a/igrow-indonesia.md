## One-liner

Indonesian agritech lender iGrow, once acquired by LinkAja as a fintech
growth bet, ended up under regulatory scrutiny with a 90-day default rate
above 46% and lenders suing over unpaid loans.

## What happened

iGrow was an Indonesian peer-to-peer lending platform (PT iGrow Resources
Indonesia) that financed smallholder farming projects, matching retail
lenders with agricultural borrowers. It operated from 2015 without a formal
P2P lending license from Indonesia's Financial Services Authority (OJK),
only registering as a service provider in 2016. In 2021 it was acquired by
LinkAja (via shareholder PT Fintek Karya Nusantara), which halted new retail
financing and began restructuring the business toward a closed-loop
financing model under the name Modalin. That restructuring didn't resolve
the platform's legacy loan book: by mid-2023, 40 lenders sued iGrow over
defaults they said cost them a combined Rp 503 billion, and OJK opened a
fraud investigation alongside a parallel probe into sister platform
TaniFund. As of the October 12, 2023 OJK disclosure, iGrow's 90-day default
rate (TWP90) stood at 46.56% — meaning under half of the loans it had
distributed were being repaid on schedule — with OJK requiring a formal
action plan and its shareholder pursuing a capital injection to absorb the
losses.

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2015–2021: Operates as an agri-lending P2P platform without a proper OJK
  license for its first year, later registering but continuing to grow its
  loan book.
- 2021: Acquired by LinkAja; retail financing halted and rebuilt as the
  closed-loop "Modalin" model — an attempt to de-risk the business model
  itself.
- Mid-2023: 40 lenders file suit over Rp 503 billion in defaulted loans;
  OJK opens a fraud investigation.
- Oct 12, 2023: OJK discloses a 46.56% 90-day default rate and orders a
  remediation plan.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A strategic acquisition doesn't retroactively fix a lending book built on
weak underwriting — LinkAja's 2021 restructuring changed how iGrow *would*
lend going forward, but the legacy defaults it inherited still surfaced as
lawsuits and a regulatory fraud probe two years later. In lending
fintech, the failure mode isn't always a shutdown; it can be a default rate
that outlives the ownership change meant to solve it.
