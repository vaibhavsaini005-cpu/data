## One-liner

Aroa Biosurgery, an Auckland company built on a veterinary surgeon's discovery that sheep-stomach tissue scaffolds can help human wounds heal, went from a 2008 founding as "Mesynthes" to a NZ$225 million ASX listing in 2020, and has since posted five consecutive years of revenue growth, reaching NZD 103.9 million in FY26.

## What happened

Aroa Biosurgery was founded in 2008 (originally as Mesynthes) by veterinary surgeon Dr Brian Ward, who identified that the extracellular matrix (ECM) found in a sheep's forestomach shares structural similarities with human tissue and carries more than 150 proteins relevant to wound healing. The company spent over a decade turning that discovery into a commercial platform, Endoform, a soft-tissue regeneration technology derived from ovine forestomach, launching its first product for chronic non-healing wounds into the US market in 2013 — the market that has remained its principal commercial base, covering wound care, hernia repair, plastics/reconstructive surgery, and trauma/limb-salvage and tumour surgery.

The company listed on the Australian Securities Exchange in July 2020, pricing a A$45 million IPO that valued Aroa at approximately A$225 million (reported by some outlets as a ~$240m float once secondary sell-down was included), with trading commencing under the ticker ARX on 30 July 2020 — ASX's 57th New Zealand-origin IPO. Rather than a one-off funding event, the listing marked a sustained growth run: founder and CEO Brian Ward told the company's FY26 annual meeting that Aroa had delivered five consecutive years of growth since listing. For the year to 31 March 2026, revenue rose 23% to NZD 103.9 million while normalised EBITDA roughly tripled to NZD 12.6 million, with the company's Endoform-based products having by then been applied to more than six million wounds cumulatively.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Commercializing a veterinary discovery for a human medical market (2008 onward).** Recognizing that a byproduct of sheep processing (forestomach ECM) had a structural analogue useful in human wound care gave Aroa a defensible, hard-to-replicate raw material and manufacturing base rooted in New Zealand's existing agricultural supply chain.
- **Targeting the US market first (from 2013).** Launching Endoform into the US, the world's largest and highest-reimbursement wound-care market, rather than scaling domestically first, set up the revenue base that later supported the ASX listing.
- **Listing on the ASX rather than the NZX (July 2020).** Choosing the larger, more liquid Australian exchange over New Zealand's own exchange gave Aroa access to a deeper pool of healthcare-sector investors and comparable listed peers, consistent with a pattern of New Zealand medtech/biotech companies cross-listing or listing directly on the ASX.
- **Sustaining growth investment post-IPO rather than treating the float as an exit.** Five straight years of revenue growth after listing, capped by FY26's EBITDA tripling, reflect continued product and market expansion (into hernia, plastics/reconstructive and trauma/tumour surgery) rather than a founder cashing out and coasting.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Aroa Biosurgery is a case of deep, patient R&D translating an unconventional raw material — a slaughterhouse byproduct — into a differentiated, high-margin medical device platform, then compounding that advantage over more than a decade before an IPO, rather than chasing an early exit. It also illustrates a common route for New Zealand medtech and agritech companies: build the core IP and initial revenue domestically and via the US market, then list on the ASX to access capital and comparables New Zealand's smaller exchange doesn't offer, while keeping R&D and manufacturing based at home.
