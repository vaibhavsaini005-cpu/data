## One-liner

JapanTaxi, the taxi-hailing app run by Tokyo's largest taxi operator Nihon Kotsu, merged with rival DeNA's competing MOV app in 2020 to form Mobility Technologies (later rebranded GO Inc.) rather than keep fighting each other while Uber and DiDi pushed into Japan — and the combined company went public on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in June 2026 in the country's largest IPO of the year.

## What happened

JapanTaxi Co., Ltd., the technology subsidiary of Nihon Kotsu, and DeNA's competing MOV taxi-hailing app announced on February 27, 2020 that they would integrate their businesses rather than continue as rival dispatch platforms in the same city. Effective April 1, 2020, JapanTaxi renamed itself Mobility Technologies Co., Ltd., absorbing MOV's user base along with DeNA's DRIVE CHART AI accident-prevention product, with Nihon Kotsu and DeNA both taking stakes in the merged entity. The company later renamed itself again in 2023 to match its consumer-facing app name, GO.

Six years after the merger, GO Inc. listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's Growth Market on June 16, 2026, raising ¥88.6 billion (about $553 million) in Japan's largest IPO of the year so far — more than 25 times oversubscribed, pricing at the top of its range, and opening 21.25% above its offer price for a roughly ¥205 billion (~$1.3 billion) market cap. Goldman Sachs, which had invested ¥10 billion in 2023 at a ¥135 billion valuation, co-led the offering alongside Nomura and Bank of America, with international investors — including BlackRock, Wellington Management, and M&G — taking 70% of the allocation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Merging with a direct competitor instead of outcompeting it**: JapanTaxi and MOV were running near-identical dispatch apps in the same market; combining them consolidated liquidity (more taxis, more riders, faster matches) in one app rather than splitting it across two, which mattered once Uber and DiDi began pushing into Japan's taxi-app market.
- **Keeping the incumbent taxi industry inside the tent**: by building on top of Nihon Kotsu's existing ~100,000-vehicle licensed fleet instead of trying to disrupt it, the merged company avoided the regulatory and labor fights that sank ride-hailing disruption plays in many other markets.
- **Renaming twice to track the product, not the deal structure**: JapanTaxi → Mobility Technologies (2020, reflecting the merger) → GO (2023, reflecting what the app was actually called) shows a company willing to keep re-branding around its consumer product rather than its corporate history.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GO Inc. is a case study in defensive consolidation working: two competing incumbents-backed apps merged not to cut costs but to build a moat against a well-funded foreign entrant (Uber/DiDi), and the combined platform ended up large and durable enough to IPO at a multi-billion-dollar valuation six years later. It's a useful counter-example to the more common "merger of equals masks two weak businesses" pattern — here the 2020 pivot from head-to-head competition to a single dominant local platform is plausibly what let the company out-execute global ride-hailing players in its home market instead of losing to them.
