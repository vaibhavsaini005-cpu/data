## One-liner

Vietnam's subscription fitness booking platform — billed as "the ClassPass of Southeast Asia" — filed for bankruptcy in May 2020 after its per-session payout model burned through cash, leaving partner gyms unpaid and subscribers stranded.

## What happened

Founded in Hanoi in 2016, WeFit let subscribers pay a fixed monthly fee for unlimited gym, yoga, and beauty sessions across 1,000 partner locations in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. The app connected users to partner facilities: WeFit collected the subscription, then paid partners a per-session rate each time a member showed up.

The structural flaw: high-frequency users visited far more sessions than the average subscriber, meaning WeFit paid out more per user than it collected. The model worked only if most subscribers bought access and rarely used it — the opposite of what health-conscious customers actually do. By late 2019, the company had accumulated unpaid debts to partner gyms. Gyms began publicly announcing they were ending their WeFit partnerships after going unpaid for months. The founder, Nguyen Khoi, stepped down as CEO in February 2020. His replacement, Nguyen Hai Dang, scrapped the unlimited-access tier, triggering subscriber backlash. COVID-19 struck in Q1 2020, forcing all gyms to close and eliminating any remaining revenue. WeFit filed for bankruptcy on April 29, 2020, and ceased all operations on May 11, 2020.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Flawed unit economics from day one**: The per-session payout to partners, combined with unlimited subscriptions, was only profitable if churn was high and usage was low — an incoherent bet for a fitness app trying to build loyalty.
- **Late pivot to capped access**: Removing unlimited sessions in early 2020 was the correct structural fix, but it came too late and destroyed subscriber trust rather than rebuilding it.
- **Dependency on COVID-sensitive partners**: Every revenue-generating touchpoint (gyms, yoga studios, beauty centers) was an in-person venue that shut down overnight when Vietnam instituted distancing measures in March 2020.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

WeFit illustrates the "high-frequency user death spiral" that kills subscription marketplaces: the customers most likely to renew are also the ones most likely to destroy margins. Vietnam's startup ecosystem had several high-profile failures in 2019–2020 (WeFit, Lozi, others) that signaled the limits of replicating Western subscription models in markets with thin consumer spending and high price sensitivity. The company had raised approximately US$1M+ in early funding but never disclosed total amount, leaving partners and customers holding losses when it folded.
