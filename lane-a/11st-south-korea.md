## One-liner

SK Square's e-commerce platform 11st missed its 2023 IPO deadline, triggering a
two-year fight with the financial investors who had bankrolled it — resolved in
late 2025 by selling the company to a sister firm instead of taking it public.

## What happened

In 2018, a consortium ("Nile Holdings") made up of the National Pension
Service, Saemaul Geumgo, and H&Q Korea's Blind Fund invested 500 billion won
($385M) into 11st, South Korea's #2 e-commerce marketplace, majority-owned by
SK Square (via SK Telecom's e-commerce arm). The shareholder agreement gave SK
Square a call option to buy back their stake — but also obligated repayment
with interest if 11st failed to IPO within five years.

That deadline landed in September 2023, and 11st didn't list. SK Square
argued the company's valuation had fallen too far to justify a listing and
initially tried to shop 11st to Qoo10 instead of exercising the buyback — talks
that collapsed by November 2023, leaving investors publicly demanding
resolution. The standoff dragged for two years while 11st kept operating as a
live marketplace throughout. It was finally resolved on October 29, 2025: SK
Square sold its entire 11st stake to affiliate SK Planet for about 467.3
billion won, using part of the proceeds (plus a 390 billion won capital
raise) to fully repay Nile Holdings' original 500 billion won principal in a
lump sum.

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2018: 11st raises 500B won from FIs under a 5-year IPO-or-buyback clause.
- Sept 2023: IPO deadline passes with no listing; dispute becomes public.
- Nov 2023: SK Square's attempt to sell 11st to Qoo10 instead of repaying
  investors falls through.
- Oct 29, 2025: SK Square transfers its 11st stake internally to SK Planet
  and repays investors in full, ending the standoff without an IPO or an
  external sale.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A growth-stage IPO-or-buyback clause is a real deadline, not a formality —
when the market window closes, the company doesn't die, but the ownership
structure gets rebuilt around whoever can actually make investors whole. 11st
never stopped operating as a marketplace; what "failed" was the exit path its
2018 capital was priced on, not the business itself.
