## One-liner

Rakuten Group bet more than ¥1 trillion of cumulative losses that a fourth Japanese carrier built on a cloud-native Open RAN network and rock-bottom pricing could replicate Reliance Jio's disruption of India; the disruption never came, but after killing its free tier, replacing its architect, buying "platinum" spectrum and re-pricing, Rakuten Mobile crossed 10 million subscribers and posted its first full-year EBITDA profit in 2025.

## What happened

**The setup (2018–2020).** Hiroshi "Mickey" Mikitani, founder and CEO of the e-commerce group Rakuten, believed Japan's mobile prices were too high and that the incumbent trio of NTT Docomo, KDDI and SoftBank were ripe for an attack. He founded Rakuten Mobile, Inc. in January 2018 and hired Tareq Amin, the network executive behind Reliance Jio's rise to Indian market leadership in under three years, to build the same thing in Japan. The technical thesis was radical for a telco: instead of buying an integrated network from Ericsson or Nokia, Rakuten would run its radio access network as software on commodity hardware and cloud infrastructure (Altiostar RAN software, NEC radios, a Cisco core, Red Hat OpenStack), claiming far lower capex and opex than incumbents. Commercial 4G service launched in April 2020 with a single plan, Rakuten UN-LIMIT, priced at ¥2,980 a month for unlimited data on the home network, and a launch campaign giving the first 3 million subscribers their first year free.

**The pressure (2020–2023).** Three problems surfaced almost immediately. First, spectrum: Rakuten held only 20 MHz paired at 1.7 GHz for 4G and no low-band "platinum" spectrum, so indoor and rural coverage depended on expensive roaming onto KDDI's network. Second, price alone did not move Japanese consumers, who, unlike Indian consumers in 2016, already had good networks and low churn; incumbents also answered with their own cut-price sub-brands. Third, the free tier attracted low-value users. The mobile segment ran operating losses of the order of ¥400 billion a year at the peak, with the worst single quarter a ¥132 billion loss in Q1 2021. By November 2023 the group had recorded 13 straight quarters of operating losses, cumulative losses of ¥819 billion, total debt above ¥1.5 trillion and ¥800 billion of bonds due by the end of 2025, with only 5.2 million subscribers and roughly 2.5% market share. To fund the network the group sold treasury shares (¥242 billion in March 2021), issued new shares (¥296 billion in May 2023), IPO'd Rakuten Bank (¥71.8 billion, April 2023) and sold stakes in Rakuten Securities to Mizuho (¥80 billion in 2022 and ¥87 billion in 2023). Mikitani's original target of EBITDA breakeven in 2023 was missed.

**The reset (2022–2025).** The company made a sequence of decisions that changed the business model without abandoning the network. In May 2022 it scrapped the "0 yen up to 1 GB" tier, accepting a first-ever quarterly subscriber decline (down 220,000 to 5.46 million by June 2022) in exchange for a paying base. In August 2023 Tareq Amin left abruptly and was replaced by co-CEOs Kazuhiro Suzuki and Sharad Sriwastawa. In October 2023 the government awarded Rakuten 700 MHz platinum-band spectrum, and commercial service on it started in June 2024, cutting the indoor-coverage handicap. The group also reworked its KDDI roaming deal (June 2023 to September 2026) to buy time while it densified its own sites. Rakuten Symphony, the 2021 spin-out meant to sell the Open RAN stack to other operators for $100–150 billion in sales by 2025, generated only about $1.76 billion in cumulative revenue and in early 2025 was refocused on licensing its software to vendors rather than selling directly to telcos.

**The outcome (2025–2026).** Q2 2025 brought the first-ever positive quarterly EBITDA (¥9.6 billion on revenue of ¥112 billion, subscribers 8.97 million). Subscribers passed 10 million on 25 December 2025, five years and eight months after launch. For full-year 2025 Rakuten Mobile reported revenue of ¥374.7 billion (up 32%), EBITDA of ¥12.9 billion (its first full-year EBITDA profit, a ¥66.7 billion swing) and a still-large non-GAAP operating loss of ¥166 billion, with net ARPU of ¥2,467. Rakuten Group's consolidated revenue hit a record ¥2.5 trillion and its non-GAAP operating income turned positive at ¥106.3 billion. Cumulative losses on the mobile venture exceed ¥1 trillion, and the group guided to capex above ¥200 billion for 2026 to keep building out 5G and subway coverage. Light Reading's verdict in September 2025 was that as a "disruption" the venture failed: Rakuten remains more than 45 million subscriptions behind third-placed SoftBank in a 216-million-subscription market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Mikitani's founding bet: build, don't buy (2018–2019).** The decision to construct a greenfield, fully virtualised Open RAN network rather than a conventional one was the strategic core. It made Rakuten the global poster child for Open RAN and spawned Rakuten Symphony, but it also meant the company was debugging a novel architecture while trying to win customers, and it underestimated how much spectrum, not software, determines perceived network quality in Japan.

- **Launching on price alone with a free first year and a ¥0 tier (2020–2021).** Mikitani's assumption, per Light Reading's post-mortem, was that Japan would behave like India: pent-up demand, low loyalty, poor incumbent networks. None of these held. The free tier filled the base with non-paying users and pushed the EBITDA target out of reach.

- **Killing the ¥0 tier (announced 13 May 2022, effective 1 July 2022).** Rakuten chose a smaller paying base over vanity subscriber growth, took the first-ever quarterly subscriber drop, and set the trajectory for ARPU growth that drove 2025's turn to positive EBITDA.

- **Replacing the architect (7 August 2023).** Tareq Amin's exit as co-CEO of both Rakuten Mobile and Rakuten Symphony, with operators Suzuki and Sriwastawa taking over, marked the shift from "build a new kind of telco" to "run a telco that has to make money." Several of Amin's planned vendor swaps (Cisco to NEC in the core, Red Hat to Rakuten Cloud, RHEL to Rocky Linux) were shelved.

- **Securing 700 MHz platinum spectrum (awarded October 2023, live June 2024) and a bridging KDDI roaming deal (2023–2026).** This addressed the coverage gap that pricing could not. Coverage quality, not price, was the barrier to switching for mainstream Japanese users.

- **Funding the losses from the parent's crown jewels (2021–2023).** Selling Rakuten Securities stakes to Mizuho, listing Rakuten Bank, and issuing equity kept the group solvent through a bond wall of ¥800 billion due by end-2025. The group chose dilution and partial divestment over abandoning mobile.

- **Refocusing Rakuten Symphony on vendors (February 2025).** Conceding that operators want pre-integrated systems, Rakuten shifted Symphony toward licensing its SymRAN software to Airspan, Cisco and Tech Mahindra, effectively acknowledging it would not displace Ericsson and Nokia.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Rakuten Mobile is the clearest documented case of "importing a playbook without importing the market conditions." Jio's playbook worked in India because the incumbents' networks were bad and demand was pent up; in Japan the incumbents were excellent, so a cheaper price with worse coverage was not a compelling trade. Operators considering a "disrupt on price" entry into a mature market should look at this case before the pitch deck.

It is also a case study in how a founder-led conglomerate can survive a multi-year, ¥1 trillion mistake: by having cash-generative assets to sell (a bank, a brokerage, a supermarket stake), by retaining control to make painful reversals (ending the free tier, replacing the star hire), and by fixing the actual constraint (spectrum) rather than the narrative one (software). The turn to positive EBITDA in 2025 shows the business is salvageable, but the still-open question, five years and ¥1 trillion in, is whether it will ever earn back the capital invested. Founders should note that "EBITDA positive" arrived only after the original go-to-market was largely abandoned, not because of it.
