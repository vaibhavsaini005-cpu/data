## One-liner

Japan's Coincheck lost ~$530 million in NEM tokens to hackers in January 2018 — the largest crypto theft on record at the time — but survived after Monex Group acquired it and eventually listed on Nasdaq via SPAC in 2023.

## What happened

Coincheck was founded in 2012 by Koichiro Wada and Yusuke Otsuka in Tokyo and grew to become one of Japan's most popular cryptocurrency exchanges. On January 26, 2018, hackers exploited Coincheck's hot-wallet storage of NEM (XEM) tokens — the exchange kept roughly 523 million NEM (~$530 million USD at the time) in a single hot wallet without multi-signature security — and drained the entire balance. It remains one of the largest single cryptocurrency thefts in history.

The Japanese Financial Services Agency (FSA) immediately issued business improvement orders and suspended new account registrations and withdrawals. Coincheck used its own reserves to begin compensating affected users at ¥88.549 per NEM (below market price but still a total payout of ~¥46.6 billion / ~$425 million). In April 2018, Tokyo-listed brokerage and fintech group Monex Group acquired Coincheck for approximately ¥3.6 billion (~$33.5 million) — a bargain price that reflected the regulatory risk and reputational damage. Monex overhauled Coincheck's security infrastructure, added multi-signature cold storage, and secured re-registration with the FSA in January 2019. Coincheck grew its user base under Monex stewardship to over 1.7 million registered users by 2022. In December 2023, Coincheck merged with Thunder Bridge Capital Partners IV via SPAC and listed on Nasdaq under ticker CNCK, becoming the first Japanese crypto exchange to reach a US public market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Storing ~$530M in a single hot wallet without multi-signature security was the core operational failure.
- The decision to compensate affected users out of own reserves (rather than declaring insolvency) preserved goodwill and gave Monex confidence to acquire.
- Monex's acquisition at a distressed price was the pivot: same brand, same market, new owner, upgraded compliance posture.
- Pursuing a Nasdaq SPAC listing in 2023 represented the post-pivot growth chapter, giving Coincheck access to US capital markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Coincheck is the canonical example of a crypto exchange "operational failure to acquired rebound" arc: a catastrophic security lapse destroyed the original shareholders' value but did not destroy the business itself, because the brand still had user trust and the FSA framework created a viable acquisition path. The Monex acquisition also illustrates how regulated financial incumbents can absorb distressed fintech assets cheaply during a sector crisis — a pattern repeated across crypto since 2018. The eventual Nasdaq listing shows that a severe failure, properly remediated, can be rebuilt into a credible public company in a 5-year window.
