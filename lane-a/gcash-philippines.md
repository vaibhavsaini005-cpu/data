## One-liner

GCash (operated by Mynt, a Globe Telecom + Ant Group joint venture) grew from a dormant SMS money-transfer service into the Philippines' dominant mobile wallet and the country's first unicorn, reaching $2B valuation in 2021 and $5B by 2022.

## What happened

GCash was originally launched in 2004 as an SMS-based mobile wallet by Globe Telecom, a Philippine telco. For most of its first decade, adoption was modest — the service worked on feature phones but faced low smartphone penetration and a population accustomed to cash.

The turning point came in 2015 when Globe formed Mynt, a fintech joint venture with Ayala Corporation and Ant Financial (now Ant Group). Ant brought the Alipay playbook: QR code merchant acceptance, deep e-commerce integration, and a "super-app" strategy layering insurance, investments, and credit onto the wallet.

COVID-19 was the accelerant: remittances, government Ayuda pandemic cash transfers, and contactless payments all spiked, pulling millions of unbanked Filipinos online for the first time. By January 2021 GCash had raised $175M in a round valuing Mynt above $1B — the Philippines' first unicorn. By mid-2022 it had 60M+ registered users (more than half the adult population) and 5M+ merchant touchpoints. A planned IPO as of 2026 would be the country's largest-ever.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2015** — Globe restructured GCash under Mynt and brought in Ant Financial's ecosystem design expertise.
- **2019** — Launched GInvest (mutual funds), GSave (savings with BPI), and GInsure, making GCash a financial super-app.
- **2020** — Became the primary distribution channel for government pandemic cash aid, onboarding millions of new users overnight.
- **2021** — $175M Series A (January), unicorn status confirmed; subsequent rounds valued Mynt at $5B by 2022.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GCash illustrates the "telco-to-fintech" reboot pattern: a dormant telco product was revived when a super-app operator brought both capital and a proven product architecture. COVID acted as a forced-adoption catalyst, compressing years of organic growth into months. It also shows how government partnerships (Ayuda distribution) can be a startup's most powerful distribution channel in an underbanked market.
