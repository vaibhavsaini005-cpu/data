## One-liner

Dunedin-founded wine-fermentation IoT startup Winely, built by CEO Jacob Manning and CTO Abbe Hyde, was put into liquidation in November 2025 owing more than NZ$2.2 million after its main funder forced an insolvency review.

## What happened

Jacob Manning (CEO) and Abbe Hyde (CTO) founded Winely in Dunedin in 2018 to sell IoT-connected sensors, cloud software and AI-based analysis that gave winemakers real-time visibility into fermentation, an area historically tracked by manual sampling. The company raised a mix of private equity, secured debt, Callaghan Innovation grant funding and a government loan (reported around NZ$400,000) to build out the product and expand, including participation in a South Australian accelerator program to reach Australian wineries.

Repayment of investor funds did not occur on the schedule the company had committed to, and earlier in 2025 its primary funder demanded an independent review of Winely's finances. That review found the company was insolvent, that its remaining inventory and IP had limited recoverable value, and that operations had effectively already stopped. On 17 November 2025, shareholders passed a special resolution appointing Andrew McKay and Rees Logan of BDO Auckland as joint liquidators, with known liabilities exceeding NZ$2.2 million and a creditor claims deadline set for 19 January 2026.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Funding fermentation-monitoring hardware with a mix of equity, secured debt and government grants**: gave Winely runway to build IoT sensors and software for a slow-moving, seasonal industry, but layered debt obligations on top of a capital-intensive hardware business.
- **Missing repayment commitments to its primary investor**: triggered the independent financial review that formally established insolvency, converting a cash-flow problem into a liquidation event.
- **Shareholder resolution to liquidate rather than seek a restructuring or sale**: taken once the review found inventory and IP had limited recoverable value, ending the company outright instead of attempting a wind-down sale of assets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Winely is a case of a hardware-plus-SaaS startup in a niche B2B vertical (winemaking) running out of room to refinance once a funder lost confidence: government grants and accelerator credibility (Callaghan Innovation, the South Australian Landing Pad program) were not enough to offset a debt structure that required on-schedule repayment from a company still building out its market. The independent-review-triggers-liquidation sequence here is a reminder that in small, geographically concentrated verticals, one funder's loss of confidence can become the actual mechanism of failure, regardless of the underlying technology's merit.
