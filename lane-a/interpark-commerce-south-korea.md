## One-liner

Interpark Commerce — the online-retail unit running Interpark Shopping, Interpark Books, and AK Mall — was declared bankrupt by a Seoul court in December 2025 after a payment crisis at sister platforms TMON and WeMakePrice drained its cash and its sellers.

## What happened

Interpark Commerce was one of several e-commerce brands under Singapore-headquartered Qoo10 Group, alongside the open-marketplace platforms TMON and WeMakePrice. In early August 2024, TMON and WeMakePrice defaulted on roughly 1 trillion won (about $737 million) in vendor settlements, triggering South Korea's largest e-commerce payment crisis in years and leaving an estimated 50,000 creditors unpaid. Interpark Commerce was not itself a runaway-marketplace platform in the same way — its 2023 revenue was 34.2 billion won against a 15.7 billion won operating loss, and its monthly transaction volume was a fraction of TMON's or WeMakePrice's — but its unsettled sales proceeds of roughly 55 billion won and its shared ownership under Qoo10 meant the crisis spread to it within two weeks. On August 16, 2024, Interpark Commerce filed for Autonomous Restructuring Support (ARS) with the Seoul Bankruptcy Court, following TMON and WeMakePrice into court protection.

The company spent over a year trying to find a buyer or strategic investor while operating under court-supervised rehabilitation, which formally began in November 2024. No acquirer emerged. In early December 2025 the rehabilitation process was terminated, and on December 16, 2025 the Seoul Bankruptcy Court declared Interpark Commerce bankrupt — one year and four months after its original restructuring filing. Sister companies fared similarly: WeMakePrice was declared bankrupt in November 2025, while TMON was acquired out of the crisis by grocery-delivery platform Oasis in June 2025.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Shared ownership as contagion risk**: Interpark Commerce's own unit economics weren't the proximate cause of its collapse — its exposure came from being financially and structurally tied to TMON and WeMakePrice under the same Qoo10 parent, so their liquidity crisis became its liquidity crisis within days.
- **Filing for court protection early (August 2024) rather than waiting**: Filing 18 days after its sister platforms bought time under legal protection but didn't produce a buyer over 16 months of searching.
- **Rehabilitation without a buyer is a dead end**: Courts terminated the rehabilitation process specifically because no acquisition candidate could be found, converting what started as a restructuring into a liquidation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Interpark Commerce shows how quickly counterparty risk can propagate through a multi-brand e-commerce holding structure: a comparatively smaller, less distressed subsidiary was pulled into bankruptcy by the cash-settlement failures of larger sister platforms it didn't control. It's also a reminder that rehabilitation/ARS filings are not survival guarantees — without a strategic buyer willing to take on a damaged brand and its creditor list, a 16-month court process can still end in liquidation rather than rescue.
