## One-liner

Auckland-founded Futureverse, once pitched as a billion-dollar "open metaverse" roll-up of 11 Web3 startups, was placed into receivership and liquidation on 30 September 2025 with $40 million owed to creditors, after co-founder and director Aaron McDonald's bet on NFTs and metaverse infrastructure collapsed alongside the broader crypto downturn.

## What happened

Aaron McDonald and Shara Senderoff built Futureverse in 2023 by merging 11 separate crypto, gaming, and metaverse companies — including Altered State Machine and the Root Network — into a single entity, raising US$54 million in a Series A led by 10T Holdings with participation from Ripple Labs in July 2023. The founders' thesis was that owning the full stack of an "open metaverse" (identity, AI agents, gaming infrastructure, and a token layer) would let Futureverse capture value as NFTs and Web3 gaming went mainstream.

That market never arrived. As NFT and metaverse demand kept falling through 2023 and 2024, McDonald and co-director Daniel Gillespie cut roughly 20% of headcount in 2023, telling staff the cuts were "necessary to become competitive in tough market conditions," but continued funding development across the rolled-up businesses. By September 2025 the company had burned through its raise: cash on hand was down to roughly NZ$109,000 against creditors owed over NZ$12 million and retained losses of NZ$137.4 million. On 30 September 2025, secured lender 50T Holdings called in its general security deed, and McDonald and Gillespie simultaneously appointed administrators; receivers from Calibre Partners were also appointed the same day. The business was sold off in a fire sale for roughly NZ$7.5 million, and unsecured creditors — including NZ$432,173 owed to Callaghan Innovation and NZ$5.3 million owed to NZ on Air — were told they were unlikely to see any recovery.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Rolling up 11 separate startups into one entity (2023)**: McDonald's bet that combining fragmented Web3 companies into a single "full-stack" metaverse platform would create defensibility instead concentrated the cash burn of 11 businesses into one balance sheet.
- **Continuing to fund development through the 2023-2024 crypto/NFT downturn**: Rather than shrinking to a sustainable core after the 20% layoff, Futureverse kept building across gaming, AI agents, and token infrastructure simultaneously, leaving it with almost no reserve when demand didn't recover.
- **Securing the 50T Holdings facility with a general security deed**: That structure let the lender trigger receivership unilaterally once the company's cash position became critical, removing the founders' ability to control the timeline of any restructuring.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Futureverse is a case study in "roll-up as a survival strategy" backfiring: merging multiple sub-scale, cash-burning startups into one company can look like consolidation and de-risking to investors, but it mainly consolidates the underlying businesses' collective burn rate under one roof. When the market the roll-up was betting on (NFTs/metaverse) failed to recover, the combined entity had no single profitable business to retreat to — unlike a single-product startup that can cut down to a sustainable core, Futureverse's founders had already fused unrelated cost centers together, making a smaller, defensible version of the company much harder to reach once external funding dried up.
