## One-liner

An Auckland utility-and-airport billing software firm nearly unravelled after a post-IPO profit warning and a COVID-driven collapse in its airport customer base, then rebuilt around a new CEO and a cloud/SaaS strategy.

## What happened

Gentrack, founded in 1989 in Auckland, builds billing and customer-management software for energy/water utilities and airport operators (its Airport 20/20 and Veovo products). It listed on the NZX and ASX in 2014. On July 25, 2019, Gentrack issued a profit warning, telling investors annual EBITDA would land at $27-28 million versus $31 million the year prior, blaming delayed customer implementations and rising bad-debt risk among its UK water-utility clients; the stock fell as much as 12% intraday to a three-year low.

The situation worsened through 2020: airport customers — a core Gentrack segment — were hit hard by COVID-era border closures just as the earlier UK utility-customer financial troubles continued to bite, and the share price fell as low as roughly $1.20, a fraction of its post-IPO highs. Facing a business in real distress on two fronts simultaneously, Gentrack's board ran a global executive search and, on September 2, 2020, appointed Gary Miles — previously of cloud/telecom software vendor Amdocs — as CEO, effective October 1, 2020. Miles pushed a shift from project-based licence sales toward recurring cloud/SaaS revenue. The turnaround succeeded commercially: Gentrack's share price and earnings recovered substantially over the following years under the new strategy.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Two compounding shocks, not one**: A 2019 UK-utility bad-debt and delivery-delay problem was still unresolved when COVID hit Gentrack's airport customer base in 2020, pushing the stock to roughly half of even its post-warning 2019 price.
- **Leadership change as the pivot mechanism**: Rather than a product pivot, the company changed course by bringing in an outside cloud-software executive (Gary Miles, ex-Amdocs) explicitly to re-architect the business model.
- **License-to-SaaS model shift**: The turnaround strategy centered on converting Gentrack's revenue base from one-off project licences to recurring subscription/cloud revenue, a structural change to how the company earns money, not just who runs it.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Gentrack shows how a niche, genuinely mission-critical B2B software vendor (utilities and airports rarely switch billing systems) can survive back-to-back demand shocks that would sink a consumer business, because its embedded customer base doesn't disappear even when near-term revenue does. It's also a clean example of a turnaround executed primarily through a CEO/strategy change rather than a product or market pivot — the company kept selling to the same two verticals, but changed how it monetized them.
