## One-liner

Tokyo-based personal finance app that pivoted into Japan's leading financial data API platform, powering two of three megabanks and 100+ enterprise clients.

## What happened

Moneytree was founded in Tokyo in April 2012 by American co-founders Ross Sharrott and Mark Makdad alongside Paul Chapman. The company launched a free iOS personal finance app in 2013 that aggregated bank accounts, credit cards, loans, and loyalty points in one view — winning Apple Japan's "Best App" award after just 8 months. Despite solid consumer traction, the founders recognized a deeper infrastructure gap: Japan's financial data ecosystem was siloed, with no standard APIs connecting banks, accountants, tax software, or fintech tools. In 2015 they launched Moneytree LINK, a financial data aggregation API connecting over 2,500 financial accounts. The consumer app continued running, but LINK became the primary revenue driver. Salesforce, the venture arms of all three Japanese megabanks (MUFG, SMBC, Mizuho), and SBI Investment backed the pivot across six funding rounds totalling $45M. By the mid-2020s, Moneytree was Japan's #1 provider of financial APIs to the financial services industry.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2013**: Consumer iOS app wins App Store "Best of" Japan — validates demand for consolidated personal finance data and builds user trust.
- **2015**: Moneytree LINK API launches in Japan — a deliberate B2B pivot targeting accountants, enterprise software firms, and financial institutions rather than end consumers.
- **2017**: LINK expands to Australia, validating the platform model internationally.
- **Megabank buy-in**: Investment from all three Japanese megabanks' VC arms was a public signal that Japan's traditional finance sector endorsed LINK as shared infrastructure rather than a threat.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Moneytree illustrates the "consumer app as proof-of-concept, API as the real business" playbook. Japan's regulatory and banking fragmentation made data aggregation a genuine infrastructure gap — the consumer app provided a data moat and credibility, while the B2B API monetized the underlying plumbing. The decision not to abandon the consumer layer helped maintain trust with end-users while the platform scaled. It also shows how incumbents (megabanks) can become the growth engine rather than the obstacle once they recognize an API provider as a utility they need.
