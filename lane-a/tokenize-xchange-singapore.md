## One-liner

Tokenize Xchange, a Singapore cryptocurrency exchange operated by AmazingTech Pte Ltd, collapsed in mid-2025 after the Monetary Authority of Singapore rejected its long-pending Payment Services Act licence application, leaving customers owed S$266.3 million against just S$2.6 million in realisable assets.

## What happened

Tokenize Xchange had operated as a cryptocurrency trading platform in Singapore under a temporary exemption while its formal licence application under the Payment Services Act 2019 remained pending with the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) — a regulatory limbo status shared by numerous crypto firms during Singapore's extended licensing transition period. When MAS rejected the company's licence application in July 2025, citing concerns that AmazingTech might lack sufficient assets, had mishandled client funds, and had misled regulators on asset segregation, the company's operations collapsed almost instantly.

Court-appointed interim judicial managers subsequently uncovered the severity of the shortfall: AmazingTech owed customers approximately S$266.3 million while holding only S$2.6 million in realisable assets — a gap the managers attributed to commingling of customer deposits with corporate funds, a fundamental breach of the asset-segregation practices crypto exchanges are expected to maintain. The fallout extended into the courts: more than 270 former customers filed a collective High Court lawsuit in November 2025 seeking S$60.5 million in damages from founder Hong Qi Yu and his wife, former chief operating officer Erin Koo Kee Hoon.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Operated for an extended period under a temporary regulatory exemption rather than securing a full licence, leaving the business exposed to sudden operational collapse the moment that exemption ended with a licence rejection.
- Commingled customer and corporate funds rather than maintaining strict asset segregation, directly causing the catastrophic gap between customer obligations (S$266.3M) and actual recoverable assets (S$2.6M) once the shortfall was formally investigated.
- MAS's decision to reject the licence application — rather than granting conditional approval or an extended remediation period — triggered immediate operational shutdown, reflecting how binding a single regulatory determination can be for an exchange operating without a permanent licence.
- Affected customers pursued a collective lawsuit directly against the founder and former COO personally, rather than only pursuing claims against the company entity, reflecting the severity of the alleged fund mishandling.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tokenize Xchange's collapse illustrates the acute fragility of crypto exchanges operating under temporary regulatory exemptions rather than full licences: a single adverse licensing decision can trigger near-instantaneous operational collapse, with none of the wind-down runway a more gradual business failure might allow. The catastrophic mismatch between customer obligations and recoverable assets — traced to fund commingling — also underscores why regulators like MAS scrutinize asset segregation so closely for crypto platforms: without it, a shortfall that might otherwise be a manageable liquidity problem instead becomes an near-total loss for depositors, since commingled funds offer no protective barrier between customer holdings and the operating entity's own financial distress.
