## One-liner

Ridi, founded in 2008 as a Korean e-book service, became the country's first content-platform unicorn in February 2022 on a $100M round from Singapore's GIC, then kept growing revenue to a record 235.4 billion KRW (~$161M) in 2024 while pushing its Manta webtoon app into the U.S. market.

## What happened

Ridi Corp. started out as "Korea's Kindle," pioneering the domestic e-book market and holding the No. 1 position there even as competitors entered. It later expanded into webtoons, launching the global subscription webcomics service Manta Comics in November 2020. On February 28, 2022, Ridi announced a 120 billion won (~$100 million) funding round led by Singapore's sovereign wealth fund GIC alongside three domestic institutional investors, valuing the company at 1.6 trillion won (~$1.3 billion) and making it the first unicorn among Korea's content platforms — with GIC becoming Ridi's first foreign shareholder.

Three years later, Ridi's 2024 annual results (reported April 8, 2025) showed record revenue of 235.4 billion KRW (~$161 million), even as the company posted an operating loss of 12.9 billion KRW (~$8.8 million), which it attributed to continued investment in original content creation and global expansion. Manta, its webtoon arm (which launched in-house content studio Manta Studios in 2023), became the No. 1 comics app on Google Play in the U.S., Mexico, and Spain during this period.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **E-books to webtoons**: rather than defend its e-book leadership alone, Ridi built a second product line (Manta) around serialized webcomics, a format with stronger global export potential than Korean-language e-books.
- **Taking foreign institutional capital**: bringing in GIC as its first foreign shareholder in 2022 gave Ridi both the unicorn valuation and capital to fund international expansion rather than staying domestically focused.
- **Prioritizing growth investment over near-term profit**: the 2024 operating loss was a deliberate trade-off — plowing record revenue back into content production and international markets (Manta Studios, U.S./Latin America expansion) rather than optimizing for immediate margin.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ridi's path shows a domestic-content incumbent using a single new product line (Manta) as the vehicle for both a unicorn valuation and genuine international expansion, and being willing to accept an operating loss at record revenue as the visible cost of that expansion rather than treating it as a warning sign. It's a useful comparison point for other content-platform success stories in this dataset where global growth is funded by reinvesting scale gained at home.
