## One-liner

Vietnam's "Uber for trucks" that raised $5.5M from GGV Capital in 2018 and shut down in 2020 after failing to displace offline broker networks that provided credit and trust relationships the platform could not replicate.

## What happened

Logivan was founded in 2017 in Ho Chi Minh City by Linh Pham and Chau Nguyen as a digital freight marketplace connecting shippers with truck drivers across Vietnam. The platform offered real-time load matching, route optimization, and digital tracking — features absent from the incumbent broker networks that dominated Vietnam's fragmented trucking industry. In July 2018, Logivan raised a $5.5M Series A led by GGV Capital, a significant international validation for a Vietnamese logistics startup. The company achieved early enterprise traction by targeting corporate shippers who valued the visibility tools. However, the core marketplace economics did not hold: truckers in Vietnam's freight market expected cash advances and payment on delivery from brokers, a credit intermediation function the platform did not provide. Displacement of offline brokers — who had multi-year personal credit relationships with truckers — proved far harder than anticipated. COVID-19 disrupted freight demand at exactly the moment Logivan needed to demonstrate profitability, and the company shut down operations in late 2020.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Focused on enterprise shippers (B2B) for early revenue, which delivered volume but created dependency on a small set of accounts with high pricing pressure
- Did not build payment-terms or cash-advance features for truckers — the platform competed on convenience but not on the financial services that made brokers indispensable
- Chose not to pivot to a SaaS-for-brokers model or an asset-light hybrid that co-opted the existing broker network rather than displacing it
- COVID-19 (Q1–Q2 2020) collapsed freight demand precisely when the company's cash position required growth to justify bridge funding

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Logivan is a clean failure case for "Uber for X" models in markets where trust and credit intermediation are inseparable from the core service. When a marketplace cannot also extend credit, absorb payment-timing risk, and maintain personal relationships, it competes only on workflow convenience — which is rarely sufficient to displace incumbents who provide all three. The lesson is specific to freight and logistics in emerging markets, where cash flow gaps are as much of the product as load matching.
