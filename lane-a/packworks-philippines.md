## One-liner

Three friends noticed neighborhood sari-sari store owners were still running inventory and credit on pen and paper during a motorcycle trip to deliver solar panels — and built a mobile ERP that now runs on top of hundreds of thousands of those stores, with the Philippine government eventually funding its AI roadmap.

## What happened

Hubert Yap, Bing Tan and Ibba Bernardo founded Packworks in 2018 after a motorcycle trip through remote Philippine communities to deliver solar panels, where they observed sari-sari store owners — the small neighborhood retailers that form the backbone of Philippine informal retail — struggling to track inventory, manage bookkeeping and restock supplies, all still done on paper ledgers. The founders built a mobile enterprise resource planning (ERP) platform aimed specifically at that segment: inventory tracking, bookkeeping, supply ordering and, over time, financial-access features, built for a merchant base that mainstream retail software largely ignored.

The company grew organically and bootstrapped for several years before raising a $2 million seed round in July 2022, led by logistics group Fast Group and global private equity firm CVC Capital Partners, with ADB Ventures, Arise, Techstars and IdeaSpace Foundation also participating — funding explicitly earmarked to scale the platform and connect its retailer network to financial institutions and consumer brands. At the time of that round, TechCrunch reported Packworks already had roughly 150,000 sari-sari stores on its platform, with an internal target of 220,000 by end of 2022 and 500,000 by 2023, tracking around 9,000 SKUs across the network. The company kept building toward that retailer base and, in July 2024, formalized a partnership with the Philippine Department of Science and Technology (DOST), securing PHP-denominated government funding (reported as roughly $60,000) to develop an AI-powered precision-marketing tool for sari-sari retailers — by that point integrated into Packworks' consumer-facing Sari.PH Pro app, which Manila Bulletin reported was then serving over 270,000 sari-sari retailers.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Choosing the most under-served, unglamorous merchant segment (sari-sari stores) rather than a venture-fashionable one.** Building specifically for stores still running pen-and-paper ledgers meant near-zero direct software competition, at the cost of a customer base with very low individual willingness/ability to pay — solved by monetizing the aggregate network (financial access, brand partnerships) rather than per-store software fees alone.
- **Bootstrapping through the founding years before raising in 2022.** Growing to roughly 150,000 stores before taking outside capital gave Packworks real usage data and network scale to raise from strategic investors (Fast Group, a logistics group) rather than pure financial VCs.
- **Formalizing a government partnership (DOST, 2024) rather than staying purely private-sector-funded.** Bringing in public funding for the AI-marketing roadmap extended Packworks' resources without diluting further, and gave it institutional credibility with the exact demographic (small, often older, small-town retailers) that's hardest for a private startup to win trust with alone.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Packworks is a pattern for founders weighing a "boring," low-margin-per-unit customer segment against a flashier one: the informal-retail merchant base that looked unmonetizable at the individual-store level became valuable in aggregate, once the platform had enough stores on it to be worth financial institutions' and brands' attention. It also shows a viable sequencing for emerging-market B2B software — bootstrap to real network scale on the unglamorous problem first, then use that scale (not a pitch deck) to raise both private capital and, later, public-sector funding.
