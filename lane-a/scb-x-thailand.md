## One-liner

Thailand's oldest bank delisted itself and re-emerged in 2022 as SCB X, a "mothership" holding company that would spin the bank down to one subsidiary among a dozen fintech, lending, crypto and venture units and reach 200 million customers and a 1 trillion baht valuation; within three years it had abandoned its flagship crypto acquisition, sold its loss-making super-app for a fraction of what it burned, and reset the strategy around cost discipline, AI and a licensed virtual bank.

## What happened

**The setup (2021).** Siam Commercial Bank (SCB), founded in 1906 and Thailand's fourth-largest lender by assets, had spent five years under CEO Arthid Nanthawithaya on a digital transformation programme, but was still valued like a Thai bank: a regulated, slow-growth balance sheet in a market where Grab, Sea, LINE and the telcos were building financial products on top of consumer apps. Arthid's diagnosis was that a bank licence had become a constraint. The Bank of Thailand limits what a bank can own and how much capital it can put into non-bank ventures, and a bank's cost base and culture were not built to launch consumer tech products at speed.

**The decision (22 September 2021).** SCB announced the "mothership" plan: create a new listed holding company, SCB X, invite shareholders to swap their SCB shares one-for-one into SCB X, delist the bank and list the holding company in its place. The bank would become a subsidiary, and beside it SCB X would own a growing portfolio of "new growth" businesses. The stated goal was to become a regional financial technology company worth more than 1 trillion baht with a customer base of over 200 million by around 2025, many times the bank's existing domestic customer base. To fund the new ventures the bank paid a special dividend up the chain of about 70 billion baht. The extraordinary shareholder meeting approved the plan in late 2021 and SCB X began trading on the Stock Exchange of Thailand in April 2022.

**What was built.** SCB X organised its ventures into three models. Direct subsidiaries carved out of the bank: CardX (credit cards and unsecured personal loans) and AutoX (title loans under the "Ngern Chaiyo" brand). Investments through new vehicles: SCB 10X (venture capital and blockchain) and Token X (digital asset tokenisation and ICO portal). Joint ventures: AISCB with telco AIS for digital lending, Alpha X with Millennium Group for premium auto finance, and SCB TechX with Publicis Sapient for technology consulting. On top sat DataX, MONIX (AI-driven digital lending), InnovestX (the rebranded securities arm), PointX (loyalty) and Purple Ventures, operator of the Robinhood food-delivery app the bank had launched in 2020 as a zero-commission, CSR-flavoured answer to Grab and LINE MAN.

**The crypto bet and retreat (November 2021 to August 2022).** Six weeks after the mothership announcement, on 2 November 2021, SCB X's board approved buying 51% of Bitkub Online, Thailand's largest cryptocurrency exchange, for 17.85 billion baht (about $537 million), valuing Bitkub at roughly 35 billion baht and making it the country's first crypto unicorn. Bitcoin peaked near $68,000 that month. Over the following nine months crypto prices collapsed, Terra and Celsius failed, and Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission issued a series of orders against Bitkub over system outages and market conduct. On 25 August 2022 SCB X withdrew, stating that Bitkub was resolving SEC issues on an uncertain timeframe, while adding that due diligence had found no "significant abnormal issues which are irremediable". The withdrawal was framed as mutual; in practice it ended the most visible symbol of the fintech-conglomerate ambition.

**The super-app bet and retreat (2020 to September 2024).** Robinhood had been launched during COVID as a social initiative: no commission for restaurants, fast payouts, riders recruited from the pandemic unemployed. Under SCB X it was pushed to become a super-app with ride-hailing, travel booking, a grocery mart and express delivery. Losses at Purple Ventures escalated every year: 87.8 million baht in 2020, 1,335 million in 2021, 1,986 million in 2022 and 2,155 million in 2023, roughly 5.6 billion baht cumulatively. On 25 June 2024 the SCB X board voted to shut the app on 31 July, saying Robinhood had "completed its mission" of relieving COVID hardship and that closure reflected "sound capital management aimed at maximizing shareholders' returns". The Travel, Ride, Mart and Express services closed as scheduled, but food delivery was kept alive when unsolicited offers arrived. On 30 September 2024 SCB X sold all of Purple Ventures to a consortium led by IT services group Yip In Tsoi, with Brooker Group, SCT Rental Car and Loxley's LOXBIT, for 400 million baht upfront and up to 1.6 billion baht more contingent on performance. Arthid set one condition: the buyer had to be a Thai group committed to keeping Robinhood a Thai platform.

**The reset (2024 to 2025).** Through 2024 Arthid reframed the group. At a Kaohoon "Battle Strategy" conference on 25 October 2024 he declared SCB X an "AI-first organisation", targeting 75% of group revenue to be AI-enabled by 2027 or 2028, training 20,000 employees, directing about 60% of a multi-billion-baht operating budget to technology, and cutting the commercial bank's cost-to-income ratio from above 40% towards 25%. The FY2024 results, published 21 January 2025, showed consolidated net profit of 43,943 million baht, up just 1.0%, and a 2025 plan built on "continuous growth from our existing businesses, particularly in consumer finance, by efficiently managing costs and improving debt collection". The one large new bet that survived is regulatory rather than venture-style: on 19 June 2025 the Bank of Thailand and Ministry of Finance awarded SCB X's consortium with China's WeBank and South Korea's KakaoBank one of three virtual bank licences, alongside Krungthai/AIS/PTT and CP Group's Ascend Money. SCB X created BankX Bank, holds 90% of it, and must be operational within a year of the approval.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Delisting the bank to escape bank-licence constraints (Arthid Nanthawithaya and the SCB board, September 2021).** This is the structural choice that everything else follows from. A holding company above the bank lets the group deploy capital into ventures the Bank of Thailand would not let a bank own directly, and lets each venture carry its own cost base, culture and eventually its own valuation. It was the first such restructuring by a major Thai bank and was explicitly pitched to shareholders as a way to be valued as a tech company. The 70 billion baht dividend up the chain is what made the venture portfolio fundable without diluting shareholders.

- **Announcing the customer and valuation targets in public (September 2021).** Setting "200 million customers and 1 trillion baht" as the headline, with a 2025 horizon, anchored the market's expectations to numbers that required regional expansion and multiple breakout consumer products. When the ventures did not deliver, the targets became the yardstick the pivot was judged against.

- **Betting on crypto at the top of the cycle, then walking away (board approval 2 November 2021; withdrawal 25 August 2022).** The Bitkub deal was the clearest expression of the "fintech conglomerate" thesis, and its collapse the clearest signal that the thesis was being repriced. The decision to cite regulatory timing rather than valuation preserved optionality and relationships, but the group never returned to a majority exchange stake.

- **Choosing to shut, then sell, Robinhood (board vote 25 June 2024; sale 30 September 2024).** After four years and about 5.6 billion baht of losses, the board chose capital discipline over the super-app narrative. The reversal from shutdown to sale within weeks, and the earn-out structure, show the group extracting residual value rather than writing the asset to zero.

- **Reframing the strategy around AI and cost, and the virtual bank licence (October 2024 to June 2025).** The pivot-within-the-pivot: the mothership structure was kept, but the growth engine shifted from launching consumer products to embedding AI in the existing lending and banking businesses, and from acquisitions to a regulated greenfield bank with foreign digital-bank partners who bring the technology.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SCB X is the reference case for **an incumbent restructuring itself to be valued like a disruptor**. The holding-company move solved a real constraint (regulatory limits on what a bank can own) and is still in place. What did not survive contact with the market was the assumption that the freed-up capital could buy or build consumer breakouts quickly. The crypto exchange was bought at the cycle top and abandoned; the super-app burned more than five billion baht competing against Grab and LINE MAN, both of whom had scale and a non-financial reason to exist. Operators will recognise the pattern from telcos and retailers that spun up "ventures" arms in 2021: the structure was right, the portfolio was built at the wrong point in the cycle, and the eventual survivors were the businesses adjacent to the core (consumer lending, cards, title loans, data) rather than the ones furthest from it.

Second, it shows **why public targets are a strategic decision, not a communications one**. "200 million customers by 2025" was a regional ambition attached to a domestic bank. It set the bar that the 1% profit growth of 2024 was measured against and made the AI-and-cost reset read as retreat even though it was arguably the more disciplined plan.

Third, the Robinhood arc is a clean example of **mission creep in a corporate venture**: a zero-commission COVID relief app with a clear, bounded purpose was extended into ride-hailing, travel and grocery because the parent needed a super-app story, not because the unit economics supported it. The exit, a Thai-only buyer condition and an earn-out, was the founder-style move of protecting the brand while cutting the losses.
