## One-liner

Wellington fintech that automates the entire tax, GST, and invoicing lifecycle for self-employed contractors, removing the need for an accountant — raised NZ$35M Series B in 2022 and expanded to Australia.

## What happened

Hnry was founded in 2017 in Wellington by brothers James Fuller and Karan Anand after experiencing firsthand the complexity of tax compliance for self-employed contractors in New Zealand. The core product integrates directly with the IRD (NZ Inland Revenue Department) to automatically calculate, deduct, and remit income tax, GST, and ACC levies on every invoice payment received — leaving the contractor with cleared, post-tax pay and nothing to file at year end. The company raised a NZ$35M (~$21M USD) Series B in August 2022 led by Hillfarrance Venture Partners, with participation from Blackbird and other investors. The capital funded an expansion into Australia (ATO integration) and product development for more complex contractor scenarios. By the time of the raise, Hnry was processing hundreds of millions of NZD in contractor payments annually for tens of thousands of customers.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built direct integrations with IRD and later ATO instead of a software wrapper around accounting tools — the regulatory integration became the core moat
- Priced as a percentage of income (1.5% of invoiced income, capped) rather than a flat SaaS subscription — aligned incentives with the contractor and lowered trial friction
- Partnered with Xero, which gave access to an existing population of self-employed Xero users without paid CAC
- Chose Australia as the first international market because of regulatory similarity and cultural proximity before considering harder markets

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Hnry is a template for regulatory-moat fintech in small-but-high-compliance markets: most founders avoid building government-API-layer products because the integrations are tedious and jurisdiction-specific, but that tedium is precisely the moat. Once a product becomes the licensed, IRD-integrated compliance layer, switching costs are extremely high for users who have entrusted their tax history to it.
