## One-liner

Lenddo, the Philippine fintech pioneer that used Facebook and social media data to score the creditworthiness of the unbanked, pivoted from P2P lending to a pure B2B data-scoring business and merged with psychometric credit firm EFL Global in October 2017.

## What happened

Lenddo was founded in 2011 by Jeff Stewart and Tim Mayopoulos, initially operating out of the Philippines (with regional offices in Hong Kong and Colombia). Its core innovation was controversial but powerful: rather than relying on traditional credit bureau data — which covered less than 5% of the Philippine population — Lenddo analysed borrowers' social media networks, digital behaviour, and telco data to predict creditworthiness and verify identity. If a borrower defaulted, their social connections were notified, creating reputational pressure to repay.

The company originated consumer loans, but by 2014–2015 it recognised that its true value lay in the underlying scoring technology, not in carrying credit risk itself. Lenddo discontinued its direct-to-consumer lending product and relaunched as a B2B technology provider, selling its alternative credit-scoring and identity-verification APIs to banks, telcos, and financial institutions across more than 20 emerging markets.

In October 2017, Lenddo merged with EFL Global (Entrepreneurial Finance Lab), a Harvard spin-out that used psychometric questionnaires to assess borrowers' character, capacity, and willingness to repay. The combined entity, LenddoEFL, offered banks a blended suite: EFL's psychometric assessments layered with Lenddo's social/digital data signals.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Dropping consumer lending (2014–2015):** The decision to stop originating loans and become a pure data/API company was the defining pivot — moving off the balance sheet and toward software margins.
- **Reputational-pressure model abandoned:** The "notify your Facebook friends if you default" mechanic attracted significant privacy criticism. Quietly dropping it was essential for regulatory acceptance and institutional bank partnerships.
- **EFL merger (2017):** Combining psychometric data (EFL's strength in low-literacy populations) with social/digital data (Lenddo's strength) created a more defensible, multi-signal scoring product for global financial institutions.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lenddo's trajectory is a classic "find the actual value" pivot: the company that appeared to be in lending discovered it was really in data intelligence. The merger with EFL is a case study in complementary-capability M&A — two companies each covering a different signal type for the same underserved population, stronger together than either alone. The privacy tensions in Lenddo's original model also prefigure broader debates about alternative data use in credit that regulators worldwide are still working through.
