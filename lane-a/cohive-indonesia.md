## One-liner

Once Jakarta's dominant coworking operator with roughly 29% of the city's CBD flexible-office footprint, CoHive was declared bankrupt by the Central Jakarta District Court in early 2023 after shrinking from 30 locations to 6.

## What happened

CoHive, operated by PT Evi Asia Tenggara, was founded in 2015 and grew into Indonesia's largest coworking-space provider, at one point occupying around 29% of flexible office space in Jakarta's central business district (per 2019 JLL Indonesia data) and raising roughly $37M in total funding from investors including SoftBank Ventures Korea, East Ventures, and Insignia Venture Partners. By October 2022, DealStreetAsia reported CoHive was preparing to shut most or all of its locations. The pandemic hit the coworking model hard industry-wide, and CoHive's rapid pre-pandemic expansion into an oversupplied Jakarta office market left it overextended once demand and fundraising dried up: the company had fallen from 30 locations in 2020 to just 6 by the time of its collapse, and had slipped from market leader to a top-five player by early 2022.

Creditors filed against the company after it failed to resolve its liabilities during a court-supervised debt-suspension (PKPU) period, and the Central Jakarta District Court formally declared CoHive bankrupt in early February 2023. A court-appointed team took over management of the company's remaining assets for confiscation and auction to repay debts. CoHive itself cited the prolonged pandemic, an unfavorable office-supply environment, and a challenging fundraising climate as the drivers of its collapse.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Aggressive pre-pandemic expansion**: Scaling to 30 locations positioned CoHive as market leader by 2019 but left it exposed to fixed lease costs when demand cratered.
- **PKPU (debt-suspension) route rather than restructuring success**: CoHive attempted a court-supervised suspension of payments to negotiate with creditors, but couldn't resolve its liabilities in that window, which converted the case into a formal bankruptcy.
- **Down to 6 locations before the ruling**: The company had already effectively wound down most of its footprint by October 2022, months before the court's bankruptcy declaration — the ruling formalized a collapse that was already visible operationally.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

CoHive is a cautionary case for asset-heavy, lease-dependent business models (coworking, similar to WeWork globally) funded on venture timelines: capital-intensive physical expansion financed by equity rounds is brittle when both real-estate demand and follow-on fundraising contract simultaneously, as happened across Southeast Asia's tech sector in 2022-2023. Its fall from ~29% market share to bankruptcy in roughly three years also shows how fast leadership positions in physical-space businesses can erode once unit economics turn negative.
