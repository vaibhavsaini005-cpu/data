## One-liner

VinFast, the VinGroup-backed Vietnamese EV maker, shipped its first cars to the US in 2022 chasing a flagship American launch, racked up $9.8 billion in losses and a collapsing dealer network, and has now pivoted its growth bet to Southeast Asia, India, and the Gulf instead.

## What happened

VinFast began as VinGroup's automotive arm and shipped its first electric vehicles to the United States in 2022, treating an American beachhead as the proof point for a global EV brand out of Vietnam. The US launch drew poor sales and disappointing reviews, and by late 2025 the company had closed at least two of its roughly 22 US dealerships with a third expected to follow, shrinking its US retail footprint to fewer than two dozen active locations — and only 17 of the remaining dealers were even listing inventory. VinFast currently sells just two models in the US, the VF8 and VF9, and the scheduled expiration of federal EV tax incentives was expected to pressure American demand further. Cumulative losses reached $9.8 billion.

Rather than persist with a US-first strategy, VinFast redirected its capital and manufacturing investment toward Asia and the Gulf. In Indonesia and the Philippines it is replicating the domestic Vietnam playbook: launching its Xanh SM e-taxi service, building local assembly plants (with the Indonesian plant targeting 50,000 units a year), and pricing below competitors like BYD. In India, it is racing to establish a market position ahead of its Tamil Nadu factory coming online in mid-2025, targeting upper-middle-class buyers with C-segment SUVs. In the Gulf, it is pursuing a lighter-touch luxury-segment approach through dealership and investment agreements, without building local manufacturing or taxi operations there. A company spokesperson said VinFast aims to break even in 2026.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Leading with the US market first**: Treating a notoriously difficult, brand-saturated EV market as the proving ground before winning easier, closer-to-home markets meant burning capital against strong incumbents (Tesla, legacy automakers) rather than establishing scale where VinFast already had manufacturing and brand advantages.
- **Cutting losses on US retail rather than doubling down**: Closing dealerships and shrinking the network instead of maintaining the earlier public commitment to US expansion, once inventory and sales data made the strategy's cost untenable.
- **Replicating the Vietnam vertically-integrated playbook abroad**: Reusing the same model — local assembly, an in-house ride-hailing/taxi channel (Xanh SM) to seed demand, and pricing below Chinese competitors — in Indonesia and the Philippines instead of designing a new go-to-market for each region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

VinFast's story is a caution against sequencing global expansion by prestige rather than by winnability: entering the US first because it's the marquee market, rather than entering adjacent Southeast Asian markets where the company's cost structure, taxi-seeded demand model, and brand recognition travel better. The pivot also shows that a company can absorb a multi-billion-dollar strategic misstep and still redeploy — VinGroup's balance sheet support let VinFast retreat from the US without an outright failure, something a less-capitalized independent startup likely couldn't survive.
