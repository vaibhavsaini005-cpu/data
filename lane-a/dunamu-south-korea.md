## One-liner

Dunamu founder and chairman Song Chi-hyung took the operator of Korea's dominant crypto exchange Upbit out of standalone independence, agreeing on 27 November 2025 to a comprehensive share-swap merger with Naver Financial rather than continue growing Upbit alone.

## What happened

Song Chi-hyung founded Dunamu in 2012 (originally an e-book and news-aggregation business before pivoting to crypto with Upbit's 2017 launch) and has chaired it since stepping back from the CEO role that year. On 27 November 2025, Dunamu's and Naver Financial's boards approved a comprehensive stock swap under which Dunamu becomes part of the Naver group, with a follow-up joint release on 28 November 2025 confirming the share-swap terms. Song, still Dunamu's largest shareholder, chose consolidation into a Naver-affiliated financial platform over keeping Upbit as an independent crypto exchange — explicitly framing it as a response to competitive pressure rather than a distress sale: "To avoid falling behind global digital finance trends, Dunamu and Naver Financial need rapid decision-making and bold initiatives that global competitors have yet to attempt." He added the combined entity would "build a new global platform model that extends beyond payments and settlements to encompass the full spectrum of financial services and, ultimately, everyday lifestyle services."

## Key decisions / turning points

- 2017: Song steps down as CEO to chairman, but retains control as Dunamu's founder and largest shareholder — the position from which he later decides the merger.
- 27 November 2025: Dunamu and Naver Financial boards approve the share-swap merger; Song publicly frames it as a deliberate strategic bet on speed and scale, not a fallback.
- 28 November 2025: Joint press release confirms the deal and reiterates Song's rationale for folding Upbit into a broader AI-and-blockchain financial platform under Naver.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

This is a founder choosing to give up outright independence for a dominant, profitable exchange at the moment of maximum leverage rather than waiting to be forced into a deal from a position of weakness — a specific, dated bet that scale-through-merger beats staying solo in a market he says is moving too fast for a standalone player to keep pace with global competitors.
