## One-liner

Roojai, a Bangkok-based direct-to-consumer online insurer founded in 2015, closed a $60M Series C in November 2025 to expand its Indonesian business and pursue M&A, a rare late-stage vote of confidence in an otherwise cautious Southeast Asian funding market.

## What happened

Roojai was founded in 2015 by CEO Nicolas Faquet as a direct-to-consumer online motor insurance platform in Thailand and has since expanded into health, personal accident, and travel insurance, becoming a reference brand for online motor insurance in the country. In November 2025, the company closed a $60 million Series C round co-led by Apis Global Growth Fund III / Apis Growth Markets Fund III (managed by Apis Partners) and Asia Partners, with continued participation from existing shareholders HDI International, Primary Group, and the International Finance Corporation (IFC). The company said the capital would fund continued growth in Thailand, accelerate its expansion into Indonesia, and support further strategic M&A.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Digital-only distribution from day one**: building as a direct-to-consumer online insurer, rather than through Thailand's traditional agent networks, let Roojai scale customer acquisition and underwriting data collection in a way legacy insurers structurally couldn't replicate quickly.
- **Category expansion before geographic expansion**: Roojai broadened from motor insurance into health, personal accident, and travel products inside Thailand first, building a multi-line base before committing fresh capital to a second country (Indonesia).
- **Raising a large late-stage round in a down market**: closing a $60M Series C in November 2025, when regional VCs were broadly pulling back from Southeast Asia, signaled investors saw Roojai's unit economics as differentiated from the growth-at-all-costs cohort that had scared off late-stage capital elsewhere in the region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Roojai's round stands out precisely because of when it happened: Southeast Asian late-stage funding was thin through 2025, with investors rewarding vertical depth (a proprietary underwriting/distribution model in one category) over horizontal marketplace breadth. It's a useful counter-example to this lane's insurtech/fintech failure and pivot stories — proof that a narrowly-scoped, digitally-native insurance model can still raise substantial late-stage capital when broader regional sentiment is risk-off.
