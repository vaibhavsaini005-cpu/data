## One-liner

Tokyo AI ad-tech startup Three Eyes built a natural-language ad-matching network called CANDY and drew backing from megabank- and brokerage-affiliated VCs, but an accounting scandal cut off its funding pipeline and the company filed for bankruptcy in August 2023 with about ¥650 million in debt.

## What happened

Three Eyes Inc. was founded in 2011 and incorporated in April 2015, based in Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo, under representative Takahiro Makinouchi. The company built CANDY, an AI-driven web advertising matching and auto-delivery network aimed at mail-order and e-commerce companies, along with an embeddable on-site recommendation widget called the CANDY module. Riding the broader AI investment wave, it attracted funding starting in 2018 from venture capital arms affiliated with a major brokerage, a megabank-linked investment firm, and a large mail-order retailer — high-credibility institutional backers for a company of its size. Reported sales reached roughly ¥1.5 billion for the fiscal year ending February 2023.

The company's trajectory turned in February 2019, when accounting irregularities emerged and damaged its standing with existing and prospective investors. Even after the company corrected its financials, the reputational damage meant fresh capital dried up while development costs for CANDY kept mounting, creating a widening cash-flow gap that ordinary revenue couldn't close. On August 23, 2023, the Tokyo District Court approved Three Eyes' bankruptcy filing, with total debts of approximately ¥650 million and Kanade Sasaki of Mori Hamada & Matsumoto appointed as bankruptcy trustee.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Taking on high-profile institutional VC money early**: Backing from brokerage-, megabank-, and retailer-affiliated investors from 2018 onward raised the reputational stakes of any governance failure far higher than an ordinary seed-stage cap table would have.
- **The 2019 accounting irregularities**: Once financial irregularities surfaced, the company's ability to raise follow-on capital was permanently impaired even though the underlying numbers were later corrected — trust, once lost with institutional investors, didn't return with the restated financials.
- **Continuing to fund CANDY's development through the funding drought**: Rather than sharply cutting product spend once external capital dried up post-2019, the company kept investing in development, extending its runway problem for four more years until it became unsustainable.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Three Eyes shows that in venture-backed markets, a governance failure can be more fatal than a product or market failure — the company had real revenue (¥1.5 billion in its final full fiscal year) and a working product, but lost access to capital the moment institutional investors stopped trusting its numbers, four and a half years before the actual bankruptcy filing. It's also a reminder that "AI" branding and blue-chip-adjacent VC backing (brokerage, megabank, and retailer-affiliated funds) don't insulate a startup from a slow-motion cash-flow death once the trust that originally unlocked that capital is gone.
