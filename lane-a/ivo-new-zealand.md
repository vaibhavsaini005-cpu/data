## One-liner

Ivo, founded in New Zealand by Min-Kyu Jung and Jacob Duligall to automate in-house legal contract review, grew annual recurring revenue 500% in a year and raised a US$55 million Series B led by Blackbird in January 2026 — at a valuation five times higher than nine months earlier, with IBM, Uber, Shopify, Reddit and Atlassian on the customer list and the headquarters now in San Francisco.

## What happened

Ivo was founded in New Zealand by Min-Kyu Jung, who serves as CEO, and co-founder Jacob Duligall, with early establishment activity in Sydney before the company relocated its headquarters to San Francisco. Its New Zealand origins remain visible in the cap table: GD1 and Icehouse Ventures, two of the country's principal venture firms, participated in the Series B alongside the international investors.

The product targets a narrow and expensive bottleneck inside large companies: in-house legal teams reviewing inbound contracts. Ivo Review automates the review workflow itself, with customers reporting savings of up to 75% of the time manual review would take. A second product, Ivo Intelligence, searches a company's existing contract library to surface key data and the connections between documents — turning an archive that most companies treat as dead storage into something queryable. Jung's framing of the goal is deliberately about experience rather than cost: "Our goal has always been to make interacting with contracts fast, accurate, and enjoyable."

The growth figures behind the round are unusually steep and, importantly, measured across several independent dimensions rather than one. Annual recurring revenue rose 500% year over year. The total customer base expanded 134%. Adoption within Fortune 500 companies grew 250% since the previous round. The company's valuation increased fivefold over nine months.

The customer list is the strongest single piece of evidence in the file, because it is composed of exactly the sophisticated technology buyers whose own legal teams would be hardest to sell: IBM, Uber, Canva, Shopify, Pinterest, Quora, BILL.com, Reddit, Atlassian and CDW.

The US$55 million Series B, announced on January 20, 2026, was led by Blackbird, with Costanoa Ventures, Uncork Capital, Fika Ventures, GD1 and Icehouse Ventures participating. The capital is earmarked for product development and for scaling across both existing and new clients — deepening work with the hundreds of organisations already on the platform — with entry into the UK market expected.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Picking in-house legal teams rather than law firms.** Law firms bill by the hour, so software that saves review time attacks their revenue model. In-house legal teams are cost centres with fixed headcount and unbounded inbound volume, which makes time saved an unambiguous win. The choice of buyer is why the 75% time-saving claim converts into purchases.
- **Relocating headquarters to San Francisco while keeping New Zealand investors.** Ivo moved to where its enterprise buyers and growth capital are, rather than attempting to sell Fortune 500 legal departments from Auckland — but retained GD1 and Icehouse through the Series B instead of cycling them out.
- **Shipping a second product (Ivo Intelligence) against the same data.** Review is a workflow sold per-contract-reviewed; intelligence is an asset play over the contract archive the customer already has. The second product expands account value without a new sale.
- **Targeting hard reference customers early.** Landing IBM, Atlassian, Shopify and Reddit creates a reference set that makes every subsequent enterprise legal sale materially easier — these are buyers other buyers trust.
- **Raising nine months after the previous round at 5x the valuation.** Ivo chose to re-raise into momentum rather than extend runway, which is the correct call when the constraint is how fast you can serve demand rather than whether demand exists.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ivo is a clean illustration of choosing the buyer whose incentives align with your product's effect. AI document review is a crowded category, and much of it is sold to law firms, where saving review time cannibalises billable hours and the buyer must be persuaded against their own economics. By selling to in-house legal teams — cost centres drowning in inbound contract volume with no ability to hire proportionally — Ivo made the same technical capability trivially easy to justify. When a single product thesis can be pointed at two different buyers, the structural question is which one profits from the outcome you create.

The second pattern is about what a New Zealand company does with geography. Ivo did not try to prove that an Auckland-based company could sell to the Fortune 500 remotely; it moved its headquarters to the customers while keeping its domestic investors on the register. That is a less romantic answer than "build a global company from New Zealand," and the 500% ARR growth and 250% increase in Fortune 500 adoption suggest it was the right one. For founders in small markets with enterprise products, the honest read is that the domestic market can fund and incubate you, but proximity to the buyer is a real input to enterprise sales velocity.

Finally, the multi-dimensional growth disclosure is worth noting as a signal in its own right. A 500% ARR increase alone could be a handful of large contracts; paired with 134% customer growth and 250% Fortune 500 penetration, it describes broad adoption rather than concentration — which is what justified a fivefold valuation increase in nine months.
