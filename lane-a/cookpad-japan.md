## One-liner

Japan's dominant recipe-sharing platform peaked at ~96 million global monthly users in 2017, then fell into sustained revenue decline as YouTube cooking content and Instagram eroded engagement and costly international expansion failed to convert.

## What happened

Cookpad was founded in 1998 by Akimitsu Sano and listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE: 2193) in 2009. By 2014 it had become Japan's single largest cooking website. At its peak around 2016–2017 it claimed ~96 million monthly active users globally (roughly 60 million in Japan alone) and operated localised versions in over 70 countries.

The core Japan business was highly profitable on a subscription model (premium members paid for advanced search). International operations, however, burned cash without reaching comparable monetisation. A 2016 pivot toward advertising revenue in international markets underperformed expectations. In early 2019, Cookpad reported its first-ever year-on-year revenue decline and cut approximately 25% of its international workforce. Its share price fell from a peak near ¥3,500 to below ¥400 within three years.

The structural problem was platform substitution: Japanese home cooks increasingly turned to free YouTube channels and Instagram food accounts for inspiration, reducing willingness to pay for Cookpad's premium search tier. International markets never developed the sticky subscriber base that Japan had.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2012**: Rakuten founder Hiroshi Mikitani joined the board, bringing e-commerce ambitions but also governance friction that continued for several years.
- **2016**: Shifted international monetisation model away from subscriptions toward advertising — the change failed to compensate for subscriber churn.
- **2019 restructuring**: Laid off ~25% of international staff and signalled a pull-back to Japan as the primary market; operating loss posted for the full year.
- **2020–2022**: Brief COVID "cooking at home" tailwind provided temporary user uplift but did not reverse the underlying engagement decline against social video competitors.
- **2023**: Revenue remained at roughly half its 2018 peak; management buyout discussions reported but no transaction completed.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Cookpad is a textbook case of **platform-substitution risk in UGC businesses**: a company can build a dominant position in one medium (text/photo recipes) only to see a richer-format successor (video) render the original experience obsolete. Cookpad also illustrates the difficulty of applying Japan's willingness-to-pay-for-subscriptions in overseas markets where free alternatives are more entrenched. The international expansion consumed capital without producing a second profitable market, creating a drag that outlasted any single leadership decision.
