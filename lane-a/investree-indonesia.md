## One-liner

Investree, Indonesia's once-prominent peer-to-peer lending fintech and a Series C-funded regional expansion play, collapsed in 2024 after a liquidity crisis cascaded into fraud allegations — its co-founder and CEO Adrian Gunadi was arrested, and Indonesia's financial regulator OJK revoked the company's license, leaving thousands of lenders unable to recover their funds.

## What happened

Founded in 2015 in Jakarta, Investree positioned itself as a technology-driven SME financing marketplace that could unlock credit for small businesses locked out of traditional banking. The model was genuinely differentiated for Indonesia's context: a largely unbanked SME sector, thin credit bureau coverage, and a bank-dominated lending ecosystem that chronically underserved growth businesses. Investree raised across multiple rounds — including a Series C with participation from SBI Holdings, a major Japanese financial group — and expanded into Thailand and the Philippines as it sought to build a regional P2P lending footprint.

The company's problems became visible in 2023 when lenders began reporting difficulty withdrawing funds, a classic early signal of a liquidity mismatch in P2P platforms where the platform has extended commitments it cannot service. By early 2024, the scale of the problem had grown to the point where the Financial Services Authority (OJK) began a formal investigation. In October 2024, OJK revoked Investree's peer-to-peer lending license — one of the most severe regulatory actions taken against an Indonesian fintech — and Indonesian authorities moved to arrest CEO Adrian Gunadi. Gunadi, who had been in Dubai, was apprehended and returned to Indonesia to face charges. The collapse left an estimated outstanding loan portfolio with significant unrecovered lender funds across a platform that had once been cited as a model for how P2P lending could serve underbanked markets in Southeast Asia.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Regional expansion before domestic stability**: Investree's push into Thailand and the Philippines ahead of demonstrating sustainable unit economics in its home market stretched management bandwidth and regulatory relationships simultaneously, while the core Indonesian book remained undiversified and exposed to concentrated SME credit risk.
- **SBI Holdings strategic partnership**: The high-profile Japanese institutional backer gave Investree credibility that supported further fundraising, but also created pressure to show growth metrics that kept a growth-stage narrative alive longer than underlying portfolio quality warranted.
- **P2P structure amplifies liquidity mismatch**: Unlike a bank, P2P platforms have no deposit insurance, no lender-of-last-resort backstop, and limited ability to gate withdrawals without triggering panic — once lender confidence broke, there was no mechanism to slow the cascade.
- **OJK regulatory action**: The license revocation rather than a supervised wind-down meant lenders had no orderly recovery process, making Investree's collapse significantly more damaging to retail participants than a regulated bank failure would have been.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Investree is the starkest Southeast Asian example of how P2P lending platforms can look like high-growth fintech success stories during the fundraising cycle and then reveal structural fragility when credit conditions tighten: the model depends on continuous origination to service existing commitments, and the moment lender sentiment shifts, the platform has no natural stabilizer. The case also illustrates a recurring pattern in Indonesian fintech: OJK's regulatory capacity was overwhelmed by the speed of P2P sector growth during the 2018-2022 boom, and enforcement came too late to protect retail lenders. For later-stage investors, the SBI relationship is a cautionary note — strategic anchor investors from large institutions can provide validation that delays necessary scrutiny of portfolio quality.
