## One-liner

Japan's largest consumer-to-consumer marketplace spent a decade and hundreds of millions of dollars trying to crack the US, then shut its American marketplace in June 2024 and pivoted fully to Japan-focused growth and its Merpay fintech arm.

## What happened

Mercari was founded in 2013 in Tokyo by Shintaro Yamada and quickly became Japan's dominant secondhand goods marketplace. In 2014, the company launched in the United States — an aggressive bet that the same C2C model would transfer to the world's largest consumer market. Mercari IPO'd on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in June 2018, raising roughly ¥100 billion (~$900M at the time) at a valuation exceeding $3.7 billion, with US expansion a central growth narrative.

The US operation ran for nearly a decade. The business grew modestly — tens of millions of app downloads, a user base, and brand recognition — but never reached unit-economic viability. The US segment consistently posted operating losses, requiring ongoing capital subsidies from the profitable Japan business. US operating losses over the full period ran into the hundreds of millions of dollars cumulatively.

In June 2024, Mercari announced it would permanently close the US marketplace, citing an inability to see a clear path to profitability against entrenched competitors (eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark, Depop). The closure was completed within weeks.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2014 US launch**: Management bet that Japan's liquid secondhand culture was globally transferable; underestimated local competition and unit economics.
- **2018 IPO**: Listed with US growth as a key investor promise, increasing pressure to sustain the effort even as losses mounted.
- **Merpay pivot (2019 onward)**: Launched Merpay, an integrated payments and BNPL product inside the Japan app, which became profitable and strategically central — proving the Japan core more valuable than any overseas expansion.
- **2024 US shutdown**: After the US team was gradually wound down through 2022–2023 layoffs, Mercari formally closed the marketplace, freeing capital and management focus.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Mercari is a textbook case of the "Japan-to-USA" internationalization trap: a category-leading domestic product backed by real capital assuming cultural/model portability that didn't exist. The US C2C resale market already had deeply entrenched network-effect players. Meanwhile, Mercari's fintech pivot in Japan (Merpay) proved the company's instinct for product innovation — the problem was geography, not product. The shutdown allowed Mercari to become profitable at the group level.
