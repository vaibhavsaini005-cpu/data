## One-liner

Bangkok-founded Pomelo Fashion raised $52 million building a "try before you buy" fashion marketplace for Southeast Asia, then sold to Thai retail conglomerate Central Group in November 2022 after failing to achieve standalone profitability at scale.

## What happened

Pomelo Fashion was founded in 2013 by David Jou and Casey Liang in Bangkok, positioning itself as a technology-forward fashion retailer for the ASEAN middle class. Its differentiator was a "try-at-home" feature — customers could receive items, try them on, and pay only for what they kept — mimicking a model that worked in the US (Stitch Fix, Trunk Club) but had rarely been tested in Southeast Asia's cash-heavy, logistics-fragmented markets.

The company raised approximately $52 million across multiple rounds, including a $22 million Series C in September 2019 backed by JD.com, Provident Capital, and Thai investors. It expanded from Thailand into Singapore, Indonesia, and Hong Kong. However, fashion e-commerce in SEA carried high return rates and customer acquisition costs that grew unsustainable after COVID disrupted supply chains and squeezed discretionary spending. In November 2022, Thailand's Central Group — operator of Central department stores and major shareholder in Italian retailers — acquired Pomelo in full. Post-acquisition, the platform continued operating as an omnichannel arm under Central's retail empire.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Try-before-you-buy logistics burden**: The model worked in the US because of dense, low-cost last-mile networks; in SEA's archipelagic geography, the returns logistics ate margin at every order and made per-unit economics harder to close.
- **Expansion before profitability**: Pomelo opened Singapore and Indonesia operations while the Thai core was still loss-making, spreading management bandwidth and marketing budgets across markets with different consumer behaviour.
- **Strategic acqui-hire by Central Group**: The exit was not zero-sum — Central gained technology talent and digital fashion expertise; Pomelo's team gained distribution through 100+ department store locations. The acquisition price was not disclosed but was widely characterised as a below-Series-C-valuation outcome for investors.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Pomelo illustrates a recurring pattern in SEA e-commerce: differentiated models borrowed from US or European contexts that require logistics infrastructure maturity those markets hadn't yet reached. The eventual trade sale to a legacy retail conglomerate also reflects a structural feature of ASEAN startup exits — domestic strategic acquirers (Central, Salim Group, etc.) absorbing digital-first players as part of omnichannel transformation, rather than IPOs or cross-border tech acquisitions.
