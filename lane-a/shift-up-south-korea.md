## One-liner

Founder and CEO Kim Hyung-tae took Shift Up public on the KOSPI on 11 July 2024, and committed the IPO proceeds to funding original games in-house rather than buying other studios' IP.

## What happened

Kim Hyung-tae, a former NCSoft art director, founded Shift Up in December 2013 and built it around subculture-focused action games (Goddess of Victory: Nikke). Ahead of the company's IPO, priced to raise up to 435 billion won ($313 million), Kim stated the company would "use the IPO proceeds mainly to develop new games instead of acquiring existing intellectual properties of other developers or publishers, to reinforce its identity as an unrivaled game developer" — a deliberate rejection of the M&A-led growth path other listed Korean game companies had taken. Shift Up debuted on the KOSPI on 11 July 2024; the stock surged to 89,500 won, briefly pushing its market cap to nearly 5.2 trillion won ($3.79 billion) and past NCSoft's valuation, before settling at 71,000 won by the close — still enough to make Shift Up South Korea's fourth-largest listed game company by market cap, behind Krafton, Netmarble and NCSoft, and to make Kim a billionaire.

## Key decisions / turning points

- June 2024: Kim publicly commits IPO proceeds to internal game development, not acquisitions — setting an explicit strategic contrast with peers ahead of pricing.
- 11 July 2024: IPO debut; stock briefly values Shift Up above NCSoft before closing lower.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kim's proceeds commitment was made *before* the IPO priced, not after — a public pre-commitment that removed the usual post-IPO ambiguity about whether a founder will actually reinvest in the product versus chase inorganic growth. It's a specific, checkable instance of a founder using the IPO moment itself to lock in a build-not-buy strategy, in a market (Korean gaming) where large listed players have often grown via acquisition.
