## One-liner

Indonesian crypto exchange Triv, founded and led by CEO Gabriel Rey, brought in MEXC Ventures as a strategic investor at a $200 million valuation to expand its coin offerings and liquidity while defending its dual regulatory licenses in Indonesia.

## What happened

Gabriel Rey founded Triv in 2015 as a cryptocurrency exchange platform for the Indonesian market, building it into a regulated operator holding licenses from both Indonesia's Financial Services Authority (OJK) and its Commodity Futures Trading Regulatory Agency (BAPPEBTI) — a dual-license position that differentiates it from unregulated offshore exchanges. By 2025, Triv served more than 3 million registered users trading over 1,000 crypto assets, including spot, staking and futures products, and had expanded into media through its CryptoWave Media unit.

On August 5-6, 2025, Rey announced that MEXC Ventures — the venture arm of global exchange MEXC — would take a stake in Triv Group at a $200 million valuation. Rey framed the deal around a specific set of product and market goals: "We are pleased to welcome the MEXC Group into the Triv Group. This partnership will enable us to expand our coin offerings, enhance liquidity, and introduce more innovative products for both new and existing users. It also strengthens our commitment to keeping CryptoWave Media (part of Triv Group) as the no.1 crypto media platform in Indonesia."

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pursuing dual regulatory licensing (OJK and BAPPEBTI) rather than operating offshore or unregulated**: Rey built Triv's credibility around a compliance-first structure that predates Indonesia's later regulatory tightening on crypto platforms.
- **Bringing in a global exchange's venture arm (MEXC Ventures) as a strategic investor rather than a purely financial one**: the deal is framed around liquidity and product expansion (more coin offerings, deeper liquidity), not just capital.
- **Building CryptoWave Media alongside the exchange itself**: Rey diversified Triv Group beyond trading fees into a media property, and explicitly protected that unit's market position as part of the investment terms.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Triv's deal shows a regulated, decade-old regional exchange using a strategic (not purely financial) investor to solve specific liquidity and product-breadth problems rather than raising generic growth capital — a distinction that matters in a sector where trust and asset depth are the main competitive levers. Building and defending a media property (CryptoWave Media) as part of the core group, rather than treating it as a side project, also shows a founder using content/media as a durable moat in a commoditized exchange market, which is uncommon among regional crypto platforms.
