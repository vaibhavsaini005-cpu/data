## One-liner

Christian Bradley, founder and CEO of Philippine car-sharing platform Carbnb, stepped down from the company he built to launch CarBEV, a battery-electric ride-hailing fleet startup, framing the switch as a values-driven pivot rather than a response to Carbnb underperforming.

## What happened

Christian Bradley founded and led Carbnb, a peer-to-peer and self-drive car rental platform in the Philippines. On October 29, 2025, it was reported that Bradley had resigned as Carbnb's CEO — with the board accepting his resignation and planning to name a successor, while Carbnb itself continued operating — to found and lead CarBEV, a new venture building the Philippines' first large-scale battery electric vehicle (BEV) fleet for transport network vehicle service (TNVS) platforms such as Grab, Angkas and inDrive. Bradley remains a major shareholder and strategic advisor to Carbnb through the transition.

Bradley was explicit that the move was not a response to Carbnb's performance but a personal and values-driven redirection: "My journey with Carbnb has been incredible, but CarBEV is a calling I can no longer ignore. It's a convergence of my faith, my desire for redemption, and my commitment to using business as a force for good." He added: "Life is short, and it's time to focus on what's right and lasting. CarBEV is my way of turning that realization into action." CarBEV's initial commercial plan centered on a 50-unit all-electric fleet partnership with AngCars, built around what Bradley called a "MiniMax" (minimum expense, maximum comfort) operating principle, targeting driver livelihoods and rider affordability alongside decarbonization.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Resigning as CEO of a company he founded while it was still operating, rather than staying to scale it further**: Bradley chose to hand Carbnb to new leadership rather than run both ventures himself.
- **Staying on as a shareholder and advisor rather than fully exiting**: preserved his financial stake and continuity for Carbnb while freeing his operating attention for CarBEV.
- **Choosing fleet ownership/electrification (CarBEV) over marketplace/peer-to-peer sharing (Carbnb) as the next model**: a bet that owning and electrifying a TNVS-facing fleet was a bigger opportunity than continuing to scale a rental marketplace.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

This is a founder-level pivot rather than a company-level one: instead of steering Carbnb itself into electric fleet ownership, Bradley separated the two models into distinct companies and moved his own attention to the new one, while leaving the original venture intact under new leadership. That is a specific, low-destruction way to pursue a strategic pivot — it avoids forcing an existing team, cap table and brand through a business-model change, at the cost of walking away from day-to-day control of the company he built. Framing the move explicitly in personal/values terms, rather than as a market or competitive necessity, is also notable — it's a founder narrative built on mission redirection rather than crisis response.
