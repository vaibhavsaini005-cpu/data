## One-liner

NocNoc, a Siam Cement Group (SCG)-backed Thai home-and-living e-marketplace, announced full shutdown by May 2026 after five straight years of losses totaling roughly 4.39 billion baht.

## What happened

NocNoc launched in 2018 under BetterBe Marketplace Co., Ltd. as a "Marketplace for Living," connecting shoppers with furniture, construction materials, home decor, and renovation/installation services in one platform. Siam Cement Group (SCG) began investing heavily from 2020–2021 through its Digital & Smart Living business group, using NocNoc as the flagship platform for its living-ecosystem ambitions. By 2023 the ownership structure shifted again: Must B Co., Ltd. — a joint venture between Fraser Property Group and Thai Beverage — partnered with SCG, each holding 50% of a venture then valued at over 3.9 billion baht.

Despite the backing, NocNoc never reached profitability. It posted a 1.25 billion baht loss on 412 million baht of revenue in 2023, and a 1.12 billion baht loss on 322 million baht of revenue in 2024. On January 9, 2026, the company announced it would stop accepting new orders on February 9, wind down after-sales support by April 8, and fully close the platform by May 9, 2026 — citing "current economic conditions and intense competition in the e-commerce market." Its closure made it the last major Thai-owned general e-marketplace still standing, following earlier Thai-market exits by international players.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Category choice worked against unit economics.** Home-and-living goods are low-frequency, high-personalization purchases with thin margins once logistics, bulky-item delivery, and installation services are factored in — a structurally harder category to scale profitably than daily consumables or fashion.
- **Repeated ownership restructuring instead of a profitability pivot.** Rather than narrowing scope or changing the model as losses mounted, the company brought in a second major shareholder (Must B) in 2023 to keep funding the same marketplace strategy.
- **AI/ML investment didn't offset the cost structure.** NocNoc had publicized machine-learning personalization work (including with AWS) to lift sales, but merchandising gains weren't enough to overcome customer-acquisition costs in a market being squeezed by foreign platforms and price wars.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

NocNoc is a case study in how strong corporate backing (SCG is one of Thailand's largest conglomerates) and real product/tech investment can't fix a category with fundamentally difficult unit economics. Five years and two rounds of strategic shareholders weren't enough to turn a home-and-living marketplace profitable once regional e-commerce competition intensified — a cautionary example for any high-touch, low-frequency vertical marketplace betting on scale to eventually produce margin.
