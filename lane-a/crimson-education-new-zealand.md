## One-liner

A 17-year-old Auckland student who won admission to 25 top universities built a premium college-prep tutoring business that grew to serve 50+ countries, raised NZD $55M from Tiger Global in 2021, and became one of New Zealand's most globally recognised EdTech success stories.

## What happened

Crimson Education was founded in 2013 by Jamie Beaton, who at age 17 had been accepted by 25 of the world's leading universities including Harvard, Stanford, Princeton, Yale, and Oxford. Beaton recognised that the gap between New Zealand students and top US/UK university admission rates was largely one of preparation and strategy rather than raw ability — New Zealand's decentralised, state-education-centric school system gave students little exposure to application norms at elite institutions.

Crimson's initial model was local tutoring and admissions mentoring in Auckland. The company expanded to Australia quickly, then moved into South Korea, Malaysia, Hong Kong, and eventually the United States and United Kingdom by the late 2010s. The core product is high-touch, personalised support: dedicated counsellors, extracurricular activity building, SAT/ACT preparation, and essay editing.

In March 2021, Crimson raised NZD $55 million (~USD $39M) in a Series B co-led by Tiger Global Management. At the time it was one of the largest private EdTech raises in New Zealand's history. The capital funded technology buildout (an AI-driven matching platform connecting students to mentors) and further international expansion. Beaton published "Accepted!" (HarperCollins, 2022), a guide to elite admissions, which became a bestseller and amplified the company's profile globally.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2013 — Local Auckland tutoring launch**: Beaton monetised his own admissions experience directly; word of mouth among aspirational families proved a highly effective initial distribution channel in NZ and Australia.
- **2017–2019 — Asia expansion**: Entered South Korea and Southeast Asian markets where competitive university admissions pressure is intense and families pay significant premiums for Western-university placement outcomes.
- **2021 — Tiger Global Series B (NZD $55M)**: Signalled global EdTech investor confidence in the premium-admissions category; capital allowed Crimson to build software infrastructure to reduce per-student counsellor load and scale mentorship.
- **2022 — "Accepted!" book launch**: Cross-marketed the company to a mass audience; Jamie Beaton became a recognisable global EdTech founder figure, providing brand differentiation impossible to buy with advertising spend.
- **2022–2024 — AI platform development**: Built a matching and progress-tracking platform to pair students with Crimson-trained mentors globally, shifting the model from pure services toward a partially scalable product layer.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Crimson Education illustrates how a founder's personal experience, when real and differentiated, is a defensible distribution moat in markets where trust is the primary purchase criterion. Parents paying $20,000–$60,000 for admissions consulting want a track record they can verify — Beaton's 25-university acceptance story is that verification. It also shows that New Zealand, often overlooked as an EdTech hub, can produce globally competitive companies when founders target the premium segment of a high-stakes global market rather than trying to out-price incumbents. The Tiger Global investment explicitly validated that the premium admissions segment in Asia-Pacific and globally is a standalone venture-scale category, not just a niche services business.
