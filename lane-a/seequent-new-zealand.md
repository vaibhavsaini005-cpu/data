## One-liner

Seequent, a Christchurch-based geoscience software company best known for its Leapfrog 3D geological modelling suite, was acquired by US infrastructure software giant Bentley Systems for approximately $1.05 billion in 2021 — one of New Zealand's largest-ever technology exits.

## What happened

Seequent traces its roots to ARANZ Geo, founded in Christchurch in the early 2000s to commercialise research from the University of Canterbury. The company's flagship product, Leapfrog, reimagined how geologists build 3D models of underground environments. Traditional geological modelling required manual section-by-section interpretation and took weeks; Leapfrog introduced implicit modelling algorithms that could automatically update the entire 3D model the moment new drill data was entered. This dramatically reduced model turnaround times and became indispensable to the mining, hydrogeology, and geothermal energy sectors.

The company rebranded from ARANZ Geo to Seequent around 2018 and expanded its product suite by acquiring Geosoft (Canada, 2020 — 3D earth modelling and geoscience data management) and GeoStudio (geotechnical stability modelling). By the time of acquisition, Seequent had more than 430 employees across 16 offices in over 100 countries, serving the world's top mining companies, major civil engineering firms, and national geological surveys.

Bentley Systems, which provides infrastructure engineering software used in bridge, rail, road, and building design, announced the acquisition on March 15, 2021. The deal closed on June 17, 2021 for approximately $910 million in cash plus Bentley stock (totalling approximately $1.05 billion). It was the largest acquisition in Bentley's history at that point. Seequent continued to operate as a distinct brand and business unit post-acquisition.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Implicit modelling as the core innovation:** Leapfrog's implicit modelling engine (based on radial basis functions) removed the dominant pain point in geological work — manual re-interpretation after each new data point — creating a product that geologists didn't just prefer but physically could not work without after adopting it.
- **Staying in Christchurch:** Rather than relocating to Wellington or Auckland (let alone Sydney or London), Seequent built world-class domain expertise in a regional NZ city, using proximity to Canterbury's earth sciences research community and lower costs to reinvest heavily in R&D.
- **Expanding via acquisitions (Geosoft, GeoStudio):** Rather than building competing capabilities from scratch, Seequent bought adjacent tools with established user bases, broadening the platform to cover hydrogeology, geophysics, and geotechnical engineering in one integrated offering.
- **Selling to Bentley, not a PE firm:** Bentley's focus on infrastructure software preserved Seequent's product and team culture better than a financial acquirer would have; Seequent retains its brand and Christchurch headquarters.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Seequent demonstrates that deep-niche, domain-specific software built outside major tech hubs can achieve tier-one exits when the underlying technology genuinely solves a workflow problem that has no easy substitute. The mining and geoscience industries are not glamorous startup targets, but their willingness to pay for accurate subsurface models — where a modelling error can mean sinking a shaft in the wrong location — makes them exceptional customers. New Zealand's tech ecosystem tends to underestimate how large the global market can be for software serving even a single industrial vertical at world-class depth.
