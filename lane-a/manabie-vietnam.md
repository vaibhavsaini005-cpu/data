## One-liner

Manabie, a Singapore-founded edtech startup that built its real scale by running physical cram schools in Vietnam, raised a $23M Series B in April 2025 to pivot from a consumer tutoring app into "ERP for schools" vertical SaaS plus AI tutoring.

## What happened

Manabie was founded in Singapore in 2019 by Takuya Homma (former co-founder of Quipper) and Christy Wong (a founding member of Lazada). Rather than growing as a pan-Southeast-Asian consumer app, the company's actual traction came from a hybrid offline-online model in Vietnam: it built and operated around 30 of its own digitized cram-school classrooms serving roughly 13,000 students, pairing in-person tutoring with a learning-management platform.

On April 29, 2025, Manabie announced a Series B round of approximately 3.3 billion yen (~$23 million), led by JIC Venture Growth Investments with participation from Mitsubishi UFJ Capital, Hulic, Z-kai Holdings, and returning investors Globis Capital Partners, Genesia Ventures, and Chiba Dojo Fund — bringing total funding to roughly 6 billion yen (~$41.2 million). The round repositioned Manabie again: from an owned-classroom operator toward a "vertical SaaS platform for educational institutions," selling its learning-management, communications, content, and billing stack to third-party cram schools and education corporations across Japan and Southeast Asia, while layering in a generative-AI "AI Tutor" feature aimed at the region's teacher shortage.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **From consumer app to owned classrooms in Vietnam**: instead of chasing a broad multi-country B2C rollout, Manabie concentrated on building and running its own cram schools in Vietnam, trading platform breadth for a defensible, revenue-generating physical footprint.
- **From owned classrooms to vertical SaaS**: the Series B marks a second pivot, from operating its own schools toward licensing the underlying "ERP for schools" software to other institutions — a shift from operator to infrastructure provider.
- **AI as the next wedge**: rather than compete purely on classroom management features, Manabie is betting the AI Tutor product, built to address teacher shortages, is what justifies expansion beyond Vietnam into new Southeast Asian markets.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Manabie's trajectory — consumer app to owned offline schools to licensed SaaS platform — shows a startup repeatedly re-anchoring its business model around whichever asset (physical classrooms, then software) actually proved defensible in a single market (Vietnam) before generalizing. It's a useful contrast to startups that try to scale a single model across many countries at once: Manabie's funding and credibility came from depth in one country first.
