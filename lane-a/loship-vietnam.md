## One-liner

Vietnamese hyperlocal delivery startup Loship, which had been chasing a $50 million Series C and a US IPO by 2024, pivoted to seeking debt financing instead of new equity in mid-2022 as venture funding across Southeast Asia dried up.

## What happened

Founded by Nguyen Hoang Trung out of e-commerce startup Lozi, Loship built a hyperlocal on-demand delivery app spanning food, groceries, medicine, laundry, flowers, and B2B supply delivery in Vietnamese cities. After raising a $12 million pre-Series C round in August 2021 (valuing it around $100 million) and entering talks with Japan's Daiwa Securities Group for a $50 million Series C in October 2021, Loship had publicly targeted a U.S. stock market listing by 2024. By mid-2022, as the regional funding climate hardened, the company shifted its priority from closing that equity round to securing debt financing instead. Vietnamese outlet VIETTIMES reported on June 6, 2022 that a source close to the company said Loship was "prioritizing survival over pursuing new equity rounds during uncertain times," while its official spokesperson declined to comment on the debt-financing strategy. DealStreetAsia/Nikkei Asia confirmed the debt-financing pivot on June 28, 2022, describing it as a move to extend runway before returning to equity fundraising.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Switching from equity to debt financing mid-fundraise**: rather than closing the Series C at a lower valuation or delaying indefinitely, Loship chose debt to preserve runway and avoid a down round during a period when Southeast Asian late-stage valuations were compressing sharply.
- **Deferring the U.S. IPO ambition**: the 2021-era public target of a 2024 U.S. listing was quietly deprioritized in favor of near-term survival, a common recalibration among Southeast Asian startups that had set aggressive growth-era targets before the 2022 funding pullback.
- **Staying quiet publicly while reporters confirmed the shift via sources**: Loship's spokesperson decline-to-comment approach meant the pivot became known through investor/press reporting rather than a company announcement — a sign of how sensitive funding-strategy pivots were treated during the downturn.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Loship's shift illustrates a broader 2022 pattern across Southeast Asian startups: when equity markets tightened, well-funded but not-yet-profitable delivery platforms turned to debt instruments to buy time rather than accept a down round or cut the business. It's a pivot in financing strategy rather than business model, but it directly reflects the macro shift in investor risk appetite that hit an entire generation of Series B/C Southeast Asian startups in the same window.
