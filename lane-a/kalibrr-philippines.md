## One-liner

Kalibrr, the first Southeast Asian startup accepted into Y Combinator, turned a 2015 $2 million seed round into a durable multi-year run as one of the Philippines' leading job-matching platforms, going on to raise further rounds from Omidyar Network, Learn Capital, and Wavemaker Partners.

## What happened

Founded in Manila by Paul Rivera and Dexter Ligot-Gordon, Kalibrr built a cloud-based recruitment platform aimed at matching Filipino jobseekers with employers more efficiently than traditional classifieds-style job boards. The company was accepted into Y Combinator's Winter 2013 batch, becoming the first Southeast Asian startup admitted to the accelerator. On March 18, 2015, DealStreetAsia reported that Kalibrr had closed a $2 million funding round led by Y Combinator, with participation from eBay founder Pierre Omidyar and Philippine-based Kickstart Ventures — at the time, one of the largest fundraises by a purely local Philippine startup. Kalibrr used the capital to expand beyond basic job-matching into skills assessments and talent management tools, and by April 13, 2015 DealStreetAsia reported the company was already in talks for a third fundraising round backed by Omidyar Network, Learn Capital, Siemer Ventures, and Wavemaker DFJ, alongside plans to launch a broader marketing campaign. Kalibrr went on to raise a further Series A extension in January 2017 and expanded regionally with a Jakarta office alongside its Manila and San Francisco bases, continuing to operate as an active recruitment platform in the Philippines and Indonesia for over a decade after its YC acceptance.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Pursuing Y Combinator as a first-mover from Southeast Asia**: being the first SEA startup in YC gave Kalibrr credibility and network access (leading directly to its $2 million seed round) that few regional competitors of that era had.
- **Expanding from job-matching into assessments and talent management**: rather than staying a pure listings board, Kalibrr broadened its product into skills assessment and employer branding tools, giving it more defensible differentiation against larger regional job portals.
- **Regionalizing early into Indonesia**: opening a Jakarta office alongside its Manila base let Kalibrr diversify beyond a single, smaller domestic market rather than remaining Philippines-only.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kalibrr's decade-plus survival and growth in a market segment (online recruitment) that saw many regional competitors fold or get absorbed shows the durability of an early accelerator credibility boost when it's followed by real product expansion — assessments and talent management, not just job listings. It's also a useful counterpoint to flashier failure stories from the same era: sustained, unglamorous execution in a "boring" vertical can outlast better-funded, higher-profile startups in adjacent categories.
