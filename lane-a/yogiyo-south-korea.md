## One-liner

Yogiyo, South Korea's second-largest food delivery app, was founded by Rocket Internet, acquired by Delivery Hero in 2014, survived a decade of brutal duopoly competition with Baemin — then was forcibly divested by Korean antitrust regulators and sold to GS Retail consortium for roughly $600 million in 2023.

## What happened

Yogiyo (요기요, "Do you have it?") launched in 2012 as a Rocket Internet venture — the German startup factory's bet on Korean food delivery. The app aggregated restaurant listings and enabled digital ordering at a time when Korean consumers were transitioning from phone calls to apps. Delivery Hero, the Berlin-based food delivery conglomerate, acquired Yogiyo in 2014 for approximately $86 million, seeing it as a foothold in one of Asia's highest per-capita food-delivery markets.

For years Yogiyo competed fiercely with Baemin (배달의민족, operated by Woowa Brothers), the dominant market leader. Both platforms grew Korea's food delivery sector dramatically — the country became one of the world's highest food-delivery-per-capita markets. In 2019, Delivery Hero acquired Woowa Brothers (Baemin's operator) for $4 billion, giving it effective control of both leading Korean platforms — a near-monopoly that controlled roughly 90%+ of Korean food delivery.

The Korea Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) approved the Woowa Brothers acquisition in 2021 only under the condition that Delivery Hero divest Yogiyo, preventing a full monopoly. After a failed earlier deal with GS Retail fell through over price disputes, a revised transaction closed in October 2023: GS Retail and financial sponsor Permira acquired Yogiyo for approximately ₩800 billion (~$600 million) — a significant discount from the originally agreed ₩950 billion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **2014 Delivery Hero acquisition**: Rocket Internet exited early; Delivery Hero began building a global food-delivery empire with Yogiyo as its Korean pillar.
- **2019 Baemin deal**: Acquiring the competitor created a regulatory problem Delivery Hero may have underestimated; the KFTC took nearly two years to decide.
- **2021 KFTC divestiture mandate**: The forced sale fundamentally changed Yogiyo's ownership trajectory and strategic independence.
- **2023 GS Retail acquisition**: The platform moved from a purely digital parent to a major Korean conglomerate with offline retail synergies (GS25 convenience stores, GS The Fresh supermarkets), opening a potential new strategic direction for quick-commerce integration.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Yogiyo's trajectory illustrates how regulatory outcomes can reshape even mature, profitable startups. A business that was a genuine market success became a chess piece in a regulatory standoff — its valuation declining by ~15% from the original deal to final close as antitrust conditions dragged on. The story also shows how "platform consolidation at all costs" strategies in high-market-concentration sectors draw regulatory scrutiny that can undo the original M&A thesis.
