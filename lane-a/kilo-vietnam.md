## One-liner

Kilo, a Hanoi-based B2B e-commerce platform connecting wholesalers with small retailers across Vietnam, ceased operations in October 2023 after raising roughly $5.7 million from investors including Altos Ventures and Goodwater Capital.

## What happened

Kilo was founded by Kartick Narayan, a former chief marketing officer at Groupon and business head at Tiki, to digitize the fragmented distribution chain between wholesalers and Vietnam's millions of micro, small, and medium-sized retailers (the neighborhood "mom-and-pop" shops that dominate the country's roughly $180 billion retail market). Distributors and retailers could order and manage inventory through Kilo's app as well as through Facebook and Zalo, the messaging platforms already embedded in how small Vietnamese merchants did business. The company raised a $5 million Series A round in late 2021 led by Altos Ventures, with January Capital, Goodwater Capital, Ascend Vietnam Ventures, Decisive Capital Management, and Ratio Ventures also participating, and reported engagement from roughly 30,000 wholesalers and retailers across 42 cities. Despite that early traction, Kilo quietly stopped operating, with its business registration listed as temporarily closed; DealStreetAsia and Vietnam Investment Review reported the shutdown in October 2023.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Betting on Facebook and Zalo as ordering channels rather than forcing adoption of a standalone app**: this lowered the barrier to entry for merchants already comfortable with those platforms, but it also meant Kilo never fully owned the customer relationship or the data layer of its own product.
- **Raising a single, relatively small Series A ($5M) into a capital-intensive, thin-margin logistics-and-distribution business**: B2B e-commerce and last-mile fulfillment require heavy working capital and warehousing investment to scale, and Kilo's round was modest next to regional peers tackling the same problem.
- **Timing into the 2022–2023 Southeast Asian venture funding contraction**: Kilo raised its Series A in November 2021 near the peak of the region's funding boom, leaving it exposed when follow-on capital for early-stage B2B commerce dried up through 2022–2023, a period that also saw Vietnamese peers like Telio shut down.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kilo's collapse is part of a broader pattern in Vietnam's B2B e-commerce sector, where multiple well-funded startups (Kilo, Telio, and others) built similar wholesaler-to-retailer marketplace models around 2020–2021 and folded within two to three years once cheap growth capital disappeared. The underlying retail fragmentation problem was real, but digitizing low-margin distribution requires either much larger balance sheets or tighter unit economics than seed-to-Series-A funding typically provides — a caution against mistaking early merchant sign-ups for a durable, capital-efficient business model.
