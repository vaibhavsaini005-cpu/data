## One-liner

After 20 years of operation, Indonesian edtech pioneer Zenius completed its shutdown in January 2024, following three successive rounds of layoffs and a failed last-minute M&A deal.

## What happened

Zenius had operated as one of Indonesia's earliest edtech platforms, predating the pandemic-era edtech funding boom that made venture-scale bets on the sector look viable. As macroeconomic conditions worsened, the company cut over 200 employees in May 2022, roughly 600 more in August 2022, and a third round in February 2023 — three separate rounds of layoffs within a single year as it tried to right-size against a shrinking funding environment.

Before fully shutting down, Zenius pursued acquisition talks with several Indonesian edtech firms, but the deal collapsed when an undisclosed investor missed the deadline to complete the M&A agreement. With no acquirer and no further funding path, Zenius completed its wind-down in January 2024, closing out two decades of operation.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Ran three distinct layoff rounds across roughly nine months (May 2022, August 2022, February 2023) rather than a single decisive restructuring, extending the period of uncertainty for remaining staff and customers.
- Pursued an M&A exit as an alternative to shutdown once organic fundraising stopped being viable.
- The M&A deal fell through due to an investor missing a deadline — an external dependency outside the company's direct control that removed its last viable path forward.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Zenius illustrates how pandemic-era edtech valuations became a liability once macro conditions normalized — a company that operated successfully for 20 years still couldn't survive the transition, because its scale and burn had been calibrated to a funding environment that stopped existing. The failed M&A also shows the fragility of "sell instead of shut down" as a fallback plan: it depends entirely on a counterparty's own capital availability, which was equally constrained industry-wide (no EdTech company globally raised $100M+ in 2023, per Crunchbase).
