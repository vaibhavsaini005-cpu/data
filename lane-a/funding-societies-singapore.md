## One-liner

Southeast Asia's largest SME digital lender built a defensible multi-country business by obtaining full regulatory licences before scaling — a slower but harder-to-replicate path that attracted a $294M round led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2 in 2022.

## What happened

Funding Societies was founded in 2015 by Kelvin Teo (CEO) and Reynold Wijaya (COO), both Harvard Business School MBA graduates who identified the severe financing gap faced by small and medium enterprises across Southeast Asia. The pair built a peer-to-peer (P2P) lending marketplace that connected retail and institutional investors with SME borrowers seeking invoice financing, working capital loans, and supply chain credit.

The company was the first P2P lender to receive a Capital Markets Services licence from Singapore's Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS). It subsequently obtained licences from Bank Negara Malaysia and Indonesia's OJK (Otoritas Jasa Keuangan), then expanded to Thailand and Vietnam. In Indonesia, the business operates under the brand Modalku.

By February 2022, when Funding Societies announced its Series C+ round, it had:
- Disbursed over US$2 billion in business financing across more than 5 million loan transactions
- Served more than 100,000 SMEs
- Maintained default rates well below regional averages through data-driven credit underwriting
- Achieved licences in all five operating markets

The February 2022 raise comprised US$144 million in equity (led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2, with Sequoia Capital India, VNG, Rapyd Ventures, EDBI, Indies Capital, Ascend Vietnam Ventures and BRI Ventures participating) plus US$150 million in debt facilities from institutional lenders, totalling approximately US$294 million.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Regulatory-first sequencing**: Funding Societies pursued licences in each market before scaling — the opposite of the "expand first, regulate later" playbook common in Asian fintech. MAS in particular is highly respected across Asia; its approval gave institutional debt providers confidence to extend the $150M debt tranche.
- **Dual-brand strategy**: Using "Modalku" in Indonesia rather than imposing the Singapore brand name reduced friction with local regulators and borrowers, and later made the company more attractive to Indonesian institutional partners including BRI Ventures (Bank Rakyat Indonesia's VC arm).
- **Credit underwriting innovation**: Funding Societies used transaction data (purchase orders, invoice flows, Shopee/Tokopedia seller metrics) rather than pure credit-bureau scores — critical in markets where most SMEs have no formal credit history.
- **Institutional LP base for debt**: Raising institutional debt lines (not just equity) allowed the company to scale loan disbursements without diluting shareholders at every funding cycle — a capital efficiency advantage over peers relying solely on retail P2P investors.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Funding Societies demonstrates that in regulated financial services across multiple jurisdictions, "compliance as a moat" is a durable strategy. Competitors that expanded aggressively without licences faced retroactive shutdowns or forced restructuring (e.g., several P2P platforms in China, Indonesia's own crowdlending crackdowns of 2018–2019). The company's credit performance — low default rates on a $2B+ disbursement book spanning five countries and five currencies — is the hard evidence that its underwriting model works, not just its fundraising headline.
