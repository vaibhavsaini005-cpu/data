## One-liner

Wellington energy-pricing startup Factor, co-founded by CEO Jessica Venning-Bryan and Simon Pohlen out of the Taiawa tech hub, was acquired by NZX-listed Gentrack for NZ$24 million plus a possible NZ$10 million earnout, less than a year after taking its product to market.

## What happened

Jessica Venning-Bryan and Simon Pohlen, who had previously worked together at Flick Electric Co., co-founded Factor (originally named Prospero Energy) to replace the spreadsheet-driven, manual pricing processes energy retailers used to design commercial electricity tariffs — accounting for batteries, micro-generation and electric vehicle demand — with API-driven, AI-powered forecasting and pricing software. The company raised an oversubscribed NZ$3.22 million pre-seed round led by Icehouse Ventures, with Motion Capital, K1W1 and Angel HQ also participating, and operated out of Wellington's Taiawa tech hub.

Factor took its product to market in June 2025 and, per Venning-Bryan, was "met with an overwhelming positive response." Less than a year later, on 15 May 2026, Gentrack — a NZX-listed energy and water utility software provider — completed a full acquisition of Factor for an enterprise value of NZ$24 million, funded from existing cash reserves, with a further NZ$10 million earnout available if Factor's annual recurring revenue grows to roughly NZ$17 million within three years. The deal, announced 18-19 May 2026, folded Factor's pricing engine directly into Gentrack's g2 retail-operations platform, and was Taiawa's first exit of a resident startup.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Building on founders' domain expertise from Flick Electric Co.**: Venning-Bryan and Pohlen targeted a specific, technical pain point (commercial energy pricing) they understood from direct industry experience rather than a generic software product.
- **Raising a small, focused pre-seed round (NZ$3.22m) rather than over-capitalizing early**: kept the company lean while it proved out the product with real retailer customers before seeking any larger raise.
- **Accepting Gentrack's acquisition offer instead of continuing to scale independently**: less than a year after going to market, the founders chose a strategic-buyer exit with an earnout tied to growth, betting that Gentrack's existing retailer relationships would scale Factor's product faster than an independent Series A path.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Factor is a fast-exit pattern: rather than following the typical multi-year path from pre-seed to Series A to eventual acquisition, the founders sold to a strategic, complementary-platform buyer within roughly a year of commercial launch, at a valuation (NZ$24m, ~7x the raised capital before any earnout) that rewarded speed and product-market fit over scale. For a small domestic market like New Zealand's energy sector, this also shows the strategic-acquirer path — being bought by an adjacent platform company (Gentrack) rather than an outside financial buyer — as a realistic, faster route to a meaningful outcome than chasing an independent growth trajectory.
