## One-liner

Auckland-founded fintech Caruso, an AI-driven registry and fund administration platform, closed a Series A round in 2024 that took total raised past $11M as assets under administration on its platform surged to roughly $55-80 billion.

## What happened

Founded by Mark Hurley and Oliver Shaw, Caruso built registry and fund administration software aimed at automating the operationally heavy back-office work fund managers traditionally handled through manual processes or outsourced administrators. The company's Series A, led by Icehouse Ventures with participation from GD1 and Balmain (an Australian private credit fund manager and Caruso customer), valued the company at roughly $100M and came alongside a tenfold increase in assets under administration on the platform over the prior year.

Caruso's growth trajectory — expanding from New Zealand into Australia and the United States while scaling to serve institutional customers like Centuria Capital Group — reflects a broader pattern of small-market fintechs using a home-market beachhead to prove the product before institutional-scale international expansion, rather than launching multi-market from day one.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted the fund administration back office specifically — a category with high manual overhead and strong willingness-to-pay among institutional fund managers — rather than a more crowded consumer or SME fintech segment.
- Brought on an existing customer (Balmain) as an investor in the Series A, aligning product feedback and capital in the same relationship.
- Expanded outward from a New Zealand/Australia base into the US market only after demonstrating a tenfold scale-up in assets under administration, sequencing geographic expansion behind proven product-market fit.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Caruso demonstrates that fintech infrastructure serving institutional back-office functions can scale efficiently from a small home market (New Zealand) by proving deep product value with a handful of large fund-manager customers before expanding geographically — assets-under-administration growth, not just revenue, became the key signal investors used to price the round. It's a useful contrast to consumer fintech, where geographic reach itself often drives valuation; here, depth of integration with a smaller set of institutional customers did.
