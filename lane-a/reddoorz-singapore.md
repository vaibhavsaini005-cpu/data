## One-liner

RedDoorz, a Singapore-headquartered budget hotel network that aggregates and
rebrands under-two-star properties across Southeast Asia, raised a $70M
Series C just five months after its Series B and kept raising funding
through 2024.

## What happened

Founded in 2015, RedDoorz built a marketplace connecting travelers to budget
hotels, hostels and guesthouses, standardizing quality through training and
giving partner properties access to booking and payments technology and the
RedDoorz brand. In August 2019 the company closed a $70 million Series C led
by Asia Partners, with Rakuten Capital and Mirae Asset-Naver Asia Growth Fund
joining as new investors — arriving less than five months after a $45 million
Series B and bringing total funding to $140 million. At the time it managed
1,400 properties across 80 cities in Indonesia, Singapore, the Philippines
and Vietnam, targeting 2,000 properties by year-end, and was using the
capital to build a second engineering hub in Vietnam alongside its existing
tech center in India. The company continued to scale afterward, reporting
roughly 3,900 locations by 2023, and closed a further Series C-II round in
April 2024.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Choosing an asset-light aggregation/franchising model (branding and
  upgrading existing budget properties) instead of owning real estate,
  letting it scale across four countries without heavy capital expenditure.
- Raising back-to-back rounds (Series B then C within five months of 2019)
  to fund aggressive multi-country expansion while capital was available.
- Building distributed engineering hubs (India, then Vietnam) rather than
  centralizing product development in Singapore.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

RedDoorz shows a budget-hospitality aggregator surviving and continuing to
raise capital years past the 2019-2020 wave that sank comparable regional
players like HonestBee and several travel startups already logged in this
sprint — its durability traces to a low-capex model and diversification
across four national markets rather than dependence on any single country's
travel demand.
