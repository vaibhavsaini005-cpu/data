## One-liner

South Korean game developer Krafton — built around PUBG, one of the best-selling video games ever — completed South Korea's largest gaming IPO in August 2021, raising 5.6 trillion won (~$4.8B) on the Korea Stock Exchange after a decade-long founder-led journey from a tiny indie studio.

## What happened

Krafton traces its origin to Bluehole Studio, founded in 2007 by Chang Byung-gyu in Seoul. The studio spent years building MMORPGs (notably TERA) before Chang hired Brendan "PlayerUnknown" Greene in 2017 to build a battle-royale title. PUBG: Battlegrounds launched in early access on Steam in March 2017 and became a worldwide phenomenon — reaching 3 million concurrent players within months, selling 70 million copies on PC, and licensing a mobile version (PUBG Mobile) that generated billions in revenue independently, particularly in South and Southeast Asia.

Krafton restructured as a holding company in 2018 (absorbing PUBG Corp and other studios), began diversifying its game portfolio, and filed for IPO in 2021. The offering priced at 498,000 KRW per share, valuing the company at approximately 24 trillion won (~$21B). On its August 10, 2021 debut, shares fell roughly 8-9% below IPO price — attributed by analysts to an expensive valuation and regulatory headwinds in China (Tencent's 15.5% stake raised approval uncertainty). Nevertheless, the IPO raised approximately 4.3 trillion won net and marked the largest Korean gaming listing on record.

By Q1 2025, Krafton reported record-high quarterly sales, driven by PUBG IP extensions, new titles, and strong PUBG Mobile performance in emerging markets.

## Key decisions / turning points

- **Hiring Brendan Greene externally**: Chang was willing to bring in an outside creative lead rather than build battle-royale internally — a talent-first decision that changed Krafton's trajectory entirely.
- **Mobile licensing to Tencent/PUBG Mobile**: Rather than building mobile in-house, licensing to Tencent (for PUBG Mobile, developed by TiMi Studio) unlocked South/Southeast Asian markets and generated billions independently — at the cost of giving Tencent a significant stake.
- **Diversification post-IPO**: Krafton invested in multiple studios and genres to reduce PUBG single-IP concentration risk, including Indian market titles and partnerships.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Krafton is a case study in a "one mega-hit" company that managed the transition from single-IP dependency to a multi-game holding company before the IP's growth plateaued. The PUBG Mobile licensing deal also illustrates a core decision point for gaming companies with global IP: build mobile or license it. Licensing at scale (particularly to a Chinese publisher with massive distribution) generated more revenue faster but gave up control over one of the world's most-played mobile games.
