## One-liner

Kaspi.kz co-founders Mikheil Lomtadze and Vyacheslav Kim took the Kazakhstan super-app to Nasdaq in January 2024, upsizing the offering from 9 million to 11.3 million depositary shares and generating $1.04 billion for selling shareholders.

## What happened

Kaspi.kz combined consumer payments, banking, travel, and commerce with a merchant super-app. Already publicly traded in Kazakhstan and London, the company and its shareholders decided to add a U.S. listing. The depositary shares began trading on Nasdaq on January 19, 2024 under the ticker KSPI.

Demand allowed Lomtadze, Kim, and Asia Equity Partners to increase the offering from 9 million to 11.3 million shares at $92 each. The completed sale generated approximately $1.04 billion for the existing shareholders. Because no new shares were issued, Kaspi.kz itself received no proceeds; the transaction instead created U.S. market access and liquidity for long-term owners.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Lomtadze and Kim chose a U.S. listing in addition to Kaspi.kz's Kazakhstan and London market presence.
- Structured the transaction entirely as a secondary sale rather than diluting the company with new shares.
- Upsized the offer from 9 million to 11.3 million ADSs in response to investor demand.
- Priced at $92 per ADS and began Nasdaq trading on January 19, completing the offer four days later.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kaspi.kz demonstrates a public-market route for a profitable technology platform from a smaller economy. The secondary structure separated shareholder liquidity from company fundraising, while the upsized $1.04 billion sale provided a direct measure of international investor demand for the Kazakhstan-built super-app.
