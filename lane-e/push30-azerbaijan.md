## One-liner

Push30, a Baku-based fitness club membership app generating over $6 million in annual recurring revenue, announced a $3.5 million Series A funding round in July 2024 to accelerate expansion across the Middle East, North Africa, and Central Asia.

## What happened

Push30 built a fitness club membership app connecting users with gym and fitness facilities, achieving over $6 million in annual recurring revenue — a substantial commercial scale for a startup from Azerbaijan's still-nascent tech ecosystem. The company emerged as one of a dozen startups supported by Sabah.hub, a Baku-based incubator and accelerator backed by a consortium of local corporations, since its 2022 founding, reflecting the role domestic accelerator infrastructure played in nurturing the country's earliest generation of scalable tech companies.

The July 2024 $3.5 million Series A round was explicitly earmarked to fund market expansion beyond Azerbaijan into the broader Middle East, North Africa, and Central Asia region, positioning the company to leverage proven unit economics from its home market to enter adjacent, larger addressable markets. Co-founder and CEO Adil Gasimov described the company as being on "the road to becoming a unicorn," reflecting significant ambition for a startup originating from a market where, per contemporaneous reporting, all of Azerbaijan's startups combined raised just $2.6 million in venture funding in 2025 — making Push30's individual raise notably larger than the entire ecosystem's typical annual total.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Achieved substantial revenue scale ($6M+ ARR) before or alongside raising institutional venture capital, giving the company a strong negotiating position and de-risking the Series A round with demonstrated commercial traction rather than pure growth-stage speculation.
- Participated in Sabah.hub's incubator/accelerator program from an early stage, tapping into domestic corporate-backed startup infrastructure rather than relying solely on international accelerators or self-funded bootstrapping.
- Explicitly targeted cross-border expansion (MENA and Central Asia) as the primary use of Series A capital, rather than continuing to deepen penetration solely within Azerbaijan's smaller domestic market.
- Positioned the company's ambitions around unicorn-scale growth despite originating from a market with minimal precedent for venture-scale outcomes, reflecting founder conviction that the fitness-membership model could scale regionally.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Push30 demonstrates that startups from smaller, less venture-covered ecosystems like Azerbaijan can achieve meaningful commercial scale domestically before using that proven traction as the foundation for regional expansion, rather than needing outsized venture funding from inception to reach product-market fit. The company's raise — larger than the entire Azerbaijani startup ecosystem's typical annual total funding — also illustrates how a single breakout company with genuine revenue traction can attract capital disproportionate to what the broader local ecosystem typically sees, since strong unit economics can outweigh investor concerns about a market's overall venture-funding maturity.
