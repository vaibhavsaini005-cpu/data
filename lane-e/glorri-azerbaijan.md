## One-liner

Baku-founded HR-tech startup Glorri, one of the first Azerbaijani companies to build an applicant tracking system covering the full hiring funnel, closed a seed round backed by 500 Global in June 2023 and gained regional visibility by winning the Warsaw chapter of the V4-Azerbaijan Tech-Bridge program.

## What happened

Founded in 2017 by Anar Gasimli, Glorri built an applicant tracking system for enterprises — HR infrastructure covering the full hiring funnel from sourcing candidates through the complete recruitment process, rather than addressing only the initial candidate-sourcing problem that many earlier HR-tech tools focused on. The company's positioning as one of the first Azerbaijani HR-tech startups attempting full-funnel coverage put it in a category with relatively little local competition, requiring it to build market education alongside product development.

Glorri's seed round in June 2023 was backed by 500 Global, gained through participation in the 500 Global Eurasia accelerator program — a regional accelerator connecting Central Asian and Caucasus startups with international venture capital networks. The company further built its regional profile by winning the Warsaw chapter of the V4-Azerbaijan Tech-Bridge, a program connecting Azerbaijani startups with Central European markets and investors, extending its visibility and network beyond Azerbaijan's borders into the broader V4 region (Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia).

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built full-hiring-funnel HR infrastructure rather than a narrower sourcing tool, addressing a more complete enterprise HR workflow — a more ambitious product scope for an early-stage startup in an underserved regional market.
- Used an international accelerator (500 Global Eurasia) as the primary funding and network access point, rather than relying solely on Azerbaijan's still-developing domestic venture capital ecosystem.
- Pursued cross-regional visibility programs (the Warsaw V4-Azerbaijan Tech-Bridge) to build investor and market relationships in Central Europe, rather than confining growth ambitions to Azerbaijan and the immediate Caucasus region.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Glorri illustrates a common pattern for startups in smaller, venture-capital-underserved markets like Azerbaijan: using international accelerator programs (500 Global Eurasia) as the primary bridge to institutional funding, since local VC ecosystems may not yet have the depth or specialization to fund a category-defining HR-tech product. Actively pursuing cross-border visibility programs like the V4-Azerbaijan Tech-Bridge also shows a deliberate strategy of building investor and customer networks beyond the home market early, rather than waiting until later-stage growth to internationalize.
