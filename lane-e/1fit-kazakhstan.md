## One-liner

1Fit, an Almaty-based subscription fitness marketplace founded in 2018, spent 2025 unwinding a string of failed international expansions — shutting down in Mexico in April and formally liquidating its Malaysian operations in June after burning $4.2 million to generate only $2.7 million in Malaysian sales — while retreating to consolidate around its core Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan and UAE markets.

## What happened

1Fit built a subscription model letting users access hundreds of gyms and fitness studios through a single membership, a model it first proved out in its home market of Kazakhstan before pushing aggressively into international expansion — including the UK (exited 2023), Russia (exited 2022), Mexico, and Malaysia. In Malaysia, the company invested $4.2 million into building out the app and market presence, offering Klang Valley users access to over 600 partner studios, but generated only $2.7 million in sales before founder and CEO Murat Alikhanov announced on June 13, 2025 that the company would initiate formal liquidation of the Malaysian entity, citing an inability to raise the additional capital needed to keep it running.

The Malaysian shutdown came just two months after 1Fit had already ceased operations in Mexico in April 2025, forming a clear pattern: rather than continuing to fund unprofitable geographic bets, the company pulled back to consolidate operations in four markets — Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, Uzbekistan, and the UAE — where its original subscription-fitness model had already demonstrated stronger unit economics.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Expanded aggressively into distant, unfamiliar markets (UK, Russia, Mexico, Malaysia) years before proving the subscription-fitness model could sustain the customer acquisition costs required outside Central Asia.
- Continued funding the Malaysian expansion to the tune of $4.2 million before conceding the unit economics (only $2.7 million in sales generated) could not be fixed with further capital.
- Chose formal, sequenced retreats — Russia (2022), UK (2023), Mexico (April 2025), Malaysia (June 2025) — rather than persisting indefinitely in underperforming markets, redirecting resources toward the four markets where the model already worked.
- Communicated the Malaysian shutdown directly to affected subscribers and staff, triggering public backlash and refund disputes even as the company framed the exit as a deliberate capital-discipline decision.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

1Fit's 2025 retrenchment illustrates a common trap for emerging-market startups: aggressive geographic expansion into markets with fundamentally different customer acquisition costs and studio-partnership dynamics can consume years of capital before the underlying unit economics reveal themselves as unworkable. The company's eventual response — sequentially shutting down four international markets to refocus on the Central Asian and Gulf markets where its original model was validated — shows that consolidating around proven markets, even after years of expansion, can be a rational strategic pivot rather than outright failure, provided the retreat is decisive rather than drawn out.
