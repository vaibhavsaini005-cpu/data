## One-liner

Tbilisi-founded fintech Payze, the first Georgian startup admitted to Y Combinator, launched a rebuilt platform called "Core" in late 2023 and expanded payment-processing operations from Georgia into Uzbekistan and other former Soviet states.

## What happened

Founded in 2019 by Giorgi Tsurtsumia and Kakha Gelashvili, Payze built payment-processing infrastructure explicitly modeled on Stripe's developer-first approach, targeting a region — the former Soviet Union — where merchants had few modern, unified payment gateway options and where international processors were often reluctant to operate due to regulatory and banking-infrastructure complexity. The company backed its ambitions with a Y Combinator placement, becoming the first Georgian startup accepted into the accelerator, and secured backing from investors including Global Founders Fund, Pipedrive founder Ragnar Sass, and Flexport founder Ryan Peterson.

In late 2023, Payze launched "Core," a rebuilt platform introducing fully digital merchant onboarding, an improved dashboard, and PCI-compliant security enhancements — replacing manual, friction-heavy processes that had limited how quickly merchants could start accepting payments. This platform rebuild coincided with the company's operational expansion beyond Georgia into Uzbekistan, supporting local-currency transactions alongside USD and EUR.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Positioned explicitly as "Stripe for the former Soviet Union" — borrowing a proven developer-first payments model and adapting it to a region with fragmented, underserved payment infrastructure rather than building a wholly novel product category.
- Used Y Combinator acceptance as a credibility and network lever to attract international investors unfamiliar with the Georgian market, rather than relying solely on regional venture capital.
- Rebuilt its core platform (the "Core" launch) years after founding rather than iterating on the original system indefinitely, prioritizing a step-change in onboarding speed and compliance over incremental feature additions.
- Expanded into Uzbekistan specifically, rather than into more saturated fintech markets, targeting a large, underbanked population with fewer existing modern payment providers.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Payze illustrates how a "local Stripe" positioning can work in underserved regions where global payment processors are absent or unreliable — but only if the startup pairs that positioning with credible international validation (Y Combinator, recognizable angel investors) to overcome geographic skepticism from capital markets. The platform rebuild before regional expansion also shows a disciplined sequencing: strengthening core infrastructure (onboarding, compliance, security) before scaling into a new country, rather than expanding on an unproven technical base.
