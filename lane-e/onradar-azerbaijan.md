## One-liner

Onradar founder Rufat Mammadli sold the Azerbaijani monitoring-software startup to a U.S.-based technology executive for a five-figure sum in August 2024, completing a handover while the product continued operating.

## What happened

Onradar developed a software service for monitoring website uptime, APIs, SSL certificates, and related cloud-service failures. In August 2024, founder Rufat Mammadli announced that a U.S.-based technology executive had acquired the startup in a private transaction.

Mammadli said he chose to sell so he could invest his time and proceeds in a new business area. The buyer remained anonymous but told Tech.az that the price was in the five figures. Mammadli agreed to work temporarily with the new owner to complete the handover. The transaction closed rather than remaining an announced deal, and Onradar's product site remained live afterward with paid monitoring plans under Altailab.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Mammadli built Onradar around a focused B2B need: monitoring websites and multi-step API flows for failures.
- Chose a private sale to a U.S.-based technology executive instead of continuing to operate the company indefinitely himself.
- Stayed for a transition period so the buyer could take control of the product and operations.
- Used the completed five-figure exit as capital and space for a future B2B venture, while Onradar continued as an operating product.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Onradar is a small but resolved founder exit, not a funding announcement or an aspirational partnership. It shows that a narrowly scoped developer tool can create transferable value without reaching venture scale: the measurable result was a completed five-figure acquisition and a product that remained available after the handover.
