## One-liner

Almaty-based fintech Solva raised a $20M investment from Neo Crystal Holdings (backed by the Sawiris family and ZCP) in January 2024 to fund its transformation from a microfinance organization into a fully licensed SME-focused bank in Central Asia.

## What happened

Solva began as a microfinance organization providing working-capital loans to small and medium-sized businesses in Kazakhstan, an underserved segment for traditional bank lending across Central Asia. Rather than staying within the more lightly-regulated microfinance category, the company set out to become a fully licensed bank — a strategic pivot requiring significantly more capital, regulatory approval, and operational infrastructure than continuing as a non-bank lender.

The January 2024 $20M investment from Neo Crystal Holdings, a vehicle backed by the Egyptian Sawiris family and investment firm ZCP, was raised specifically to support this transition. The funding was earmarked for Solva's banking license application and the build-out of full-service banking capabilities, while the company continued to position itself around its core competency: underwriting and serving small and medium-sized enterprises that larger, traditional Kazakh banks had historically underserved.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Chose to pursue a full banking license rather than remain a microfinance organization, trading regulatory simplicity for expanded product capability (deposits, broader lending products) and greater institutional credibility with SME customers.
- Attracted international, non-regional investor capital (the Egypt-linked Sawiris family via Neo Crystal Holdings) for a Central Asian fintech transition, signaling investor confidence extending beyond Kazakhstan's borders.
- Maintained focus on the SME lending niche throughout the transition rather than broadening into consumer banking, preserving the specific underwriting expertise built during its microfinance years as its core differentiator going into the banking license process.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Solva's transition from microfinance organization to licensed bank illustrates a specific growth pathway available to successful non-bank lenders in emerging markets: once a lender has proven its underwriting model within a lighter regulatory category, converting to a full banking license unlocks lower-cost deposit funding and a broader product suite, funded by outside capital specifically earmarked for the regulatory transition rather than pure growth. Retaining a narrow niche focus (SME lending) through that transition, rather than diluting it with a broader banking mandate, kept the company's competitive edge intact during a structurally risky conversion process.
