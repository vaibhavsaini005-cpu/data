## One-liner

Founder Balaji Srinivasan put Network School's planned RM500 million-plus Malaysian expansion on hold on July 17, 2026; four days later Johor authorities revoked its business licence and ordered all Forest City operations to cease from July 22.

## What happened

Srinivasan established Network School in Forest City, Johor, in October 2024 as a residential community for founders, engineers, investors, and other technology workers. He said the company invested more than RM100 million without government funding, employed dozens of Malaysians, and attracted visitors from more than 70 countries.

After scrutiny of the campus triggered inspections, Srinivasan announced on July 17, 2026 that further Malaysian investment was on hold. He said Network School had been preparing an expansion worth more than RM500 million and a global merit scholarship, but would not proceed without assurances from the government. On July 21, Johor Chief Minister Onn Hafiz Ghazi announced that the Iskandar Puteri City Council had revoked the Malaysian operator's business licence and ordered all Network School activity in Forest City to stop effective July 22. Reuters reported that the decision followed inspection reports, enforcement findings, and the company's representations; immigration officials separately said the 266 foreign nationals inspected had valid documents.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Srinivasan chose Forest City for Network School in October 2024 and committed more than RM100 million to the Malaysian campus.
- On July 17, 2026 he paused a planned RM500 million-plus expansion while seeking government assurances and a meeting with the prime minister.
- The local council reviewed the company's submissions and enforcement record, then revoked its licence rather than allowing operations to continue conditionally.
- The cessation order took effect July 22, converting the paused expansion into a resolved shutdown of the Malaysian operation.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Network School Malaysia shows that large private-capital commitments do not substitute for durable local operating permission. The measurable result was not merely reputational uncertainty: more than RM500 million of planned expansion was halted and the existing campus lost its licence. Businesses built around unconventional community models need regulatory and premises-use compliance to be as explicit as their product vision.
