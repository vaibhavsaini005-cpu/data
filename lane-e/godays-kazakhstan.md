## One-liner

GoDays, a Kazakhstan-founded AI tool that analyzes keyboard usage patterns to prevent employee burnout, raised $490,000 in March 2024 from 500 Global and other investors to scale into South Korea and Japan, working with global clients including Shell and Toyota.

## What happened

GoDays, founded in June 2022, built an AI-based system that analyzes employees' keyboard usage patterns as a proxy signal for detecting early burnout risk, giving employers a passive, non-intrusive monitoring approach rather than relying solely on periodic surveys or self-reporting, which can miss burnout developing between check-ins. The company's client base already spanned Central Asia, South Korea, and Japan by the time of its funding round, working with recognizable global brands including Shell, Toyota, and Choco — a notable roster of enterprise clients for a company originating from Kazakhstan's still-developing tech ecosystem.

The March 2024 $490,000 round came from 500 Global, DOMiNO Ventures, AloqaVentures, and individual investors including Murat Abdrakhmanov, Anvar Bakiyev, and Asima Abdrakhmanova. The capital was earmarked specifically for scaling GoDays' presence in South Korea and Japan — markets where workplace burnout and overwork are widely recognized cultural and policy concerns — and for enhancing the scientific rigor of the product's burnout-detection features.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built burnout detection around passive keyboard-usage-pattern analysis rather than active self-reporting surveys, capturing a continuous behavioral signal rather than depending on employees to proactively flag their own stress levels.
- Targeted enterprise clients directly (Shell, Toyota, Choco) from an early stage rather than starting with smaller SMB customers, establishing credibility with globally recognized brands despite the company's Kazakhstan origin.
- Explicitly prioritized South Korea and Japan as expansion markets for the round's use of funds, targeting geographies where workplace burnout is a well-documented, high-salience social and corporate issue likely to drive strong product-market fit.
- Attracted 500 Global, a globally recognized early-stage investor, as part of the round, lending international credibility to a Central Asian HR-tech startup.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GoDays demonstrates that HR-tech startups from smaller, less internationally visible ecosystems like Kazakhstan can win recognizable global enterprise clients (Shell, Toyota) by solving a genuinely underserved problem — passive, continuous burnout detection — rather than needing to be based in a major startup hub to earn enterprise trust. Deliberately targeting expansion into markets with acute cultural salience for the product's core problem (South Korea and Japan's well-known overwork concerns) also illustrates a focused go-to-market strategy: rather than expanding broadly, the company prioritized markets where the pain point it solves is most acutely felt and most likely to translate into paying enterprise demand.
