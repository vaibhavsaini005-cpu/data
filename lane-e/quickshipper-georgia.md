## One-liner

QuickShipper, a Tbilisi-based SaaS platform helping online merchants manage multi-channel delivery operations, secured a seed investment from European fund New Nordic Ventures in February 2024 to fund expansion beyond Georgia into wider European markets.

## What happened

QuickShipper built a delivery management platform that lets e-commerce businesses consolidate order fulfillment, route planning, order batching, and courier dispatching across multiple sales channels into a single system — addressing a gap for online merchants in Georgia and the wider Caucasus region who previously had to coordinate delivery logistics manually or through fragmented, single-purpose tools. The platform positioned itself as infrastructure for the growing regional e-commerce sector rather than a delivery service itself, competing on the software layer rather than owning fleets or couriers directly.

In February 2024, QuickShipper announced a seed investment from New Nordic Ventures, a European venture fund whose initial checks typically start in the EUR 200-300k range, explicitly earmarked to support the company's expansion beyond its home Georgian market into wider European territory. The round followed the company's earlier public introduction as a new delivery-management solution for Georgian businesses in late 2023, showing a rapid trajectory from local market entry to institutional European investor backing within a matter of months.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a software infrastructure layer for delivery management rather than operating delivery fleets directly, positioning QuickShipper to scale across multiple markets without the capital intensity of owning logistics operations.
- Launched first in the Georgian domestic market to establish a functioning product and customer base before pursuing international investor attention.
- Attracted a European venture fund (New Nordic Ventures) specifically focused on funding cross-border expansion, rather than remaining reliant on regional or Georgian-only capital sources.
- Moved from public market launch to secured seed investment within roughly three months, reflecting investor confidence in the underlying product-market fit demonstrated in the Georgian market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

QuickShipper demonstrates that logistics-software infrastructure businesses — rather than asset-heavy delivery operators — can attract international venture capital from a Caucasus base by first proving the model domestically before pursuing cross-border investor attention. The company's ability to secure a European fund's backing specifically for international expansion also reflects a broader pattern of Western European investors increasingly looking to the Caucasus and Central Asia for e-commerce infrastructure plays as those regions' online retail sectors mature.
