## One-liner

PeerHive co-founder Vincent Yeo announced on June 28, 2026 that the Malaysian stablecoin-based SME lender would shut down and withdraw from the Securities Commission's sandbox after approved stablecoins remained unavailable and startup funding dried up.

## What happened

PeerHive was founded in December 2022 to let investors finance small-business loans through smart contracts and stablecoins. In November 2025 it became one of six companies selected for Malaysia's first Securities Commission regulatory sandbox, receiving approval in principle to prepare a controlled deployment.

The model depended on stablecoins approved through Bank Negara Malaysia's Digital Asset Innovation Hub. Yeo said implementation remained a long way off, preventing PeerHive from progressing beyond preliminary sandbox approval. With outside funding also unavailable, he decided the company could no longer sustain itself, notified the regulator, and announced the shutdown on June 28, 2026.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Yeo pursued a regulated sandbox route rather than launching the stablecoin lending protocol outside the supervisory framework.
- Waited for approved settlement assets instead of substituting unapproved stablecoins.
- Withdrew from the sandbox when regulatory dependencies and financing runway could no longer be reconciled.
- Closed the company and moved into a product role at another fintech rather than extending an unfunded pre-launch period.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

PeerHive shows that sandbox admission is not the same as permission to operate. A regulated fintech can satisfy the first gate and still fail because a dependent part of the regulatory stack is unavailable. Founders building around controlled digital assets must finance for the full dependency timeline, not merely the expected product-testing period.
