## One-liner

TBC Uzbekistan CEO Nika Kurdiani led the November 2024 launch of the TBC Salom debit card, then expanded it onto Visa in November 2025; by February 2026 the bank had issued more than one million cards.

## What happened

TBC Uzbekistan built a mobile-first banking ecosystem around TBC Bank and payment service Payme. In November 2024, the bank added TBC Salom as its flagship debit card for daily transactions, positioning it as an entry point into the wider ecosystem rather than a standalone card product.

The bank kept developing the product after launch. In November 2025 it began issuing TBC Salom on Visa and added 1% cashback on all purchases plus 5% cashback at selected international marketplaces. By February 2026, a little over a year after launch, more than one million cards had been issued. The bank also reported that users were receiving about 12 billion Uzbek som in bonuses each month.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Kurdiani's team launched a mass-market debit card in November 2024 to extend TBC from digital lending and payments into customers' everyday banking.
- Made issuance and delivery free, paid 12% annually on eligible balances, and reimbursed selected cash-handling fees to reduce switching friction.
- Expanded the card to Visa in November 2025 and added broader cashback benefits instead of treating the initial launch as a finished product.
- Used the card as a cross-selling gateway into TBC Uzbekistan's credit, payments, insurance, and buy-now-pay-later services.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

TBC Salom provides a resolved measure of product-led expansion: more than one million cards issued within roughly fifteen months. The case shows how a digital bank can turn a narrowly defined daily-use product into an acquisition channel for a wider financial ecosystem, provided it keeps improving the proposition after launch.
