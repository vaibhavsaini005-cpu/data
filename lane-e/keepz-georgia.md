## One-liner

Keepz, a Tbilisi-based fintech founded by former National Bank of Georgia Vice-Governor Papuna Lezhava, launched Georgia's first 24/7 instant interbank transfer service in late 2025, integrating five banks including Credo Bank, PASHA Bank Georgia, and Silk Bank to let customers move money nationwide at any hour, including weekends and holidays.

## What happened

Keepz built open banking infrastructure enabling instant, round-the-clock money transfers between different Georgian banks — a capability that had not previously existed in the country's banking system, where interbank transfers were typically constrained by business hours and settlement delays. The company's founder, Papuna Lezhava, brought direct regulatory and central-banking experience from his prior role as Vice-Governor of the National Bank of Georgia, giving Keepz credibility and relationship access within Georgia's banking sector that a founder without that background might have struggled to build.

The service, launched in late 2025, integrated five banks — including Credo Bank, PASHA Bank Georgia, and Silk Bank — into a single instant-transfer network supporting multi-currency transactions in Georgian lari, U.S. dollars, and euros, with daily transfer limits of up to 100,000 GEL. By building infrastructure that multiple competing banks all plugged into, rather than a single-bank product, Keepz positioned itself as neutral, shared financial infrastructure for the Georgian banking sector — a model that required convincing multiple separate banking institutions to integrate with a common third-party platform rather than building competing proprietary solutions independently.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Founded by a former central bank Vice-Governor, giving Keepz direct regulatory credibility and banking-sector relationships that likely eased the process of convincing multiple competing banks to integrate with a shared platform.
- Built genuinely shared, multi-bank infrastructure (integrating five separate banks) rather than a single proprietary banking product, positioning the company as neutral open-banking infrastructure rather than a competitor to any individual bank.
- Supported multi-currency transfers (lari, dollars, euros) from launch rather than a single-currency-only service, addressing Georgia's economy where foreign-currency transactions are common alongside the domestic lari.
- Launched with meaningful transaction limits (up to 100,000 GEL daily) rather than a token pilot-scale service, positioning the product for genuine commercial and consumer transaction volume from the outset.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Keepz demonstrates how open banking infrastructure fintechs can succeed by convincing multiple competing banks to integrate with shared, neutral infrastructure — a coordination challenge that is often harder to solve than building a single proprietary product, but one that founders with direct regulatory or central-banking experience may be better positioned to navigate given their existing relationships and credibility within the banking sector. The launch of Georgia's first genuinely 24/7 instant interbank transfer service also illustrates how smaller national banking markets can leapfrog toward modern payment infrastructure once a credible infrastructure provider assembles the right multi-bank coalition, rather than needing to wait for each individual bank to build equivalent capabilities independently.
