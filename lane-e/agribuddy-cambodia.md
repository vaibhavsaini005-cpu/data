## One-liner

Agribuddy, a Siem Reap-headquartered agritech company serving thousands of rice, cassava, and corn farmers across seven Cambodian provinces, secured a $2.8M Series A led by Smart Axiata's Digital Innovation Fund (SADIF) in October 2018, followed by investment from footballer Keisuke Honda's KSK Angel Fund in 2019.

## What happened

Agribuddy built a cloud-based platform combining farmer financing, agronomy data, and business intelligence for smallholder rice, cassava, and corn farmers across seven Cambodian provinces (Siem Reap, Banteay Meanchey, Oddar Meanchey, Battambang, Pailin, Pursat, and Kampong Thom). Rather than serving farmers only through a direct digital app, the company employed a network of over 600 rural "Buddies" — local entrepreneurs providing hands-on technical assistance — bridging the gap between a purely digital platform and farmers who may have limited digital access or literacy.

The October 2018 $2.8M Series A, led by Smart Axiata's Digital Innovation Fund (SADIF) and reported by Khmer Times, made Agribuddy the fourth company in SADIF's portfolio at the time. The company's momentum continued in 2019 when KSK Angel Fund — founded by Japanese footballer Keisuke Honda — named Agribuddy as one of its top two investees. By the time of these rounds, the company was already serving over 50,000 local farmers, giving it real operational scale as a proof point for investors betting on agritech-driven financial inclusion in Cambodia's large but underserved agricultural sector.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Combined a "Buddy" network of local rural entrepreneurs with the digital platform rather than launching purely as an app, directly addressing a practical barrier — limited digital literacy and access among smallholder farmers — that a pure-software approach would have left unsolved.
- Focused specifically on rice, cassava, and corn farmers across defined provinces rather than attempting to serve all Cambodian agriculture broadly, allowing deeper crop-specific agronomy expertise and data collection.
- Attracted telecom-linked venture capital (Smart Axiata's SADIF) as an early lead investor, tapping into a strategic investor with existing infrastructure and market reach across Cambodia's telecom-connected population.
- Secured a second, high-profile international investor (Keisuke Honda's KSK Angel Fund) within roughly a year of the Series A, reflecting sustained investor confidence following the initial round.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Agribuddy demonstrates that agritech platforms serving smallholder farmers in markets with limited digital infrastructure often need a hybrid human-plus-technology model — a network of local, trusted intermediaries (the "Buddies") — rather than relying solely on a direct-to-farmer app, since digital literacy and access barriers can otherwise exclude the very population the platform is meant to serve. The company's ability to attract both a strategic telecom-linked investor and a high-profile international angel fund within about a year also illustrates how demonstrated on-the-ground scale (50,000+ farmers served) can be a more persuasive signal to follow-on investors than novelty alone.
