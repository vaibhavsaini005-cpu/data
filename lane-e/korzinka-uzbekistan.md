## One-liner

Founder and chairman Zafar Khashimov chose a US$110 million minority-equity round for Uzbek supermarket chain Korzinka in April 2025 while declining another US$280 million of demand; by year-end the retailer had opened 62 stores, reaching 217 locations.

## What happened

Khashimov took Korzinka's investment project to four leading banks in London and received interest from all four. On April 18, 2025, he explained that the company selected US$110 million from Abu Dhabi Uzbek Investment and the Uzbek-Oman Investment Company because it did not need the additional US$280 million offered.

The transaction closed on June 10 as minority equity rather than a sale of control. The capital supported a new 49,000-square-metre distribution center, smaller Korzinka Mahalla stores and the Korzinka Go online business. By December 30, Korzinka reported 62 openings during 2025—38 supermarkets and 24 Mahalla stores—bringing the network to 217 locations; Korzinka Go also expanded beyond Tashkent into multiple regions.

## Key decisions / turning points

- In April 2025, Khashimov chose to take only US$110 million despite US$390 million of total investor interest, limiting dilution to the capital the expansion plan required.
- Korzinka closed the minority investment on June 10 and added investor representatives and international retail expertise to its supervisory board.
- The company paired the equity round with a US$50 million, 49,000-square-metre distribution center designed to support national expansion.
- During 2025, Korzinka opened 62 stores, finished with 217 across all formats and expanded online grocery delivery into regional cities.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Demand for capital is not the same as a need to accept it. Khashimov matched the round to a defined logistics and store-expansion program while retaining majority ownership; the completed infrastructure and 62 openings show that the capital was converted into measurable operating capacity within the year.
