## One-liner

Nham24, a homegrown Cambodian food delivery, ride-hailing, and quick-commerce platform launched in 2016, was fully acquired by Nasdaq-listed Grab in a deal completed in early 2025, hailed as the largest venture capital exit in Cambodia's startup history.

## What happened

Nham24 built a multi-service platform in Cambodia spanning food delivery, ride-hailing, travel, courier, groceries, and quick-commerce — a broad "super-app" style model addressing multiple everyday consumer needs within a single homegrown Cambodian product, in a market that regional giants like Grab had not yet deeply penetrated with a comparable local service breadth. Growing steadily since its 2016 launch, Nham24 became one of Cambodia's most recognized and successful technology companies well before any acquisition talks.

Grab's acquisition of Nham24, finalized in December 2024 and completed with Cambodian Competition Commission approval by early 2025, represented Grab's strategy of acquiring proven local platforms to accelerate its own market entry rather than building comparable multi-service infrastructure from scratch in a new market. Rather than keeping Nham24 as a standalone brand, Grab subsequently announced in May 2025 that it would fully integrate Nham24 into its unified Grab app, phasing out the standalone Nham24 app entirely and shifting all of its services exclusively into the regional Grab ecosystem.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a broad multi-service "super-app" model (food, rides, travel, courier, groceries, quick-commerce) from a homegrown Cambodian base rather than focusing on a single vertical, creating a platform valuable enough for a regional super-app leader to acquire outright rather than compete against.
- Achieved sufficient scale and market position over roughly eight years (2016-2024) to become, according to industry commentary, the largest venture capital exit in Cambodia's startup history.
- Accepted full acquisition and eventual brand integration into Grab's platform rather than continuing to operate as an independent standalone brand, prioritizing an outright exit over maintaining separate market identity.
- Required and obtained Cambodian Competition Commission regulatory approval before the deal closed, reflecting the transaction's scale relative to the domestic market.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Nham24's acquisition demonstrates how regional super-app platforms like Grab increasingly prefer acquiring dominant homegrown local players — even in smaller markets like Cambodia — over attempting to build comparable multi-service local infrastructure independently, since local platforms typically hold deeper merchant, rider, and consumer relationships built over years that would be costly and slow to replicate. The characterization of this deal as Cambodia's largest-ever startup exit also signals a broader validation moment for the country's tech ecosystem: a credible, sizeable acquisition by a Nasdaq-listed regional leader can help establish a market's startup scene as investable, even where overall historical venture funding has remained comparatively small.
