## One-liner

Uzbek digital payments app Payme reached full acquisition in May 2023, when Georgia's TBC Bank Group bought the remaining 49% stake for $55.7M, completing a buyout that valued the company at $113.6M and gave TBC full ownership.

## What happened

Payme built a leading digital payments app in Uzbekistan, serving individuals and small businesses with payments, microloans, and merchant services. TBC Bank Group first took a 51% stake in Payme in April 2019 as part of a strategy to establish a fintech ecosystem in Uzbekistan, structuring the deal with a shareholder agreement that set out the path to eventual full ownership.

That path completed on May 22, 2023, when TBC exercised the agreement and acquired the remaining 49% for $55.7M, valuing Payme at $113.6M and giving TBC 100% ownership. The acquisition was explicitly positioned to let TBC integrate Payme with its digital banking unit, TBC UZ, to build a combined fintech ecosystem in the country rather than operate the payments app as a standalone minority-owned asset.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Accepted a majority (51%) strategic investor in 2019 rather than staying independently owned, trading some autonomy for institutional backing and a defined path to full acquisition.
- Structured the initial 2019 deal with a shareholder agreement that pre-defined the terms for TBC to acquire the remaining stake, removing negotiation uncertainty from the eventual full buyout.
- Completed the transition to full subsidiary status in 2023, aligning with TBC's broader strategy to build an integrated banking-plus-payments ecosystem in Uzbekistan.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Payme's trajectory shows a structured, multi-year path to a full-ownership exit as a viable outcome distinct from either an IPO or outright failure — the 2019 majority stake sale effectively pre-negotiated the eventual full acquisition, giving both sides certainty years in advance. For a fintech in a market like Uzbekistan where full independent scaling to profitability is harder without institutional backing, aligning early with a strategic acquirer's roadmap converted into a clean, well-valued exit once the broader banking group's ecosystem strategy matured.
