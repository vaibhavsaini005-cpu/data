## One-liner

Co-founders Artem Markevich and Martin Čermák sold Czech voice-agent startup VOCALLS to CallMiner on June 3, 2025; within three months its technology shipped in OmniAgent, and both founders remained in senior automation and engineering roles at the buyer.

## What happened

Markevich and Čermák spent eight years building VOCALLS into a voice-first automation platform serving companies in 27 countries. Markevich said they chose CallMiner after looking for a partner that shared their approach to human-centered, data-informed automation and could help the much smaller Czech company compete globally.

CallMiner completed the acquisition on June 3, 2025 and combined VOCALLS' agents with its conversation analytics. On August 20, the buyer released OmniAgent across voice, chat and email using the acquired automation technology. In 2026, Čermák was serving as CallMiner's VP of Engineering and Automation and Markevich as SVP of Automation, confirming that the product and founders were integrated rather than discarded.

## Key decisions / turning points

- In June 2025, Markevich and Čermák chose a strategic sale to CallMiner after building independently since 2017.
- Their pre-sale platform automated more than 75% of customer requests, cut waiting times by 70% and served customers in 27 countries.
- By August 20, 2025, CallMiner had converted the combined technology into OmniAgent, an omnichannel virtual-agent product.
- Both founders stayed in executive operating roles responsible for automation and engineering after the acquisition.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

The useful test of a technology acquisition is whether the buyer ships the acquired capability and retains the people who built it. VOCALLS cleared both tests quickly: its automation became a named CallMiner product and its co-founders remained responsible for the combined platform.
