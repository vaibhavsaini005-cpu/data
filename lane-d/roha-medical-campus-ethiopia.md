## One-liner

Roha Medical Campus secured $42M in first-tranche equity funding in February 2024 — the largest single funding round in Ethiopia that year — to build a 350-bed multispecialty hospital in Addis Ababa as part of a broader $130M phase-1 investment.

## What happened

Roha Medical Campus is building a world-class private hospital complex in Addis Ababa, located less than a kilometer from East Africa's busiest international airport, directly addressing two linked problems: Ethiopia's $500 million annual healthcare outflow (patients traveling abroad for care unavailable domestically) and the broader continent's $5 billion medical tourism opportunity that Ethiopia had not previously captured. The February 2024 $42M equity injection represented the first tranche of a $130M total investment planned for the hospital's first phase, with an additional $70 million in debt financing led by a development finance institution earmarked for the same period.

The project's ambitions extend well beyond the initial 350-bed hospital: a further $200M investment is planned to expand the campus into additional specialized hospitals, plus a teaching, research, and innovation hub — building not just a single medical facility but an integrated healthcare and medical-education campus. The facility is also being built to be the first IFC EDGE-certified hospital building in Ethiopia, incorporating solar power generation, battery storage, and a thermally-activated heating and cooling system, positioning sustainability as a core design principle rather than an afterthought.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Targeted the specific, quantifiable problem of Ethiopia's healthcare capital outflow ($500M annually spent by patients traveling abroad) as the core investment thesis, giving investors a clear, measurable addressable opportunity rather than a generic "healthcare access" pitch.
- Structured financing across multiple tranches and instrument types (equity now, debt from a development finance institution to follow, additional equity for later expansion phases) rather than attempting to raise the full $130M+ needed upfront in a single round.
- Located the campus strategically near East Africa's busiest international airport, directly supporting its medical-tourism ambitions by minimizing travel friction for international patients.
- Built sustainability certification (IFC EDGE) into the foundational design rather than retrofitting it later, aligning with development-finance institution priorities that likely eased access to the debt financing planned for later in 2024.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Roha Medical Campus demonstrates how large-scale healthcare infrastructure investment in emerging markets can be justified and funded by framing the opportunity around capital outflow reduction (keeping domestic healthcare spending in-country) combined with medical-tourism revenue capture — a dual economic argument that resonates with both impact-oriented and purely commercial investors. The multi-tranche, multi-instrument financing structure (equity now, DFI debt next, more equity later for expansion) is also a useful model for other large infrastructure projects in emerging markets: sequencing capital by phase and source type reduces the pressure to raise an entire project's capital requirement in one round.
