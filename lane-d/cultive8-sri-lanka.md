## One-liner

Sri Lankan agritech startup Agrithmics rebranded as Cultive8 in June 2023 alongside a $1.75M pre-Series A raise from BRAC Osiris Impact Ventures, repositioning explicitly for expansion beyond Sri Lanka into South and Southeast Asia.

## What happened

Agrithmics built agricultural technology for Sri Lankan farmers, becoming the first Sri Lankan agritech startup to reach an $8M valuation within three years of founding. In June 2023, the company both closed a $1.75M pre-Series A round led by BRAC Osiris Impact Ventures and rebranded from Agrithmics to Cultive8 — pairing the funding milestone with a deliberate identity change tied to regional expansion ambitions.

The rebrand was explicitly framed around expansion strategy: moving from a Sri Lanka-specific identity and positioning ("Agrithmics") to a name intended to work across South Asian and Southeast Asian markets, ahead of the company's actual geographic expansion. BRAC Osiris Impact Ventures' involvement — an impact-focused investor — also signaled the company's positioning around measurable agricultural-development outcomes for smallholder farmers, not just commercial agritech.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Achieved an $8M valuation and "first" status within Sri Lanka's small agritech sector before pursuing wider regional ambitions, establishing home-market credibility first.
- Paired a funding round directly with a rebrand, rather than treating them as separate events — using the capital raise as the moment to reposition the company's market identity.
- Chose an impact-focused investor (BRAC Osiris Impact Ventures) whose mandate aligns with smallholder-farmer outcomes, rather than a purely commercial VC, shaping the company's stated mission alongside its capital structure.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Cultive8's rebrand-plus-raise shows a deliberate use of a funding milestone as the inflection point to signal strategic repositioning to the market — rather than quietly renaming later, the company used investor validation and capital as social proof for a name and identity built for regional ambition. For agritech specifically, aligning with an impact investor at this stage also positions the company favorably for future development-finance or blended-capital rounds common in agricultural technology serving smallholder farmers.
