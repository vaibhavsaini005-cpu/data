## One-liner

Paperfly's Bangladeshi co-founders bought back Ecom Express's entire 82% stake in July 2024 and rebuilt the logistics company after a cash-crunch halt; it resumed at 26,000–30,000 deliveries a day with a new emphasis on B2B outsourcing and profitability.

## What happened

Paperfly stopped accepting orders on September 25, 2023 after promised investment did not arrive and more than Tk10 crore was trapped in deposits and receivables. The interruption hit a network that had served more than 35,000 merchants across 491 subdistricts.

Founder Shahriar Hasan said the team resumed operations within weeks. In July 2024, co-founder Rahath Ahmed confirmed that the local founders had completed the purchase of Ecom Express's 82% holding, restoring founder control while Paperfly broadened its B2B logistics work.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Rather than liquidate after the September 2023 halt, Hasan and the founding team restarted operations using existing infrastructure and loyal customers.
- The co-founders negotiated the buyback of the foreign investor's full 82% stake, completed in July 2024.
- Paperfly increased its focus on outsourced B2B distribution alongside consumer e-commerce deliveries.
- By July 2024, it was processing 26,000–30,000 orders daily and expected more than Tk40 crore in 2024 revenue.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

A financing crisis does not always mean the operating asset is broken. Paperfly's founders separated the ownership problem from market demand, regained control at a reset valuation and reused a nationwide network instead of rebuilding from zero.
