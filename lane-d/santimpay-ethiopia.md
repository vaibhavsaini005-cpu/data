## One-liner

SantimPay, an Ethiopian digital payments fintech established in 2019, announced a five-year strategic partnership with Visa in September 2025 to deploy 100,000 low-cost point-of-sale terminals across Ethiopia — starting with 20,000 units in 2026 — with Visa funding the upfront device cost.

## What happened

SantimPay built payment infrastructure enabling merchants across Ethiopia to accept digital payments, operating in a market where cash has historically dominated retail and merchant transactions due to limited POS terminal penetration. The company's core value proposition — modern payment acceptance infrastructure for Ethiopian merchants — positioned it as a natural partner for Visa as the global payments network sought to expand its footprint in one of East Africa's largest and most populous, but still under-penetrated, digital payments markets.

Announced at the Visa Connect Ethiopia 2025 summit on September 30, 2025, the five-year partnership commits to rolling out 100,000 locally assembled POS terminals across Ethiopia, with an initial deployment of 20,000 units beginning in 2026 and continuing in batches of approximately 20,000 per year. Critically, Visa is funding the upfront cost of the terminals while SantimPay handles distribution — a capital-light expansion model for SantimPay that lets it scale merchant acceptance infrastructure without bearing the full hardware capital expenditure itself, while Visa gains expanded transaction volume and network presence in the Ethiopian market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Structured the partnership so Visa funds the upfront POS hardware cost while SantimPay manages distribution, letting SantimPay scale its merchant network dramatically without needing to raise or deploy its own capital for the hardware itself.
- Committed to a multi-year (five-year), phased rollout (roughly 20,000 units annually) rather than attempting a single large deployment, allowing both partners to manage operational and logistical scaling incrementally.
- Partnered with a global payments network (Visa) rather than building purely domestic payment rails independently, gaining access to Visa's international transaction infrastructure and brand credibility alongside the hardware funding.
- Announced the partnership at a dedicated Visa-hosted summit (Visa Connect Ethiopia 2025) specifically focused on the Ethiopian market, reflecting Visa's own strategic prioritization of Ethiopia as a growth market worth a dedicated regional event.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

SantimPay's Visa partnership demonstrates a capital-efficient expansion model for payments infrastructure fintechs in underpenetrated markets: rather than raising venture capital to fund hardware deployment directly, a domestic fintech can partner with a global payments network willing to fund infrastructure costs in exchange for expanded transaction volume and market access. This pattern is particularly relevant for markets like Ethiopia, where cash dominance and limited POS penetration represent a large addressable opportunity that neither a purely domestic fintech nor a global network could efficiently capture alone — the domestic fintech provides local distribution and merchant relationships, while the global network provides capital and payment-rail infrastructure.
