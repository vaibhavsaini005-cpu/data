## One-liner

Co-founder Ahmed Khan decided in August 2024 to shut Cheetay entirely after nine years and roughly $30 million raised, as weak quick-commerce economics, Pakistan's funding drought and worsening internet restrictions made further investment untenable.

## What happened

Cheetay began as a restaurant-delivery aggregator and expanded into grocery delivery and quick commerce. By January 2024, it had already cut half its staff, closed food delivery and was preparing to end the remaining quick-commerce operation because demand did not justify the model's burn.

Khan continued trying to raise capital, but told Rest of World in August that investors no longer believed Pakistan was worth the risk. He then chose to close the company completely.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Cheetay expanded from restaurant aggregation into groceries and dark-store quick commerce during the pandemic.
- After demand and funding weakened, management cut half the workforce and closed food delivery before confronting the remaining business.
- In August 2024, Khan made the decision to shut all operations rather than continue fundraising amid economic and internet-policy uncertainty.
- The closure ended a nine-year company that had raised around $30 million.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Thin-margin delivery businesses need both dense demand and reliable digital infrastructure. Even after operational retrenchment, country-level financing and connectivity risk can make a surviving unit uneconomic and unfinanceable.
