## One-liner

KUNA founder Mykhailo Chobanian decided on January 30, 2025 to cease the exchange's commercial operations worldwide after a Ukrainian court block; a platform serving more than 500,000 users officially stopped operating on March 31.

## What happened

KUNA was Ukraine's oldest cryptocurrency exchange. After a Kyiv district court granted a Bureau of Economic Security request to block the service in Ukraine, internet providers began restricting access in late January 2025. Kyiv Post reported that KUNA had more than 500,000 users and approximately $3.7 million in 24-hour spot trading volume shortly before the shutdown decision.

Chobanian said the company had not been represented at the court hearing and learned about the block from users. On January 30 he chose to end all commercial operations globally rather than sell the company or fight the state, gave customers a two-month withdrawal window, and said he would shift his attention to social projects and artificial intelligence. KUNA's own shutdown page confirms that the platform officially ceased operations on March 31 and records the staged delisting and withdrawal deadlines.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Chobanian made the shutdown global even though the immediate access block applied to users in Ukraine.
- Chose an orderly wind-down with advance withdrawal and asset-conversion dates instead of an abrupt service cutoff.
- Declined to sell the exchange and said he would not enter a conflict with the state.
- Redirected his effort toward social projects, AI, and his proposed "Symbiocracy" initiative after closing the exchange.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

KUNA shows how concentrated regulatory exposure can erase the value of an otherwise international digital platform. Cloud infrastructure and overseas registration did not insulate the business when its home-country access channel was blocked. The orderly withdrawal window reduced customer harm, but the case still ended a company with more than half a million users and millions of dollars in daily trading volume.
