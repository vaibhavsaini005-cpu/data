## One-liner

Payhawk co-founder and CEO Hristo Borisov led a 24-month rebuild of the Bulgarian-founded fintech's products and internal operations around autonomous AI agents, reaching $100 million ARR by July 2026 while growing net-new business 159% with a 10% smaller sales team.

## What happened

Payhawk started as a corporate-card and expense-management company, becoming Bulgaria's first unicorn. Over the 24 months ending July 2026, it repositioned both its product suite and operating model around autonomous AI agents, expanding beyond cards into accounts payable, procurement, travel, and supplier payments.

The pivot produced measurable changes across the business. Payhawk reported $100 million in ARR, 95% year-over-year growth in payment volume, and a 75% increase in ARR per employee to about $238,000. AI handled 76% of support requests without human intervention, while product and engineering shipped 57% more features at lower regression rates. Borisov said the company had converted roughly $120 million of lifetime net cash burn into $100 million of ARR while using only about half of the capital raised.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Rebuilt the company around AI as an operating layer, not merely a customer-facing feature, applying agents to support, sales, product development, travel booking, and invoice collection.
- Expanded from corporate cards into a broader finance platform spanning expenses, accounts payable, procurement, travel, and supplier payments.
- Accepted a 10% smaller sales organization while using AI-enabled productivity to generate 159% more net-new business year over year.
- Set an explicit product objective of enabling a 500-person company to operate its finance function with only two people.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Payhawk offers a measurable version of an AI pivot: the change affected product scope, staffing leverage, support automation, and engineering throughput rather than branding alone. Its results suggest that an incumbent SaaS platform can use proprietary workflow and transaction context to turn AI agents into operating leverage, but the relevant proof is unit productivity—ARR per employee, support resolution, and output per team—not the number of AI features announced.
