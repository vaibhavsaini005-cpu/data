## One-liner

Tencore, a Ukrainian developer of the battle-tested TerMIT unmanned ground vehicle already deployed on the front lines, received a $3.74 million investment from Ukrainian-American fund MITS Capital in July 2025 — one of the largest publicly confirmed investments in Ukrainian defense tech and the first US-Ukraine deal structured under the Diia.City legal regime.

## What happened

Tencore built TerMIT, a NATO-standardized, multi-mission unmanned ground vehicle (UGV) platform that has moved beyond prototype status to genuine battlefield deployment, with the company having already produced over 800 units by the time of the investment. This operational track record — real production volume and confirmed front-line use rather than early-stage development — distinguished Tencore within Ukraine's broader defense-tech sector, where most 2025 deals remained at much smaller early-stage funding sizes.

The July 2025 $3.74 million investment from MITS Capital, announced at the Ukraine Recovery Conference (URC2025) in Rome, was structured through Ukraine's Diia.City legal regime and deployed directly into Tencore's Ukrainian legal entity — making it the first investment deal between a US investor and a Ukrainian defense-tech company under that specific legal framework. The capital is earmarked to scale both R&D and manufacturing capacity, with Tencore targeting production of 2,000 TerMIT units by the end of 2025, a more than doubling of its output at the time of the raise.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Achieved real production scale (800+ units) and confirmed front-line deployment before raising this round, giving investors concrete operational proof points rather than requiring them to bet on unproven prototype technology.
- Used Ukraine's Diia.City legal regime to structure the deal, establishing a legal and regulatory template that other US investors could potentially follow for future Ukrainian defense-tech investments.
- Targeted a specific, ambitious production scale-up (from 800 to 2,000 units by year-end) as the explicit use of funds, giving the investment a clear, measurable operational milestone rather than open-ended growth capital.
- Attracted a Ukrainian-American investment fund specifically focused on military innovation (MITS Capital) rather than a generalist VC, aligning investor expertise directly with the sector-specific risks and opportunities of defense technology.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Tencore's raise demonstrates that Ukrainian defense-tech companies with genuine battlefield-proven products and real production scale can access significantly larger investment than the sub-$1M rounds typical of the broader sector in 2025, since operational validation under actual combat conditions provides investors a uniquely credible signal of product-market fit. The deal's structuring under the Diia.City legal regime — as the first such US-Ukraine defense-tech transaction — also illustrates how wartime Ukraine has been actively building specialized legal and regulatory infrastructure specifically to make its defense-tech sector more accessible to foreign capital, addressing a structural barrier that might otherwise deter international investors from a market perceived as legally complex or risky during active conflict.
