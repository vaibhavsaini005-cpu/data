## One-liner

Addis Ababa-based Gebeya evolved from a coding-education startup into a freelance marketplace and then productized its marketplace capabilities as configurable, AI-powered Talent Cloud SaaS.

## What happened

Gebeya started as an EdTech business in 2017, training African technology talent. It then became a multi-sided marketplace connecting vetted professionals with work. In February 2024 the company formalized another strategic shift as "Gebeya 3.0," repositioning itself as an all-in-one Talent Cloud provider whose customers can launch and configure their own talent ecosystems.

The new model turns capabilities Gebeya developed for its own marketplace—sourcing, vetting, skilling, community management, and opportunity matching—into reusable software for organizations. Early deployments supplied evidence for the model: a Microsoft partnership targeted a 300,000-person African talent cloud, while the UNHCR-backed Boundless Skills platform had onboarded nearly 300 refugees and host-community members by March 2024.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Moved from directly training developers to matching a broader pool of vetted talent with employers.
- Productized its internal marketplace infrastructure instead of remaining only an operator of one marketplace.
- Repositioned around configurable public, private, strategic, and sourcing Talent Clouds for institutional customers.
- Used anchor partnerships such as Microsoft and UNHCR to validate the SaaS-enabled platform in distinct use cases.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Gebeya illustrates a platform-enablement pivot: operating a marketplace first can reveal repeatable workflows that later become higher-leverage software. The move also changes the scaling constraint from building one supply-and-demand network to enabling partners with their own brands, participants, and distribution. Large partners can validate the platform, though their programs' targets should not be confused with completed outcomes.
