## One-liner

Founder and CEO Tomáš Čupr decided in June 2024 to turn Rohlik's in-house logistics technology into a product for other grocers while expanding into 10 more cities; the company had 800,000 customers, 40% average post-pandemic revenue growth and three profitable operating regions.

## What happened

Rohlik differentiated its online grocery service through smaller warehouses, local suppliers, a 17,000-item assortment and one-to-two-hour delivery. After proving the operating model in several European markets, Čupr chose to add a second growth engine: licensing Rohlik's logistics, analytics and warehouse-automation technology to other delivery companies.

The Czech company raised $170 million in June 2024 to support that platform launch and continued geographic expansion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Čupr committed to launching the technology-licensing platform later in 2024 rather than keeping Rohlik's systems solely for internal use.
- He paired the software initiative with a plan to enter 10 additional cities over six years.
- Rohlik served 800,000 customers in 2023, and its Czech, Hungarian and Munich units were profitable by June 2024.
- Revenue had grown by an average 40% after the pandemic, while the company targeted €1 billion in revenue and positive cash flow by the end of 2024.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Operational software can become a second product once the underlying business proves it under real volume. Rohlik turned a grocery-delivery cost center into licensable infrastructure without abandoning the consumer operation that validates it.
