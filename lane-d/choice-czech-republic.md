## One-liner

Prague-founded Choice, an all-in-one restaurant SaaS platform processing 1.5 million monthly orders across nine CEE markets, raised a $7.1M Series A in March 2026 (bringing total funding to $11.6M) after reaching operating profitability and surpassing $5M in annual recurring revenue.

## What happened

Choice built a consolidated operating system for independent restaurants, combining QR-code ordering, payments, reservations, and marketplace integrations into a single platform rather than requiring restaurants to stitch together multiple separate point solutions. The platform scaled to processing 1.5 million monthly orders across nine Central and Eastern European markets, establishing meaningful regional density before pursuing broader European expansion.

Notably, Choice reached operating profitability in October 2025 and surpassed $5M in annual recurring revenue before closing its $7.1M Series A in March 2026 — a sequencing where the company demonstrated financial discipline and unit economics ahead of, rather than instead of, raising growth capital. The round was led by Alea Capital Partners with participation from Reflex Capital, Smartlink, and J&T Ventures, and is earmarked for AI product development and international expansion beyond the company's existing CEE footprint.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a single consolidated platform (ordering, payments, reservations, marketplace integrations) rather than a point solution, reducing the number of separate vendors independent restaurants would otherwise need to manage.
- Achieved operating profitability and meaningful ARR ($5M+) before raising its Series A, rather than relying on growth-at-all-costs fundraising — a sequencing that likely strengthened its negotiating position and valuation with investors.
- Established deep multi-market density (nine CEE markets, 1.5 million monthly orders) before pursuing broader pan-European expansion, prioritizing proven regional saturation over premature geographic sprawl.
- Positioned the new capital specifically around AI product development alongside international expansion, signaling a deliberate move to layer AI-driven differentiation onto an already-profitable core business rather than chasing AI hype without underlying traction.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Choice demonstrates that reaching profitability and strong revenue metrics before a growth-stage raise — rather than the more common venture pattern of raising ahead of proven unit economics — can position a company favorably with investors while preserving founder leverage and reducing dilution risk. Consolidating multiple restaurant-operations functions (ordering, payments, reservations) into one platform is also a durable competitive moat in a market where independent restaurants have limited time and resources to manage fragmented software vendors.
