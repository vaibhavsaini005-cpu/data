## One-liner

Savyour, Pakistan's first cashback rewards platform which had distributed over Rs400 million to 4 million customers across 58 cities and previously secured $3.3M in seed funding, announced its full shutdown in February 2025, with the platform ceasing operations by March 20, 2025.

## What happened

Savyour launched in August 2020 as Pakistan's first cashback rewards platform, letting customers earn cashback on purchases across a network of participating merchants. The company secured $3.3M in seed funding in 2021 from Zayn Capital and Global Founders Capital, and expanded aggressively — reaching a reported 52% month-over-month growth rate and extending services to 58 cities across Pakistan, distributing more than Rs400 million (roughly $1.4M at typical exchange rates) in cashback rewards to 4 million customers over its operating life.

Despite this apparent growth trajectory and consumer reach, the company announced in late February 2025 that it would fully discontinue operations, with the app and website becoming inaccessible after March 20, 2025 (with cashback withdrawals remaining available only through that date). Notably, the company disclosed that the decision to shut down had actually been made roughly 12 months earlier and that the intervening year had been spent on an "actively managed" wind-down process, rather than announcing an abrupt closure. The company did not publicly disclose the specific reasons behind the shutdown decision.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built rapid multi-city expansion (58 cities) and reported strong month-over-month growth (52%) as core growth metrics, yet these consumer-reach indicators ultimately did not prevent the underlying shutdown decision — suggesting the business's unit economics or path to sustainable profitability were not aligned with its user-growth trajectory.
- Made the decision to shut down roughly a year before publicly announcing it, choosing a long, managed wind-down process over either continuing operations under strain or an abrupt closure — a decision that allowed for what the company described as a smooth transition but also meant operating for a year with a known end date.
- Declined to publicly disclose the specific reasons for the shutdown, limiting external understanding of whether the failure stemmed from unit economics, competitive pressure, funding availability, or a combination of factors.
- Operated in the broader context of an 88% decline in Pakistani startup funding (from $355M in 2022 to $43M in 2024), a macro funding environment that likely constrained Savyour's ability to raise follow-on capital regardless of its consumer-facing growth metrics.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Savyour's shutdown illustrates that strong consumer-facing growth metrics (city expansion, month-over-month growth rate, cashback volume distributed) do not guarantee business sustainability if the underlying unit economics of a cashback or rewards model don't scale profitably — a pattern common to reward-subsidy-driven fintech products operating in capital-constrained markets. The company's choice to plan and execute a year-long managed wind-down, rather than an abrupt shutdown, is also a notable operational pattern: it suggests founders recognizing an unsustainable trajectory early can choose to protect customer trust and orderly fund withdrawal even when the underlying business has already been deemed non-viable internally.
