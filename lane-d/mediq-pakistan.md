## One-liner

MedIQ, an Islamabad-headquartered digital healthcare platform founded in 2020 by Dr. Saira Siddiqui, raised a $6 million Series A in May 2025 led by Qatar's Rasmal Ventures and Saudi Arabia's Joa Capital to scale its "healthcare operating system" across the GCC, bringing total funding to $9.8 million.

## What happened

MedIQ built a digital "healthcare operating system" in Pakistan, a software platform designed to digitize and connect various parts of the healthcare delivery process — a structural gap in a country where much of healthcare administration, from patient records to provider coordination, has historically remained fragmented and paper-based. Founded by Dr. Saira Siddiqui, a physician with a PhD in Health Economics, the company built its initial platform and traction within Pakistan before targeting cross-border expansion.

The $6 million Series A, announced in May 2025, was notably led by two Gulf investors — Qatar's Rasmal Ventures and Saudi Arabia's Joa Capital — rather than Pakistani or South Asian funds, with the capital earmarked specifically to scale MedIQ's operations in Saudi Arabia. This GCC-led funding and expansion strategy reflects a broader pattern among Pakistani startups: given the size and healthcare-spending capacity of Gulf markets relative to Pakistan's own capital-constrained environment, expanding into Saudi Arabia and the wider GCC has become an increasingly common path to scale for Pakistani-founded healthtech and fintech companies. By the time of the round, MedIQ already employed 180 people split across Islamabad and Riyadh.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a physician-founded healthcare platform grounded in direct clinical and health-economics expertise (the founder's PhD in Health Economics), rather than a purely technical or generalist founding team, likely aiding credibility with healthcare providers and investors alike.
- Targeted Saudi Arabia as the primary expansion market for new capital rather than deepening the Pakistani home market further, betting on GCC healthcare spending capacity as the larger near-term growth opportunity.
- Attracted lead investors specifically from Qatar and Saudi Arabia rather than Pakistani or Western venture funds, aligning its investor base directly with its GCC expansion strategy from the Series A stage onward.
- Built a bi-national workforce split between Islamabad and Riyadh (180 employees total) ahead of and during the round, reflecting operational commitment to the GCC market rather than treating it as a purely aspirational expansion target.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

MedIQ demonstrates a specific playbook increasingly used by Pakistani startups facing a capital-constrained domestic funding environment: building initial product and traction at home, then raising growth capital specifically from Gulf investors to fund expansion into GCC markets, where healthcare and broader consumer spending capacity substantially exceeds Pakistan's own market. The company's physician-led founding team also illustrates how domain expertise in a technical, regulated sector like healthcare can be a meaningful differentiator for South Asian health tech startups seeking credibility with both healthcare providers and international investors.
