## One-liner

Allonic, a Budapest-founded robotics startup building automated manufacturing systems for complex robot bodies, raised a $7.2M pre-seed round led by Visionaries Club in February 2026 — the largest pre-seed round in Hungary's history — with angel backing from figures at OpenAI and Hugging Face.

## What happened

Allonic, founded in 2025 by Benedek Tasi, Dávid Pelyva, and David Holló, developed a novel manufacturing method called 3D Tissue Braiding for producing complex, dexterous robotic bodies: the process builds a skeletal scaffolding first, then weaves soft, load-bearing fibers around it, integrating actuators and tendons directly into the structure during production rather than assembling separately-manufactured components afterward. This directly addresses a bottleneck in humanoid and advanced robotics: building sophisticated, muscle-like robotic bodies at scale remains slow and manual, constraining how quickly the broader robotics industry can move from prototype to production hardware.

The February 2026 $7.2M pre-seed round, led by Visionaries Club with participation from Day One Capital, Prototype, SDAC Ventures, TinyVC, and individual angel investors connected to OpenAI, Hugging Face, ETH Zurich, and Northwestern University, was reported as the largest pre-seed round ever raised by a Hungarian startup. The company maintains a Budapest base alongside a US headquarters, reflecting the dual-market positioning increasingly common among deep-tech startups seeking both European engineering talent access and US venture capital and customer proximity.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Developed a fundamentally different manufacturing approach (3D Tissue Braiding) rather than incrementally improving existing robot-assembly methods, targeting the structural bottleneck constraining the broader robotics industry's ability to scale production of complex robotic bodies.
- Attracted angel investment specifically from individuals connected to leading AI labs (OpenAI, Hugging Face) and top research institutions (ETH Zurich, Northwestern), signaling technical credibility validation from figures embedded in frontier AI development.
- Maintained dual Budapest/US headquarters from an early stage, positioning the company to access both Central European engineering talent and US venture capital and robotics-industry customer relationships simultaneously.
- Achieved the largest pre-seed round in Hungarian startup history in a deep-tech, capital-intensive hardware category — a segment where pre-seed rounds are typically smaller than in software due to higher technical and manufacturing risk.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Allonic demonstrates that Central European deep-tech startups tackling fundamental manufacturing bottlenecks in frontier hardware categories (like humanoid robotics) can attract record-setting early-stage capital and credible AI-industry angel backing, when the underlying technical approach addresses a structural constraint the broader industry recognizes as a genuine blocker. Maintaining dual regional headquarters (Budapest and the US) from the earliest funding stage is also a notable pattern for deep-tech startups: it lets founders access differentiated regional advantages — European engineering and manufacturing expertise alongside US capital and AI-industry proximity — without forcing an early choice between the two ecosystems.
