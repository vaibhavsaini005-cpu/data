## One-liner

Nagad, a Dhaka-based mobile financial services provider backed by the Bangladesh Post Office and launched in March 2019, became Bangladesh's fastest-ever unicorn in June 2023 after reaching an $1 billion valuation in just four years, growing to more than 80 million customers and posting a 67% year-on-year profit increase in 2024.

## What happened

Nagad built a mobile financial services (MFS) platform offering digital payments, transfers, and financial services to Bangladesh's largely underbanked population, entering a market already anchored by established competitor bKash. Launched in March 2019 with backing from the state-run Bangladesh Post Office, Nagad leveraged that institutional connection to rapidly build trust and distribution, expanding its customer base to more than 80 million users within four years — an unusually fast growth trajectory for a financial services company in an emerging market.

Nagad's rise to $1 billion valuation and unicorn status in June 2023, achieved in just four years since launch, made it Bangladesh's fastest company to reach that milestone. The company's continued financial performance after achieving unicorn status reinforced the durability of its business model: Nagad posted a profit of Tk 315.77 crore (roughly $2.6 million) in 2024, a 67% year-on-year increase, with daily transaction volumes averaging approximately BDT 1,300 crore ($10.8 million), demonstrating that its rapid customer growth translated into genuine transactional scale and profitability rather than growth metrics alone.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Launched with direct backing from the state-run Bangladesh Post Office, using that institutional affiliation to build consumer trust and distribution reach more quickly than a purely private-sector fintech entrant might achieve.
- Entered a market already dominated by an established incumbent (bKash) and still achieved unicorn status within four years, demonstrating that mobile financial services markets in large emerging economies can support multiple large-scale competitors rather than consolidating around a single winner.
- Sustained continued profit growth (67% year-on-year in 2024) well after reaching unicorn valuation, showing that its rapid customer acquisition converted into durable transactional revenue rather than plateauing after the initial growth phase.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Nagad demonstrates that mobile financial services markets in large, underbanked emerging economies like Bangladesh can support genuine multi-player competition at scale — the company achieved unicorn status and sustained profitability despite entering a market with an established incumbent, rather than the mobile-money sector consolidating around a single dominant provider as has occurred in some other markets. The company's state-backed launch model, using an existing government institution (the Postal Service) to accelerate consumer trust and distribution, also illustrates a distinctive scaling pathway for fintech platforms in markets where formal banking penetration remains limited: institutional affiliation can substitute for the years of brand-building that a purely private entrant might otherwise require.
