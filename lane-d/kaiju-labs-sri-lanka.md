## One-liner

Kaiju Labs, a Sri Lankan wallet-as-a-service provider enabling developers to add Web3 functionality to games and apps, was acquired by Singapore-based Web3 fintech firm KAST Finance in November 2024 — the first exit for early-stage investor nVentures, which recorded a 2X multiple on invested capital.

## What happened

Kaiju Labs built wallet-as-a-service infrastructure that let game and app developers integrate Web3 functionality — such as crypto wallets and on-chain interactions — without building that infrastructure themselves, positioning the company as a picks-and-shovels layer for the broader Web3 gaming and app ecosystem rather than a consumer-facing product.

The November 2024 acquisition by KAST Finance, a Singapore-based Web3 fintech firm, gave nVentures — the Sri Lankan early-stage VC firm that had been Kaiju's initial investor — its first exit, with the firm reporting a 2X multiple on invested capital and a 48.6% internal rate of return at a reported valuation of $2M. For Sri Lanka's still-nascent startup investment ecosystem, a clean, profitable acquisition exit for a local VC firm's portfolio company represented a notable proof point that early-stage bets originating in the country could return capital to investors via a cross-border acquisition.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built infrastructure (wallet-as-a-service) for other developers to build on, rather than a standalone consumer Web3 product, positioning the company to be acquired by a larger Web3 platform seeking to add that capability.
- Attracted early-stage local Sri Lankan venture capital (nVentures) rather than relying solely on international crypto-focused funds, demonstrating that domestic capital could back a Web3 infrastructure bet.
- Was acquired by a regional (Singapore-based) rather than global Western acquirer, reflecting the more active Web3/crypto infrastructure investment appetite in Southeast Asian markets relative to some other regions during this period.
- The acquisition closed as a full exit rather than an ongoing partnership or licensing deal, giving nVentures a clean, quantifiable return (2X MOIC, 48.6% IRR) to point to.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kaiju Labs' acquisition demonstrates that infrastructure-layer Web3 startups — building tools other developers rely on rather than consumer-facing products — can find acquirers among larger regional platforms seeking to add specific technical capabilities, providing a viable exit path even from smaller, less-covered startup ecosystems like Sri Lanka's. It also illustrates a broader pattern for early-stage investors in nascent ecosystems: a single successful, well-documented exit can serve as an important credibility signal for the local investor base and the wider startup ecosystem, beyond just the returns to that individual fund.
