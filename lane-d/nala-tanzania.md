## One-liner

Tanzania-founded fintech NALA raised a $40M Series A in July 2024 — one of Africa's largest Series A rounds to date — led by San Francisco's Acrew Capital, to expand its consumer remittance app beyond Africa and build Rafiki, a new B2B cross-border payments platform.

## What happened

NALA built a consumer money-transfer app enabling users in Europe, the UK, and the US to send secure, reliable payments into Tanzania, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda, and Ghana — addressing a remittance corridor traditionally burdened by high fees and slow, opaque transfer processes through legacy money-transfer operators. The July 2024 Series A, an oversubscribed round led by Acrew Capital with participation from DST Global, Norrsken22, HOF Capital, and continued backing from existing investors Amplo and NYCA Partners, also drew individual investment from prominent fintech entrepreneurs including Chime's Ryan King and Robinhood's Vlad Tenev.

The capital was allocated toward two distinct goals: strengthening the reliability of NALA's existing African payment rails, and building an entirely new B2B product, Rafiki, aimed at providing payment infrastructure for other businesses moving money into and across emerging markets — expanding NALA's ambitions beyond consumer remittances into becoming underlying payments infrastructure for other companies, and extending its geographic reach into Asian and Latin American markets.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built initial traction and credibility in the consumer remittance product (Africa-bound transfers) before raising the capital and ambition to build B2B payment infrastructure (Rafiki) — sequencing consumer trust and volume before infrastructure expansion.
- Attracted a US-based lead investor (Acrew Capital) and prominent Silicon Valley fintech operator-angels (Chime, Robinhood founders) rather than raising primarily from African or emerging-market-focused funds, signaling the round was pitched and validated as a global fintech infrastructure opportunity, not a regionally-bounded African fintech story.
- Expanded product scope from single-purpose consumer remittances to broader payment-rail infrastructure (Rafiki) within the same funding round, rather than raising separate rounds for each product line.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

NALA's raise shows that consumer remittance apps serving high-fee corridors (like African diaspora remittances) can use proven consumer traction as the foundation to raise much larger rounds aimed at building broader payment infrastructure — turning a single-product fintech into platform-level rails serving other businesses. The investor mix (global VC plus prominent US fintech founder-angels investing personally) is also a signal worth recognizing: African fintechs solving large structural problems (remittance costs, payment reliability) can attract top-tier global capital when the business case is framed as infrastructure with global expansion potential, not a regionally-limited product.
