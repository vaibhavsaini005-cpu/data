## One-liner

Rwandan health-commerce company Kasha expanded from consumer delivery into wholesale health distribution, grew annual recurring revenue 50-fold after its Series A, and raised a $21 million Series B in 2023.

## What happened

Founded in Rwanda in 2016, Kasha initially delivered health products for women and newborns directly to consumers. When small retailers began ordering the same goods, the company obtained a pharmaceutical license and expanded into wholesale service for pharmacies, hospitals, clinics, and small resellers while retaining consumer channels such as its website and USSD.

Kasha subsequently expanded into Kenya and built a health-focused distribution model spanning pharmaceuticals and fast-moving consumer goods. In July 2023, it announced a $21 million Series B led by Knife Capital. Founder and CEO Joanna Bichsel said annual recurring revenue had grown 50-fold since the company's late-2020 Series A; the new funding was intended to support further African expansion.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Began with a focused direct-to-consumer proposition for underserved women's and newborn health needs.
- Followed observed customer demand into wholesale and secured the license needed to serve regulated health buyers.
- Kept health as the strategic wedge while broadening both the product range and customer base.
- Added telehealth, inventory credit, and last-mile distribution capabilities around the core commerce platform.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kasha's growth shows how a mission-led consumer beachhead can uncover a larger B2B distribution opportunity. The company expanded its addressable market without abandoning the trust, regulated sourcing, and health specialization that differentiated it from generalist commerce platforms. Its 50-fold ARR growth is company-reported, but the independently reported Series B provides a concrete external milestone.
