## One-liner

Ampersand, a Rwandan electric motorcycle manufacturer and financing company founded in 2016, secured $19.5M in January 2024 (later extended by an additional $2M) from AHL Venture Partners, Everstrong Capital, and Beyond Capital Ventures, to expand its electric alternative to East Africa's five million petrol motorcycle taxis.

## What happened

Ampersand assembles and finances electric motorcycles designed specifically to compete with — and outperform on cost — the petrol motorcycle taxis ("boda bodas") that form a massive, informal transportation backbone across East Africa, with roughly five million petrol motorcycles currently in use across the region. The company's electric motorcycles are claimed to be 45% cheaper to operate and produce 75% fewer emissions than their petrol equivalents, targeting motorcycle-taxi drivers whose daily earnings depend heavily on minimizing fuel and maintenance costs — making the economic case for switching, not just an environmental one, the company's core value proposition.

The January 2024 funding round brought in $19.5M from a syndicate of impact-focused investors (AHL Venture Partners, Everstrong Capital, Beyond Capital Ventures), later extended by an additional $2M announced in August 2024, bringing total funding to $21.5M. The capital is earmarked for expanding Ampersand's operations into other East African countries beyond Rwanda, supporting the company's stated goal of deploying 5 million electric motorcycles by 2033 — essentially aiming to fully electrify the region's existing petrol motorcycle-taxi fleet over roughly a decade.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built the business case around operating cost savings for motorcycle-taxi drivers (45% cheaper to operate) rather than purely environmental benefits, aligning the product's core value proposition with the immediate financial self-interest of its target customers.
- Combined vehicle manufacturing/assembly with financing, addressing both the product-availability and capital-access barriers that would otherwise prevent cash-constrained motorcycle-taxi drivers from switching to electric vehicles.
- Set an explicit, large-scale target (5 million electric motorcycles by 2033) matching the full size of the existing petrol motorcycle-taxi fleet across East Africa, framing the company's ambition as full category replacement rather than niche market capture.
- Extended its funding round with an additional tranche eight months after the initial close (January to August 2024), suggesting sustained investor confidence and possibly extending runway for accelerated regional expansion.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Ampersand demonstrates that electric-vehicle adoption in emerging markets scales fastest when the value proposition leads with immediate economic benefit (lower operating costs) for cash-flow-sensitive customers, rather than relying primarily on environmental messaging — the emissions reduction becomes a secondary benefit that appeals to impact investors, while the cost savings drive actual customer adoption. Pairing vehicle manufacturing with embedded financing is also a critical unlock for capital-intensive product categories serving low-margin, cash-constrained customer segments like informal-economy motorcycle-taxi drivers, who would otherwise be unable to afford the upfront cost of switching technologies even when the long-term economics clearly favor doing so.
