## One-liner

Woltair CEO Jan Hanuš cut 38 of roughly 200 roles in February 2024 and accelerated a digital-first operating model after Germany's subsidy shock drove that market down 90%; he reported order processing and delivery had become as much as ten times faster than a year earlier.

## What happened

The Czech climate-tech company installs residential heat pumps and solar systems. Under Hanuš, Woltair had expanded across four countries, but its bookings stagnated at roughly CZK 1 billion during 2023 even as recognized revenue rose more than 300%. Germany was especially exposed: Hanuš said the withdrawal of subsidies caused that market to fall 90% year over year.

In February 2024, Hanuš announced an organizational redesign affecting 38 positions, about one-fifth of a workforce of roughly 200. He tied the decision to a broader shift toward a leaner, more digital and replicable model, replacing manual coordination where software could do the work and applying lessons from newer operations in Germany and Italy to the older Czech organization. Hanuš said Czech operations had posted positive EBITDA for many months and that the redesign made order processing and delivery as much as ten times faster than a year earlier.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Hanuš responded to a 90% contraction in the German market by redesigning operations rather than preserving the headcount and processes built for a subsidy-supported growth environment.
- Eliminated 38 roles, or about 19% of the company, as a one-time organizational change centered on digitization and flexibility.
- Shifted toward a lighter model that relies on a technician and supplier marketplace instead of large in-house teams, with future country organizations intended to number in the tens rather than hundreds.
- Kept the international expansion thesis intact while requiring mature Czech operations to generate profitability and newer markets to show a monthly path toward breakeven.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Woltair is a useful example of a policy-sensitive climate company treating a demand shock as an operating-model reset. The measurable lesson is not simply that layoffs lower costs: standardizing workflows across countries and digitizing coordination can reduce organizational complexity while materially improving service speed. It also exposes the risk of building permanent capacity around temporary subsidies.
