## One-liner

Pivot Payments founder and managing director Shamirah Kimbugwe rebuilt the fintech's board, locked in the required UGX 500 million reserve, and overhauled its systems after a February 2024 license suspension; the Bank of Uganda reinstated its licenses in December.

## What happened

The Bank of Uganda suspended Pivot Payments' Payment Service Provider and Electronic Money Issuer licenses on February 29, 2024. Kimbugwe acknowledged that the board was incomplete and transaction volume had outgrown the company's capital reserves. The suspension interrupted a business that had registered more than 100,000 customers, processed more than 1.4 million transactions, and moved over UGX 70 billion.

Kimbugwe kept customer data and funds accessible, then spent the suspension addressing the regulator's requirements. By December, Pivot had installed a full board, locked in UGX 500 million of reserves, overhauled its systems, and secured a new security provider. The licenses were reinstated that month and the company announced that operations would resume on December 20. Kimbugwe also began seeking $10 million to recapitalize and rebuild the business after the eight-month interruption.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Kimbugwe acknowledged the governance and capital gaps rather than contesting the regulator's diagnosis.
- Maintained the application and payout access during the suspension so customers could retrieve funds.
- Reconstituted the board and locked the required UGX 500 million reserve to satisfy licensing conditions.
- Used the downtime to overhaul systems and engage a new security provider before relaunching.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Pivot Payments is a regulatory-recovery case rather than uninterrupted growth. A license suspension exposed governance and capitalization that had not kept pace with transaction volume. The founder's decision to treat compliance as an operational rebuild produced a measurable outcome: both licenses returned, a complete board was in place, and the regulated payment business could resume.
