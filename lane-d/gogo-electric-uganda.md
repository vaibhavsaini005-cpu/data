## One-liner

GOGO Electric, a homegrown Ugandan e-mobility startup building electric motorcycles and a battery-swapping network, secured a $3M capital boost from the Uganda Development Bank in April 2025 to scale local assembly capacity and expand rider-friendly financing models.

## What happened

GOGO Electric builds electric motorcycles for the Ugandan market alongside the battery-swapping infrastructure needed to make electric two-wheelers practical for commercial riders (such as boda-boda operators), addressing both the vehicle and the charging-infrastructure sides of the e-mobility transition simultaneously rather than only manufacturing vehicles and leaving charging access to others.

The April 2025 $3M facility from the Uganda Development Bank — a state-backed development finance institution rather than a private VC — specifically targeted local manufacturing capacity, reflecting a policy interest in building domestic assembly capability rather than only importing finished electric vehicles. The financing followed earlier capital from EU-backed EDFI ElectriFI and the Africa Go Green Fund, indicating GOGO had already built a track record with development-finance and impact investors before this round. The capital is earmarked for scaling assembly capacity, broadening the battery-swapping network, and introducing more rider-friendly financing models so commercial riders can afford the upfront cost of switching to electric vehicles.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built both vehicles and battery-swapping infrastructure rather than focusing on manufacturing alone, addressing the practical range-and-charging barrier that determines whether commercial riders can actually adopt electric motorcycles for daily income-generating use.
- Targeted local assembly capacity specifically, rather than relying purely on imported finished vehicles, aligning with domestic industrial-policy interests and likely improving access to government and development-finance capital.
- Built rider-friendly financing models into the offering, recognizing that commercial riders (often operating on thin margins) need financing support to afford the upfront cost of an electric motorcycle even where running costs are lower than petrol alternatives.
- Sequenced funding from development-finance institutions (EDFI ElectriFI, Africa Go Green Fund, then Uganda Development Bank) rather than chasing a single large early round, building a track record with impact-aligned capital before scaling further.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

GOGO Electric demonstrates that e-mobility adoption in markets with large commercial motorcycle-taxi economies depends on solving vehicle, charging-infrastructure, and financing access together — a vehicle-only strategy leaves the range-anxiety and upfront-cost barriers unaddressed for the commercial riders who are the primary customer base. The pattern of sequential development-finance-institution funding (EU-backed funds, then a national development bank) also illustrates a viable capital-raising path for infrastructure-heavy climate/mobility startups in markets where private VC risk appetite for capital-intensive manufacturing may be more limited.
