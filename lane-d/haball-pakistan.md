## One-liner

Haball, a Karachi-headquartered B2B fintech founded in 2017 offering shariah-compliant supply-chain financing and digital payments, secured $52 million in combined equity and strategic financing in March 2025 — $5 million in equity led by Zayn VC plus $47 million in strategic financing from Meezan Bank — to fund Pakistan operations and expansion into Saudi Arabia.

## What happened

Haball built B2B supply-chain financing and digital payment infrastructure structured to comply with Islamic finance (shariah) principles, addressing working-capital needs for businesses operating in Pakistan's financial system, where a large share of banking activity operates under Islamic finance frameworks rather than conventional interest-based lending. The company's long-standing partnership with Meezan Bank — Pakistan's largest Islamic bank, dating back to 2018 — gave it a structural advantage in building shariah-compliant financial products at scale.

The $52 million round, announced in March 2025, was deliberately structured with a small equity component ($5 million, led by Zayn VC) alongside a much larger $47 million strategic financing facility from Meezan Bank itself — reflecting how Haball's core supply-chain financing business is fundamentally capital-intensive, requiring loan capital to fund the receivables and payables financing it extends to business customers, rather than primarily requiring equity to build software. The funding supported both continued growth in Pakistan and the company's planned expansion into Saudi Arabia, another shariah-finance-oriented market.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built specifically shariah-compliant supply-chain financing products, directly serving Pakistan's Islamic finance-oriented banking sector rather than a generic B2B fintech model.
- Formed a deep, multi-year strategic partnership with Meezan Bank (dating to 2018) well before this funding round, which culminated in the bank itself providing the large majority ($47M of $52M) of the round's capital.
- Structured the round with a small equity tranche and a much larger strategic financing tranche, matching the capital structure to a lending-intensive business model rather than raising primarily dilutive equity capital.
- Targeted Saudi Arabia specifically for international expansion, extending its shariah-compliant financing model to another market with a large Islamic finance sector rather than a market requiring a fundamentally different product approach.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Haball's funding structure illustrates how B2B fintechs built around capital-intensive lending products — particularly shariah-compliant supply-chain financing — can access large strategic financing facilities directly from banking partners who have a long-standing operational relationship with the company, rather than relying primarily on venture equity to fund loan books. The company's deep, multi-year partnership with Meezan Bank converting into that bank becoming its primary funding source also demonstrates how sustained strategic banking relationships in regulated financial sectors can evolve into structured capital partnerships that a pure venture-equity relationship would be poorly suited to replace.
