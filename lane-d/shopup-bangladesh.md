## One-liner

Bangladeshi B2B e-commerce platform ShopUp secured $30M in debt financing from Lendable and The City Bank in February 2023, continuing to scale sourcing, logistics, and digital credit services for small retailers even as overall Bangladesh startup funding fell 42% year-over-year.

## What happened

ShopUp built a B2B commerce platform connecting small retailers ("mom-and-pop" shops) across Bangladesh with sourcing, logistics, digital credit, and business-management tools — digitizing supply chains for a segment of the economy that traditionally ran on informal, cash-based relationships with local distributors. In February 2023, the company secured $30M in debt financing from Lendable (a global provider of debt financing to emerging-market fintechs) and The City Bank, a structure distinct from raising further dilutive equity.

The raise came in a year when Bangladesh's overall startup funding fell 42% to $72M across just 45 deals — meaning ShopUp's ability to close a substantial financing round, structured as debt rather than equity, signaled that its underlying credit and logistics business had reached a scale where debt financing (typically requiring demonstrated cash-flow predictability) was viable, distinct from the earlier-stage equity most other Bangladeshi startups were still competing for in a shrinking pool.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built a multi-service platform (sourcing, logistics, credit, business management) around the small-retailer segment rather than a single point solution, increasing the company's role in and data visibility into each merchant's operations.
- Raised debt financing rather than equity for this round, a structure that requires demonstrated revenue/cash-flow predictability and avoids further founder/investor dilution.
- Scaled during a year of broad sectoral funding contraction in Bangladesh, distinguishing itself from earlier-stage peers competing for a shrinking equity pool.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

ShopUp's debt-financed raise during a funding downturn shows how B2B platforms with embedded credit products can access non-dilutive capital once their lending book demonstrates enough predictability — a maturity signal equity-stage peers can't yet access. This is a useful pattern for embedded-finance startups broadly: the debt-financing option becomes available specifically once the underlying credit exposure is well-enough understood by specialized lenders like Lendable to price it.
