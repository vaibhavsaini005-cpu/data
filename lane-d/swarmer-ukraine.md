## One-liner

Swarmer, a Ukrainian defense-tech company building AI software that lets combat drones coordinate as autonomous swarms, raised a $15M Series A led by US-based Broadband Capital Investments in September 2025 — the largest defense-tech investment in Ukraine since the full-scale war began.

## What happened

Swarmer, founded in May 2023 by Serhii Kupriienko and Alex Fink, built autonomy software products (Styx AI, Minas, and Trident) that manage coordinated drone-swarm operations across unmanned vehicles from different manufacturers, including encrypted communications between units. The system is trained on data from over 82,000 of Swarmer's own combat missions plus millions more flown by other operators, giving the company a real-world operational dataset directly informing its AI models — a significant advantage in a category where combat-validated training data is scarce and difficult to simulate.

The September 2025 $15M Series A, led by Broadband Capital Investments with participation from returning investors including American defense-tech firm R-G.AI, Radius Capital, Green Flag Ventures, D3 Ventures, and Network VC, was reported as the largest single investment in Ukraine's defense-technology sector since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022. Industry estimates placed Swarmer's valuation at $35-45 million, though the company did not disclose an exact figure. By the time of the raise, Swarmer had already signed $16.3M in contracts, with a further $16.8M in agreements planned for 2026-2028.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Built software specifically compatible across drone hardware from different manufacturers, rather than a proprietary hardware-software bundle, positioning Swarmer as an interoperability layer for the broader unmanned-systems ecosystem rather than a single-vendor solution.
- Trained its AI models on a massive real-world combat dataset (82,000+ of its own missions plus millions more from other operators), an advantage difficult for competitors without comparable battlefield access to replicate.
- Attracted primarily US-based venture capital and defense-tech strategic investors rather than relying solely on Ukrainian or European capital, reflecting growing Western investor confidence in Ukrainian defense-tech innovation during wartime.
- Secured substantial forward-contracted revenue ($16.3M signed, $16.8M more planned) ahead of and alongside the Series A, demonstrating commercial demand beyond speculative growth potential.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Swarmer demonstrates how wartime conditions, while devastating, can accelerate a specific category of defense-tech innovation by providing combat-tested operational data at a scale and pace peacetime R&D cannot replicate — turning what would elsewhere be a multi-year simulation-based development cycle into rapid, real-world-validated iteration. The company's ability to attract the largest defense-tech investment in Ukraine's wartime history from primarily US investors also signals a broader pattern: international capital is increasingly willing to back Ukrainian defense-tech specifically because of, not despite, its battlefield-proven technology.
