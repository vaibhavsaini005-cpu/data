## One-liner

Lssya founder Lidia Solomon decided in July 2024 to close Ethiopia's pioneering online-psychotherapy startup after six years, low conversion from more than 2,000 free Telegram users, and founder burnout; the legal wind-down finished in June 2026.

## What happened

Solomon began Lssya as a solo founder and initially delivered free online therapy through Telegram. After four years of free service, a $5,000 equity-free grant financed a dedicated platform and enabled Lssya to begin charging clients and paying therapists. The commercial launch did not validate the model: Solomon wrote that the more than 2,000 people served on Telegram did not meaningfully convert to the paid website, while frequent turnover among unpaid team members slowed progress.

After a three-month founder program in Rwanda, Solomon returned to Ethiopia, consulted her team, and made the decision to close Lssya. In July 2024 she began the legal process even though another grant was awaiting disbursement and an unnamed buyer had offered to acquire the company if she remained CEO. The wind-down took nearly two years; she received the final closure document in June 2026 after being assessed more than ETB 70,000 in estimated tax.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Used Telegram to prove demand and deliver care before Lssya could afford a purpose-built product, ultimately serving more than 2,000 people through the free channel.
- Spent a $5,000 grant on building and launching the paid platform, but the existing free audience did not convert at a level that could sustain the business or its team.
- Solomon chose closure after a direct discussion with her team in 2024, declining both a pending grant and an acquisition proposal that required her to remain CEO for several more years.
- Began the formal closure process in July 2024; the process concluded only in June 2026 and carried an estimated tax bill above ETB 70,000.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Lssya shows that social impact and user reach are not substitutes for paid demand. A large free-service audience can still fail to convert when the product moves behind a paywall, particularly in a stigmatized and price-sensitive category. The case also makes founder capacity a concrete strategic constraint: Solomon ended the company despite fresh grant money and a buyout offer because both options prolonged a role she no longer had the capacity to sustain.
