## One-liner

Kayko, a Kigali-based fintech founded in 2021 by brothers Crepin and Kevin Kayisire, raised $1.2M in seed funding in December 2025 from Burrow Capital, the Luxembourg Development Agency, Hanga Ignite by BRD, and develoPPP Ventures, to expand a micro-ERP platform that turns informal SME transaction data into credit access for over 8,500 businesses.

## What happened

Kayko began as a university capstone project before evolving into a micro-ERP and data layer that captures sales, expenses, inventory, and tax-compliance signals from small and medium-sized enterprises in real time. The core insight driving the business is that millions of African SMEs conduct daily transactions but lack the usable, structured financial data that banks and lenders require to extend credit — Kayko's platform converts that everyday business activity into a data trail financial institutions can use for credit scoring and working-capital decisions.

The December 2025 seed round brought together a mix of impact-oriented and development-finance-linked investors (Burrow Capital, the Luxembourg Development Agency, Hanga Ignite by BRD, and develoPPP Ventures) rather than a pure commercial VC syndicate, reflecting the financial-inclusion framing of the business. By the round, Kayko was already serving more than 8,500 SMEs, giving it real transaction-data scale to point to when discussing credit-scoring use cases with financial institution partners. The capital is earmarked for strengthening platform infrastructure and expanding data-driven lending and credit-scoring capabilities.

## Key decisions / turning points

- Started as a university capstone project rather than a conventional startup launch, evolving into the current business only after founders identified the SME data gap as a real, monetizable problem.
- Built a micro-ERP as the core product — a tool SMEs use daily for their own operations — rather than a standalone lending or scoring product, generating the transaction data as a byproduct of genuine day-to-day utility rather than requiring separate data-collection effort.
- Positioned the company as data infrastructure for other lenders (banks, financial institutions) rather than becoming a direct lender itself, avoiding balance-sheet lending risk while still monetizing the data asset.
- Attracted a syndicate weighted toward development-finance and impact investors rather than traditional consumer-tech VCs, aligning capital sources with the financial-inclusion mission.

## Why it matters (pattern / lesson)

Kayko demonstrates a reusable pattern for financial inclusion in markets with large informal SME sectors: building a genuinely useful operational tool (a micro-ERP) that SMEs adopt for its own sake, then using the transaction data it generates as the foundation for credit scoring, rather than trying to collect financial data directly from underserved borrowers who have no independent reason to share it. The Rwanda base also illustrates that fintech infrastructure businesses can attract international development-finance capital even from a market not typically top-of-mind for African startup funding coverage.
